# De Mundo — Dialogue II (De forma mundi, seu de motu magnorum corporum), Knot xiii

*Latin title:* Nodus XIII. Astronomiam esse Errori subiectam [margin: ex raritate observationum]  
*English title:* Knot XIII. That Astronomy is subject to Error [margin: from the rarity of observations]  
*Pages:* PDF pp. 267–270 = printed pp. 256–259 (from Ereunius’ ‘Enimvero me movet’ on p. 256 to ‘…liberorum operatio est.’ on p. 259, where Knot xiv begins).

## Dialogue II, Knot xiii (printed pp. 256–259)

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| [p. 256] Ereu. [Margin: Nodus XIII. Astronomia errori subiecta ex raritate observationum] Enimvero me movet & quam plurimum, Asphali, & miseret Astronomorum qui tam levibus nixi fundamentis tanto se labore damnant. Quale enim est ut in minimis differentiis uno aut altero experimento (quod quanta cura tentatum sit ignoramus) fundetur dogma quod tot laborum deinde sit origo? sicut in eo quod commemoras accidit: ut inventu sint difficillima in pluribus saeculis. Copernicus se non invenisse testatur, usumque fuisse duabus quarum altera sibi contigerit, altera anno septimo Ptolomaei Philometoris, hoc est longe ante Christum natum. Vide modo credibilitatem quam tale exemplum facere possit. Est Eclipsis apparentia facile transiens, multis mediis opus habens ut mensura iusta capiatur: Annorum ratio perturbate expressa, immo plerumque [p. 257] mutila, reparanda per computa ex singulorum hypothesibus fabricata: Et experimentum quod non possit secundo capi & plerumque non a pluribus, sed ab uno exercitatum. Miror proinde non pigere homines sensatos tali arena ingentes calculorum & theorematum moles superstruere. | [p. 256] Ereu. [Margin: Knot XIII. Astronomy subject to error from the rarity of observations] Indeed [Enimvero] [it] moves me, and as much as can be [quam plurimum], Asphalius, and I pity [miseret] the Astronomers who, leaning on such light foundations, condemn themselves to [damnant … labore] so great a labour. For of what sort is it, that on the smallest differences, on one or another experiment [experimento] (with how great care it was tried we do not know), a dogma should be founded which then should be the origin of so many labours? as happens in that which you mention: that they are most difficult to find [inventu] in several centuries. Copernicus testifies that he did not find [them], and that he used two [eclipses], of which one befell himself, the other in the seventh year of Ptolemy Philometor, that is, long before Christ [was] born. See only [modo] the credibility which such an example can make. The appearance of an Eclipse is an easily passing [thing], having need of many means [mediis] that a just measure may be taken: the reckoning [ratio] of the years [is] confusedly [perturbate] expressed, nay mostly [p. 257] mutilated, to be repaired through computations fabricated out of the hypotheses of individual [men] [singulorum]: and [it is] an experiment which cannot be taken a second time, and mostly practised [exercitatum] not by several but by one. I wonder accordingly that it does not irk [pigere] sensible [sensatos] men to build upon such sand huge masses of calculations and theorems. |
| Et quoniam in hoc incidimus, Astronomiam scientiarum paene dixerim Reginam (ut quae situm motumque mundi quae quasi ipsius texturam & formam effingunt, contemplatur) inutilem fere redditam nobis incuria maxime cultorum non mirandum putas & plangendum? | And since we have fallen upon [incidimus] this: Astronomy, the Queen of the sciences I might almost have called [it] [dixerim] (as [being one] which contemplates the situation [situm] and motion of the world, which as it were fashion [effingunt] its texture and form), rendered almost useless to us by the neglect [incuria] chiefly of [its] cultivators [cultorum] — do you think [it] not to be wondered at, and to be lamented [non mirandum putas & plangendum]? |
| [Margin: Item ex instrumentorum defectu.] In primis enim instrumentorum sive exiguitas, sive vitium quos ludos facit? Ptolomaeus Archimedaeum instrumentum habuit suspectum: Tycho Hassianum Torquetum reprehendit. At quot sunt Hassianae observationes quae ex hoc ambiguae audiunt? Quot veterum eodem Archimedis invento vacillaturae? Tychonica instrumenta quanto maiora tanto & minus credibile est absque errore esse facta (latent enim parvi errores) & maiori difficultate in eodem vigore sunt conservabilia. Et tamen imperceptibile paene vitium insignem errorem facit, ut Torquetum illud Hassianum decem paulo minus graduum. | [Margin: Likewise from the defect of instruments.] For in the first place, what games [ludos] does either the smallness or the fault of the instruments make? Ptolemy held the Archimedean instrument suspect: Tycho censured [reprehendit] the Hessian Torquetum. But how many are the Hessian observations which from this are spoken of [audiunt] as ambiguous? How many of the ancients’ [veterum] [observations are] going to waver [vacillaturae] through the same invention of Archimedes? The Tychonic instruments, by how much [quanto] greater, by so much [tanto] both is it less credible that they were made without error (for small errors lie hidden), and with greater difficulty are they preservable [conservabilia] in the same vigour. And yet an almost imperceptible fault makes a notable error, as [it did in] that Hessian Torquetum, [an error] of a little less than ten degrees [decem paulo minus graduum]. |
| [Margin: Item ex refractionibus.] Sequitur refractionum ius in has observationes maxime in regionibus [p. 258] Septentrionalibus ubi crassior subinde nos infestat aer. Errant enim qui refractiones tantummodo in superficie convexa vaporum existimant, neque circa Zenith fieri, fiunt enim etiam in medio aere. | [Margin: Likewise from refractions.] There follows the right [ius] of refractions over [in] these observations, chiefly in the [p. 258] Northern regions, where a thicker air from time to time [subinde] infests us. For they err who reckon refractions [to be] only at the convex surface of the vapours, and [that they are] not made around the Zenith; for they are made also in the middle air. |
| Memini me in nebula maxima Londini aliquando prope Lincolniense hospitium puerum mihi obvium dum longiuscule distaret virum credidisse: Et cum Ulyssipone degerem, Equites in monte adverso ad aerem nocturnum obambulantes augustiori specie quasi gigantaeos esse conspicatum. Audio quoque urinatores in mediis aquis omnia maiora aestimare. | I remember that once, in a very great mist [nebula] at London, near Lincoln’s Inn [Lincolniense hospitium], I believed a boy coming to meet me [mihi obvium], while he was rather far off [longiuscule], [to be] a man: and when I was living at Lisbon [Ulyssipone], [that I] beheld Horsemen [Equites] on the opposite mountain, walking about [obambulantes] in the night air [ad aerem nocturnum], of a more august appearance [specie], as if gigantic. I hear also that divers [urinatores] in the midst of the waters estimate all things larger. |
| Sunt autem refractiones difficillimae aestimationis & saepissime fallunt etiam accuratos. Effectus quoque insignes habent. Cum solem & lunam in Eclipsi posita simul ostendant, cumque quindecim totos dies solis ortum in summo Septentrione aliquando praeverterint; & experientia me propria docuerit quidlibet nonaginta gradibus minus valere vim refractivam. | But refractions are of most difficult estimation, and very often deceive even accurate [men]. They have also notable effects: since they show the sun and the moon, placed in Eclipse, at the same time [simul]; and since they have once [aliquando] anticipated [praeverterint] the rising of the sun in the uttermost North by fifteen whole days; and [since] my own experience has taught me that the refractive force [vim refractivam] avails [valere] at whatever [quidlibet] [is] less than ninety degrees. |
| His adiice virorum etiam magnorum indulgentiam, aliorum sese temere miscentium imperitiam, & nescio quomodo insitam humano generi & paene inevitabilem quandam vim, qua visa ad praeconceptas notitias proclives trahimus: & credimus visum potius labascere quam temere fortassis praesumptam scientiam. | To these add the indulgence of even great men, the unskilfulness [imperitiam] of others rashly mixing themselves [in], and a certain force, implanted I know not how in the human race and almost inevitable, by which we, inclined [proclives], drag the things seen [visa] to [our] preconceived notices [notitias]: and we believe that the sight [visum] totters [labascere] rather than the science [scientiam], perhaps rashly presumed. |
| Ex hoc [p. 259] alius immedicabilis error nascitur, ut quoniam Astronomia ex plurium variis temporibus acceptis experimentis pendeat, Errores autem praeteritorum emendari non possint: rursus tanta ubique succrescat Astronomorum copia: Nesciat fere Astronomus quem sequatur, cui inhaereat; & tantundem fere sit periculi, si anxie optimis inhaereamus, quantum si temere occurrentibus iungamur. Ita summa cura & periculo & difficultate res Astronomica geri debet, & paucorum, & sapientum & foelicium, & liberorum operatio est. | From this [p. 259] another incurable [immedicabilis] error is born: that — since Astronomy depends on experiments of many [men], taken at various times, but the Errors of past [men] [praeteritorum] cannot be emended; [and] again so great an abundance of Astronomers grows up [succrescat] everywhere — the Astronomer scarcely [fere] knows whom he should follow, to whom he should cling; and there is almost as much of danger if we anxiously cling to the best as if we rashly join ourselves to those who come in the way [occurrentibus]. So with the highest care and danger and difficulty must the Astronomical business [res Astronomica] be carried on [geri], and it is the operation of few, and of wise, and fortunate [foelicium], and free [liberorum] [men]. |

## Words and phrases with alternative or special meanings

- ***experimentum*** — rendered “experiment”. observation; trial; experience White uses experimentum for a single astronomical observation (here an eclipse record); not a controlled experiment in the modern sense.
- ***raritas (observationum)*** — rendered “rarity”. scarceness, infrequency; also thinness Marginal title: astronomy is error-prone because decisive observations (eclipses at given positions) recur rarely.
- ***dogma*** — rendered “dogma”. doctrine, tenet Greek loan, neutral sense: an established astronomical doctrine (e.g. lunar apogee/perigee values).
- ***inventu*** — rendered “to find”. supine: in the finding Supine in -u with difficillima: ‘most difficult to find’.
- ***ratio (annorum)*** — rendered “reckoning”. account, computation, chronology Chronology of ancient regnal years, which must be reconstructed by each chronologer’s hypothesis.
- ***hypothesis*** — rendered “hypothesis”. assumption; chronological/astronomical model Here the chronological and astronomical assumptions of individual computers.
- ***sensatus*** — rendered “sensible”. judicious, possessed of sense Late-Latin; ironical against astronomers.
- ***arena*** — rendered “sand”. sandy ground Biblical allusion (Matt. 7:26, house built on sand).
- ***Astronomia scientiarum Regina*** — rendered “the Queen of the sciences”. — Usually said of theology or metaphysics; White applies it (with paene dixerim) to astronomy as contemplating the world’s ‘texture and form’ — tie to Dialogue II ‘de forma mundi’.
- ***situs*** — rendered “situation”. position, site Arrangement of the world’s parts; with motus constitutes its ‘form’ in White’s Dialogue II.
- ***incuria / cultores*** — rendered “neglect / cultivators”. carelessness / practitioners, devotees Blame placed on practitioners, not the science.
- ***ludos facere*** — rendered “make games”. play tricks, cause mischief Idiom: ‘what tricks does… play!’.
- ***Archimedaeum instrumentum*** — rendered “Archimedean instrument”. — Probably Archimedes’ device for the sun’s apparent diameter (Sand-Reckoner), or an equinoctial ring; identification uncertain (see notes).
- ***Torquetum Hassianum*** — rendered “Hessian Torquetum”. torquetum = equatorial/ecliptic armillary-type instrument Instrument of Landgrave Wilhelm IV of Hesse-Kassel’s observatory, whose data Tycho criticised.
- ***ambiguae audiunt*** — rendered “are spoken of as ambiguous”. have the reputation of being doubtful audire = be called, be reputed (bene/male audire).
- ***vacillare*** — rendered “waver”. totter, be unsteady Of the reliability of old observations.
- ***conservabilis*** — rendered “preservable”. maintainable Large instruments are hard to keep ‘in the same vigour’ (true adjustment).
- ***refractio / vis refractiva*** — rendered “refraction / refractive force”. bending of light Atmospheric refraction, the third cause of error; White denies that it occurs only at the ‘convex surface of the vapours’ and not near the zenith.
- ***ius (refractionum)*** — rendered “right”. jurisdiction, power, sway Refractions ‘have jurisdiction over’ observations.
- ***Lincolniense hospitium*** — rendered “Lincoln’s Inn”. — The London Inn of Court; autobiographical detail.
- ***Ulyssipo*** — rendered “Lisbon”. — White taught at the English College, Lisbon (1630s).
- ***urinator*** — rendered “diver”. — Classical (Varro, Livy); magnification under water.
- ***Cum … ostendant (eclipse)*** — rendered “show the sun and moon … at the same time”. — Horizontal eclipse (selenelion): eclipsed moon and sun both visible above the horizon because of refraction.
- ***quindecim totos dies … praeverterint*** — rendered “anticipated … by fifteen whole days”. — Alludes to the Dutch wintering on Nova Zembla (Barents, 1597), who saw the sun about 15 days before its computed return (the ‘Novaya Zemlya effect’).
- ***notitia*** — rendered “notice”. cognition, concept Glossary term: preconceived notions bend perception.
- ***visum / visa*** — rendered “sight / things seen”. vision; appearances Contrast of the senses and ‘presumed science’.
- ***immedicabilis*** — rendered “incurable”. not to be remedied Ovidian/Virgilian adjective.
- ***praeteritorum*** — rendered “of past [men]”. of past things/times Masc. (earlier astronomers) or neut. (past observations); the sense is close either way.
- ***occurrentibus*** — rendered “those who come in the way”. whoever one happens upon Contrast with optimis (the best authorities).
- ***foelix*** — rendered “fortunate”. happy; successful Spelling foelix for felix kept.
- ***liberi*** — rendered “free [men]”. children; also liberal Free = at leisure, of independent means (liberal arts); not ‘children’.

## Translator's notes

- Unit: Dialogue II, Knot xiii (complete in one part). PDF pp. 267–270 = printed pp. 256–259. Offset +11 holds (running heads 256–259; signatures Kk p. 257, Kk ij p. 259). Begins with Ereunius’ ‘Enimvero me movet…’ (margin ‘Nodus XIII’, p. 256); ends ‘…liberorum operatio est.’ on p. 259, where Knot xiv (‘Falsam esse motus circularis suppositionem’, margin) begins with ‘Oblitus eram malorum…’ (still Ereunius’ speech). The FM2 table title is the shorter ‘Astronomiam esse Errori subiectam’.
- The whole knot is a single speech of Ereunius; it is divided into rows by paragraph/topic (eclipses; astronomy the queen of sciences; instruments; refraction; personal examples; effects of refraction; human factors; multiplicity of authorities).
- Marginal notes: p. 256 ‘Nodus XIII. Astronomia errori subiecta ex raritate observationum’; p. 257 ‘Item ex instrumentorum defectu’ and ‘Item ex refractionibus’ (both placed at the lines where they stand).
- Uncertain points: (1) ‘quidlibet nonaginta gradibus minus valere vim refractivam’ — read as ‘refraction has force at any altitude less than 90°’ (i.e. everywhere except the zenith), consistent with the preceding denial; ‘quidlibet’ could also be taken as ‘to any extent’. (2) ‘decem paulo minus graduum’ — an error of almost ten degrees in the Hessian torquetum is implausibly large; the text clearly prints ‘graduum’ (minutes may be meant); the construction is elliptical. (3) ‘non mirandum putas & plangendum?’ — literally ‘do you think [it] not to be wondered at and to be lamented?’; possibly ‘do you not think it to be wondered at and lamented?’ (non attached to putas). (4) ‘Archimedaeum instrumentum’ and Ptolemy’s suspicion of it are not securely identified. (5) Copernicus’ two eclipses (one his own, one in the 7th year of Ptolemy Philometor, 174 BC) presumably refer to De revolutionibus IV on lunar latitude/anomaly; not checked against Copernicus.
- Verification pass: 5 corrections after the sentence-by-sentence check — (1) ‘quod … deinde sit origo’ given subjunctive force (‘should be’); (2) ‘facere possit’ rendered ‘can make’ not ‘could make’; (3) ‘reprehendit’ taken as perfect (‘censured’) to match ‘habuit’; (4) the ut-clause of p. 259 restructured: quoniam governs pendeat / possint / succrescat, and the result is ‘nesciat … sit’; (5) transcription ‘proclives’ (print ‘procliues’), first misread as ‘procliviores’.
- Conventions: as in earlier units — long s normalised, æ/ę → ae, accents dropped, u/v normalised to v; Ptolomaeus, foelix kept as printed; supplied words in [square brackets]; Latin of technical or ambiguous words in [brackets].
