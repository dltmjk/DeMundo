# De Mundo — Dialogue I (De materia mundi), Knot vi

*Latin title:* Nodus VI. Unde invaluerit de orbibus coelestibus opinio  
*English title:* Knot VI. Whence the opinion about the heavenly orbs grew strong [invaluerit]  
*Pages:* PDF pp. 55–58 = printed pp. 44–47 (from 'Asphal. Etiam hoc, Ereuni, nobis aperi' on p. 44 to '…hic turpissimus error permanavit.' on p. 47; Knot vii begins lower on p. 47)

## Dialogue I, Knot vi (printed pp. 44–47)

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| [p. 44] [Margin: Nodus VI. Unde invaluerit de orbibus coelestibus opinio.] Asphal. Etiam hoc, Ereuni, nobis aperi, si quid de eo cogitasti, unde Aristoteli Philosophiae Principi, viro subtilissimi ingenii, in scientiis versatissimo, et quod caput est, Dialecticae praeceptis imbutissimo acciderit, in errorem adeo insipidum et primo se se ostentantem aspectui, incidere. Fateor enim stupore me corripi, quoties hoc attentus considero. | [p. 44] [Margin: Knot VI. Whence the opinion about the heavenly orbs grew strong [invaluerit].] Asphal. This also, Ereunius, open to us, if you have thought anything about it: whence it befell [acciderit] Aristotle, the Prince of Philosophy, a man of most subtle wit [ingenii], most versed in the sciences [scientiis], and, what is the head [caput] [of it], most imbued with the precepts of Dialectic, to fall into an error so insipid and showing itself [se se ostentantem] at the first look [aspectui]. For I confess that I am seized with stupor as often as, attentive, I consider this. |
| Ereu. Adiice his ingentem Philosophorum turbam, tot saeculis consecutam, et hos non Ethnicos tantummodo, sed ex Christianis et Mauris, viros eminenter doctissimos. Aristoteles enim, quantus fuerit, homo fuit, neque in quocumque homine, non animadversam habuisse aliquam sequelam, quicquam miraculi est. Cum et in summis Geometris, Paralogismum aliquem eos fefellisse notatum sit. | Ereu. Add to these the huge crowd of Philosophers which followed [consecutam] through so many ages, and these not Heathens [Ethnicos] only, but from among Christians and Moors, men eminently most learned. For Aristotle, however great he may have been [quantus fuerit], was a man; nor in any man whatever is it anything of a miracle [quicquam miraculi] to have had some consequence [sequelam] not noticed. Since [Cum] even in the highest Geometers it has been noted that some Paralogism has deceived them. |
| Possim et in huius, et in praecedentis saeculi summis viris, exempla urgere, si quid attineret. Sed quale est tot tantosque viros (et quos videas nihil existimavisse scrupuli, ab ipsius authoritate discedere) per tot saecula in errorem et talem pertraxisse. Talem (inquam) Asphali: [p. 45] Non enim quae de Arthuro et ipsius Equitibus finguntur, vel Homericas fabulas persuasu magis difficiles opiner, quam illam Coelorum compositionem, quam proxima nos saecula erudierunt. | I could also, both in the highest men of this and of the preceding age, press [urgere] examples, if it were anything to the purpose [si quid attineret]. But of what sort is it [quale est] to have drawn [pertraxisse] so many and so great men (and [men] whom you may see to have reckoned it nothing of a scruple [nihil … scrupuli] to depart from his authority) through so many ages into an error, and such [an error]. Such (I say), Asphalius: [p. 45] for I would not think the things which are feigned about Arthur and his Knights, or the Homeric fables, more difficult in persuasion [persuasu] than that composition of the Heavens which the nearest ages have taught [erudierunt] us. |
| Neque tamen unquam vel maiori copia vel foelicioribus ingeniis, naturam se iactavisse mihi persuadeam. Ut autem unde haec nobis contagio increverit, videas; Astronomiam in ipsis primordiis, Aristotelis aetate fuisse cogita: Motus quosdam Astrorum, sed regulares comprehensos; periodos Solis et Lunae calculatas, admirationem mundo et ingentem de Circulari Coelorum constantia opinionem concitavisse. | And yet I would not persuade myself that nature ever vaunted herself [se iactavisse] with either a greater abundance [copia] or more fortunate wits [ingeniis]. But that you may see whence this contagion has grown upon [increverit] us: consider that Astronomy was in its very first beginnings [primordiis] in the age of Aristotle; [that] certain motions of the Stars, but [only] regular [ones], comprehended [comprehensos], [and] periods of the Sun and Moon calculated, had stirred up admiration in the world and a huge opinion about the Circular constancy of the Heavens. |
| Erant praeterea spectatissima universi membra, quae vocamus Astra, etiam tum communi, non vulgi dumtaxat, sed et sapientium suffragio dignissima, quae bonorum nobis omnium causae haberentur, et eo nomine Deorum qualitate consecrarentur. | There were besides the most regarded [spectatissima] members of the universe, which we call Stars, even then, by the common vote [suffragio] not of the vulgar only but also of the wise, most worthy that they should be held causes for us of all good things, and on that account [eo nomine] be consecrated with the quality of Gods. |
| Hoc in rerum statu celebrem illam demonstrationem de motu recto et circulari procreavit Aristoteles, et systema illud telluris pro centro obfirmatae, circumeuntiumque ordine magnorum corporum, iam per ipsius demonstrationem, in Deos relatorum, ingentis plausus et ad famam momenti expertus, quid mirum si quae sequerentur, incongrua, vel non advertit, vel tantae posthabuit famae? | In this state of things Aristotle procreated [procreavit] that celebrated demonstration about straight and circular motion; and having found by experience [expertus] that system — of the earth made fast [obfirmatae] for a centre, and of the great bodies going around in order, [bodies] already, through his demonstration, referred among the Gods — [to be] of huge applause and of moment [momenti] for fame, what wonder if the incongruous things which would follow he either did not advert to, or set behind [posthabuit] so great a fame? |
| Subsecuti sunt Astronomi [p. 46] Physicarum contemplationum, ut usu fit plerumque, negligentiores. Qui cum allegata pro terrae stabilimento, invicta crederent; Circularem vero Astrorum et Coelestium cursum unicum postulatum haberent, quo accepto, eorum motus certis calculis constringerent, et fortasse aliquod in ipsis numen vel revererentur vel metuerent, pro motuum necessitate, corpora immensa, quae Aristoteles coniectura sola statuerat, ipsi non confirmarunt modo; sed et Excentricis et Epicyclis secuerunt et auxerunt; in hoc a sententia Aristotelis alieni, si quid ego capio. | There followed Astronomers, [p. 46] more negligent, as happens by usage [usu] for the most part, of Physical contemplations. Who, since they believed the things alleged for the stabiliment [stabilimento] of the earth [to be] unconquered; but held the Circular course of the Stars and Heavenly [bodies] as their one postulate [postulatum], which being accepted, they might constrain their motions by certain calculations; and perhaps either revered or feared some divinity [numen] in them: [therefore], for the necessity of the motions, the immense bodies which Aristotle had set up [statuerat] by conjecture alone, they themselves not only confirmed, but also cut and increased with Excentrics and Epicycles; in this alien from the opinion [sententia] of Aristotle, if I grasp anything. |
| Ipse enim Diis suis membra discontinua, deformia, nullum astrum in se se deferentia, et motibus, non ipso corpore, sed extima tantum superficie circularibus, neque ipsi mundo concentricis, neque sibi, indigna et respuenda censuisset. Sed Astronomis haec necessaria erant, ut per circuli sectiones et homogeneos calculos, astrorum motus in carceres et artem reducerent. | For he himself would have judged members discontinuous, deformed, carrying [deferentia] no star in themselves, and with motions circular not in the body itself but only in the outermost surface, concentric neither to the world itself nor to themselves, unworthy of his Gods and to be spat out [respuenda]. But these things were necessary to the Astronomers, so that through sections of the circle and homogeneous calculations they might bring back [reducerent] the motions of the stars into barriers [carceres] and art. |
| Interea senescebant sensim caeterae Philosophorum tribus, et ad Peripateticam, et ratio, et iam multitudo discipulos cogebat. Cuius Magistri cum a Mathematicis, Astronomica quasi obsignata acciperent, praeterea et Deorum suorum metu, et Magistri reverentia tenerentur, ruitum est passis, ut aiunt, valuis, in hoc dogma. | Meanwhile the other tribes of the Philosophers were growing old by degrees, and to the Peripatetic [tribe] both reason [ratio] and now [its] multitude was driving [cogebat] disciples. The Masters of which [Cuius Magistri], since they received from the Mathematicians the Astronomical [things] as if sealed [obsignata], [and] besides were held both by fear of their Gods and by reverence of the Master, there was a rushing [ruitum est] with doors spread open [passis valuis], as they say, into this dogma. |
| Debuerat profecto Christianis et Mauris cautelam praestitisse Gentilium superstitio; Christianis maxime, quorum parentes a Coeli immortalitate plerumque abhorruerant. Sed occupaverat eos et praesumpta de Magistro opinio, et Astronomorum consensio, et denique stupenda illa demonstrationis facies, quae fulgore suo omnium oculos perstrinxerat, ut potius superstruere positis, quam eorum firmitatem tentare elegerint. Et sic ad nos usque diffusus hic turpissimus error permanavit. [Knot vii follows on p. 47.] | Surely the superstition of the Gentiles ought to have furnished [praestitisse] caution to Christians and Moors; to Christians most, whose parents [parentes] had for the most part shrunk from the immortality of the Heaven. But there had seized [occupaverat] them both the opinion presumed [praesumpta] about the Master, and the consent of the Astronomers, and lastly that stupendous face of the demonstration, which by its brightness had dazzled [perstrinxerat] the eyes of all, so that they chose rather to build upon the things laid down [positis] than to try their firmness. And thus, diffused all the way to us, this most foul error has flowed through [permanavit]. [Knot vii follows on p. 47.] |

## Words and phrases with alternative or special meanings

- ***invalescere*** — rendered “grow strong”. 'prevail', 'gain currency'. Margin title: how the opinion of solid celestial orbs came to prevail.
- ***Philosophiae Princeps*** — rendered “Prince of Philosophy”. 'chief', 'foremost' of philosophy. Standard scholastic honorific of Aristotle ('the Philosopher').
- ***quod caput est*** — rendered “what is the head [of it]”. 'what is chief', 'most important of all'. Ciceronian idiom (caput = main point).
- ***se ostentare primo aspectui*** — rendered “show itself at the first look”. 'obvious at first sight'. The error is evident even to a glance; White's rhetorical stress on the absurdity of solid orbs.
- ***Ethnici*** — rendered “Heathens”. 'Gentiles', 'pagans' (Greek ἐθνικοί). Contrasted with Christians and Moors (Arab/Muslim philosophers such as Averroes).
- ***Mauri*** — rendered “Moors”. 'Arabs', i.e. Islamic philosophers. Early-modern Latin for the Arabic commentators (Averroes, Avicenna, al-Bitruji).
- ***sequela*** — rendered “consequence”. 'what follows' logically; 'implication'. As glossary; a consequence of his own thesis that Aristotle failed to notice.
- ***quicquam miraculi*** — rendered “anything of a miracle”. 'anything to wonder at'. Partitive genitive; miraculum in its root sense 'thing to marvel at'.
- ***paralogismus*** — rendered “Paralogism”. 'fallacious reasoning' (a formally invalid argument unwittingly made). Aristotelian logic (Soph. El.); even geometers commit them.
- ***si quid attineret*** — rendered “if it were anything to the purpose”. 'if it were relevant'.
- ***nihil scrupuli existimare*** — rendered “reckon it nothing of a scruple”. 'have no hesitation'; scrupulus = small sharp stone, hence doubt. Refers to later men who had no qualms about departing from Aristotle elsewhere, yet kept this error.
- ***persuasu difficilis*** — rendered “difficult in persuasion”. 'hard to be persuaded of / believe'. Supine-like ablative of persuasus.
- ***Arthurus et Equites*** — rendered “Arthur and his Knights”. Arthurian romance. Romance and Homer as the type of incredible fable.
- ***erudire*** — rendered “teach”. 'instruct', 'train'.
- ***se iactare*** — rendered “vaunt herself”. 'boast', 'display herself'. Nature never showed off more abundant or happier talents than in Aristotle's age — or (other reading) never since; White's point is that great wits erred nonetheless.
- ***contagio*** — rendered “contagion”. 'infection'; 'contact'. The error spreads like a disease.
- ***primordia*** — rendered “first beginnings”. 'origins', 'rudiments'.
- ***comprehendere*** — rendered “comprehend”. 'grasp', 'determine'. As glossary; here observationally determined motions.
- ***spectatissimus*** — rendered “most regarded”. 'most conspicuous', 'most esteemed'. Both senses: most visible and most honoured.
- ***suffragium*** — rendered “vote”. 'judgment', 'support'. As glossary.
- ***eo nomine*** — rendered “on that account”. lit. 'under that name/head'. Commercial-legal idiom.
- ***procreare*** — rendered “procreate”. 'beget', 'produce'. Kept literal; the demonstration as offspring.
- ***obfirmare*** — rendered “make fast”. 'fix firmly'. The earth fixed as centre.
- ***momentum*** — rendered “moment”. 'weight', 'importance'; also mechanical 'moment'. Here 'of importance for fame'.
- ***posthabere*** — rendered “set behind”. 'subordinate', 'neglect in favour of'.
- ***stabilimentum*** — rendered “stabiliment”. 'stability', 'fixing'. The arguments for the earth's immobility.
- ***postulatum*** — rendered “postulate”. 'assumption demanded'. Euclidean/astronomical sense: uniform circular motion as the astronomers' sole hypothesis (cf. 'saving the phenomena').
- ***numen*** — rendered “divinity”. 'divine power', 'nod/will of a god'. Ancient star-worship.
- ***excentrici / epicycli*** — rendered “Excentrics / Epicycles”. eccentric deferents and epicycles. Ptolemaic devices; White argues Aristotle's homocentric spheres would have rejected them (a point also made by Averroes and al-Bitruji).
- ***deferre / deferens*** — rendered “carry”. the 'deferent' circle of Ptolemaic astronomy. Play on the technical term: orbs that carry no star.
- ***extima superficies*** — rendered “outermost surface”.  The orbs move circularly only at their surface, not in their whole body.
- ***carceres*** — rendered “barriers”. 'starting-gates' of a racecourse; also 'prisons'. The astronomers 'confine' stellar motions in a racecourse image (cf. cursus); keeps both senses.
- ***respuere*** — rendered “spit out”. 'reject with disgust'.
- ***tribus*** — rendered “tribes”. 'sects', schools. Philosophical schools as tribes.
- ***obsignata*** — rendered “sealed”. 'under seal', i.e. authenticated, not to be opened. Legal image: received like a sealed document.
- ***ruitum est passis valuis*** — rendered “there was a rushing with doors spread open”. 'they rushed headlong / with open arms'. Proverbial ('as they say'); impersonal passive.
- ***parentes*** — rendered “parents”. = the Church Fathers (forefathers in the faith). The Fathers (e.g. Basil, Augustine) generally rejected the Aristotelian incorruptibility of the heavens.
- ***Coeli immortalitas*** — rendered “immortality of the Heaven”. 'incorruptibility'. Aristotelian doctrine of the ungenerated, incorruptible fifth body (De caelo I).
- ***praesumpta opinio*** — rendered “presumed opinion”. 'preconceived opinion', 'prejudice'.
- ***perstringere oculos*** — rendered “dazzle the eyes”. lit. 'graze', 'blind'. Classical idiom.
- ***superstruere positis*** — rendered “build upon the things laid down”. 'build on established foundations'. Architectural image: positis = foundations/posits.
- ***permanare*** — rendered “flow through”. 'spread', 'penetrate'.

## Translator's notes

- Unit: Dialogue I, Knot vi, complete in one part. PDF pp. 55–58 = printed pp. 44–47. It begins on p. 44 with Asphalius' 'Etiam hoc, Ereuni, nobis aperi' (margin 'Nodus VI. Unde inualuerit de orbibus coelestibus opinio', as in FM2) and ends on p. 47 with Ereunius' '…turpissimus error permanavit.' Knot vii ('Demonstratio Aristotelis pro Immortalitate Coeli', so the margin; FM2: 'Demonstratio Aristotelis de natura Coeli') begins on p. 47 at Asphalius' 'At nisi quicquam praefers' (PDF 58). Offset +11 holds (p. 47 = PDF 58).
- Rows: one row per speech, Ereunius' long speech split at sentence breaks; printed page breaks marked [p. n]. Only one marginal note in this unit (the knot title).
- Conventions as in glossary.md: long s → s; æ/œ → ae/oe; consonantal u → v; accents dropped; '&' → et; 'Demõstratio' etc. expanded. Spellings kept as printed: 'Adiice', 'authoritate', 'foelicioribus', 'caeterae', 'coniectura', 'Coelorum'.
- Readings: (a) p. 45, '&iam tum' is read as 'etiam tum' (the &-ligature for et), 'even then'. (b) p. 45, 'iactauisse' is partly broken in the scan but legible at 300 dpi. (c) p. 46, 'Cuius Magistri' is taken as nominative plural ('the masters of which [sect]'), the subject of acciperent/tenerentur; it could be genitive ('of whose Master'), but then the clause lacks a subject, and 'Magistri reverentia' follows separately. The sentence is an anacoluthon: the cum-clause has its subject, but the main verb is impersonal ('ruitum est'). (d) p. 44, 'Sed quale est … pertraxisse' is printed with a full stop, though it reads as an exclamation; the punctuation is kept. No illegible words.
- Interpretation: (i) In 'naturam se iactavisse' (p. 45) the sense may be either that nature never showed greater talents than in Aristotle's age, or that it never showed greater ones than in recent ages; the translation keeps the Latin's ambiguity. (ii) 'parentes' (p. 47) is rendered literally; it almost certainly means the Church Fathers.
- Verification pass: the transcription was checked again against the page images (300 dpi crop of p. 45), and the English against the Latin, sentence by sentence (cases, tenses, moods, negatives, comparatives, omissions). It produced 3 corrections: '& iam tum' re-read as 'etiam tum' and the English changed from 'and now then' to 'even then'; 'Cuius Magistri' changed from genitive 'of whose Master' to nominative plural, with the anacoluthon noted; the double negative 'neque … non animadversam … quicquam miraculi est' restored to 'nor … is it anything of a miracle to have had some consequence not noticed' (the draft had dropped 'not').
