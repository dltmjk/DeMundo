# De Mundo — Dialogue III (De causis mundi), Knot xviii

*Latin title:* Nodus XVIII. An mundus sit finiendus?  
*English title:* Knot XVIII. Whether the world is to be ended [finiendus]?  
*Pages:* PDF pp. 446–454 = printed pp. 435–443 (from Andabata’s ‘Actum entis in potentia’ on p. 435 to Ereunius’ ‘…ex necessitate finem habiturum’ on p. 443).

## Dialogue III, Knot xviii (printed pp. 435–443)

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| Andab. Actum entis in potentia prout in potentia; seu, quod idem est, rei quae ad [Margin: Nodus XVIII. An mundus sit finiendus?] perfectionem tendit, viam seu progressionem ad eam, dum neque adhuc illam habet & tamen versus illam agitur a bono genio. | Andab. The act [actum] of a being in potency, according as [it is] in potency; or, which is the same, of a thing which tends to perfection, the way or progression to it, while it neither yet has it and yet is driven [agitur] towards it by a good genius [a bono genio]. [Margin: Knot XVIII. Whether the world is to be ended [finiendus]?] |
| Ereu. Hoc si sit, iam de motus ratione erit terminus, ut motus existere non possit nisi propter terminum, id est, ut movens non possit movere nisi ex intentione termini: & quod ex hoc sequitur, motum non posse intendi, nisi propter illum, seu non esse bonum ex se, sed utile quiddam ratione termini. | Ereu. If this be so, already the term [terminus] will be of the account [ratio] of motion, so that motion cannot exist except for the sake of the term, that is, so that the mover cannot move except out of intention [intentione] of the term: and, what follows from this, that motion cannot be intended [intendi] except for the sake of that [term], or that it is not good of itself, but something useful by reason [ratione] of the term. |
| Andab. Sequuntur quae dicis ex hac definitione, quae tamen non usquequaque video cum rerum natura cohaerere; video enim aliquos motus sine terminis; sicut est motus Musicus, & ii communiter qui propter ipsam iucunditatem quae in motu est, petuntur. Aliam itaque definitionem subiicio; ut eum dicam non aliud esse quam continuam mutationem: hanc enim video vel Mathematico motui convenire, ubi nulla ratio boni respicitur, cum Aristoteles ex natura definitionem confecerit. | Andab. The things which you say follow from this definition, which [definition] however I do not see to cohere in every respect [usquequaque] with the nature of things; for I see some motions without terms; as is Musical motion, and those commonly which are sought for the sake of the very pleasantness [iucunditatem] which is in the motion. Another definition therefore I put in its place [subiicio]: that I should say it [motion] to be nothing other than continuous change [mutationem]; for this [definition] I see to agree even with Mathematical motion, where no account [ratio] of good is regarded, whereas Aristotle made up [confecerit] the definition out of nature. |
| Ereu. Mutatio vero quid est? | Ereu. But change [mutatio], what is it? |
| Andab. Aiunt quidem, esse post non esse, vel non esse post esse; Verum quoniam quod non est, neque mutari potest, erit mutatio tantummodo non esse post esse. | Andab. They say indeed [that it is] to be after not to be, or not to be after to be; But since that which is not cannot be changed either, change will be only not to be after to be. |
| Asphal. Magno fructu definitionem mutavisti, cum enim prius motus non esset bonum, nunc etiam malum fecisti, de esse enim transire ad non esse, malum est. | Asphal. With great profit [fructu] have you changed the definition: for whereas before motion was not a good, now you have made it even an evil; for to pass from to be to not to be is evil. |
| Andab. Foret, nisi esse τῷ non esse adiunctum foret, cuius coniunctio si melioris sit, faciet motum bonum. | Andab. It would be, unless to be were joined to the not to be [τῷ non esse]; whose conjunction, if it be of a better [thing], will make the motion good. |
| Ereu. Ergo hoc ubique constat, motum idcirco tantummodo bonum esse, quia res mutatur ad melius: melius autem terminus est; motus itaque non nisi respectu termini bonus est, neque desiderabilis, neque qui sub intentionem cadere possit. Immo hoc etiam nacti erimus, ut ii motus in quibus terminus, ut vocant, ad quem aequalis vel deterior sit termino a quo, non possint desiderari, & propter sese intendi. Propone modo tibi, si humana natura ex nostro orbe deforet, caeterorum animalium, & plantarum, & mixtorum successivae generationes & corruptiones, num aliud praeter continuum quendam motum sine termino forent? | Ereu. Therefore this stands everywhere, that motion is good only on this account, because the thing is changed to the better: but the better is the term; motion therefore is not good except in respect of the term, nor desirable, nor [such] as can fall under intention [intentionem]. Nay, we shall also have obtained [nacti erimus] this, that those motions in which the term, as they call it, to which [ad quem] is equal or worse than the term from which [a quo], cannot be desired, and be intended for their own sake. Propose now to yourself, if human nature were lacking from our orb [orbe], [whether] the successive generations and corruptions of the other animals, and of plants, and of mixed [bodies] [mixtorum] would be anything other than a certain continuous motion without term? |
| Andab. Praeterea nihil. | Andab. Besides that, nothing. |
| Ereu. Verum hic motus si infinitus foret, nunquam acciperet terminum, & quod sequitur, propter terminum intendi non posset. Num itaque infinitus esse posset, vide. | Ereu. But this motion, if it were infinite, would never receive a term, and, what follows, could not be intended for the sake of a term. Whether therefore it could be infinite, see. |
| Andab. Sed hoc considerare oportet, certos ubique terminos huic motui statos esse in nova videlicet substantiarum generatione, quae finis & termini generationem habet ad alterationes praecedentes. | Andab. But this one must consider, that fixed terms are everywhere set [statos] to this motion, namely in the new generation of substances, which has the generation [generationem] (i.e. the character) of end and term in regard to the preceding alterations. |
| Ereu. Totius itaque motus nullus finis est, neque intendi potest, quod tamen necessarium est ut sit, sed partes: itaque vide, num melius est animal sequens praecedente? filius patre? si non est, neque cur decedat pater filio ulla superest ratio. Si vero sit, spectandum num tanti sit haec melioratio, ut motus propter eam futurus sit? | Ereu. Of the whole motion therefore there is no end, nor can it be intended (which however it is necessary that it be), but [only] the parts: see therefore whether the following animal is better than the preceding? the son than the father? If it is not, there remains no reason [ratio] either why the father should give way [decedat] to the son. But if it be, it must be looked into whether this betterment [melioratio] be of such worth [tanti] that the motion should be going to be [futurus sit] for its sake? |
| Andab. Non hoc disputo. Palam siquidem est peiores potius patribus filios & semper progeniem vitiosiorem quantum a primigenia natura aberramus; vel certe si quando aliqua sit melior ratio, cito defluere & in idem rediri. Ex hac itaque parte collectionem tuam non criminor, sed hoc obiicio non posse ex causis naturalibus aliter contingere, quam ut perpetua successione continuentur animalium saecula. | Andab. This I do not dispute. For it is plain that sons are rather worse than their fathers, and the progeny ever more vicious, in so far as [quantum] we stray from the first-born [primigenia] nature; or certainly, if at any time some stock [ratio] be better, [that] it quickly flows down [defluere] and [that] there is a return [rediri] to the same [state]. On this side therefore I do not accuse your inference [collectionem]; but this I object: that it cannot happen from natural causes otherwise than that the generations [saecula] of animals are continued by perpetual succession. |
| Ereu. Hoc itaque subiicio, neque posse ipsa saecula perpetuari: quod enim ex intentione fieri non potest; neque fieri potest quia malum & inordinatum est: Entia vero nolunt male gubernari: quia scilicet ab ipsa bonitate unde oriuntur, bonitatis & τοῦ bene esse necessitatem traxerunt. | Ereu. This therefore I put next [subiicio]: that the generations [saecula] themselves cannot either be perpetuated: for what cannot come about out of intention [ex intentione], cannot come about either [neque], because it is evil and disordered: but [vero] beings [Entia] will not be governed badly: because, namely, from the very goodness whence they arise they have drawn the necessity of goodness and of the well-being [τοῦ bene esse]. |
| Andab. Siquidem Deus ex intentione boni assequendi mundum condidisset, viderentur mihi quae dicis legitime disputata. Nunc postquam decretum est Deum ex bonitate non cum movente, sed iam subsistente, ubertatem suam profundere, non intelligo quare illa bonitas non effunderet creaturas, quales esse possent, si non esse possent meliores. | Andab. If indeed God had founded the world out of intention of obtaining a good, the things which you say would seem to me lawfully argued. Now, after it has been decreed [decretum est] that God, out of a goodness not as moving [him] [non cum movente], but already subsisting, pours forth his abundance [ubertatem], I do not understand why that goodness should not pour out creatures such as they could be, if they could not be better. |
| Ereu. Hoc fortassis dici potest, nisi hoc ipsum incommodi iam incidisset non posse illas esse, quales deberent esse: Si enim motum sine termino intrinsece esse malum iam conclusum est, si creaturas non posse aliter se habere intendas, intrinsece malas esse affirmes; quod non est sustinere, sed cedere, & etiam fonti illarum apros, ut loquitur poeta, immittere. Hoc itaque conclusum esto, motum orbis, excluso genere humano, infinitum non fuisse futurum. | Ereu. This perhaps can be said, unless this very [piece] of inconvenience [incommodi] had already befallen [incidisset]: that they cannot be such as they ought to be. For if it has already been concluded that motion without a term is intrinsically evil, if you hold [intendas] that creatures cannot hold themselves otherwise, you would affirm them to be intrinsically evil; which is not to hold out [sustinere], but to yield, and even to let boars, as the poet speaks, into their spring. Let this therefore be concluded: that the motion of the orb [orbis], the human race being excluded, would not have been [going to be] infinite. |
| Asphal. Nisi me memoria destituat, Aristotelis de mundi aeternitate argumentum ex natura motus circularis, non ex hoc capite infirmasti, sed ex eo quod sol staret, alioqui demonstrationis vim habere. | Asphal. Unless memory forsakes me, the argument of Aristotle on the eternity of the world from the nature of circular motion you weakened not from this head [capite], but from this, that the sun stood [still] [staret], [granting it] otherwise to have the force of demonstration. |
| Ereu. Quaestio tunc erat de infinitudine antecedente, non secutura, & lis de inceptione quam nullam esse posse affirmabam in indifferentia Mathematica. Nunc de fine quaestio est ad quem satis est causa negativa, id est, non esse cur ulterius procedat. Sed si de fine seu termino motus proponatur eadem quaestio, violentiam patietur Aristotelis collectio. Ex eadem enim ratione colligo ubique esse terminum, & quod sequitur, omnium motuum maxime defectibilem esse circularem, & quod ipsa res habet, non posse nisi propter finem extrinsecum esse & continuari. Cum enim calefactio, sanatio, aedificatio, & similes motus finem aliquem connaturalem sibi habeant ex specie motus, similiter ascensus & descensus. Circularis motus ubique in fine est, neque ullum passum dat, nisi propter extrinsecam bonitatem. Quare non nisi ab intelligentia originaliter procedere potest, neque naturae mobilis proprie esse potest, sive secundum principia intrinseca activa, cum non perficiat mobile in quo est, sed tantum extrinseca, quae propter aliud illum efficiant. Sed postquam mundum neque in infinitum duraturum, neque fortassis omnino absque generis humani privilegio futurum, perfectum est, hoc videamus: quid in humano genere continuationis mundi causa esse possit. Neque difficilis est aestimatio, cum enim duo sola in homine reperiantur corpus & anima: corpus autem nihil praecipuum prae caeteris animalibus habeat, ut quod similiter nascitur & corrumpitur; in animam certe reiicienda erit haec diversitas, quae cum corpori superstes sit, aeviternam etiam esse consentaneum est. | Ereu. The question then was of the antecedent infinitude, not of the [infinitude] to follow, and the dispute [lis] [was] about the inception [inceptione], which I affirmed could be none in the Mathematical indifference [indifferentia]. Now the question is of the end, for which a negative cause is enough, that is, that there is not [a reason] why it should proceed further. But if the same question be proposed of the end or term of the motion, Aristotle's inference [collectio] will suffer violence. For from the same reason I gather that there is everywhere a term, and, what follows, that of all motions the circular is the most defectible [defectibilem], and, which the thing itself holds [quod ipsa res habet], that it cannot be and be continued except for the sake of an extrinsic end. For whereas heating, healing, building, and like motions have some end connatural to themselves from the species of the motion, likewise ascent and descent; circular motion is everywhere at its end [in fine], nor does it give any step [passum] except for the sake of an extrinsic goodness. Wherefore it cannot proceed originally except from an intelligence, nor can it properly be of the nature of the mobile, or according to intrinsic active principles, since it does not perfect the mobile in which it is, but [perfects] only extrinsic [things], which bring it about for the sake of another. But after it has been accomplished [perfectum est] that the world [is] neither going to last into infinity, nor perhaps going to be at all without the privilege [privilegio] of the human race, let us see this: what in the human race can be the cause of the continuation of the world. Nor is the estimation difficult; for since two things alone are found in man, body and soul, and the body has nothing special [praecipuum] before the other animals, as that which is likewise born and corrupted, onto the soul certainly will this diversity have to be thrown back [reiicienda]; which, since it is surviving [superstes] the body, it is consonant [consentaneum] that it be also aeviternal [aeviternam]. |
| Andab. Verum ex hoc mundum aeternum, hoc est, nunquam desiturum aperte conficitur; [Margin: Motum futurum non posse esse infinitum.] animarum enim quanto maior est numerus, tanto profecto etiam excellentior est bonitas. Nullum itaque huic terminum sisti ipsa bonitas iubet. His ego nec rerum metas neque tempora pono. Praesertim cum infiniti labes in futurum non cadat ut quod infinitum sit evasurum nunquam. | Andab. But from this the world [to be] eternal, that is, never going to cease, is openly concluded [conficitur]; [Margin: That future motion cannot be infinite.] for the greater is the number of souls, the more indeed also more excellent is the goodness. Goodness itself therefore bids that no term be set [sisti] to this [number]. For these I set neither bounds [metas] of things nor times. Especially since the stain [labes] of the infinite does not fall upon the future, as that which is infinite [is] never going to have come out [evasurum] [to completion]. |
| Ereu. Quid itaque putas, si infinitae futurae sint animae, non certis singulae temporibus e corpore evolabunt? vel si lapsus sim, si in infinitum futurae sint animae, non idem eveniet? Non necessum est singularum a praevidente conditore determinatas esse horas & momenta? Si autem sic sese res habet, quid profectum erit? Et cur non aliqua infinitas inter Adamum & sese conclusas videbit, aut si nunquam erit vide num in infinitum progressus erit? aut si non erit in infinitum progressus, quid sit hoc quod loquimur, an aliud quam tenebras nobis ipsis offundimus, dum dicimus: in infinitum erunt animae; sed non erunt in infinitum, aut quae patentior contradictio expectari potest, quam ut idem fore idem non fore uno spiritu affirmes? vel diversos nobis eiusdem locutionis expedi sensus. | Ereu. What therefore do you think: if the souls are going to be infinite, will they not each fly out of the body at fixed times? or, if I have slipped, if the souls are going to be into infinity [in infinitum], will not the same come about? Is it not necessary [necessum] that the hours and moments of each one be determined by the foreseeing founder? But if the thing holds itself so, what will have been gained [profectum]? And why will not some [soul] see infinite [souls] [infinitas] enclosed between Adam and itself; or, if there never will be [such a one], see whether there will be a progress into infinity? or, if there will not be a progress into infinity, what this is which we are saying, whether [it is] anything other than [that] we pour darkness over ourselves, while we say: there will be souls into infinity; but there will not be [souls] into infinity; or what more patent contradiction can be expected than that you should affirm with one breath [spiritu] the same to be going to be [and] the same not to be going to be? Or else unfold [expedi] for us diverse senses of the same locution. |
| Andab. Uno dicimus motum sine fine futurum, altero eundem nunquam tantum evasurum ut sine fine sit, vel rectius uno dicimus nunquam fore, ut non alia motus pars futura supersit, reliquo semper futuram novam motus partem. | Andab. By the one we say that motion will be [futurum] without end; by the other, that the same will never come out [evasurum] so great as to be without end; or more rightly, by the one we say that it will never be that another part of the motion does not remain [as] future; by the remaining [one], that there will always be a new part of the motion. |
| Ereu. Nescio utrum melius dictum sit. Posteriorum enim iidem prorsus sunt sensus, priorum aperte contradictorii; neque credo unquam explicatum iri quomodo motus in infinitum futurus sit, neque sit futurus infinitus; si enim quod posset in infinitum esse diceretur, intelligerem utique naturam potentiae differre a natura actus, sin utrumque fore dicitur, utrumque parem actus naturam induit parem que contradictionis necessitatem. Neque hoc attendis? idem esse rem esse futuram & fore praeteritam; si itaque nihil evidentius sit quam infinitum non posse esse praeteritum, eadem certitudine non poterit esse futurum. | Ereu. I know not whether it has been better said. For of the latter [pair] the senses are altogether the same; of the former, openly contradictory; nor do I believe it will ever be explained how motion is going to be into infinity [in infinitum], and is not going to be infinite: for if it were said that it could be into infinity, I would understand at any rate that the nature of potency [potentiae] differs from the nature of act; but if each is said to be going to be, each puts on the equal nature of act and the equal necessity of contradiction. Nor do you attend to this? that it is the same for a thing to be future [esse futuram] and [for it] to be going to be past [fore praeteritam]; if therefore nothing be more evident than that the infinite cannot be past, with the same certainty it will not be able to be future. |
| Andab. Num maior est in his necessitas quam in cognitione infinitarum specierum sive in numeris sive in figuris. Hanc enim non potes potentiam appellare. | Andab. Is the necessity in these greater than in the knowledge [cognitione] of infinite species, whether in numbers or in figures? For this you cannot call a potency. |
| Ereu. Et cur non possim? nisi unicam fortassis potentiam materialem statuas. Scire enim posse appellant Philosophi, & ad causam efficientem reducunt; Sicut itaque in potentia materiei confusio quaedam est effectuum educibilium, sic in efficiente eorumdem unitas est multitudinis divisioni opposita. Esto tamen possibilitas futuri in infinitum, ratio illa certe boni seu valde boni quae in proportione partium ad totum & inter se in toto, ex mundo praecipuo Dei opere exulabit. Maneat itaque certum etiam hoc nullo modo posse infinitas successive mutationes esse: & vides quid factum sit, mundum, videlicet, ex necessitate finem habiturum. | Ereu. And why could I not? unless perhaps you lay down [statuas] the material potency as the only one [unicam]. For Philosophers call knowing [scire] a power [posse], and reduce it to the efficient cause; As therefore in the potency of matter there is a certain confusion of the effects able to be educed [educibilium], so in the efficient [cause] there is a unity of the same [effects] opposed to the division of multitude. Let there be, however, a possibility of the future into infinity: that account [ratio] certainly of the good, or of the very good [valde boni], which [lies] in the proportion of the parts to the whole and among themselves in the whole, will be exiled from the world, the chief work of God. Let this therefore also remain certain: that in no way can there be infinite changes successively: and you see what has been done [factum sit]: namely, that the world of necessity will have an end. |

## Words and phrases with alternative or special meanings

- ***actus entis in potentia prout in potentia*** — rendered “the act of a being in potency, according as [it is] in potency”. Aristotle's definition of motion (Phys. III.1, 201a10–11: ἡ τοῦ δυνάμει ὄντος ἐντελέχεια, ᾗ τοιοῦτον). The scholastic standard definition; Andabata glosses it teleologically as 'way to perfection', which Ereunius turns into the claim that the term is of the ratio of motion.
- ***a bono genio*** — rendered “by a good genius”. by a good guardian spirit; by a good inborn nature (genius = innate disposition). Unusual in a definition of motion; perhaps Andabata's flourish for the end-directed drive of nature. Reading is clear in the print; sense is noted as uncertain.
- ***terminus a quo / ad quem*** — rendered “term from which / to which”. starting-point and end-point of a change. Standard scholastic vocabulary for the two extremes of motion (Phys. V.1).
- ***intendi / intentio*** — rendered “be intended / intention”. be aimed at; purpose. Not 'intension' (increase of degree), though the same verb has that sense elsewhere. Kept as 'intend/intention' throughout (glossary: intentio = intention); here clearly final-causal.
- ***ratio (motus / boni / termini)*** — rendered “account / reason”. essential notion, definition; reason, ground; (of a stock) kind, breed. 'de motus ratione' = belongs to the essential account of motion; 'ratione termini' = by reason of the term; 'melior ratio' (of offspring) = a better kind/stock.
- ***mutatio: esse post non esse / non esse post esse*** — rendered “change: to be after not to be / not to be after to be”. generation / corruption as the two kinds of substantial change. Scholastic definition of mutatio; Andabata reduces change to the second because 'what is not cannot be changed'.
- ***τῷ non esse / τοῦ bene esse*** — rendered “to the not to be / of the well-being”. Greek article used to substantivize a Latin infinitive phrase. Scholastic-humanist device (cf. τῷ magis & minus, D3-N16).
- ***mixta*** — rendered “mixed [bodies]”. compounds of the elements. Aristotelian mixtio (De gen. et corr. I.10).
- ***generationem (finis & termini) habet*** — rendered “has the generation [generationem] of end and term”. probably for rationem 'has the character of end and term'. The printed word is clearly generationem; a compositor's echo of 'generatione' in the line above is possible. Rendered literally with gloss.
- ***saecula (animalium)*** — rendered “generations [saecula]”. ages, lifetimes, races. Lucretian sense (saecla ferarum = races of animals).
- ***Entia nolunt male gubernari*** — rendered “beings will not be governed badly”. beings refuse to be badly governed. Aristotle, Metaph. Λ 10, 1076a3–4: τὰ δὲ ὄντα οὐ βούλεται πολιτεύεσθαι κακῶς (scholastic: entia nolunt male disponi).
- ***bonitas non cum movente, sed iam subsistente*** — rendered “a goodness not as moving [him], but already subsisting”. goodness not as final cause moving God, but as his own possessed nature. White's distinction: God creates by diffusion of goodness (bonum diffusivum sui), not to gain a good.
- ***fonti apros immittere*** — rendered “to let boars into their spring”. to foul one's own source. Virgil, Ecl. 2.58–59: 'liquidis immisi fontibus apros'.
- ***sol staret*** — rendered “the sun stood [still]”. the sun was at rest. Reference back to the earlier rebuttal of Aristotle's argument for eternity from circular motion (in Dialogue II/III), framed on the sun's being at rest (Copernican-Galilean hypothesis).
- ***indifferentia Mathematica*** — rendered “Mathematical indifference”. the neutrality of a mathematical (uniform circular) motion to any beginning-point. Earlier argument: a uniform circular motion gives no reason for any inception.
- ***causa negativa*** — rendered “negative cause”. a cause by absence (there being no reason to go further). Scholastic: causa per remotionem prohibentis / deficiens.
- ***collectio*** — rendered “inference [collectio]”. collecting; conclusion. As in glossary (D3-N16 p2).
- ***in fine est (motus circularis)*** — rendered “is at its end”. has its term everywhere, i.e. gains nothing by moving. Aristotle: circular motion has no extreme (Phys. VIII.8–9); White turns this into a sign that it is for an extrinsic end.
- ***passum dat*** — rendered “gives a step [passum]”. takes a step, advances. Vivid idiom: circular motion makes no progress for its own sake.
- ***intelligentia*** — rendered “intelligence”. separate mover, angel. Glossary: intelligentiae = intelligences.
- ***privilegium (generis humani)*** — rendered “privilege”. special prerogative, special law. The world's duration is bound up with the human race's special standing.
- ***perfectum est*** — rendered “it has been accomplished [perfectum est]”. has been proved/established. Cf. conficere = conclude.
- ***aeviternus*** — rendered “aeviternal”. belonging to aevum (between time and eternity). Scholastic: aevum = duration of incorruptible created beings (angels, souls).
- ***His ego nec rerum metas neque tempora pono*** — rendered “For these I set neither bounds of things nor times”.  Virgil, Aen. 1.278 (Jupiter of Rome: 'his ego nec metas rerum nec tempora pono; imperium sine fine dedi').
- ***infiniti labes*** — rendered “the stain [labes] of the infinite”. flaw, defect; also 'fall, collapse'. The defect that makes an actual infinite impossible (that it must be completed), which Andabata says does not attach to the future.
- ***evadere (evasurum)*** — rendered “come out [evasurum]”. turn out, end up, be completed. Used of a series that never 'comes out' complete.
- ***necessum*** — rendered “necessary [necessum]”. archaic form of necesse. Plautine/Lucretian form.
- ***Adamus*** — rendered “Adam”.  The first soul; a series from Adam to any later soul is finite.
- ***spiritu (uno)*** — rendered “with one breath [spiritu]”. in one breath. Idiom, not 'spirit'.
- ***expedi*** — rendered “unfold [expedi]”. explain, disentangle (imperative).
- ***esse futuram / fore praeteritam*** — rendered “to be future / to be going to be past”.  White's key argument: the future infinite would have to become past, and the past cannot be infinite.
- ***scire posse appellant*** — rendered “call knowing a power [posse]”.  Knowledge counted as an active potency reducible to the efficient cause (cf. potentia activa vs passiva).
- ***educibilis*** — rendered “able to be educed [educibilium]”.  Scholastic eductio formarum e potentia materiae.
- ***valde bonum*** — rendered “very good [valde boni]”.  Gen. 1:31 'et erant valde bona'.

- Break before Knot XIX: the margin 'Nodus XIX. Quando finis mundi sit futurus?' stands on p. 443 beside '(Horum autem quis sit…' in the middle of a long speech of Ereunius that began on p. 442 ('Et cur non possim?') with the argument about potency, which belongs to Knot XVIII. Rather than move the whole speech into Knot XIX, this Knot ends at the sentence '…finem habiturum.' and Knot XIX is to begin with 'Quando inquies? Nescio cum enim…' (p. 443, PDF 454), which opens the question of the margin. (The table of Knots, FM2, prints the title as 'Quando finis mundo sit futurus?'; the margin reads 'mundi'.)
- Inner marginal heading: 'Motum futurum non posse esse infinitum.' (p. 441) placed in its row.
- Uncertain or noteworthy readings: (1) p. 435 'agitur a bono genio' — the print is clear; the sense in a definition of motion is odd and is noted in the terms list. (2) p. 437 'quae finis & termini generationem habet' — probably for rationem; kept as printed. (3) p. 439 'alioqui demonstrationis vim habere' — infinitive without a governing verb; rendered '[granting it] otherwise to have'. (4) p. 440 'Cum enim calefactio … similiter ascensus & descensus. Circularis motus…' — the full stop after descensus breaks a cum-clause from its main clause; translated as one sentence. (5) p. 441 'Et cur non aliqua infinitas inter Adamum & sese conclusas videbit' — read aliqua as nominative [anima] and infinitas as accusative plural agreeing with conclusas. (6) p. 436 'Aliam itaque definitionem subiicio; ut eum dicam' — eum refers to motum.
- Verification pass: the Latin was re-read sentence by sentence against the page images and the English. Corrections made: 4 — (a) p. 438 'quod enim ex intentione fieri non potest; neque fieri potest quia malum…' first rendered 'cannot come about [at all]', losing neque; corrected to 'cannot come about either [neque]'; (b) same row, 'Entia vero' first rendered 'and beings'; corrected to 'but [vero] beings'; (c) p. 440 'cum non perficiat mobile in quo est, sed tantum extrinseca' — extrinseca is the object of perficiat; '[perfects]' supplied so the English does not read it as a subject; (d) p. 442 'nisi unicam fortassis potentiam materialem statuas' first rendered 'a single, material potency'; corrected to 'the material potency as the only one [unicam]'. Also checked and confirmed: 'Posteriorum … priorum' (p. 442) refers to the latter and former pair of Andabata's formulations; 'quae tamen non usquequaque video…' (p. 436) taken with the definition (quae fem. sing.), though it could refer to the consequences (neut. pl.). Andabata's 'Num maior est in his necessitas…' (p. 442) ends with a full stop in the print but is translated as a question (num). The transcription was checked against 300 dpi crops at pp. 435, 441 and 443.
- Conventions as in earlier units: long s, ligatures and accents normalized; u/v normalized as in previous units; supplied words in [square brackets]; Latin of technical or ambiguous words in brackets after the English; nodus = Knot.
