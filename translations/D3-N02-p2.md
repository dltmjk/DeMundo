# De Mundo — Dialogue III (De causis mundi), Knot ii, part 2 of 2

*Latin title:* Nodus II. Motum universi a principio externo esse  
*English title:* Knot II. That the motion of the universe is from an external principle  
*Pages:* PDF pp. 289–296 = printed pp. 278–285 (from Ereunius’ ‘Ad ardua me cogis’ on p. 278 to the end of Asphalius’ speech, ‘…nos perducito.’, on p. 285; Knot iii begins on p. 285 with ‘Rem magnam postulas’).

## Dialogue III, Knot ii, part 2 (printed pp. 278–285)

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| [p. 278] Ereu. Ad ardua me cogis, & quae si aggressus fuero, ea locuturus sim, quae neque ipse plene intelligo, neque quibus verbis intelligi me faciam, satis scio. | [p. 278] Ereu. To arduous things you compel me, and [things] which, if I shall have attacked [aggressus fuero] [them], I would be going to speak those things which neither I myself fully understand, nor do I sufficiently know by what words I may make myself understood. |
| Veniam itaque hanc postulo, ut ad ipsa viscera penetrare non compellas, sed ubi vera esse quae dixi, compereris, subtiliora insuper habeas. [Margin: Incorporea non mota movere.] | This indulgence [veniam], therefore, I request: that you do not compel [me] to penetrate to the very entrails [viscera], but, when you shall have found [compereris] the things which I said to be true, you hold the subtler [things] of no account [insuper habeas]. [Margin: That incorporeal things, not moved, move.] |
| Reflecte proinde, si libet, oculos ad mentem nostram, quam (si quis est tractabilis a nobis spiritus) unicum fatemur, & hoc attende, quomodo unum propter aliud faciat & partem propter totum. | Turn back [Reflecte], therefore, if it pleases, [your] eyes to our mind [mentem], which (if any spirit [spiritus] is tractable [tractabilis] by us) we confess [to be] the only [one]; and attend to this: how it does one thing for the sake of another, and a part for the sake of the whole. |
| Vide cum disponit aedificium, quomodo fundamentum, & muros, & tectum toti quod machinatur, accommodat. Vide non ingredi viam nisi cum destinavit ad certum terminum eam perducere: & cum satis [p. 279] haec & similia quibus vita tota integratur, aspexeris, ad horum principium contemplationem reclina, & vide num haec valeret anima, nisi simul & semel esset longa materiatarum actionum series, sive eam in sese haberet, & ex virtute totius seriei ageret. | See, when it disposes a building, how it accommodates the foundation, and the walls, and the roof to the whole which it contrives [machinatur]. See [that it does] not enter upon a road except when it has determined [destinavit] to bring it through to a certain end [terminum]: and when you shall have sufficiently looked upon [p. 279] these and similar things by which the whole life is integrated [integratur], lean back [reclina] [your] contemplation to the principle [principium] of these, and see whether the soul [anima] would be capable of [valeret] these, unless it were at once and once for all [simul & semel] a long series of materiate [materiatarum] actions, or had it [the series] in itself, and acted from the virtue [virtute] of the whole series. |
| Quod si vides, etiam hoc vides, ex animi natura esse, ut immota, id est, secundum unum esse & actum, ad plura sese materiata effundat. | Which if you see, you see this also: that it is from the nature of the mind [animi] that, unmoved, that is, according to one being [esse] and act, it pours itself out to many materiate [materiata] things. |
| Vides itaque, quoniam Animam spiritum esse censemus, hoc in spiritali natura admitti debere. | You see, therefore, since we judge the Soul [Animam] to be a spirit, that this must be admitted in a spiritual [spiritali] nature. |
| Et si radicem quaeras, statim patet ex comparativa potestate hoc ei contingere, & ex hoc quod plura simul eidem inesse possunt. | And if you seek the root, it is at once plain that this befalls it from [its] comparative power [comparativa potestate], and from this, that many [things] can be in the same at once [simul]. |
| Corporum vero primam legem hanc esse, ne plura simul consistant, experimur. Quin etiam in ipsa imaginatione nostra, necesse est ut praetervolet unum quo alteri locum cedat. | But that this is the first law of bodies, that many [things] do not stand together [consistant] at once, we experience. Nay even in our very imagination it is necessary that one fly past, in order that [quo] it may yield place to another. |
| Hoc itaque palam est neque corpori plurium ex uno sui esse actionem competere, & superiorem esse spirituum vim. Corpori quoque hoc competere, quia corpus est, hoc est, ingenii ad unum limitati subiectumque loci temporisque finibus, a quibus absolutam mentem nostram & incorporeorum gentem satis ex animae nostrae Analogia percipimus; | This, therefore, is plain: both that to a body there does not belong [competere] an action of many [things] out of one being [esse] of itself, and that the force [vim] of spirits is superior. [And] that this belongs to body too because it is body, that is, of a character [ingenii] limited to one [thing] and subject to the bounds of place and time, from which [bounds] we perceive our mind and the race [gentem] of incorporeal [things] [to be] absolved [absolutam], sufficiently from the Analogy of our soul [animae]; |
| Ingredi vero specialem existentiae seu durationis discussionem, neque praesentis est instituti, & abstractius quam ut [p. 280] crasso hoc, quo disputamus, modo comprehendi queat. | but to enter upon a special discussion of existence or duration is neither of the present undertaking [instituti], and [it is] more abstract than that it could be comprehended [p. 280] in this gross [crasso] manner in which we dispute. |
| Asphal. Etsi aegre me contineam, ne invitum te ad aliquid de existentiarum differentiis disserendum permoveam; tamen tam seriae tuae postulationi cedo, & quod mihi cum ad Andabatam ageres, succurrit, ita expono, ut ubi tuum subiverit calculum, rectum sim habiturus. [Margin: Corpora in perpetuo motu esse.] | Asphal. Although I may hardly restrain myself from moving you, unwilling, on to discoursing [disserendum] something about the differences of existences, nevertheless I yield to so serious a request of yours; and what occurred to me while you were dealing with Andabata I set out in such a way that, when it shall have undergone your vote [calculum], I shall hold [it] right [rectum sim habiturus]. [Margin: That bodies are in perpetual motion.] |
| Visum mihi est dum omnem motum desiturum sublato principio primo confirmares, aliud quoque effectum esse, scilicet omnia corpora in perpetua quadam alteratione versari. | It seemed to me, while you were confirming that all motion would cease, the first principle being taken away, that something else also had been effected [effectum esse]: namely, that all bodies are engaged [versari] in a certain perpetual alteration. |
| Necesse si quidem apparuit, primum cui motus ab incorporeo affluit, in perpetua novitate esse & ex consequenti respectu caeterorum perpetuo alterius & alterius causae rationem subire; neque video cur non idem de sequentibus asseramus, hoc est, ex motu primi mobilis causas ipsum proxime sentientes, etiam ipsas & perpetuam novitatem pati & eandem sibi propinquis inferre. | Since indeed it appeared necessary that the first [thing], to which motion flows from the incorporeal, is in perpetual newness [novitate], and consequently [ex consequenti], in respect of the others, perpetually undergoes the nature [rationem] of another and another cause; nor do I see why we should not assert the same of the following [ones], that is, that the causes feeling it [ipsum] most nearly [proxime] from the motion of the first mobile [primi mobilis], they too both suffer a perpetual newness and bring the same upon [things] near to themselves. |
| Quare si ad minima quaeque pertingit, ut dicebas, primi moti incitatio, etiam illa perpetuis erunt subiecta mutationibus. | Wherefore if the impulse [incitatio] of the first moved [thing] reaches to every least [thing], as you were saying, those too will be subject to perpetual changes. |
| Ereu. Rem ipsam tenes & quies quam nominamus, & cernere videmur; non quies, sed insensibilis est motus: sicut enim stellas & umbram non moveri, sed esse mota cernimus, sic [p. 281] & in pluribus liquoribus quorum profunditas non duas vel neque unam ulnam excedat, descendere faeces pluribus diebus & mensibus experimur, cum tamen sensibus nostris nihil esse quietius videatur: | Ereu. You hold the thing itself; and the rest [quies] which we name, and seem to discern, is not rest, but insensible motion: for just as we discern the stars and a shadow not to be moved, but to have been moved, so [p. 281] also in many liquors whose depth does not exceed two, or not even one, ell [ulnam], we experience the dregs to descend for many days and months, although to our senses nothing seems to be more quiet: |
| quod idem in mixtis omnibus aetatum & saeculorum mensuris perfici dubitare nequaquam possumus; quandoquidem poros & partium inaequalitatem clare in his dignoscimus. | which same [thing] we can by no means doubt is accomplished in all mixts [mixtis] by measures of ages and centuries [saeculorum]; since we clearly discern pores and inequality of parts in these. |
| Andab. Talionem tibi rependo, Asphali; nam & cum tecum dissereret Ereunius, occurrit mihi quod explicatu non indignum censeo. | Andab. I pay you back the like [talionem], Asphalius; for also, when Ereunius was discoursing with you, there occurred to me [something] which I judge not unworthy of unfolding [explicatu]. |
| Novistis enim credo quanta lis Aristoteli intenta fuerit, hac de causa quod ex motione coeli motorem arguerit infinitum & quanto labore, Caietanus ad illud sustentandum desudaverit. [Margin: An motus aeternus arguat infinitatem motoris.] | For you know, I believe, how great a suit [lis] was brought [intenta] against Aristotle for this cause, that from the motion of the heaven he argued the mover [to be] infinite; and with how great labour Cajetan sweated [desudaverit] to sustain that. [Margin: Whether eternal motion argues infinity of the mover.] |
| Ego vero, ut me fateor non prius vel Aristoteli, vel Caietano acquievisse, sic nunc non ausim diffiteri aliquam mihi lucem discludi, quae latentis & hactenus invisi fulgoris sit nuncia. | But I, as I confess that I did not before acquiesce either in Aristotle or in Cajetan, so now I would not dare to deny that some light is disclosed [discludi] to me, which may be the messenger [nuncia] of a lurking and hitherto unseen brightness [fulgoris]. |
| Apparet siquidem mihi ex dissertatione facta (si eam recte intellexi) spiritum propterea corporibus esse motionis principem, quod plures motus partes simul intelligat & intendat. | For it appears to me from the discourse [dissertatione] made (if I have understood it rightly) that a spirit is the prince [principem] of motion to bodies for this reason, that it understands and intends many parts of the motion at once [simul]. |
| Eum itaque spiritum qui motum infinitum operaturus est, eum totum comprehendere debere colligo; alioquin enim non ut spiritus, hoc est, ex in-[p. 282]tellectione & intellectuali propensione acturus est. Eum rursus qui simul & semel infinitum cognoscit, cui dubium esse potest, quin infinitus sit? | That spirit, therefore, which is going to operate an infinite motion, I gather must comprehend it [eum, the motion] whole; for otherwise it will not act as a spirit, that is, out of in-[p. 282]tellection and intellectual propension [propensione]. Him, again, who knows the infinite at once and once for all [simul & semel], to whom can it be doubtful that he is infinite? |
| Ereu. Mihi, Andabata, nisi aliud afferas. Non enim vides unam superficiem quae finitissima sit, infinitis adaequari lineis? corpus minimum totidem superficiebus? | Ereu. To me, Andabata, unless you bring something else. For do you not see that one surface, which is most finite, is made equal [adaequari] to infinite lines? a least body to as many surfaces? |
| Quare reminiscendum est, corpus inferioris rationis esse ad spiritum: quantumvis itaque spiritus infinita corpora comprehendat, non propterea absolute infinitum; sed respectu corporis affirmem; atque adeo sub entis genere limitem habere, si aliud non officiat, non repugnabo. | Wherefore it must be remembered that body is of an inferior nature [rationis] relative to [ad] spirit: however much, therefore, a spirit may comprehend infinite bodies, not on that account would I affirm [it] absolutely infinite, but [infinite] in respect of body; and so [atque adeo] that it has a limit under the kind of being [sub entis genere], if something else does not stand in the way [officiat], I shall not resist. |
| Non tamen propterea prorsus infirmandum hoc argumentum velim; neque enim inficiari possum, ens illud cui naturale sit motum absolute infinitum, hoc est, aeternum, qualem Aristoteles posuit, efficere; ipsum quoque naturaliter aeternum esse, neque illud cui naturalis sit aeternitas, in genere entis esse infinitum. | Not, however, on that account would I wish this argument to be altogether weakened [infirmandum]; for neither can I deny that that being [ens] to which it is natural to effect a motion absolutely infinite, that is, eternal, such as Aristotle posited, is itself also naturally eternal; nor [can I deny] that that to which eternity is natural is infinite in the kind of being [in genere entis]. |
| Unde vides hoc confici, si mundus aeternus foret, motores infinitos futuros. Sed non est hoc quod modo tractamus. | Whence you see this to be concluded [confici]: if the world were eternal, the movers would be going to be infinite. But this is not what we are now treating. |
| Andab. Magnam profecto ab Aristotele gratiam promeritus es, si hoc non infirmas argumentum; cum alia & illa quidem quae demonstrativa docuit omnis Peripateticorum [p. 283] Schola; ea dico quibus mundum aeternum esse tam falso quam verisimiliter conclusit, prorsus reieceris. | Andab. Great thanks [gratiam] indeed have you earned from Aristotle, if you do not weaken this argument, since you have altogether rejected others, and those indeed which the whole School of the Peripatetics [p. 283] taught [to be] demonstrative — those I mean by which he concluded the world to be eternal as falsely as plausibly [verisimiliter]. |
| Ereu. Hoc si fecero, tecum, ut credo, fecero, quantumvis satis haereo an iure facias. Responde si quidem, num existimes soli & Coelis non aeque naturales esse suos motus, quam ascensum & descensum gravibus & levibus? | Ereu. If I shall have done this, I shall have done [it] with you, as I believe, however much I sufficiently hesitate [haereo] whether you do [it] by right [iure]. Answer, then [si quidem]: do you think that to the sun and the Heavens their motions are not equally natural as ascent and descent [are] to heavy and light [things]? |
| Andab. Existimo. | Andab. I think [so]. |
| Ereu. Motus itaque Coelis & soli ex horum an illorum intuitu ordiendum credis; maxime si cum Aristotele coelorum immortalitatem defensites. | Ereu. Do you believe, therefore, that [the beginning] of motion [motus … ordiendum] for the Heavens and the sun is to be made out of regard [intuitu] for these or for those; especially if with Aristotle you keep defending [defensites] the immortality of the heavens? |
| Andab. In illa sententia clarissimum est, soli coeloque initium tribuendum, cum iis corporibus nihil non necessario competat, vel certe quam maxime naturaliter. [Margin: Ex immortalitate sequi aeternitatem Coelorum.] | Andab. In that opinion [sententia] it is most clear that the beginning is to be attributed to the sun and the heaven, since to those bodies nothing belongs not necessarily, or at least as naturally as possible. [Margin: That from immortality follows the eternity of the Heavens.] |
| Ereu. Rursus arbitrarisne quicquam eorum interesse (postquam immortalia sint) unde principium motus sumatur, modo per eandem lineam fiat motus: & tantumdem est de signo temporis, in quo motus initium futurum est. | Ereu. Again, do you judge that it matters [interesse] anything to them (since [postquam] they are immortal) whence the beginning [principium] of motion be taken, provided the motion come about along the same line: and just as much [tantumdem] is [true] of the sign [signo] of time in which the beginning of motion is going to be. |
| Andab. De loco nescio, fortassis enim a disci centro incipiendus erit: de tempore confitentem habes. | Andab. About place I do not know, for perhaps it will have to be begun from the centre of the disc [disci]: about time you have [me] confessing. |
| Ereu. Ut in caeteris praeferatur discus, ad [p. 284] octavam seu stellatam non hoc applicabis, ubi si punctum aliquod sumpseris, alia Astra in ius te vocaverint, de capitis sui diminutione & censurae iniquitate. | Ereu. Granted [Ut] that in the others the disc be preferred, to the [p. 284] eighth or starry [sphere] you will not apply this, where, if you shall have taken some point, the other Stars will have summoned you into court [in ius], concerning the diminution of their head [capitis … diminutione] and the iniquity of the censure [censurae]. |
| Primo autem mobili quid ages quod undique uniforme est; Deinde non de partibus disci, sed de parte coeli, qua discus primo collocandus erit, quaestio proposita est. | But with the first mobile, which is uniform on every side, what will you do? Next, the question has been proposed not about the parts of the disc, but about the part of the heaven where [qua] the disc is first to be placed. |
| Andab. Esto, quid inferas expecto. | Andab. Be it so; I await what you infer. |
| Ereu. Ego vero praeter tua ipsius dicta nihil. Dicis enim motum ex ipsorum Coelorum natura ortum accipere debere, adeo ut nihil nisi necessario in iis ponendum sit, nihil ex libito vel contingenter. | Ereu. I, indeed, [infer] nothing beyond your own sayings. For you say that motion must receive [its] rise [ortum] from the nature of the Heavens themselves, so far that nothing except necessarily is to be posited in them, nothing at pleasure [ex libito] or contingently. |
| Idem tu affirmas neque quoad locum, neque quoad tempus esse in Coelis, unde primo sumatur motus initium: Hoc itaque vides nullum esse potuisse. | You the same [Idem] affirm that there is [anything] in the Heavens neither as to place nor as to time whence the beginning of motion may first be taken: this [beginning], therefore, you see can have been none. |
| Et totus hic discursus ex iis compositus est, quae posita Coeli inalterabilitate, per concatenationem principiorum Peripateticorum, per necessitatem sese inferunt. | And this whole discourse [discursus] is composed of those [things] which, the unalterability [inalterabilitate] of the Heaven being posited, infer themselves through the concatenation of Peripatetic principles, through necessity. |
| Vide itaque & Aristotelem plura falso, nihil non docte dixisse, & in quas angustias sese cogant qui inalterabilitatem Coeli cum fide Christiana componere moliuntur. | See, therefore, both that Aristotle said many things falsely, nothing not learnedly, and into what straits they force themselves who strive to compose [componere] the unalterability of the Heaven with the Christian faith. |
| Andab. Proh! quam verum est parvum in principio errorem sensim in latissimum ampliari. | Andab. Oh! how true it is that a small error in the beginning [principio] is by degrees enlarged into a very broad [one]. |
| Ereu. Hoc interea, Andabata, vides quam [p. 285] constanter eorum quae pridie de Coelorum ingenio collocuti sumus, lex in hodiernam refunditur disputationem. | Ereu. This meanwhile, Andabata, you see: how [p. 285] constantly the law of those things which we talked over the day before [pridie] about the character [ingenio] of the Heavens is poured back [refunditur] into today’s disputation. |
| Asphal. Non satis est, Ereuni, aliorum errores commeminisse, nisi ipsam nobis veritatis lucem affundas. Fac igitur mundum ab aeterno non fuisse, nobis dilucescat. | Asphal. It is not enough, Ereunius, to have recalled [commeminisse] the errors of others, unless you pour upon us the very light of truth. Make, therefore, [that] it dawn upon us [dilucescat] that the world has not been from eternity. |
| Ereu. Hoc a te primo interrogandum est, quid mundum voces? an haec dumtaxat quae oculis collustramus diffusa corporum spatia, vel etiam motum hunc qui totius machinae quasi vita & anima est? | Ereu. This is first to be asked of you: what do you call the world? only these diffused spaces of bodies which we survey [collustramus] with the eyes, or also this motion which is as it were the life and soul of the whole machine? |
| Asphal. Utrumque, nam etsi videam a ratione abhorrere, corpora infinitum sine motu extitisse, & nescio quo bono genio, tandem in Pyrrhicam hanc quam exercent, exiluisse. Quoniam tamen coaevam Deo materiam, non ignobiles naturae scrutatores autumaverunt, etiam in hanc veritatem nos perducito. | Asphal. Both; for although I see [that] it is abhorrent from reason that bodies existed infinitely [infinitum] without motion, and, by I know not what good genius, at length leapt out into this Pyrrhic [dance] [Pyrrhicam] which they perform; since, nevertheless, not ignoble searchers of nature have declared [autumaverunt] matter coeval with God, lead us through also into this truth. |

## Words and phrases with alternative or special meanings

- ***insuper habere (subtiliora insuper habeas)*** — rendered “hold of no account [insuper habeas]”. lit. ‘have over/besides’; idiom = disregard, pass over (cf. glossary, D2). Ereunius asks Andabata to let the subtler metaphysics go, which frames the whole speech as a deliberately ‘gross’ account.
- ***comparativa potestas*** — rendered “comparative power [comparativa potestate]”. the power of comparing / relating many things together; not ‘relative power’. White grounds the spirit’s ability to act on many things at once in its intellectual capacity to hold several objects together (plura simul eidem inesse) — a Digbean/Whitean theme from the Two Treatises.
- ***simul & semel*** — rendered “at once and once for all”. together and in a single act; ‘all at once’. Marks the non-successive mode of spiritual knowing and acting, contrasted with bodily succession.
- ***materiatus, -a, -um*** — rendered “materiate [materiata/materiatarum]”. embodied in matter, material; ‘materialised’. Kept from part 1 (materiatum agens): the soul unfolds itself into a series of material actions while itself unmoved.
- ***secundum unum esse & actum*** — rendered “according to one being [esse] and act”. ‘in a single existence and act’. Scholastic esse/actus: the mind acts without succession of its own being.
- ***ex uno sui esse*** — rendered “out of one being [esse] of itself”. ‘from a single existence of its own’. The law of bodies: a body cannot produce an action of many things from one state of its being.
- ***ingenium (of body / of the Heavens)*** — rendered “character [ingenii / ingenio]”. nature, disposition, temper; ‘wit’ for persons. Glossary convention: ‘character’ for things.
- ***capitis diminutio; in ius vocare; censura*** — rendered “diminution of [their] head; summon into court; censure”. Roman law: capitis deminutio = loss of civil status; in ius vocare = summon before the praetor; censura = the censor’s rating of citizens. Comic legal conceit: the other stars would sue for loss of rank if one point of the starry sphere were chosen as the beginning of motion.
- ***talio (talionem rependo)*** — rendered “the like [talionem]”. lex talionis, like for like; retaliation. Andabata ‘repays in kind’ Asphalius’ interjection with one of his own.
- ***lis intenta*** — rendered “suit brought [intenta]”. legal: litem intendere = to bring an action. Refers to the long controversy over Aristotle’s inference (Phys. VIII.10) from eternal motion to an infinite (in power) mover.
- ***Caietanus*** — rendered “Cajetan”. Thomas de Vio, Cardinal Cajetan (1469–1534). Thomist commentator who defended Aristotle’s argument for an infinite first mover.
- ***discludere (lucem discludi)*** — rendered “disclosed [discludi]”. classical: to shut off, separate; neo-Latin also ‘disclose’. Context (light that is ‘messenger of a hidden brightness’) requires ‘disclosed/opened’.
- ***propensio intellectualis*** — rendered “intellectual propension [propensione]”. inclination, tendency of the will/intellect. Spirit acts by understanding and by intellectual inclination, i.e. as a final, not merely efficient, cause.
- ***genus entis (sub entis genere / in genere entis)*** — rendered “kind of being”. genus of being; the order of being as such. Infinite ‘in the kind of being’ = absolutely infinite, as opposed to infinite only ‘in respect of body’.
- ***inalterabilitas Coeli*** — rendered “unalterability of the Heaven”. incorruptibility, impassibility of the celestial bodies. Aristotelian doctrine (De caelo I.3); White argues it entails the eternity of the world and so cannot be squared with Christian faith.
- ***parvus in principio error*** — rendered “a small error in the beginning”.  Allusion to Aristotle, De caelo I.5 (271b8–13), via Aquinas, De ente et essentia, prologue: ‘parvus error in principio magnus est in fine’.
- ***octava seu stellata (sphaera)*** — rendered “the eighth or starry [sphere]”. the sphere of the fixed stars. As in Dialogue II (glossary: octava sphaera).
- ***Pyrrhica*** — rendered “Pyrrhic [dance] [Pyrrhicam]”. the Pyrrhic war-dance of the Greeks. Image of the world’s motion as a dance into which bodies ‘leapt’; ironical against an eternal matter that began to move at some moment.
- ***autumare*** — rendered “declare [autumaverunt]”. assert, affirm, think (archaic/poetic). Those who held matter coeval with God (Platonists, some Peripatetics).
- ***discursus*** — rendered “discourse [discursus]”. reasoning, chain of argument; distinct from dissertatio (discourse [dissertatio]). Scholastic: discursive reasoning from principles.

## Translator's notes

- Unit: Dialogue III, Knot ii, part 2 of 2 (end of the Knot). - Uncertain readings: (1) p. 281 ‘cum tecum dissereret Ereunius’ — the print looks like ‘differeret’; read as long-s ‘diſſereret’ (dissereret, ‘was discoursing’), which the sense requires. (2) p. 283 ‘num existimes … non aeque naturales esse suos motus, quam ascensum…’ — taken literally (‘not equally natural as’); Andabata’s ‘Existimo’ and his next answer show the intended sense is that the heavenly motions are no less natural than the motions of heavy and light bodies. (3) p. 283 ‘Motus … ex horum an illorum intuitu ordiendum’ — the gerundive is impersonal and ‘motus’ (genitive) is construed with an understood ‘initium’/‘beginning’; ‘horum … illorum’ taken as the Heavens and sun vs. the heavy and light bodies.
