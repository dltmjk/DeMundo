# De Mundo — Dialogue III (De causis mundi), Knot vi

*Latin title:* Nodus VI. Mundum qui est esse creabilium optimum  
*English title:* Knot VI. That the world which is, is the best of creatables  
*Pages:* PDF pp. 326–334 = printed pp. 315–323 (from Ereunius’ ‘Si me consulis’ on p. 315 to Ereunius’ ‘…potuerint non fuisse.’ on p. 323, where Knot vii begins).

## Dialogue III, Knot vi (printed pp. 315–323)

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| [Margin, p. 315: Nodus VI. Mundum qui est esse creabilium optimum.] | [Margin, p. 315: Knot VI. That the world which is, is the best of creatables [creabilium].] |
| Ereu. Si me consulis, quia hic omnium optimus est, postquam enim inter demonstrata relatum est non posse esse electionem ubi non est differentia, neque minus certum sit Deum semper quod optimum est agere, fit mea opinione clarissimum, mundum hunc ex omnibus creabilibus absolute optimum & dignissimum esse. | Ereu. If you consult me, because this [world] is the best of all; for after it has been reported [relatum est] among the things demonstrated that there cannot be election [electionem] where there is not difference, nor is it less certain that God always does what is best, it becomes [fit], in my opinion, most clear that this world is, out of all creatables, absolutely the best and the most worthy. |
| Asphal. Unde enim constat vel qua necessitate Deum quod est optimum agere? | Asphal. For whence is it established [constat], or by what necessity, that God does what is best? |
| Ereu. Ea profecto necessitate qua Deum certum est a virtute non deviare vel ratione, cum ipsamet virtutis & rationis essentia essentialiter existat. Clarum autem est rationem sicut inter bona & mala eligit bona, sic inter plura bona magis bonum eligere, si sibi permittatur. Idem enim est magis bonum & magis agendum, si ex omnibus ad actionem spectantibus consideratio desumatur. Sicut itaque ratio non recta foret, quae in agibilibus quid optimum actu non designaret, sic actio non potest esse secundum rationem quae non in id consentit quod esse agendum vel optimum actu ratio dictaverit. Certe ex alio principio quam ex ratione procedere actionem, quae mundum non perfectissimum creaverit necessum est: quod non esse melius ratione evidens est, cum illud decretum sit esse optimum quod ratio esse tale dictaverit: quare cum rationi dissentiat, prius esse oportet. | Ereu. By that necessity, assuredly [profecto], by which it is certain that God does not deviate from virtue or from reason, since He exists essentially [as] the very essence [ipsamet … essentia] of virtue and of reason. But it is clear that reason, just as between goods and evils it elects the goods, so between more [than one] goods [it elects] the more good, if it be permitted to itself. For the more good and the more to-be-done [magis agendum] are the same, if the consideration be taken [desumatur] from all the things looking to [spectantibus] action. Just as therefore that reason would not be right [recta] which in things doable [agibilibus] did not designate what is best in act [actu], so an action cannot be according to reason which does not consent to that which reason shall have dictated to be to-be-done, or best in act. Certainly it is necessary [necessum est] that the action which should have created [creaverit] a world not most perfect proceed from another principle than from reason: which [principle] it is evident is not better than reason, since that is decreed [decretum sit] to be best which reason shall have dictated to be such: wherefore, since it dissents from reason, it must be prior [prius] [to it]. |
| Andab. Sed respondebit quispiam non dissentire hoc principium a ratione, neque rationem imperare semper agere quod optimum est. | Andab. But someone will answer that this principle does not dissent from reason, nor does reason command [imperare] always to do what is best. |
| [Margin, p. 316: Deum facere quod esse optimum.] | [Margin, p. 316: That God does what is [esse, sic] best.] |
| Ereu. Quicquid de imperio sit, certe rationem iudicare illud esse agendum prae caeteris, id est, relictis caeteris non pote dubitari, cum idem sit iudicare quod ostendere esse optimum. Utrum autem alium imperii actum habeat praeter iudicium ego nescio, sed dissentiens a iudicio ut habeat prorsus impossibile esse video, cum a iudicio hoc procedere debeat, si quidem rationis sit. Contraria enim sunt omnibus positis, hoc esse agendum & hic nunc aliud esse agendum, cum hic & nunc idem significet quod omnibus positis. | Ereu. Whatever be [the case] about command [imperio], certainly that reason judges that [thing] to be to-be-done before [prae] the rest, that is, the rest having been left [relictis], cannot [non pote] be doubted, since to judge is the same as to show [it] to be best. But whether it has another act of command [imperii] besides judgement, I do not know; but that it should have [one] dissenting from judgement I see to be altogether impossible, since this must proceed from judgement, if indeed it be [an act] of reason. For these are contraries: ‘all things being posited [omnibus positis], this is to be done’, and ‘here [and] now another thing is to be done’; since ‘here and now’ signifies the same as ‘all things being posited’. |
| Andab. Sed sicut ex Deo tu concludis hunc mundum esse optimum, sic ex eodem & aequali evidentia ego demonstro mundum hunc non esse optimum. Cum enim hoc extra litem & aleam positum sit Deum infinitum esse, & hunc mundum quantus quantus est (quoniam finitus est) nullo eum modo demetiri, neque ipsi comparari posse, reliquum est infinitos mundos perfectiores & perfectiores in Deo contineri, quorum nunquam terminabili alterius super alterum perfectionis excessu exprimi semper possit incomprehensibilis illa essentia. Hoc ergo perspicuum est, mundum hunc non esse perfectissimum. Quomodo vero tua ratio solvatur ipse videris, postquam hoc certum est hunc non esse perfectissimum. | Andab. But just as from God you conclude that this world is the best, so from the same [God] and with equal evidence I demonstrate that this world is not the best. For since this has been placed outside dispute and hazard [extra litem & aleam], that God is infinite, and that this world, however great it is [quantus quantus est] (since it is finite), can in no way measure Him out [demetiri], nor be compared to Him, it remains that infinite worlds, more perfect and more perfect, are contained in God, by whose never-terminable excess of perfection, of one over another, that incomprehensible essence could always be expressed. This therefore is perspicuous, that this world is not most perfect. But how your reason [ratio] is to be solved, you yourself will see to [videris], after this is certain, that this [world] is not most perfect. |
| Ereu. Sed non videris, Andabata, argumentum ex proprio loco collegisse. Respondeat itaque cui hoc libet, cum omnes creaturas possibiles simul in unum compuleris corpus, nunquid Deum adaequasti? Numquid plus ad eum accessisti? Et si dixeris magis Deum expressisse: replicabit, quo fine hoc agis? propterne homines in hac vita mortali degentes? At his, quem spectamus, est ex maxima parte super, cum neque minimam operum divinorum particulam demonstratione complecti valeamus. Propter separatos itaque Spiritus, sed his ipsa Dei facies omnes expressiones superans promittitur. Nullo itaque fine melior erit mundus aliquis. | Ereu. But you do not seem [videris], Andabata, to have gathered the argument from its proper place [ex proprio loco]. Let him therefore answer to whom this is pleasing: when you shall have driven together all possible creatures at once into one body, have you then equalled God? Have you at all approached more to Him? And if you shall say [that you have] expressed God more: he will reply, to what end do you do this? Is it for the sake of [propterne] men passing [their days] [degentes] in this mortal life? But for these [men] this [world] which we behold is for the greatest part over and above [super], since we are not able to embrace by demonstration even the least particle of the divine works. For the sake, then, of separated Spirits? But to these the very face of God, surpassing all expressions, is promised. For no end, therefore, will some [other] world be better. |
| Andab. Neque ego hoc urgebam, ut finis aliquis esse crederetur ob quem conderentur, sed ex infinita Dei participabilitate dumtaxat esse possibiles. | Andab. Nor was I urging this, that some end should be believed to be on account of which they would be founded [conderentur], but merely [dumtaxat] that from the infinite participability [participabilitate] of God they are possible. |
| Ereu. Sed hoc ipsum incassum & sine viribus arguisti. Si enim omnibus possibilibus collectis participabilitas aequalis, vel certe eminentia intacta maneat, ex qua non possis adhuc perfectiores creaturas possibiles arguere, cur magis ex eminentia supra hunc mundum efficis alios mundos possibiles, quam ex eminentia super omnes possibiles, alias rursus possibilitate donas? Quantumvis enim infinitas nihil Dei eminentiam adaequare vel metiri confiteris? Sicut enim, si quis ex eminentia superficiei supra lineam, posse semper lineas esse maiores & maiores argueret, aliquam infinitam posset inferre, neque tunc quiesceret, sed alias insuper addere posset, neque superficiei rationem adaequaret; sic ex eminentia Dei seorsim a natura rerum considerata plura creaturis repugnantia sine fine inferre non fuerit paradoxum. Vide ergo ex creaturarum ingenio & non ex infinitate Dei arguendum esse si quid praestare confidas. | Ereu. But this very thing you have argued in vain and without strength. For if, all possibles having been collected, the participability remains equal, or at least [certe] the eminence remains untouched, from which you could not still argue more perfect possible creatures, why do you rather [magis], from the eminence above this world, make [efficis] other worlds possible, than, from the eminence over all possibles, you again endow others with possibility? For do you confess that infinite [creatures] [infinitas], however [many] [quantumvis], in no way [nihil] equal or measure the eminence of God? For just as, if someone, from the eminence of a surface above a line, should argue that lines can always be greater and greater, he could infer some infinite [line], and would not then rest, but could add others besides, and yet would not equal the notion [rationem] of a surface; so from the eminence of God considered apart [seorsim] from the nature of things, it would not be [non fuerit] a paradox to infer, without end, more [things] repugnant to creatures. See therefore that one must argue from the native character [ingenio] of creatures, and not from the infinity of God, if you trust [confidas] to make good [praestare] anything. |
| Andab. Verum ipse ex natura Dei arguisti ad creata. | Andab. But you yourself argued from the nature of God to created things. |
| Ereu. Sic tamen ut factum Dei, id est, ipsum mundum in argumenti partem assumerem. Quod si neque sic contentus es, respice Philosophi iudicium asserentis entia nolle male gubernari. Cuius sententiae eam vim esse existimo, ut quaeque perfectissima quasi sponte nasci ex ipsa entium coaptatione credat. Cum enim haec entia divinarum idearum partus sint, neque dubium esse possit optimam rerum dispositionem summa necessitate in iis consistere, effici videtur tantumdem in his expectandum. Praeterea si de rerum generibus quaeritur, ex hesterna disputatione decretum manet alia nusquam esse posse: si autem Elementa & rerum genera eadem esse necesse sit: video mundorum varietates ad eorum multiiugam dispositionem esse reducendas. Has iterum dispositiones certis limitibus cogi & coarctari, adeo ut omnes lato hoc universi campo non esse comprehensas ego neutiquam affirmare ausim. Reducetur itaque tot creaturarum possibilitas ad species quasdam imaginabiles, sed societatibus entium, id est, mundis vel impossibiles vel incommodas: & fiet facile mundum alium hoc nobiliorem condi non posse. | Ereu. Yet so that I took up [assumerem] the deed [factum] of God, that is, the world itself, into a part of the argument. But if you are not content even so, look back at [respice] the judgement of the Philosopher, asserting that beings [entia] are unwilling to be governed badly. The force of which sentence I reckon to be this, that he believes all the most perfect things [quaeque perfectissima] to be born as if of their own accord [sponte] from the very co-adaptation [coaptatione] of beings. For since these beings are the offspring [partus] of the divine ideas, and it cannot be doubtful that the best disposition of things stands [consistere] in them by the highest necessity, it seems to be brought about [effici] that just so much is to be expected in these. Besides, if it is asked about the kinds [generibus] of things, from yesterday’s disputation it remains decreed that others can be nowhere: but if it is necessary that the Elements and the kinds of things be the same: I see that the varieties of worlds are to be reduced to their many-yoked [multiiugam] disposition. [I see] these dispositions again to be compelled and confined within certain limits, so much so that I would by no means [neutiquam] dare to affirm that all [of them] are not comprehended in this broad field of the universe. The possibility, therefore, of so many creatures will be reduced to certain imaginable species, but either impossible or unfitting [incommodas] for societies of beings, that is, for worlds: and it will become easy [fiet facile] [to see] that another world nobler than this cannot be founded. |
| Andab. Coniicis, Ereuni, non demonstras. At mihi occurrit perfecta demonstratio, quam ut fatear a tua non multum abludere, sic a priori est, cum tua tantum a posteriori nos incitet: In eo autem consistit quod cum Deus omnium Essentiarum non actualium modo, sed etiam earum quas potentiae tenebris obvolutas media dumtaxat luce dispicimus, author sit; conficitur inde esse bonum aliquid, quoniam ab ipso originem trahit: quare & hoc optimum quod magis ab ipso originem habet, & tandem non aliquid ex sese melius vel deterius esse: sed idcirco talia esse quia Deo vel magis vel minus placuerunt. Cum itaque certissimum sit hunc rerum universi mundum prae caeteris placuisse, hunc quoque optimum esse dubitari nullatenus posse. | Andab. You conjecture, Ereunius, you do not demonstrate. But there occurs to me a perfect demonstration, which, though [ut] I confess that it does not much differ [abludere] from yours, yet [sic] is a priori, whereas yours spurs us on [incitet] only a posteriori: and it consists in this, that since God is the author of all Essences, not of the actual ones only, but also of those which, wrapped in the darkness of potency, we discern [dispicimus] by a merely middling [media dumtaxat] light; it is made out [conficitur] thence that something is good because it draws its origin from Him: wherefore also that [is] best which has its origin more from Him; and finally, that something is not better or worse of itself: but that [things] are such for this reason, because they pleased God either more or less. Since therefore it is most certain that this world of the universe of things pleased [Him] before the rest, it can in no way [nullatenus] be doubted that this [world] also is the best. |
| Ereu. Tantumdem dicis, ac si canem vivum asseras leone mortuo meliorem, sic existentem mundum non existentibus non singulis tantum, sed omnibus coniunctim praestare asseris. Et ego assentior, sed an si existentes forent alii, tum hic praestantior futurus foret, nihil ex dictis tuis intelligo. Quod autem quae arguebam, in coniecturas referas, & ego tecum facio; non enim omnes modos quibus elementa in mundum componi possint, comprehendisse me crediderim. Esse tamen finitos hos modos non temere assero, cum hoc in universum verum deprehendatur, latas differentias nequaquam sine fine consistere: cum per contradictionem sibi invicem opponantur. Si autem finiti sint modi in tanta mundi vastitate, vel posuit omnes Deus, vel non omnes ponere vidit melius esse. | Ereu. You say just as much as if you should assert a living dog [to be] better than a dead lion: so you assert that the existing world excels [praestare] the non-existing [worlds], not singly only, but all conjointly. And I assent; but whether, if others were existing, this would then be going to be more excellent, I understand nothing from your sayings. But as to your referring what I was arguing to conjectures, I too side with you [tecum facio]; for I would not believe that I have comprehended all the modes by which the elements could be composed into a world. Yet that these modes are finite I do not rashly assert, since this is detected [deprehendatur] to be true universally [in universum], that broad [latas] differences by no means stand [consistere] without end: since they are opposed to one another by contradiction. But if the modes are finite in so great a vastness of the world, either God posited all, or He saw it to be better not to posit all. |
| Asphal. Nisi temere coniicio, Ereuni, in eam sententiam propendes, quae Deum docet ex consideratione creandorum per motum, haec illis praelegisse; cum tamen Authores quibus te delectatum credo, in oppositam declinent. | Asphal. Unless I conjecture rashly, Ereunius, you lean [propendes] toward that opinion [sententiam] which teaches that God, out of a consideration of the things to be created, through a motion [per motum], chose these before those [praelegisse]; though the Authors with whom I believe you have been delighted decline toward the opposite. |
| [Margin, p. 321: Creaturas etiam possibilitatem a Deo habere.] | [Margin, p. 321: That creatures have even [their] possibility from God.] |
| Ereu. Ne mihi crimen hoc impingito, Asphali, nimis immemor sim principiorum quibus imbutus sum, si in tam clara luce halluciner. Sic ergo habeto. Quod diximus Deo competere ut intelligens esset, nimirum ut plura in ipso cum suis proprietatibus & distinctionibus simul praesto essent, non ad ea quae ibi insinuavimus dumtaxat sese porrigere, sed ad universam rerum naturam, non existentium modo, verum etiam earum quas causarum adytis secretas & sacratas quasi venerabundi inquirimus. Competit autem hoc Deo ex ipsa existentiae existentis natura, ut late tradunt Metaphysici, unde vides Deo esse & intelligere esse idem. | Ereu. Do not fasten [impingito] this charge upon me, Asphalius; I would be too unmindful of the principles with which I have been imbued, if in so clear a light I should be deceived [halluciner]. Hold it [habeto], then, thus. What we said belongs [competere] to God, that He should be understanding [intelligens], namely that more [than one] things should be present [praesto] in Him at once with their properties and distinctions, [you must hold] extends itself not only to those which we there insinuated, but to the universal nature of things, not of existing ones only, but also of those which, set apart [secretas] and consecrated in the inner shrines [adytis] of causes, we inquire into as if in veneration [venerabundi]. And this belongs to God from the very nature of existing existence [existentiae existentis], as the Metaphysicians hand down at large [late]; whence you see that for God to be and to understand are the same. |
| Quare cum experiamur, res in intellectu artificum operum ideas esse, vides ea quae possibilia dicimus, quoad suas rationes ab intellectu divino pendere. Et cum Deum sese diligere necessum sit, eorumdem rationes etiam a voluntate divina possibilitatem sortiri. Haec si crassiori exemplo delineari postulas, aspice illa marmora, quorum naevi sunt luxuriae nostrae irritamenta, & cogita si a perito artifice exscinderentur hae maculae, suisque quaeque limitibus continerentur, non idcirco has futuras singularum figuras, quod artificis manum senserint: verum idcirco talem ab artifice impositum iis modum, quia in toto marmore illud praehabebant: sic itaque hominem, Equum, leonem, ovem tales esse dicito, quia in illa existentia existente tales sunt & ante ipsam possibilitatem fuerunt. | Wherefore, since we experience that things in the understanding [intellectu] of craftsmen [artificum] are the ideas of [their] works, you see that those which we call possible depend, as to their notions [rationes], upon the divine understanding. And since it is necessary [necessum] that God love Himself, [you see] that their notions also obtain [sortiri] possibility from the divine will. If you demand that these things be outlined by a grosser [crassiori] example, look at those marbles whose spots [naevi] are incitements of our luxury, and consider: if these markings [maculae] were cut out by a skilled craftsman, and each were contained within its own limits, these would not be going to be the figures of the single [pieces] for this reason, that they had felt the hand of the craftsman: but [rather] such a mode [was] imposed on them by the craftsman for this reason, because they had it beforehand [praehabebant] in the whole marble: so therefore say that a man, a Horse, a lion, a sheep are such, because in that existing existence they are such, and were [such] before possibility itself. |
| Verumenimvero quoniam hoc a te magis spectari in interrogatione tua crediderim, an bonitas creaturarum Deum ad eas faciendas impulerit? hoc ipsum omnino rationi & Philosophiae repugnare accipito, non eo solum nomine, quod Deus immutabilis sit motionemque in eo ponere sit absurdum: hoc enim quoquo modo vitari posset, cum causalitas finalis sine motu concipi possit (quomodo ipsum Deum a semetipso diligi confitemur) verum ex eo quod inter motorem & motum proportionem aliquam & vim in motore respectu mobilis aliquam intelligamus: concedere vero creatis bonis aliquam ad voluntatem divinam proportionem, vel virtutem aliquam qua in eam agat, non sit in sapiente tolerandum. Denique quam boni rationem creatis respectu aeterni Dei assignabilem credas? Cui esse quod est omne bonum includit, quodque nullo bono destitui possit, cum tamen creata possint non esse, potuerint non fuisse. | But truly [verumenimvero], since I would believe that this was looked at [spectari] by you more in your question, whether the goodness of creatures impelled God to making them? accept [accipito] that this very thing is altogether repugnant to reason and to Philosophy, not on that account [nomine] only, that God is immutable and to posit a motion [motionem] in Him is absurd: for this could be avoided in some way or other [quoquo modo], since final causality can be conceived without motion (in which way we confess that God Himself is loved by Himself); but from this, that between mover and moved we understand some proportion, and some force in the mover with respect to the movable: but to concede to created goods some proportion to the divine will, or some power [virtutem] by which it might act upon it, would not be tolerable in a wise man. Lastly, what notion [rationem] of good would you believe assignable to created things with respect to the eternal God? For whom to be that which He is includes all good, and who can be destitute of no good; whereas created things can not be, [and] could have not been. |

## Words and phrases with alternative or special meanings

- ***optimum / mundus optimus*** — rendered “best / the best world”. the best absolutely vs. best of its kind; ‘optimism’ in the later Leibnizian sense. The Knot anticipates the Leibniz–Malebranche debate on whether God must create the best world; White argues from the identity of God with reason that He must, Andabata from divine infinity that no world can be best.
- ***electio / differentia*** — rendered “election / difference”. choice; distinguishing feature, ground of preference. Principle (from the previous Knot) that there is no choice without a difference between the options — the Buridan problem and later principle of sufficient reason.
- ***magis agendum / agibilia*** — rendered “more to-be-done / things doable”. gerundive of obligation; Aristotelian τὰ πρακτά, the sphere of practical reason. Scholastic practical-reason vocabulary (recta ratio agibilium = prudence, Aquinas ST I-II q.57 a.4).
- ***imperium / imperare*** — rendered “command”. the act of ‘command’ (imperium) following judgement and choice in the Thomist analysis of the human act. Andabata invokes a voluntarist gap between reason’s judgement and its command; Ereunius doubts a separate act of imperium exists (cf. Aquinas ST I-II q.17).
- ***omnibus positis / hic et nunc*** — rendered “all things being posited / here and now”. ‘all requisites given’; the practical judgement in concreto. Scholastic formula for the last practical judgement: contrary practical judgements ‘all things posited’ are contradictory.
- ***prius esse oportet*** — rendered “it must be prior”. prius = earlier, prior; possibly misprint for ‘peius’ (worse). As printed the sense is that a non-rational principle would have to precede reason; ‘peius’ would fit the comparative ‘non melius’ better. Kept as printed.
- ***extra litem & aleam*** — rendered “outside dispute and hazard”. lis = lawsuit, alea = dice-game, risk. Legal and gaming idiom: ‘beyond all question’.
- ***quantus quantus est*** — rendered “however great it is”. reduplicated relative, classical (Terence). Kept literally.
- ***participabilitas*** — rendered “participability”. the capacity of the divine perfection to be shared by creatures. Neoplatonic-scholastic participation metaphysics: Andabata argues that infinite participability entails infinitely many possible, ever better worlds.
- ***eminentia*** — rendered “eminence”. superiority; ‘eminent’ containment of creaturely perfections in God. Scholastic: God contains perfections ‘eminently’; Ereunius’ geometrical analogy (a surface exceeds any line) shows eminence implies nothing about further possibles.
- ***videris (Andab.; Ereu.)*** — rendered “you will see to / you seem”. future perfect ‘you will have seen to it’ (‘that’s your affair’) vs. passive ‘you seem’. Andabata’s ‘ipse videris’ is the classical brush-off; Ereunius’ ‘non videris’ is ‘you do not seem’.
- ***ex proprio loco*** — rendered “from its proper place”. locus = topic (Aristotelian/Ciceronian topica), a source of arguments. Dialectical: the argument is drawn from the wrong topic (God’s infinity rather than creatures’ nature).
- ***super (est)*** — rendered “is over and above”. superest = remains, is superfluous; here adverbial ‘more than enough’. This world already exceeds what men in this life can grasp, so a better one would serve no end.
- ***separati Spiritus*** — rendered “separated Spirits”. angels, or souls separated from bodies. Scholastic term for intelligences without bodies; promised the beatific vision (‘the face of God’).
- ***Philosophi iudicium … entia nolle male gubernari*** — rendered “the judgement of the Philosopher … that beings are unwilling to be governed badly”.  Aristotle, Metaphysics XII.10, 1076a3–4 (τὰ δὲ ὄντα οὐ βούλεται πολιτεύεσθαι κακῶς), closing Book Λ with Homer’s ‘let there be one lord’.
- ***coaptatio*** — rendered “co-adaptation”. fitting together, harmony of parts. White’s mechanist-providential view: the best order emerges ‘of itself’ from the mutual fitting of beings.
- ***hesterna disputatio*** — rendered “yesterday’s disputation”. the previous day’s dialogue. Refers back to Dialogue I/II, where it was argued that there can be no other elements or kinds of things than ours.
- ***multiiugus*** — rendered “many-yoked”. manifold, of many combinations. The variety of possible worlds reduces to combinations of the same elements.
- ***societates entium, id est, mundi*** — rendered “societies of beings, that is, worlds”.  Worlds conceived as communities of mutually fitted beings.
- ***a priori / a posteriori*** — rendered “a priori / a posteriori”. from cause to effect / from effect to cause. Scholastic (not Kantian) sense of demonstration propter quid vs. quia.
- ***abludere*** — rendered “differ”. to be out of tune, unlike (opp. alludere). Rare classical verb (Horace, Sat. II.3.320).
- ***placere (Deo)*** — rendered “please (God)”.  Andabata’s voluntarist argument: things are good because they please God (Ockhamist/Cartesian voluntarism), which Ereunius turns into a mere tautology.
- ***canis vivus … leo mortuus*** — rendered “a living dog … a dead lion”.  Ecclesiastes 9:4: ‘melior est canis vivus leone mortuo’.
- ***tecum facio*** — rendered “I side with you”. facere cum aliquo = to take someone’s part. Classical idiom.
- ***latae differentiae*** — rendered “broad differences”. differences of wide extent, i.e. fundamental contrary differences. They cannot be multiplied endlessly because contraries are bounded by contradiction.
- ***per motum*** — rendered “through a motion”. by being moved (as by a final cause). Asphalius suspects Ereunius of making God moved by the goodness of creatures; Ereunius denies it.
- ***existentia existens*** — rendered “existing existence”. God as subsistent existence (ipsum esse subsistens). Thomist: God’s essence is His existence; hence His being and understanding are identical.
- ***halluciner*** — rendered “I should be deceived”. hallucinor = wander in mind, blunder. Deponent subjunctive; classical sense ‘err’, not visual hallucination.
- ***adyta*** — rendered “inner shrines”. sanctuaries, innermost recesses of a temple. Religious metaphor for the hidden causes of possible natures.
- ***rationes (rerum possibilium)*** — rendered “notions”. the intelligible essences of possibles. Augustinian-Thomist doctrine that possibles have their rationes in the divine intellect (ideas); White adds that they get possibility also from the will by which God loves Himself.
- ***sortiri*** — rendered “obtain”. receive by lot, get. As in glossary.
- ***naevi / maculae (marmorum)*** — rendered “spots / markings”. birthmarks, veins of marble. The marble analogy: the cutter only frees figures already present in the veining — cf. Michelangelo’s and Leibniz’s marble image (Nouveaux Essais, preface).
- ***praehabere*** — rendered “have beforehand”. pre-contain. The figures pre-exist in the marble as the natures pre-exist in the divine existence.
- ***causalitas finalis*** — rendered “final causality”. the causing proper to an end, which moves as loved. Aristotle Met. XII.7 (κινεῖ ὡς ἐρώμενον): God loves Himself without being moved; creatures can have no ‘proportion’ to move His will.
- ***virtus*** — rendered “power”. virtue, active power, force. Here physical-causal ‘power’, distinguished from moral ‘virtue’ earlier in the Knot (bracketed).
