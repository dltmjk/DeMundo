# De Mundo — Dialogue I (De materia mundi), Knot iii

*Latin title:* Nodus III. Mundos non esse plures (margin: Non esse plures mundos)  
*English title:* Knot III. That there are not more [than one] worlds  
*Pages:* PDF pp. 35–43 = printed pp. 24–32 (from 'Ereu. Etsi non credam Andabatam' to the end of Asphalius' speech '…tam serio inculcatum axioma'; Knot iv begins lower on p. 32)

## Dialogue I, Knot iii (printed pp. 24–32)

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| [p. 24] [Margin: Nodus III. Non esse plures mundos.] Ereu. Etsi non credam Andabatam de hac re negotium mihi facessere velle (quorsum enim tot stellae? et quantum ad nos attinebunt? [p. 25] si intermundiis quibusdam inanibus disseptae neque lumine neque alio opere sensus nostros percellere vel naturam adiuvare possint.) Si tamen nihil illi ad manum sit quod huic praevertendum autumet; ego tuae voluntati obsecundem libens. | [p. 24] [Margin: Knot III. That there are not more [than one] worlds [plures mundos].] Ereu. Although I would not believe that Andabata wishes to make trouble [negotium facessere] for me about this matter (for to what end [quorsum] so many stars? and how much will they pertain to us, [p. 25] if, partitioned off [disseptae] by certain empty inter-worlds [intermundiis], they can neither by light nor by other work strike [percellere] our senses or aid nature?) If nevertheless nothing is at hand to him which he would declare [autumet] must be put before [praevertendum] this, I would gladly comply with your will. |
| Andab. Nihil hercle magnum impraesentiarum occurrit, et si quid foret, non tam avidus sim, ut Asphalio tantillum non possim temporis commodare: Quin et ipsa disputatio mihi periucunda futura est, et si quae in me est opera, quae eam promovere possit, libenter indulgebo. | Andab. Nothing great, by Hercules, occurs [to me] at present [impraesentiarum], and if there were anything, I would not be so greedy [avidus] that I could not lend [commodare] to Asphalius so little [tantillum] of time: nay rather [quin], the disputation itself also is going to be very pleasant to me, and if there is any effort [opera] in me which could promote it, I shall willingly grant [indulgebo] [it]. |
| Ereu. Hoc itaque non tam sciscitor quam assumo, novi enim te dissentire non posse. Quod nihil est, nihil prorsus esse, neque effectus, neque notitiam ullam habere, neque quod magis est, partes. | Ereu. This therefore I do not so much inquire [sciscitor] as assume [assumo], for I know that you cannot dissent: that what is nothing is altogether [prorsus] nothing, and has neither effects, nor any notice [notitiam], nor, what is more, parts. |
| Andab. Habes me confitentem. | Andab. You have me confessing. |
| Ereu. Sed eane fide huic dicto adhaerescis qua isti: Ens esse ens et non esse non ens, par siquidem huic videtur non ens esse non ens, et nullatenus esse ens; hoc est ea fide ut omnibus opinionibus et Philosophorum authoritati, imo ipsis sensibus mendacium imponas, si huic obiecerint veritati. | Ereu. But do you adhere to this saying with that faith with which [you adhere] to that [one]: that Being is being and is not non-being [Ens esse ens et non esse non ens] — since equal to this [par … huic] seems [this]: that non-being is non-being, and in no way [nullatenus] is being; that is, with such faith that you would impose a lie [mendacium imponas] upon all opinions and upon the authority of the Philosophers, nay upon the senses themselves, if they should have objected [obiecerint] [anything] to this truth? |
| Andab. Adhaeresco. Clare enim cerno nullam esse intellectus, nullam scientiae, nullam humanae actionis constantiam, si quae labes [p. 26] huic principio, i. [id est] ipsi naturae entis impingatur. Neque aliud esse de hoc dubitare quam ambigere, an idem simul ens et non ens esse possit, sive consistere in sese contradictio. | Andab. I adhere. For I clearly discern that there is no constancy of the intellect, none of science [scientiae], none of human action, if any stain [labes] be thrust [p. 26] upon this principle, i.e. upon the very nature of being [entis]; and that to doubt of this is nothing other than to waver [ambigere] whether the same [thing] can be at once being and non-being, or [whether] a contradiction [can] stand together [consistere] in itself. |
| Ereu. Vir es, Andabata, si dictum praestas. Itaque progredior. Infinitos posuit mundos Epicurus; sed pauci meo usui satis sunt. Si itaque duo tantummodo mundi forent et ii figura utrimque sphaerica a se disclusi; praeterea vero nihil, contiguosne putes; an aliquo a se invicem distractos intervallo? | Ereu. You are a man, Andabata, if you make good [praestas] [your] word [dictum]. Therefore I proceed. Epicurus posited infinite worlds; but few are enough for my use. If therefore there were only two worlds, and these, with [their] figure spherical on both sides, shut off [disclusi] from each other; but besides [them] nothing — would you think [them] contiguous, or drawn apart [distractos] from one another by some interval? |
| Andab. Utrum velis, nihil enim certi a natura haberent quo altera pars ex necessitate affirmanda sit. | Andab. Whichever you wish; for they would have nothing certain from nature by which the one side [altera pars] must of necessity be affirmed. |
| Ereu. Sunto igitur distracti, et unus quidem hic, reliquus illic, hoc enim consequens est; quid censes τὸ hic et illic in iis significare. | Ereu. Let them be, therefore, drawn apart, and the one indeed here, the remaining [one] there, for this is consequent; what do you reckon [censes] the [τὸ] ‘here’ and ‘there’ signify in them? |
| Andab. Quid enim nisi hunc in hac spatii imaginarii parte situm esse, alterum vero in alia. | Andab. For what [else] but that this one is situated in this part of imaginary space [spatii imaginarii], but the other in another. |
| Ereu. Siccine mecum agis Andabata, et tam cito tot verbis depactam retractas fidem? Spatium enim imaginarium ipsa voce prodit inanitatem suam et sese nihil esse coarguit, nihilo autem partes et effecta proxime detraxisti? | Ereu. Is it thus [siccine] you deal with me, Andabata, and so quickly retract the faith [fidem] pledged [depactam] with so many words? For imaginary space by its very word [voce] betrays [prodit] its emptiness [inanitatem] and convicts [coarguit] itself of being nothing; but from nothing you just now [proxime] took away [detraxisti] parts and effects? |
| Andab. Recte reprehendis Ereuni, quare eam responsionem, quantumvis gravissimorum sit Philosophiae principum, desero, et potiorem ab aequalibus Magistris desumptam [p. 27] antefero. Aio igitur mundos dissitos esse per diversas ubicationes: est autem ubicatio (ne forte interrogare tibi necesse sit) ens quoddam a subiecto inseparabile, cuius virtus et tota ratio sit constituere corpus hic vel illic secundum speciem ubicationis quam sortitur. | Andab. Rightly you reprove [me], Ereunius; wherefore that response, however much it be [the response] of the weightiest princes of Philosophy, I desert, and a better [one] [potiorem], taken from equal [aequalibus] Masters, [p. 27] I prefer [antefero]. I say therefore that the worlds are set apart [dissitos] through diverse ubications [ubicationes]: and ubication is (lest perhaps it be necessary for you to ask) a certain being [ens] inseparable from [its] subject, whose power [virtus] and whole reason [ratio] is to constitute a body here or there according to the species [speciem] of ubication which it obtains [sortitur]. |
| Ereu. Nescio quantum te emendaveris. Prius enim responsum speciem aliquam satisfactionis habuit, visus es enim aliquam loci et distantiae notitiam assignasse, nunc puris putis illudis voculis. Pensa enim tecum si domus, Cathedrae, plateae, navis, alicuiusve alterius quod ignorares, Entis explicationem peteres: referres autem domum esse rem, cuius natura esset esse domum, et cathedram similiter vel navim res esse, quarum haec natura foret navim et Cathedram essendo esse: poteras aliter quam vel luteum existimare doctorem vel irrisorem? Nescis hoc primum Dialecticorum in condendis definitionibus esse praeceptum ne definitum in definitione maneat inclusum. | Ereu. I know not how much you have amended yourself. For the prior response had some appearance [speciem] of satisfaction, for you seemed to have assigned some notice [notitiam] of place and distance; now you mock [illudis] [me] with pure, mere [puris putis] little words [voculis]. For weigh [pensa] with yourself: if you were to ask the explication of a house, of a Chair [Cathedrae], of a street [plateae], of a ship, or of any other Being which you did not know: and you were to bring back [referres] [the answer] that a house is a thing whose nature was to be a house, and similarly that a chair or a ship are things whose nature this were, to be a ship and a Chair by being [essendo]: could you [poteras] have reckoned [your] teacher otherwise than either [a teacher] of mud [luteum] or a mocker [irrisorem]? Do you not know that this is the first precept of the Dialecticians in founding [condendis] definitions, that the defined [definitum] not remain included in the definition? |
| Nunc cum a te postulo in mundis illis tuis quid hic et illic significent? refero significare entia quae ex notione sua habeant hic vel illic constituere. Vide quantis magistris credideris. Et potuissem ipsa te loci notione coegisse ab dicto discedere: sive enim ad orientem te converteris, sive peragratas Hispanorum classibus gentes interrogaveris, sive excultos [p. 28] legibus et disciplinis populos, sive rapto in diem viventes barbaros adieris, unam communemque loci apud omnes intentionem didiceris, haustam a sensibus, haustam a natura, ideoque nullam locorum diversitatem passam. | Now when I demand of you what ‘here’ and ‘there’ signify in those worlds of yours, I bring back [refero] [the answer]: that they signify beings which from their notion have [it] to constitute here or there. See to how great masters you have given credit [credideris]. And I could have compelled you by the very notion of place to depart from [your] statement [dicto]: for whether you turn yourself to the east, or question the nations traversed [peragratas] by the fleets of the Spaniards, or approach peoples cultivated [excultos] [p. 28] by laws and disciplines, or barbarians living on plunder [rapto] from day to day, you will have learnt one and common intention [intentionem] of place among all, drawn from the senses, drawn from nature, and therefore having suffered [passam] no diversity of places. |
| Ad hos omnes appello et quotidianas eorum voces, quibus ubi situm sit quippiam, exponunt, si non omnes ambiens quippiam et ut Aristoteles vocat, vas quoddam immobile pro loco reddant. Quod si verum est, quid clarius esse potest quam positam in nihilo rem locum nullum habere? Unde duos illos mundos neutrum, si dissiti sunt, locum habere, et quod consequens est, loco disparari nequaquam posse perfectum est. | To all these I appeal, and to their daily words [voces], by which they set forth where anything is situated, [to see] whether they do not all render [reddant] for place something surrounding [ambiens], and, as Aristotle calls [it], a certain immobile vessel [vas]. And if this is true, what can be clearer than that a thing placed in nothing has no place? Whence it has been made complete [perfectum est] that neither of those two worlds, if they are set apart [dissiti], has place, and, what is consequent, that they can in no way be separated [disparari] by place. |
| Et quod de loci notione locutus sum, eandem vim in omnibus decem generum intentionibus obtinet. Omnes enim a natura sunt, et toti hominum generi communes, et ex communibus hominum de iis effatis rationes eorum colligendae sunt, et non ex sententiis Doctorum, vel usu tralatitio Rhetorum. | And what I have spoken about the notion of place holds the same force in all the intentions [intentionibus] of the ten kinds [decem generum]. For all are from nature, and common to the whole race of men, and their reasons [rationes] are to be gathered from the common pronouncements [effatis] of men about them, and not from the opinions [sententiis] of the Doctors, or from the transferred [tralatitio] usage of the Rhetoricians. |
| Sed ut ad nostram disputationem redeamus, hoc attende. Cum non aliud praeter duos ipsos mundos sit, neque seipsis distare possint, prorsus inter ἀμήχανα referendum esse ut distent. Num vel hoc non vides? distare aliqua non posse sine distantia, hoc est, sine intervallo medio. Intervallum autem et spatium sine partibus [p. 29] non esse, quas non Entis nullas affirmas? non minus itaque evidens esse debet non distare illa, quae cum sint, nihil divisibile interiectum habent. Si ergo plures mundi sunt, contigui sunt, si autem contigui, ne graveris quonam contigui sunt, seu qua vi et natura constat contiguos eos esse, declarare. | But that we may return to our disputation, attend to this: [that,] since there is nothing other besides the two worlds themselves, and they cannot be distant [distare] by themselves, it must be referred altogether among the impossibles [ἀμήχανα] that they be distant. Or [num] do you not see even this? that some [things] cannot be distant without distance, that is, without an interval in the middle [intervallo medio]. And that interval and space are not without parts, [p. 29] which [parts] you affirm [to be] none of non-Being? Therefore it ought to be no less evident that those [things] are not distant which, when they are, have nothing divisible thrown between [interiectum]. If then there are more worlds [than one] [plures], they are contiguous; and if contiguous, do not be reluctant [ne graveris] to declare by what [quonam] they are contiguous, or by what force and nature it is established [constat] that they are contiguous. |
| Andab. Cur id petis? cum enim videam ex natura ipsorum hoc esse ut et remoti et contigui esse possint, satis video ex natura ipsorum neutrum proficisci posse; neque ut separati neque ut contigui sint: nihil autem praeterea supponis, videtur itaque mihi potius dicendum quod in abstracto quodam statu collocati sint, et neque remoti neque tamen proximi dicendi sint. | Andab. Why do you ask that? For since I see that this is from their nature, that they can be both removed [remoti] and contiguous, I see sufficiently that neither can proceed from their nature — neither that they be separated nor that they be contiguous: but you suppose nothing besides; it seems therefore to me rather to be said that they are placed in a certain abstract state [statu], and are to be called neither removed nor yet next [proximi]. |
| Ereu. Siccine non vides quod si interiectio spatii realis sit remotio, interiectio maioris vel minoris sit maior vel minor remotio, minor vero remotio sit approximatio, et quod approximatio sit motus ad coniunctionem, ita ut finita approximatione ex necessitate maneat coniunctio: quare sublata remotione ex necessitate manet coniunctio. Sunto itaque duae quantitates cum tertia mediante hoc ipso sunt separatae et sunto eaedem sine tertia mediante, eo ipso sunt coniunctae, neque haec accidentia tollere possis nisi ipsam quoque quanti et distantiae rationem et naturam tollas. | Ereu. Do you thus [siccine] not see that if the throwing-between [interiectio] of real space is removal [remotio], the throwing-between of a greater or a lesser [space] is a greater or lesser removal, but a lesser removal is approximation [approximatio], and that approximation is motion toward conjunction [coniunctionem], so that, approximation having been finished [finita], conjunction remains of necessity: wherefore, removal having been taken away, conjunction of necessity remains. Let there be therefore two quantities with a third mediating: by this very [fact] they are separated; and let the same be without a third mediating: by that very [fact] they are conjoined; nor could you take away these accidents unless you take away also the very reason [rationem] and nature of the quantum [quanti] and of distance. |
| [p. 30] Andab. Sic est Ereuni et necesse est esse, quandoquidem sublatis mente quibuscumque aliis, clarum est ex ipsa natura quantitatum, cuncta quae commemoras evenire, et quo magis magisque cogitationem in re ipsa figo, eo evidentior et certior mihi sese aperit veritas. | [p. 30] Andab. So it is, Ereunius, and it is necessary that it be, since, all others whatsoever having been taken away by the mind, it is clear from the very nature of quantities that all [the things] which you recount come about; and the more and more I fix [my] thought on the thing itself, by so much [eo] the more evident and more certain the truth opens itself to me. |
| Ereu. Euge, Andabata, oculos mentis fortiter intende, et videbis nihil in disciplinis quae tantum de sua evidentia gloriantur vel magis perspicuum, vel magis forte et firmum haberi posse; sed aliqua animi contentione opus est, quoniam haec figuris ob oculos, sicut illa nequaquam proponi possunt. | Ereu. Well done [euge], Andabata, strain [intende] the eyes of the mind strongly, and you will see that nothing in the disciplines which glory so much about their evidence [evidentia] can be held either more perspicuous, or more strong and firm; but there is need of some straining [contentione] of the mind [animi], since these [things] can in no way be set forth before the eyes by figures, as those [can]. |
| Et nunc converso intuitu vide quid confectum tibi sit non infinitos illos sortilegii Epicurei mundos dumtaxat disparuisse, sed neque duos quidem ullos esse posse invicem a se distractos; sed quicquid usquam est quanti id in unum contiguitate et vicinia certe mundum conspirare. | And now, with [your] gaze [intuitu] turned about, see what has been accomplished [confectum] for you: not only that those infinite worlds of the Epicurean lot-drawing [sortilegii] have vanished, but that not even any two can be drawn apart from one another; but that whatever of quantum [quanti] there is anywhere, that certainly conspires [conspirare] by contiguity and neighbourhood [vicinia] into one world. |
| [Margin: Non dari vacuum.] Vide etiam aliud ingens ad totam Philosophiam principium, te in sui cognitionem passis, ut aiunt, ulnis invitans. Est autem celebre illud, vacuum in natura nullum esse, nullum induci posse, quod ipsum evidentia nihilo minori sese ostentat. Si enim vacuum est locus sine corpore, hoc est, corpus concavum, sine aliquo quod cavitatem impleat, nonne vides concavi latera quanta esse, sine tertia [p. 31] mediante. Vides ergo ex ipsa notione vacui coniuncta esse latera, et si coniuncta sint, iam nullum reliquum esse vacuum, cum ipsa sui definitione non esse coniuncta involvat. | [Margin: That a vacuum is not given [non dari].] See also another principle, huge for the whole of Philosophy, inviting you to the knowledge of itself with outspread arms [passis ulnis], as they say. And it is that celebrated [principle], that there is no vacuum in nature, that none can be induced [induci], which itself displays itself with no less evidence. For if a vacuum is place without body, that is, a concave body without anything which would fill the cavity, do you not see that the sides of the concave are quanta [quanta], without a third [p. 31] mediating? You see therefore from the very notion of a vacuum that the sides are conjoined, and if they are conjoined, that now no vacuum remains, since by the very definition of itself it involves [that they are] not conjoined. |
| Accipe et tertium. [Margin: Rarum et densum in complexione {?quantitatis} cum quanto consistere.] Rara quae dicimus et densa ipsa quantitatis ad subiectum proportione talia esse, cum enim nihil clarius sit quam aerem aqua, aquam terra magis esse divisibilem, hoc nusquam negabile est proportionem divisibilitatis seu quantitatis suae ad aerem maiorem esse quam divisibilitatis suae ad aquam; et eodem modo de aqua ad terram disputari potest. | Take also a third. [Margin: That rare and dense consist [consistere] in the complexion [complexione] of quantity with the quantum.] That [the things] which we call rare and dense are such by the very proportion of quantity to [its] subject; for since nothing is clearer than that air is more divisible than water, water than earth, this is nowhere deniable: that the proportion of its divisibility or quantity to air is greater than [that] of its divisibility to water; and in the same mode it can be disputed about water to earth. |
| Hoc autem cum laxatione pororum et admixtione vacui (quod nullum est) confieri nequaquam fas sit, sequitur non ex alio quam ex ipsa quantitatis ad quantum habitudine originem trahere. Quantumvis enim aliquod corpus subingressu rarioris, ipsum rarefieri contingat, tamen hoc ipsum de corpore subingresso affirmari non potest: vel si de uno affirmetur, certe si semper et ulterius repetatur quaestio, ad aliquod deveniendum erit, quod cum rarius sit non a mixtione adventitii corporis raritatem acceperit. | And since it is in no way right [fas] that this be brought about [confieri] by the loosening of pores and the admixture of vacuum (which is none), it follows [that it] draws [its] origin from nothing other than from the very habitude [habitudine] of quantity to the quantum. For however much it may happen that some body itself is rarefied by the entering-under [subingressu] of a rarer [body], yet this same cannot be affirmed of the body that entered under: or if it be affirmed of one, certainly, if the question be repeated always and further, one will have to come down [deveniendum erit] to something which, though [cum] it is rarer, will not have received [its] rarity from the mixture of an adventitious body. |
| Asphal. Praeclara sunt haec quidem sed fortassis adeo subtilia ut a plerisque non sine studio et labore capiantur, et quod consequens est, idonea paucis, moribus nostris adeo ad otium defluentibus, ut ad primam laboris men-[p. 32]tionem nauseabundi avertamur. | Asphal. These [things] are splendid indeed, but perhaps so subtle that by most they are not grasped [capiantur] without study and labour, and, what is consequent, [they are] suitable for few, our manners [moribus] so flowing down [defluentibus] to leisure [otium] that at the first men-[p. 32]tion of labour we turn away nauseated [nauseabundi]. |
| Sed est quod moneam, ne forte per oblivium praetereris. Fama enim celebri fertur opinio Ioannis cuiusdam cognomento Grammatici, qui dimensiones reales subsistentes et non imaginarias introduxit, contra quas nihil possit tuum de non Ente tam serio inculcatum axioma. | But there is [something] which I should warn [you of], lest perhaps you pass [it] over through forgetfulness [oblivium]. For by celebrated fame is reported [fertur] the opinion of a certain John, by surname the Grammarian [Grammatici], who introduced real subsistent [subsistentes] and not imaginary dimensions [dimensiones], against which your axiom about non-Being, so seriously inculcated, could do nothing. |

## Words and phrases with alternative or special meanings

- ***intermundia*** — rendered “inter-worlds”. The Latin calque (Cicero, De natura deorum 1.18) of Epicurus' μετακόσμια, the empty spaces between the worlds where Epicurus placed the gods. Ereunius' aside (why would God make stars sealed off in intermundia, useless to us?) sets up the Epicurean plurality of worlds that the knot refutes.
- ***negotium facessere*** — rendered “make trouble”. 'cause difficulty', 'raise objections'; facessere can also mean 'carry out'. Same idiom as in Knot ii (glossary); here Ereunius doubts Andabata will contest the point.
- ***autumare*** — rendered “declare”. 'assert', 'think', 'reckon'; archaic/comic (Plautine) word. White's taste for archaic Latin (cf. puris putis, impraesentiarum).
- ***impraesentiarum*** — rendered “at present”. Archaic adverb (in praesentia rerum) = 'for the time being'. Colloquial-archaic register of the dialogue.
- ***notitia*** — rendered “notice”. 'knowledge', 'cognition', 'concept'. Scholastic: an act or item of cognition; 'nothing has no notice' = it cannot be an object of knowledge (glossary).
- ***Ens esse ens et non esse non ens*** — rendered “Being is being and is not non-being”. Could be taken as 'that being is being and that not-being [is] not being' (non esse as noun phrase), but the parallel 'non ens esse non ens' shows non esse is the verb. The principle of identity / non-contradiction, Aristotle Metaphysics IV.3–4; White makes it the foundation of 'all constancy of intellect and science'.
- ***labes*** — rendered “stain”. 'blot', 'flaw', 'fall/collapse'. A flaw imputed to the first principle.
- ***ambigere*** — rendered “waver”. 'doubt', 'be in two minds', 'dispute'. Etymologically 'drive both ways', apt for the 'being and non-being at once' contradiction.
- ***consistere (in sese contradictio)*** — rendered “stand together”. 'subsist', 'hold together', 'consist'. Whether a contradiction can 'stand' as a coherent thing; later (margin p. 31) consistere = 'consist in'.
- ***spatium imaginarium*** — rendered “imaginary space”. 'space of the imagination', 'possible space'. Late-scholastic term (Suárez, the Coimbrans, the Jesuit cursus) for the infinite 'space' beyond the world in which God could create other worlds; usually said to be nothing positive. White exploits the admission that it is 'nothing'.
- ***depactam fidem*** — rendered “the faith pledged”. depacisci = 'bargain for, agree'; fides = 'faith', 'promise', 'credence'. Refers back to Andabata's adherence 'ea fide' to the principle of non-being.
- ***aequales Magistri*** — rendered “equal Masters”. 'Masters of equal standing' (to the 'princes of Philosophy'), or 'contemporary Masters' (aequalis = contemporary). Probably ironical: the second answer (ubications) is from the newer scholastics. Both senses possible; the English keeps 'equal'.
- ***ubicatio*** — rendered “ubication”. 'whereness', 'place-mode'; = ubi, the category 'where'. Late-scholastic (Suárez, Disp. Met. 51; Hurtado, Arriaga): an intrinsic mode, inseparable from its subject, that makes a body be 'here'. White (a Digbean anti-'modist') treats it as a definition that contains the defined.
- ***virtus et tota ratio*** — rendered “power and whole reason”. virtus = 'force', 'efficacy'; ratio = 'formal character', 'essence', 'account'. Scholastic: the ratio of a thing is its formal definition.
- ***sortiri*** — rendered “obtain”. 'get by lot', 'receive', 'be allotted'. Echoed by sortilegium below.
- ***species*** — rendered “species / appearance”. Rendered 'species' (kind) of ubication, but 'appearance' in 'speciem satisfactionis' (Glossary: species (veritatis) = appearance). The double sense lets Ereunius suggest ubication has only the look of an explanation.
- ***puris putis voculis*** — rendered “pure, mere little words”. purus putus = 'unadulterated', 'sheer' (Plautine/archaic doublet). Archaism; verba vs res: words with no notion behind them.
- ***luteus (doctor)*** — rendered “[a teacher] of mud”. 'worthless', 'dirty'; also 'yellow, saffron' (luteus from lutum, weld). Cicero (In Pisonem; Verrines 2.3.35) 'homo luteus' = a worthless fellow.
- ***referre / refero*** — rendered “bring back [the answer]”. 'report', 'reply', 'refer'. The construction is elliptical in both places (referres, p. 27; refero, p. 27): the answer one 'carries back'.
- ***condere definitiones*** — rendered “found definitions”. 'compose', 'lay down'. Non-theological sense of condere (glossary). The rule that the definitum must not enter the definition is from Aristotle, Topics VI.4.
- ***intentio (loci; decem generum)*** — rendered “intention”. 'notion', 'concept', 'meaning'; scholastic intentio prima = concept of a thing. White: the 'common intention' of place, drawn from senses and nature and shared by all peoples, decides its meaning, not the Doctors.
- ***ambiens / vas immobile*** — rendered “something surrounding / immobile vessel”. 'encompassing'; 'container'. Aristotle Physics IV.4, 212a14–21: place is 'the first immobile limit of the containing body', 'like a vessel that cannot be moved' (ἀγγεῖον ἀμετακίνητον). Knot iv takes up the immobility of place.
- ***decem genera*** — rendered “ten kinds”. = the ten categories (praedicamenta). Aristotelian categories; 'genus' rendered 'kind' throughout (glossary).
- ***effata*** — rendered “pronouncements”. 'sayings', 'utterances', 'axioms' (Cicero uses effatum for ἀξίωμα). Ordinary speech as the source of the rationes of the categories.
- ***tralatitius (usus)*** — rendered “transferred [usage]”. (1) translaticius = 'metaphorical, figurative'; (2) tralaticius = 'customary, handed down, commonplace'. Both fit 'of the Rhetoricians': figurative usage or merely conventional usage.
- ***ἀμήχανα*** — rendered “the impossibles”. Greek: 'without means', 'impracticable', 'impossible'. Printed in Greek in the text; kept.
- ***distare / distantia / intervallum medium*** — rendered “be distant / distance / interval in the middle”.  Core argument: distance requires a divisible intervening quantum; nothing has no parts, so worlds separated by nothing are contiguous.
- ***interiectio / interiectum*** — rendered “throwing-between / thrown between”. 'interposition', 'interposed'. Kept literal to show the spatial image.
- ***remotio / approximatio / coniunctio*** — rendered “removal / approximation / conjunction”. 'distance', 'drawing-near', 'contact/union'. White's quasi-mathematical argument that zero removal = contact.
- ***contiguus / contiguitas / vicinia*** — rendered “contiguous / contiguity / neighbourhood”. Aristotle Physics V.3: contiguous (ἐχόμενον) = touching with nothing between.
- ***proximi*** — rendered “next”. 'nearest', 'adjacent'. Andabata's 'abstract state' — neither removed nor next — is the scholastic escape Ereunius rejects.
- ***sortilegium Epicureum*** — rendered “Epicurean lot-drawing”. 'divination by lots', 'sortilege', or 'sorcery'. Mocks the Epicurean worlds produced by chance (the fortuitous concourse of atoms); possibly also 'magic trick'.
- ***conspirare*** — rendered “conspire”. 'breathe together', 'agree', 'unite'. Stoic-tinged word (cosmic sympathy); here literally 'come together into one'.
- ***quantum / quanta / quanti*** — rendered “quantum / quanta / of quantum”. A thing having quantity, as distinct from quantitas (the accident). White, following Digby, distinguishes quantity and the quantum; rare and dense are explained by the 'habitude of quantity to the quantum'.
- ***vacuum (non dari)*** — rendered “vacuum (is not given)”. dari = 'exist', 'occur' in scholastic Latin. Aristotle Physics IV.6–9; against atomists and against Galileo's interstitial vacua (Two New Sciences, 1638).
- ***passis ulnis*** — rendered “with outspread arms”. Proverbial ('as they say').
- ***rarum / densum; raritas*** — rendered “rare / dense; rarity”. 'thin/thick', 'loose/compact'. White (with Digby, Two Treatises 1644) makes rarity and density primary differences of quantity, without pores or vacuum.
- ***complexio*** — rendered “complexion”. 'combination', 'composition', 'embrace'. In the margin; = the joining of quantity with the quantum (the margin reading quantitatis is uncertain).
- ***habitudo*** — rendered “habitude”. 'relation', 'disposition', 'proportion'. Scholastic habitudo = relation of one thing to another.
- ***laxatio pororum / admixtio vacui*** — rendered “loosening of pores / admixture of vacuum”.  The atomist and Galilean explanation of rarefaction that White rejects.
- ***subingressus*** — rendered “entering-under”. 'penetration', 'insinuation' (of a subtler body). The Cartesian/peripatetic explanation of rarefaction by a subtle matter entering the pores; White argues it leads to a regress.
- ***Ioannes cognomento Grammaticus*** — rendered “John, by surname the Grammarian”.  John Philoponus (6th c.), who in his commentary on Physics IV argued for place as a real, three-dimensional extension (διάστημα) distinct from bodies. Knot iv answers him.
- ***dimensiones reales subsistentes*** — rendered “real subsistent dimensions”. subsistens = 'existing by itself' (not inhering in a subject). Philoponus' space as an empty but real extension.
- ***oblivium*** — rendered “forgetfulness”. Rare form for oblivio. Kept as printed.
