## Dialogue II, Knot ix, part 1 (printed pp. 212–221)

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| [p. 212] Ereu. [Margin: Nodus IX. Corpus loco moveri.] Permitte ut paulo altius rem incipiam, & spero me nobili tuae quaestioni (talis enim reapse est) lucem aliquam allaturum, no-[p. 213]visti igitur in duo significata propagari a Physicis τὸ loco moveri. [Margin: per se vel per accidens.] Quaedam enim per se alia per accidens loco moveri ab iis dicuntur. | [p. 212] Ereu. [Margin: Knot IX. That a body is moved in place [loco moveri].] Permit that I begin the matter a little more deeply [altius], and I hope that I shall bring some light to your noble question (for such it is in fact [reapse]); you [p. 213] know, then, that ‘the being moved in place’ [τὸ loco moveri] is spread [propagari] by the Physicists into two significations. [Margin: by itself [per se] or by accident [per accidens].] For some things are said by them to be moved in place by themselves [per se], others by accident [per accidens]. |
| Et ne te significationum varietas distentum teneat: locum nosti dici extimam rei superficiem, qua cum superficie ambientis coniungitur: Rursus vides omne quod movetur partes habere, unde semper divisibile est in aliquam partem proximam superficiei ambientis, & in alias quae his partibus externis coniunguntur. Si itaque quando quaeritur de eo quod est loco motum, de toto quaeritur, seu de eo in quo est superficies superficiei loci contermina, dicimus illud per se loco moveri. Si vero de illo quaeratur quod continetur infra totum, sic ut superficies ipsius locum non contingat, illud dicimus per accidens seu per aliud loco motum. Et hic esto sensus horum terminorum impraesentiarum, aliis enim locis alia significare possunt. | And lest the variety of the significations should hold you distracted [distentum]: you know that place [locum] is said [to be] the outermost [extimam] surface [superficiem] of a thing, by which it is joined with the surface of the surrounding [body] [ambientis]. Again, you see that everything which is moved has parts, whence it is always divisible into some part nearest to the surface of the surrounding [body], and into others which are joined to these external parts. If therefore, when it is asked concerning that which is moved in place, it is asked concerning the whole, or concerning that in which is the surface bordering on [contermina] the surface of the place, we say that it is moved in place by itself [per se]. But if it be asked concerning that which is contained beneath the whole, so that its surface does not touch the place, that we say [is] moved in place by accident [per accidens] or through another [per aliud]. And let this be the sense of these terms at present [impraesentiarum]; for in other places they can signify other things. |
| Declaratis terminis non erit alienum etiam rationem diversitatis indicare. Manifestum igitur est τὸ in loco esse, ex sese relativum quippiam esse sicut esse simile, aequale, &c. quae autem huiusmodi sunt, necessum est alias habere notiones priores in quibus fundentur, seu in quibus comparatio fiat: ut quae in qualitatibus comparamus, similia dicimus vel dissimilia, quae iuxta quantitatem, aequalia vel inaequalia. τὸ au-[p. 214]tem in loco esse quid praehabeat difficile est aestimare. Sed necesse est esse hanc coniunctionem superficierum loci & locati quam exposuimus, in quocumque demum ea consistat. Aliquando quidem in unitate seu continuitate positam esse conspicuum est: an semper vero ita sit, in medium relinquo. | The terms having been declared, it will not be foreign also to indicate the reason [rationem] of the diversity. It is manifest, then, that ‘the being in place’ [τὸ in loco esse] is of itself something relative [relativum], as [are] being similar, equal, etc.; and the things which are of this sort, it is necessary [necessum est] that they have other prior notions [notiones] in which they are founded [fundentur], or in which the comparison is made: as the things which we compare in qualities we call similar or dissimilar, those [which we compare] according to quantity, equal or unequal. But what ‘the [p. 214] being in place’ [τὸ in loco esse] pre-has [praehabeat] is difficult to estimate. But it is necessary that there be this conjunction of the surfaces of the place and of the placed [locati] which we have set out, in whatever at last it may consist. Sometimes indeed it is conspicuous that it is set [positam] in unity or continuity: but whether it be always so, I leave in the middle [in medium relinquo]. |
| Utcumque sit, ex his vides id corpus, in quo vere & realiter fit mutatio coniunctionis superficiei ad superficiem, vere & realiter habere eam affectionem, ex qua per se sequitur esse loco mutatum (etsi contingere possit hoc ipsum non esse loco mutatum) illud vero cuius superficies non mutat realiter coniunctionem suam, hoc non habere. Unde quod priori modo loco mutatur, in potiori sensu loco motum appellatur, quam quod in posteriori. Sed & vides ad mutandum aliquid priori modo necessariam esse veram & Physicam actionem & propriam causam assignari. Ad hoc autem quod posteriori tantummodo sensu loco motum dicitur sufficere, quod spectet modo debito ad aliud, circa quod actio & causa realis versentur. | However it be, from these things you see that that body in which truly and really [realiter] there comes about a change [mutatio] of the conjunction of surface to surface truly and really has that affection [affectionem] from which by itself [per se] it follows that it is changed in place [loco mutatum] (although it could happen that this very [body] is not changed in place), but that [body] whose surface does not really change its conjunction does not have this. Whence that which is changed in place in the former mode [modo] is called moved in place in a more powerful [potiori] sense than that which [is so] in the latter. But you see also that, for changing something in the former mode, it is necessary that a true and Physical action and a proper cause be assigned. But for this, which is said to be moved in place only in the latter sense, [you see] that it suffices that it should look [spectet] in due mode to another, about which the action and the real cause are turned [versentur]. |
| Haec si vides, vel magis quia vides, ad hypothesim nos restringamus. Globus ex tellure & aere, illud est corpus quod loco movetur, huius globi superficies in aere est, cuius suprema superficies superficiei effluviorum alicuius alterius Astri sive contigua, sive [p. 215] continua est. Totus itaque globus locum mutat per hoc quod hae superficies secundum partes sese invicem deserant. Et si qua causa idonea est ad hoc ut superficies sic permutentur, caeteris eodem modo se ad sese habentibus: sufficit haec causa ad totum globum circulo movendum. Et quoniam caetera sic se habere sicut habent, non est motus, sed magis negatio motus & mutationis, causa ad hoc peti non debet neque impetus. Quare manifestum est impetum non esse in iis, quae pure, quoad seipsa, eodem semper modo se habent, etsi penes extrinseca sint mutata. | If you see these things, or rather because you see [them], let us restrict ourselves to the hypothesis [hypothesim]. The globe [made] of the earth [tellure] and air, that is the body which is moved in place; the surface of this globe is in the air, whose highest surface is either contiguous or [p. 215] continuous with the surface of the effluvia [effluviorum] of some other Star [Astri]. The whole globe therefore changes place through this, that these surfaces, according to [their] parts, desert one another in turn. And if any cause is fit for this, that the surfaces be so exchanged [permutentur], the rest holding themselves in the same mode to themselves: this cause suffices for moving the whole globe in a circle. And since [for] the rest to hold themselves thus as they hold is not motion, but rather the negation of motion and of change, a cause ought not to be sought for this, nor an impetus. Wherefore it is manifest that impetus is not in those things which purely, as to themselves, hold themselves always in the same mode, although in respect of [penes] extrinsic things they be changed. |
| Asphal. Ratiocinationem tuam & acutam fuisse, & suo etiam pondere gravem inficiari non possum. Sed in applicatione duo desidero. Collocas enim motum omnem in superficie suprema, cum mihi manifestum sit in ipsa tellure & motum, & totam motus causam haerere. Unde enodandum tibi est posterius, quomodo motus a terra in supremas partes pervadere possit, absque eo quod medias partes pervellat, quod nimis quam obscurum mihi & perplexum apparet. | Asphal. That your reasoning [ratiocinationem] was both acute and also heavy [gravem] with its own weight, I cannot deny. But in the application I miss [desidero] two things. For you place all motion in the highest surface, whereas it is manifest to me that both the motion and the whole cause of the motion cling [haerere] in the earth [tellure] itself. Whence you must unknot [enodandum] the latter [point]: how motion can pervade from the earth [terra] into the highest parts without this, that it pluck [pervellat] the middle parts; which appears to me exceedingly [nimis quam] obscure and perplexed. |
| Ereu. Pro tua urbanitate, Asphali, & suavissimi ingenii luce & subtilitate locutus es, & quod in me est, parem referre gratiam [p. 216] laborabo. Sed primo dicto (mea fortassis culpa) aliquantum lapsus es. Neque enim ex me audivisti tellurem non moveri, vel non in ea potissimam motus aeris causam residere; utrumque enim agnosco. Sed quod dicebam, erat. Etsi solummodo motus in extrema superficie foret, satis proposito foret: cuius non absimiliter exemplum erit ipsa tellus, cuius exteriores dumtaxat partes a fluctibus circumligantibus verberantur, & totum nihilominus corpus movetur ad earum motum. | Ereu. In accordance with your urbanity, Asphalius, and with the light and subtlety of [your] most sweet wit [ingenii], you have spoken; and, as far as is in me, I shall labour to return equal thanks [gratiam]. [p. 216] But in the first saying [dicto] (by my fault perhaps) you have slipped somewhat. For you did not hear from me that the earth [tellurem] is not moved, or that the chiefest cause of the motion of the air does not reside in it; for I acknowledge both. But what I was saying was [this] [erat]: even if the motion were only in the outermost surface, it would be enough for the purpose [proposito]; of which, not unlike, an example will be the earth [tellus] itself, whose exterior parts only are beaten by the waves binding [it] round [circumligantibus], and the whole body nevertheless is moved at their motion. |
| Habet autem tellus in nostra quaestione suum motum ab attritione ad undas, a quibus partim recedit, partim eas secum trahit: & quatenus recedit, eatenus propriam motus conditionem persentiscit, quatenus eas secum defert, communi eiusdem natura contenta est. Vides enim & proprium undarum motum, quo telluris alias & alias semper partes aliis & aliis undis semper alluunt: & vides communem, quo terrae tergo in circuitum gestantur. Neque quicquam latius, vel dilucidius hanc rem tibi eglomerandi necessitas ulla subest. Postquam enim patet partes aquae partibus terrae mutari, nulla superest de motu dubitatio: qui etiamsi sit ab ipsa aqua ortus, tamen fortius ferit tellurem. | But the earth [tellus] in our question has its motion from the attrition [attritione] against the waves, from which it partly recedes, [and] partly draws them with itself: and insofar as it recedes, so far it perceives thoroughly [persentiscit] the proper condition of motion; insofar as it carries them with itself, it is content with the common nature of the same. For you see both the proper motion of the waves, by which always other and other parts of the earth [telluris] wash against [alluunt] always other and other waves; and you see the common [motion], by which they are carried round on the back of the earth [terrae]. Nor does any necessity underlie [subest] of unwinding [eglomerandi] this matter for you anything more broadly or more lucidly. For after it is plain that the parts of the water are exchanged [mutari] for the parts of the earth [terrae], no doubt remains concerning the motion: which, even if it be sprung from the water itself, nevertheless strikes the earth [tellurem] more strongly. |
| Causa est telluris densitas, densiora enim fortius universim moventur, motus autem hic, non solam [p. 217] tellurem, sed omnia ei adhaerentia percellit, usque ad supremam aeris superficiem: sed petis quomodo sine motu mediorum suprema movet? Et ego rursus a te, an non sit aequale suprema sine mediis movere, & suprema plus, media minus movere? Vide autem molas istas quibus ferramenta atteruntur, non conspicaris medium lentius, magis exteriora circumvolvi? & sic in omni motu circulari contingere, ut quamvis ab interiori parte, ut in molis illis a capulo saxi medium tenentis, proficiscatur motus, tamen in extremis potentior sit? | The cause is the density of the earth [telluris]; for denser things are universally [universim] moved more strongly; and this motion strikes not the earth [p. 217] [tellurem] alone, but all things adhering to it, as far as the highest surface of the air. But you ask how, without the motion of the middle things, it moves the highest? And I [ask] in turn of you, whether it be not equal [aequale] to move the highest without the middle, and to move the highest more, the middle less? But look at those grindstones [molas] by which iron tools are ground: do you not behold the middle turned round more slowly, the exterior parts more? and that so it happens in every circular motion, that although the motion set out from the interior part, as in those grindstones from the hilt [capulo] holding the middle of the stone, nevertheless it is more powerful in the extremes? |
| Neque alia reddenda est ratio quam subiecti ipsius conditio, quae habet remotiora simul cum proximioribus permutari: conditio autem elongatorum est ut maiori circulo, id est, longiori linea ferantur, hoc est velocius quam minus dissita. Et eadem ratione ad nostrum institutum utor. Postquam enim conditio nostri mobilis hoc habet, ut nulla possit esse superficierum permutatio, praeterquam vel in summo vel in imo. Fit ut nullus in medio proprie dictus motus esse possit. Nisi quatenus idem plurium sese contingentium aliquo modo vicem & potestatem sortitur. Quare sicut in omnibus rotabilibus cum minore motu in medio, maior in extremitate est, sic hic cum nullo motu medii in superficie insignis existit. | Nor is another reason [ratio] to be rendered than the condition of the subject [subiecti] itself, which has [it] that the more remote things are exchanged [permutari] together with the nearer: and the condition of the things set at a distance [elongatorum] is that they be borne in a greater circle, that is, in a longer line, that is, more swiftly than the things less distant [dissita]. And I use the same reason [ratione] for our purpose [institutum]. For after the condition of our mobile has this, that there can be no exchange [permutatio] of surfaces except either at the top or at the bottom, it comes about that no motion properly so called can be in the middle, except insofar as the same [motion] in some mode is allotted [sortitur] the turn [vicem] and power of many [surfaces] touching one another. Wherefore, just as in all rotatable things, with a lesser motion in the middle, a greater is in the extremity, so here, with no motion of the middle, a notable [one] exists in the surface. |
| [p. 218] Asphal. Non decepisti me, Ereuni, neque enim minus expectabam quam a te decipi. Sed interea in his quae in Galilaeo legi, & quae a te accepi, videor mihi & consonantiam & dissonantiam videre, ut incertus sim quando tuam, quando ipsius disciplinam sequar. Gratificare proinde mihi etiam hoc, ut in quo conveniatis, in quo dissideatis enucleate & articulatim exponas, quo cuinam sententiae me potius addicam, cum luce videam. | [p. 218] Asphal. You have not deceived me, Ereunius; for neither did I expect less than to be deceived [decipi] by you. But meanwhile, in these things which I have read in Galileo and which I have received from you, I seem to myself to see both consonance and dissonance, so that I am uncertain when I should follow your discipline, when his. Do me the favour [gratificare] accordingly of this also, that you set out, cleanly [enucleate] and point by point [articulatim], in what you agree, in what you disagree, so that I may see with light to which opinion [sententiae] I should rather bind [addicam] myself. |
| Ereu. [Margin: Quomodo motus communis non impediat particulares?] Idem uterque persequimur, hoc enim intendimus explicare, quomodo motus communis circa motus particulares nullam vim habeat, hoc est, quomodo cum tellus & aer ingenti velocitate versus Orientem convertantur, hoc nihil impediat eos motus qui circa tellurem, vel in oppositam vel in diversam partem concitantur. Hoc, cum duobus modis contingere possit, videlicet ut motus communis nullam habeat vim in ea quae particulariter moventur, neque proinde quicquam in iis mutet, vel mutare possit; vel e diverso ut habeat ex sese movendi vim; sed a contrario aliquo motu impeditus sit, vel motus particulariter factus aliunde compensatus: hanc posteriorem viam Galilaeus ingressus est, nos priorem. | Ereu. [Margin: How the common motion does not impede the particular [motions]?] We both pursue the same [thing]; for we intend to explicate this: how the common motion has no force [vim] about the particular motions, that is, how, when the earth [tellus] and the air are turned with huge velocity towards the East, this in no way [nihil] impedes those motions which are stirred up about the earth [tellurem] either into the opposite or into a different direction [partem]. This, since it can happen in two modes — namely, that the common motion have no force in the things which are moved particularly, and accordingly neither change anything in them nor be able to change [it]; or, on the contrary [e diverso], that it have of itself the force of moving, but be impeded by some contrary motion, or the motion made particularly be compensated from elsewhere — this latter way Galileo has entered upon, we the former. |
| Ille ergo motum communem etiam respectu particularium vere es-[p. 219]se motum contendit: Ego non esse. Ille impetum in singulis mobilis partibus ponit: Ego non nisi in iis quae compressionem aliquam vel attritionem ab extrinseco sentiunt. Ille impetum esse qualitatem impressam in ipsum corpus priorem motu. Ego non aliud quam motum velocem ipsius corporis. Ille duos per se motus sibi contrarios in eodem corpore. Ego unum tantummodo verum motum, alium per accidens & non verum. Et procedunt hae diversitates omnes ex eo quod ego locum sine corpore ambiente neque verum motum sine vera mutatione huius ambientis intelligam: ipse utrumque admittat. | He therefore contends that the common motion, even in respect of the particular [motions], truly [p. 219] is motion: I, that it is not. He places impetus in the single parts of the mobile: I, only in those which feel some compression or attrition from outside [ab extrinseco]. He [contends] that impetus is a quality impressed into the body itself, prior to the motion. I, [that it is] nothing other than the swift motion of the body itself. He [places] two motions by themselves [per se] contrary to each other in the same body. I, one only true motion, the other by accident [per accidens] and not true. And all these diversities proceed from this, that I understand neither place without a surrounding body, nor true motion without a true change of this surrounding [body]: he admits both. |
| Asphal. Videtur mihi haec disputatio inter vos magis esse Metaphysica & de principiis in quae resolvendi sint effectus, quam ut novitatem aliquam Physicam & sensibus perceptibilem inferat, unde ad nostrum finem non multum conducet. | Asphal. This disputation between you seems to me to be Metaphysical, and about the principles into which effects are to be resolved, rather than such as to bring in some Physical novelty, perceptible to the senses; whence it will not much conduce to our end. |
| Ereu. Non abs re loqueris: vix enim aliud impraesentiarum adiuvat, nisi quod Galilaeo difficile erit hoc explicare, cur tam vehemens motus quem ponit, non sentiatur: in iis enim motibus quos per se contrarios experimur, utrumque motum sensu agnosci comperimus, ut cum navis contra fluminis ictum vel aestus marini vel venti agitur, contrarii enim [p. 220] nixus aperti sunt. Sic itaque deberet & in his motibus contingere. Rursus operosior est explicatio contingentium in illius, quam in nostra via, ubi cum semel responsum est, nihil aliter sese habere, eo ipso omnia argumenta conciderunt. | Ereu. You speak not beside the matter [non abs re]: for hardly anything else helps at present [impraesentiarum], except that for Galileo it will be difficult to explicate this: why so vehement a motion, which he posits, is not felt; for in those motions which we experience [as] contrary by themselves [per se], we find that each motion is recognised by sense, as when a ship is driven against the stroke [ictum] of a river or of the sea-tide [aestus marini] or of the wind; for [then] the contrary [p. 220] strainings [nixus] are open. So therefore it ought to happen also in these motions. Again, the explication of the things that happen [contingentium] is more laborious in his way than in ours, where, once it has been answered that nothing holds itself otherwise, by that very [fact] all the arguments have fallen down. |
| Sed in duobus novitas aliqua apparebit, videlicet in casibus quos fingere licet, etsi in natura nunquam apparituros; qualis est si contingeret tellurem subito conquiescere. Rursus in consequentia doctrinae, hoc est, si eadem doctrina ad exempla particularia adhibeatur, & in caeteris effectis naturalibus eadem ratiocinatio teneatur; tunc fortassis apparebit ipsis sensibus utra potior sit explicandi ratio. Hoc liceat pro nostro explicandi modo dicere, quod Aristotelis & Peripateticorum est, & non incidit in illam ignotarum qualitatum excetram, quae ubi disputationem aliquam apprehendit, omnis tum intelligendi, tum rationem effectuum reddendi, tum ulterius discursu tendendi, via subito devorata est. | But in two [things] some novelty will appear: namely, in the cases which it is allowed to feign, although never about to appear in nature; such as is, if it should happen that the earth [tellurem] suddenly came to rest. Again, in the consequence of the doctrine, that is, if the same doctrine be applied to particular examples, and in the other natural effects the same reasoning [ratiocinatio] be held; then perhaps it will appear to the very senses which [of the two] is the more powerful [potior] manner [ratio] of explicating. This let it be allowed [me] to say on behalf of our mode of explicating: that it is Aristotle's and the Peripatetics', and does not fall into that water-snake [excetram] of unknown qualities, which, when it has seized upon any disputation, every way — both of understanding, and of rendering the reason [rationem] of effects, and of reaching further by discourse [discursu] — is suddenly devoured. |
| Asphal. Si ita res habet, necessarium mihi intellectu hoc videtur quomodo in particulares effectus procedendum sit. | Asphal. If the matter holds so, this seems to me necessary to be understood [intellectu]: how one must proceed into the particular effects. |
| Ereu. Fiet fortasse non incommode, dum Andabatae postulata absolvo, qui iam expectatione aeger proculdubio est, tanto dilatus tempore. | Ereu. It will be done perhaps not inconveniently while I discharge [absolvo] the demands [postulata] of Andabata, who is already, without doubt, sick with expectation, put off [dilatus] for so long a time. |
| [p. 221] Andab. Quod Andabatam spectat agito quidlibet. Is enim quid a te expectare potest postquam conclusisti non moveri illa quae ipse nullus dubitabat quin suo certius esse, mota sciret & etiam videret. A Galilaei te umbra subduxisti, a tuis ipsius dictis duorum motuum bifido flexu elaberis, motum communem usque supra lunam extulisti, & omnia mihi dubia simul interemptorie confecisti. Hoc itaque potius cogita, num in iis argumentis quae tibi cum Galilaeo communiter obiectantur, quicquam sit, in quo diversus ab eo abeas. | [p. 221] Andab. As for what concerns Andabata, do [agito] whatever [you like]. For what can he expect from you, after you have concluded that those things are not moved which he himself in no way [nullus] doubted but that he knew [them] moved, more certainly than his own being [suo … esse], and even saw [them]? You have withdrawn yourself from the shade [umbra] of Galileo; from your own sayings you slip away by the two-forked bending [bifido flexu] of the two motions; you have raised the common motion as far as above the moon; and you have dispatched [confecisti] all my doubts at once, peremptorily [interemptorie]. Consider this therefore rather: whether in those arguments which are objected [obiectantur] to you in common with Galileo there be anything in which you go away differing [diversus] from him. |
| Asphal. Opportune locutus est Andabata, & ego tibi pro indiculo serviam, ne memoria nimium labores. Primum itaque argumentum quod Galilaeo obiicitur est de iis quae ab alto decidunt, & hoc maxime quod ex malo navis demitteretur. | Asphal. Andabata has spoken opportunely, and I shall serve you in place of a little index [indiculo], lest you labour too much with memory. The first argument therefore which is objected to Galileo is about those things which fall down from on high, and this especially: that which should be let down from the mast of a ship. |

## Words and phrases with alternative or special meanings

- ***τὸ loco moveri / τὸ in loco esse*** — rendered “‘the being moved in place’ / ‘the being in place’”. Greek article substantivising a Latin infinitive. Scholastic-humanist device for naming a predicate as such; White asks what relation ‘being in place’ presupposes.
- ***per se / per accidens (per aliud)*** — rendered “by itself / by accident (through another)”. essential vs. incidental; directly vs. through something else. Aristotle, Physics IV.4 & V.1, VI.10: a thing is moved per se when its own surface changes relation to its place; a part inside is moved only per accidens (as a sailor in a ship).
- ***locus = extima superficies*** — rendered “place = the outermost surface”. Aristotelian place as the inner surface of the surrounding body [ambiens]. White reverses the stress: place is the outer surface of the located body as conjoined with the surrounding surface; see glossary (D1-N04).
- ***relativum*** — rendered “relative”. belonging to the category ad aliquid (relation). Relations need prior ‘foundations’ (fundamenta): likeness is founded on quality, equality on quantity; White asks what founds ‘being in place’.
- ***praehabere*** — rendered “pre-have”. to presuppose, have beforehand. Rare; = what prior foundation ‘being in place’ requires.
- ***in medium relinquo*** — rendered “I leave in the middle”. I leave undecided / open to all. Idiom: White declines to say whether contact of surfaces always consists in continuity.
- ***affectio*** — rendered “affection”. property, disposition, state. Scholastic: the real state from which ‘being moved in place’ follows per se.
- ***hypothesis*** — rendered “hypothesis”. the particular case under discussion (vs. general thesis); also astronomical hypothesis. Rhetorical sense (see glossary): the case of the earth–air globe.
- ***effluvia (alterius Astri)*** — rendered “effluvia [of another Star]”. emanations, exhalations surrounding a heavenly body. White's (and Digby's) plenist cosmos: each star or planet is wrapped in its own effluvia, so the air's outer surface touches the effluvia of another body; this is the ‘place’ of the earth-globe.
- ***impetus*** — rendered “impetus”. impressed force (Buridan/Galileo) vs. White's ‘swift motion of the body itself’. White denies that impetus is a quality impressed prior to motion (the Galilean/late-scholastic view), holding it to be nothing but rapid motion where there is compression or attrition.
- ***negatio motus*** — rendered “negation of motion”. privation; absence of change. Parts keeping the same mutual relation are not ‘in motion’ and need no cause — Galilean relativity of shared motion re-argued in Aristotelian place-terms.
- ***penes extrinseca*** — rendered “in respect of extrinsic things”. in relation to outside things; extrinsic denomination. Cf. glossary ‘denominatio extrinseca’: change only as named from outside does not require impetus.
- ***enodare*** — rendered “unknot”. explain, untie. Pun on nodus (see glossary).
- ***pervellere*** — rendered “pluck”. twitch, pull at, disturb. How motion passes from the earth to the top of the air without stirring the intermediate layers.
- ***nimis quam*** — rendered “exceedingly”. ‘too much than’ = very much. Colloquial intensive (Plautine).
- ***persentiscere*** — rendered “perceive thoroughly”. feel, become aware of (inchoative). Applied to the earth ‘feeling’ the proper condition of motion.
- ***alluere (+dat.)*** — rendered “wash against”. to wash, flow against. Subject ambiguous: parts of the earth (as taken) or the waves; the sense is the same — perpetual relative change of contact.
- ***eglomerare*** — rendered “unwind”. to unwind a ball of thread (glomus); to unravel, expound. Rare, poetic (Lucretius-like) metaphor.
- ***molae / capulus*** — rendered “grindstones / hilt”. molae: millstones, grinding-wheels; capulus: handle, hilt, here the axle/crank. Workshop grindstone for iron tools: the rim moves faster than the hub though the drive starts from the centre.
- ***vicem & potestatem sortiri*** — rendered “be allotted the turn and power”. to take the place and function of. The single motion stands in for the many contiguous surfaces of the middle layers.
- ***rotabilia*** — rendered “rotatable things”. wheels, turning bodies. Rare adjective used substantively.
- ***decipi*** — rendered “to be deceived”. also ‘beguiled, charmed’. Asphalius' quip is ambiguous: either he expected nothing less than a clever deception, or ‘beguiling’ in the sense of being charmed; kept literal.
- ***enucleate / articulatim*** — rendered “cleanly / point by point”. literally ‘with the kernel taken out’ / ‘joint by joint’. Classical (Cicero).
- ***addicere se sententiae*** — rendered “bind oneself to an opinion”. to devote, assign oneself. Legal/sacral verb (addictus).
- ***motus communis / particulares*** — rendered “common motion / particular motions”. the shared diurnal motion of earth and air vs. motions of bodies relative to them. Central to the Galilean debate (Dialogo, Second Day): why the earth's rotation does not disturb falling or projected bodies.
- ***qualitas impressa*** — rendered “impressed quality”. impetus as a quality imprinted in the body. Late-scholastic and Galilean impetus theory, rejected by White and Digby.
- ***nixus*** — rendered “strainings”. efforts, pressures, strivings. Felt resistances of contrary motions (ship against current).
- ***excetra*** — rendered “water-snake”. hydra; the Lernaean Hydra (Plautus, Cicero). Classical allusion: occult/unknown qualities as a hydra that devours all explanation — Digbean/Whitean polemic against occult qualities.
- ***discursus*** — rendered “discourse”. reasoning that runs from one thing to another; cf. glossary ‘running-about’. Here the logical sense.
- ***umbra (Galilaei)*** — rendered “shade [umbra]”. shadow, protection, cover; also a ghost. Andabata: Ereunius has left Galileo's protection.
- ***bifidus flexus*** — rendered “two-forked bending”. a forked turning, evasion. Mock-epic: the two motions (true and by accident) as an escape route.
- ***interemptorie*** — rendered “peremptorily”. by killing, destructively (interimo). Rare; legal flavour like ‘peremptorie’.
- ***indiculus*** — rendered “little index”. a short list or reminder. Asphalius will act as a list of the anti-Copernican arguments (falling body from the mast).
- ***ex malo navis*** — rendered “from the mast of a ship”. malus = mast (also ‘evil’, ‘apple-tree’). The classic ship's-mast experiment (Galileo, Dialogo, Second Day; Gassendi, De motu impresso 1642).
