# De Mundo — Dialogue II (De forma mundi, seu de motu magnorum corporum), Knot v, part 1

*Latin title:* Nodus V. Nullam esse resistentiam terrae ad motum  
*English title:* Knot V. That there is no resistance of the earth to motion  
*Pages:* PDF pp. 184–193 = printed pp. 173–182 (from ‘Ereu. Siccine arbitraris?’ on p. 173 to Ereunius' ‘…tam angusto coarctantur alveo.’ on p. 182; part 2 begins with ‘Andab. Ingeniosam excogitasti fugam’).

## Dialogue II, Knot v, part 1 (printed pp. 173–182)

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| [p. 173] Ereu. Siccine arbitraris? Hoc itaque imprimis responde quidnam esse causae putes, cur tellus uni sic depacta sit loco ut Archimede ad eam movendam opus sit? [Margin: Nodus V. Nullam esse resistentiam in terra ad motum] | [p. 173] Ereu. Is it so you think [Siccine arbitraris]? This then first of all answer: what do you think is [of] the cause [quidnam esse causae], why the earth [tellus] is so fixed down [depacta] to one place that there is need of an Archimedes to move it? [Margin: Knot V. That there is no resistance [resistentiam] in the earth [terra] to motion.] |
| Andab. Quis nescit esse immensam quam in tanta mole obtinet gravitatem, cui non sit alia in toto universo par vel similis, ideoque nullus eam movere possit Archimedes? | Andab. Who does not know that immense is the heaviness [gravitatem] which in so great a bulk [mole] it holds [obtinet], to which there is no other in the whole universe equal or similar, and that therefore no Archimedes can move it? |
| Ereu. Egregie, sed omissa quam in cumulum addidisti coronide, hoc expone gravitas haec quae terram firmat, quam partem versum nititur, seu in quam basim eam depangit? | Ereu. Excellently; but, omitting the coronis [coronide] which you added for a heap [in cumulum], expound this: this heaviness which makes the earth [terram] firm, toward what part does it strive [nititur], or into what base [basim] does it fix it down [depangit]? |
| [p. 174] Andab. Ad medium ipsius terrae, atque adeo mundi; vel certe sphaerae gravium & levium, ne iterum quid omittendum loquar. | [p. 174] Andab. To the middle of the earth [terrae] itself, and so [atque adeo] of the world; or certainly of the sphere of heavy and light [things] [gravium & levium], lest again I say something [that is] to be omitted. |
| Ereu. Ergo motui qui neque ipsam terram e centro tollit, neque partem aliquam terrae a centro plus removeat nihil obstabit haec gravitas. | Ereu. Therefore to a motion which neither lifts [tollit] the earth [terram] itself from the centre, nor removes any part of the earth more from the centre, this heaviness will stand in the way in nothing [nihil obstabit]. |
| Andab. Nihil. | Andab. In nothing. |
| Ereu. Certusne tibi es? | Ereu. Are you certain to yourself? |
| Andab. Certus. | Andab. Certain. |
| Ereu. Itaque motui quem diurnum vocant, qui fiat conversione terrae circa suum centrum, nihil obstat gravitas. Siquidem neque ipsam tellurem e centro tollet, neque partem aliquam luxabit, quo a centro magis distet. | Ereu. So then to the motion which they call diurnal, which is made [fiat] by the turning [conversione] of the earth [terrae] about its own centre, heaviness stands in the way in nothing. Since it will neither lift the earth [tellurem] itself from the centre, nor dislocate [luxabit] any part, so that it should be more distant from the centre. |
| Andab. Immo resistet: Videmus enim saxa & metalla per conatum quem centrum versus habent, suis contumaciter haerere sedibus, quantumvis per motum a propinquitate ad centrum non pellantur. | Andab. Nay, it will resist: for we see stones and metals, through the endeavour [conatum] which they have toward the centre, stubbornly [contumaciter] cling to their seats [sedibus], however much through the motion they are not driven from [their] nearness to the centre. |
| Ereu. Sed etiam vides eadem saxa & metalla quo politiora sunt, & ipsa & subiecta ipsis corpora, eo facilius moveri. Adde his figuram, ut non plana sed sphaerica sint, cum suppositae bases sint aequissimae, & percipies adeo imminutam esse vim, qua subiectis copulabantur, ut poene citra omnem vim loco de[p. 175]pellantur. Tantumdem est si in aere pendula consistant, etsi tunc per motum ab ipso centro depellantur, tamen quam faciliter hoc efficiatur vides, intelligis ergo profecto resistentiam eam quam obiecisti, non ex pura gravitate, sed ex adhaerescentia supposito corpori ex gravitate nata, esse profectam. In tellure vero non esse adhaerescentiam ullam qua impediatur cum in aere medio conquiescat. Cum itaque videas exempla tua nihil momenti ad te confirmandum habere, consule rationem. | Ereu. But you see also the same stones and metals, by as much as [quo] they are more polished, both they themselves and the bodies placed under [subiecta] them, by so much [eo] the more easily to be moved. Add to these the figure [figuram], that they be not flat but spherical, when the bases placed under [them] are most level [aequissimae], and you will perceive the force [vim] by which they were coupled [copulabantur] to the things placed under to be so diminished, that almost without [citra] any force they are driven from [their] place. [p. 175] It is just as much [Tantumdem est] if they stand [consistant] hanging [pendula] in the air: although then through the motion they are driven away from the centre itself, yet how easily this is effected you see; you understand therefore surely that that resistance which you objected [obiecisti] has proceeded [profectam] not from pure heaviness, but from an adhesion [adhaerescentia] to the underlying [supposito] body, born from heaviness; but that in the earth [tellure] there is not any adhesion by which it might be impeded, since it rests in the middle air [in aere medio]. Since then you see your examples to have nothing of moment [nihil momenti] for confirming you, consult reason [rationem]. |
| Andab. Si consulam rationem, scio rem expeditam esse. Agedum igitur non gravitas sed ipsa magnitudo motui resistit. | Andab. If I consult reason, I know the matter to be settled [expeditam]. Come on then [Agedum]: not heaviness but the magnitude [magnitudo] itself resists motion. |
| Ereu. Ain vero? Adeone es Aristotelis & commentatorum ipsius oblitus, ut audeas negare affectionem esse corporis esse mobile? Nonne vides te naturam ex natura tollere nisi singulorum corporum motus aliquos esse naturales concedas? Et tandem nihil posse moveri nisi corpus? Unde si corpus quatenus corpus, sit oppositum motui, & non saltem indifferens & capax motus, necessum erit omnes motus esse violentos & innaturales. | Ereu. Do you say so indeed [Ain vero]? Are you so forgetful of Aristotle and his commentators, that you dare to deny that to be mobile is an affection [affectionem] of body? Do you not see that you take away nature out of nature, unless you grant some motions of single bodies to be natural? And finally that nothing can be moved except body? Whence if body, inasmuch as [quatenus] [it is] body, be opposed to motion, and not at least indifferent and capable of motion, it will be necessary [necessum] that all motions be violent and unnatural [innaturales]. |
| Andab. Quid (malum!) vis ut dicam? num ad saltum pulicis trepidare universam naturam? si enim nulla est resistentia, hoc necesse est dici. | Andab. What (curse it! [malum]) do you want me to say? that at the leap of a flea universal nature trembles [trepidare]? for if there is no resistance, this must [necesse est] be said. |
| [p. 176] Ereu. De hoc tu videris. Ego enim subtilitates tam sublimes non considero. Scio quidem Mathematicos & mechanicos hoc pro principio statuere totum ex minimis partibus consistere. Unde si minima pars pro suo modulo nihil agat, neque totum quicquam effecturum. Sed ego Physicum ago: postquam sensum effugiunt, nisi effectus aliquis notior ex iis dependeat, ea insuper habeo, quod & tu ages si me audis: horae enim nostrae melioribus & utilioribus impendi possunt. | [p. 176] Ereu. Of this you will see [De hoc tu videris]. For I do not consider subtleties so sublime. I know indeed that Mathematicians and mechanicians [mechanicos] lay down this for a principle [principio], that the whole consists of smallest parts. Whence, if the smallest part according to its own little measure [modulo] does nothing, neither will the whole effect anything. But I play the Physicist [Physicum ago]: after they escape sense, unless some more known [notior] effect depends on them, I hold them of no account [insuper habeo], which you too will do if you listen to me: for our hours can be spent on better and more useful [things]. |
| Andab. Ergo divina virtute manebit immota tellus & praesidio angelico. | Andab. Therefore by divine power [virtute] the earth [tellus] will remain unmoved, and by an angelic guard [praesidio]. |
| Ereu. Si hoc ex divinis oraculis habes, ego acquiesco. | Ereu. If you have this from the divine oracles, I acquiesce. |
| Asphal. Interea tecum reputa, Andabata, si necessariae sunt hae scientiae ad Sacrarum literarum dignam interpretationem cum de naturae mysteriis loquuntur, quo te scelere obstringas, si illotis (ut aiunt) manibus ad eas accedas, hoc est, sine necessaria harum scientiarum supellectile. | Asphal. Meanwhile reckon with yourself, Andabata, if these sciences [scientiae] are necessary for a worthy interpretation of the Sacred Letters [literarum] when they speak of the mysteries of nature, with what crime you would bind yourself, if you approach them with unwashed hands (as they say), that is, without the necessary furniture [supellectile] of these sciences. |
| Andab. Enecatis me. Sed quoniam ita vultis, esto nulla telluris repugnantia ad conversionem circa suum centrum. At nulla est motus causa agens & sine causa neque effectus ullus existit. | Andab. You are killing me [Enecatis me]. But since you so will, be there [esto] no repugnance [repugnantia] of the earth [telluris] to turning [conversionem] about its own centre. But there is no agent cause of the motion, and without a cause neither does any effect exist. |
| Ereu. Inquiramus itaque, si enim nulla est tibi accedo. Tu modo dic nobis. Quod terram [p. 177] habeat convertere, quidnam circa ipsam immediate effecturum sit? | Ereu. Let us inquire then; for if there is none, I side with you [tibi accedo]. Only do you tell us: what that which should have [habeat] to turn the earth [terram] [p. 177] would be going to effect immediately about it [the earth] itself? |
| Andab. Nunquid ariolum me putas? ut sciam quid illud facturum sit, quod nec est, neque fuit, neque extabit unquam. | Andab. Do you think me a soothsayer [ariolum]? that I should know what that would do, which neither is, nor was, nor will ever be extant [extabit]. |
| Ereu. Nil opus est, sed credam te scire quod omnes pueri quibus consuetum est, turbines flagello versare, quos scio te satis meminisse, vel utraque manu (cum turbo grandior est) vel digitis (si minor est) ex oppositis turbinis partibus applicatis idemque versus motis, turbini ipsi eadem motum imprimere, vides itaque nihil maioris operae requiri quam ut terra ex omnibus suis partibus aliquid comprimens habeat, quod eadem via versetur: tellurem enim, cum nullam resistentiam habeat, sequi hunc motum necesse erit. Et quo melius intelligas. Finge quod reipsa factum audivi, suspensam inter duos altissimos parietes qui axim eius sustineant ingentem rotam, scriniis undique canthi loco munitam, quae longitudinem in debita proportione habeant: In his arenae sacculos repositos qui cum impetu in devexa rota descendant, in rota vero acclivi molliter evehantur. Hanc in aere suspensam fac moveri: non vides continuam hanc deiectionem sacculorum arenae, continui in circumferentiam motus futuram originem? Vides ergo quid rotae, quid telluri facien[p. 178]dum sit, ut circulo versetur. [Margin: Rotae se moventis compositio.] | Ereu. There is no need; but I would believe [credam] that you know what all boys [know] to whom it is customary to spin [versare] tops [turbines] with a whip—which [tops] I know you remember well enough—: that, either with both hands (when the top is bigger) or with the fingers (if it is smaller) applied from opposite parts of the top and moved toward the same [way] [idemque versus], they impress on the top itself a motion the same way [eadem]. You see then that nothing of greater labour [operae] is required than that the earth [terra] should have from all its parts something compressing [comprimens] [it], which should be turned [versetur] the same way: for the earth [tellurem], since it has no resistance, will necessarily [necesse erit] follow this motion. And that you may understand better: imagine [Finge] what I heard was done in very fact [reipsa]: a huge wheel suspended between two very high walls which should sustain its axle, fortified on all sides with cabinets [scriniis] in place of a rim [canthi], which should have length in due proportion; in these, little sacks of sand stowed [repositos], which should descend with impetus on the down-sloping [devexa] [side of the] wheel, but on the up-sloping [acclivi] [side of the] wheel be gently carried up. Make this, suspended in the air, be moved: do you not see this continual throwing-down [deiectionem] of the little sacks of sand to be the future origin of a continual motion in a circumference? You see therefore what must be done to the wheel, what to the earth [telluri], [p. 178] that it be turned in a circle. [Margin: Composition of a self-moving wheel.] |
| Asphal. Potitusne est artifex desiderato eventu? | Asphal. Did the artificer get possession of [Potitusne est] the desired outcome [eventu]? |
| Ereu. Qui narravit, dixit rotam postquam semel ad convenientem velocitatem fuerat perducta, a sese motum concepisse auxisseque continuo, donec axis incanduit, & priusquam ignis extingui posset, totam machinam qua casu, qua ustulatione corrupit. Sed praeter auditum nihil paciscor: ab alio enim accepi semetipsum impletis argento vivo scriniis tantumdem nullo effectu tentavisse. Potuit fortasse proportio iusta, potuit magnitudo, potuit aliud ignotum nobis deesse. Ego nihil decido, sed quod affero ad explicationem & apprehensionem, non ad probationem vel confirmationem allego. Cogita modo, Andabata, rotam esse ipsam tellurem, scrinia, littus maris & fundum, volumina illa aquae de quibus tam prolixe supra (quae diximus ex pacifico in Indicum eiecta, & sic post circumitum reliquum orbem pacifico reddita) seu sacculos arenae, seu fluidum argentum interpretare, & vide in nulla quam concessisti, telluris resistentia quid secuturum sit? Addo quoque duas circumstantias, altera est, quod in rota altera ponderum moventium portio moveat altera motui resistat, non sic accidere in tellure: quae ex omni parte principium motus habeat, nus[p. 179]quam resistentiae. Altera est, quod rotae motus primus author post aliquantum temporis discessit, & rotam sibi permisit, Motor autem telluris qui est ventus ille quem ex Oriente in Occidentem porrectum habuimus, perpes assistit & afflat. Vel si vis ex turbine similitudinem trahere: Turben est terra, manus comprimentes & in eandem partem fluentes sunt aestus marini, puer ventus motum adprimens. [Margin: Telluris motum sequi ad aquae motum.] | Ereu. He who narrated [it] said that the wheel, after it had once been brought to a fitting speed, conceived motion from itself [a sese] and increased [it] continually, until the axle grew white-hot [incanduit], and before the fire could be extinguished, it ruined the whole machine, partly by falling [qua casu], partly by scorching [qua ustulatione]. But beyond hearsay [auditum] I bargain for nothing [nihil paciscor]: for from another I received [word] that he himself, with the cabinets filled with quicksilver [argento vivo], had tried just the same [tantumdem] with no effect. Perhaps just proportion could have been lacking, size could, something else unknown to us could. I decide [decido] nothing, but what I bring forward, I allege for explication and apprehension, not for proof or confirmation. Only think, Andabata, that the wheel is the earth [tellurem] itself; the cabinets, the shore of the sea and the bottom; those volumes of water about which [we spoke] so prolixly above (which we said [were] thrown out of the pacific into the Indian, and so after the going-round [circumitum] the rest of the globe [orbem], restored to the pacific) interpret either as little sacks of sand or as fluid silver; and see, in the no resistance of the earth [telluris] which you conceded, what will follow? I add also two circumstances: the one is that, [whereas] in the wheel one portion of the moving weights moves, the other resists the motion, it does not so happen in the earth [tellure], which should have from every part a beginning [principium] of motion, no[p. 179]where [a beginning] of resistance. The other is that the first author of the wheel's motion after some time went away, and left [permisit] the wheel to itself; but the Mover [Motor] of the earth [telluris], which is that wind which we have had stretched out [porrectum] from the East into the West, perpetually [perpes] stands by [assistit] and blows upon [afflat] [it]. Or if you will draw a likeness from the top: the Top [Turben] is the earth [terra]; the hands compressing and flowing toward the same part are the marine tides [aestus marini]; the boy, the wind pressing on [adprimens] the motion. [Margin (p. 178): That the motion of the earth [telluris] follows upon [sequi ad] the motion of the water.] |
| Andab. Ha, Ha, He! Immo sol est puer, ventus manubrium, undae ipsa scutica, verte te terra. | Andab. Ha, Ha, He! Nay, the sun is the boy, the wind the handle, the waves [undae] the whip [scutica] itself: turn yourself, earth [verte te terra]. |
| Ereu. Rides, Andabata, sed nunquid auditu dignum reponis. Totum quantus est discursum articulatim repete: Vide num desit aliquid ad integram perfectamque fidem. Vide num stare possit motus aquae in orbem sine motu terrae consequente? Habes causam quam notum sit existere, potentem ad hunc effectum, & non deficientem. Ne te in tantum occupatum captivent praeacceptae phantasiae ut ad apertam lucem caliges. | Ereu. You laugh, Andabata, but do you reply [reponis] anything worthy of hearing? Repeat the whole discourse [discursum], as great as it is, joint by joint [articulatim]: see whether anything is lacking for an entire and perfect credit [fidem]. See whether the motion of the water in a circle [in orbem] can stand without the motion of the earth [terrae] following? You have a cause which is known to exist, powerful for this effect, and not failing. Let not preconceived phantasies [praeacceptae phantasiae] hold you captive, occupied to such a degree [in tantum], that you should be dim-sighted [caliges] at open light. |
| Asphal. Ego hac vice, partes Andabatae (permissu ipsius) agam. Videris enim mihi in aliquo defecisse. In turbine enim & in rota contingit qua scutica movetur vel manus, vel defluentia pondera, illac moveri & turbinem & rotam; sed in conversione telluris ac[p. 180, misprinted 181]cidit adversum. Maris enim cursus ab Oriente fertur in Occidentem, at telluris motus in Orientem ab Occidente: quomodo itaque a motu aquarum continget nasci telluris conversionem? [Margin: Cur tellus in Orientem feratur.] | Asphal. I on this turn [hac vice] will play the parts [partes] of Andabata (by his permission). For you seem to me to have failed in something. For in the top and in the wheel it happens that, which way [qua] the whip is moved, or the hand, or the down-flowing weights, that way [illac] both the top and the wheel are moved; but in the turning [conversione] of the earth [telluris] it has [p. 180, misprinted 181] happened contrariwise [adversum]. For the course of the sea is borne from the East into the West, but the motion of the earth [telluris] into the East from the West: how then will it come about [continget] that the turning of the earth [telluris] is born from the motion of the waters? [Margin: Why the earth [tellus] is borne into the East.] |
| Ereu. Caute advigilasti, Asphali, sic enim sese res habet. | Ereu. Warily have you kept watch [Caute advigilasti], Asphalius, for so the matter holds itself [sese res habet]. |
| Andab. Et ego satis sciebam aliquem in herba latere anguem; sed acutiores habet oculos noster Asphalius. Ubi nunc gloriatio tua, Ereuni, ubi insultatio misero Andabatae facta? Ecce quomodo uno spiritu texta tua telis aranearum nihilo fortiora in inane dissiluerunt. | Andab. And I knew well enough that some snake lay hidden in the grass; but our Asphalius has sharper eyes. Where now [is] your boasting [gloriatio], Ereunius, where the insult [insultatio] made to wretched Andabata? Behold how with one breath [spiritu] your weavings [texta], by nothing stronger than the webs of spiders, have burst apart into the void [in inane]. |
| Ereu. Hoc credis, Andabata, Responde igitur, mare tellurem superficie sua summa vel ima verberat? | Ereu. You believe this, Andabata. Answer then: does the sea strike [verberat] the earth [tellurem] with its highest or lowest surface? |
| Andab. Ima: Ea enim proxima est, summa remota. | Andab. With the lowest: for that is nearest, the highest remote. |
| Ereu. Summam vero aquam iam constat ab Oriente in Occidentem ferri. Sed hoc cupio, aequaliterne moventur aquae summa & ima, vel velocius altera? | Ereu. But it is now established that the highest water is borne from the East into the West. But this I desire [to know]: are the waters, highest and lowest, moved equally, or the one [altera] more swiftly? |
| Andab. Clarum est summam velocius ferri debere quia ventus (qui est motus causa) in summo est, & supremam immediatus agitat. | Andab. It is clear that the highest must be borne more swiftly, because the wind (which is the cause of the motion) is at the highest [in summo], and being immediate [immediatus] drives [agitat] the uppermost. |
| Ereu. Praeveniet itaque inferiorem summa aqua, sed numquid etiam supernatare credis, an pertundere subiectam. | Ereu. The highest water then will outrun [Praeveniet] the lower; but do you believe that it also swims over [supernatare], or beats through [pertundere] the [water] lying beneath [subiectam]? |
| Andab. Non dubium quin eam expel[p. 181]lat pro modo suo. | Andab. No doubt but that it expels [expel-][p. 181][-lat] it after its own mode [pro modo suo]. |
| Ereu. In quam igitur partem? | Ereu. Into what part then? |
| Andab. Occidentem versus: illo enim totus aquae conatus vergit. | Andab. Toward the West: for thither the whole endeavour [conatus] of the water tends [vergit]. |
| Ereu. Sed dixeras antevertisse celeritate sua inferiorem aquam, quod autem descendit, eam quae est ante partem (fors ita sit) protrudit; sed eam quae retro est, retrorsum aget. | Ereu. But you had said that [the highest water] had outstripped [antevertisse] by its speed the lower water; but what descends pushes forward [protrudit] that which is in the fore part (be it perchance so [fors ita sit]); but that which is behind, it will drive backward. |
| Andab. In utramque partem ergo aequaliter agetur flumen. | Andab. Into both parts then equally the stream [flumen] will be driven. |
| Ereu. Recte si in utramque partem superius aequaliter feratur; sed si hoc statutum sit, magis & toto flumine superius in Occidentem ferri, & hoc velocius quam feratur pars inferior, necessum erit inferiorem quoque toto flumine retro agi in Orientem. Et ne ludere te videar abstractis rationibus, ipsa re tibi hoc exercitum ostendam. Cum a portu viridi, (quo saepe concedis) Londinum proxime reverteris, percunctare remiges tuos, cum prope Pontem eligere sibi iter conspexeris, maxime si remisso aestu declivior sit fluvius, quae causa sit? & ipsi tibi exponent quomodo ex eo quod totum flumen sub arcubus coactum sit vehementior descendat ab arcubus aquae motus. Ex hac autem vehementia sequatur aquam adversum arcuum interstitia, sursum versus, ad Pontem contra fluminis ictum cogi, ita [p. 182] fieri ut in eodem flumine contrarii sint motus, & via sit facilis & expedita contra flumen. Hoc ipsi me docuerunt. Hoc docet ratio. Hoc oculis & experientia, hoc ratione discere potes si velis. Cogit enim fortior fluctus imbecilliorem sibi cedere, imbecillior cedendo ad humiliora (quae relinquit fortior) defertur: sic utrimque fit motus. | Ereu. Rightly, if above it is borne equally into both parts; but if this be established, that it is borne above more, and with the whole stream, into the West, and this more swiftly than the lower part is borne, it will be necessary [necessum] that the lower also be driven back with the whole stream into the East. And lest I seem to you to play with abstract reasons [abstractis rationibus], I will show you this practised [exercitum] in the thing itself. When from the green harbour [a portu viridi, i.e. Greenwich] (whither you often withdraw [concedis]) you next [proxime] return to London, ask your rowers [remiges], when you see them choose [their] route [iter] near the Bridge, especially if, the tide being slackened [remisso aestu], the river is more down-sloping [declivior], what the cause is? and they themselves will expound to you how, from this, that the whole stream is constrained [coactum] under the arches, the motion of the water descends more vehemently from the arches. And from this vehemence it follows [sequatur] that the water over against [adversum] the interstices of the arches is driven [cogi] upward, toward the Bridge, against the stroke [ictum] of the river, so [p. 182] that it comes about that in the same river there are contrary motions, and the way is easy and unencumbered [expedita] against the stream. This they themselves taught me. This reason teaches. This by eyes and experience, this by reason you can learn, if you will. For the stronger wave [fluctus] compels the weaker to yield to it; the weaker, by yielding, is carried down to the lower [places] (which the stronger leaves): so on both sides [utrimque] motion takes place. |
| Asphal. Non ulterius miror neque interrogo, cur tanto impetu pacificum aequor in Atlanticum sese egerat, postquam inferiorum aquarum cursus in Orientem tendit. [Margin: Cur mare pacificum in Atlanticum defluat.] | Asphal. No further do I wonder or ask why with so great impetus the pacific sea-plain [aequor] had driven [egerat] itself into the Atlantic, since [postquam] the course of the lower waters tends into the East. [Margin: Why the pacific sea flows down [defluat] into the Atlantic.] |
| Ereu. Sed neque alia crediderim ostia quibus Oceani fluxus deprehendi possit, nusquam enim quod novimus, praeterea ipsa maria tam angusto coarctantur alveo. | Ereu. ## Words and phrases with alternative or special meanings

- ***resistentia*** — rendered “resistance”. opposition, reluctance to being moved; scholastic 'resistentia mobilis' The knot's thesis: the earth offers no intrinsic resistance to rotation, against the Aristotelian argument from the earth's bulk and heaviness (cf. Galileo, Dialogo, Day 2).
- ***gravitas / gravia et levia*** — rendered “heaviness / heavy and light [things]”. weight; gravity; the Aristotelian sphere of heavy and light bodies White restricts heaviness to a tendency toward the centre, so it cannot oppose a rotation that moves no part away from the centre.
- ***depactus / depangere*** — rendered “fixed down / fix down”. driven in like a stake; planted Image of the earth staked to its place; echoed in 'in quam basim eam depangit'.
- ***Archimedes*** — rendered “Archimedes”. proverbial 'give me where to stand' Allusion to the lever boast (δός μοι πᾶ στῶ) as the measure of moving the earth.
- ***coronis; in cumulum addere*** — rendered “coronis; add for a heap”. finishing flourish; an extra thrown on top Ereunius waves away Andabata's rhetorical tail ('no Archimedes could move it'). Classical idiom.
- ***nititur*** — rendered “strives”. leans on, presses toward, tends Physical 'nisus' of heavy bodies toward the centre.
- ***atque adeo*** — rendered “and so”. and indeed, or rather Corrective particle: middle of the earth, i.e. of the world.
- ***luxare*** — rendered “dislocate”. put out of joint, displace Medical verb used of displacing a part of the earth from the centre.
- ***conatus*** — rendered “endeavour”. tendency, effort, striving Glossary rendering; here the stones' tendency to the centre and the water's tendency westward.
- ***adhaerescentia*** — rendered “adhesion”. clinging, friction-like attachment White's diagnosis: observed resistance comes from contact with a supporting surface, not from heaviness as such (cf. Galileo's polished spheres on a level plane).
- ***aequissimus*** — rendered “most level”. most even, most equal Of bases placed under spheres: a perfectly level plane.
- ***in aere medio*** — rendered “in the middle air”. in mid-air; in the air as medium The earth 'rests in mid-air' and has no base to adhere to.
- ***momentum (nihil momenti)*** — rendered “nothing of moment”. no weight, no importance Ordinary sense here (importance), cf. physical 'momentum' in D1-N16.
- ***affectio corporis*** — rendered “affection of body”. property, attribute (πάθος) Scholastic: mobility as a proper attribute of body qua body (Aristotle, Physics III, VIII; the commentators).
- ***naturalis / violentus / innaturalis*** — rendered “natural / violent / unnatural”. according to nature / against nature / not natural Aristotelian division of motions; if body as such resisted motion all motion would be violent.
- ***malum!*** — rendered “curse it!”. plague!, damn! Colloquial Plautine/Terentian interjection.
- ***De hoc tu videris*** — rendered “Of this you will see”. that's your affair, see to it yourself Colloquial future perfect of dismissal.
- ***Physicum agere*** — rendered “play the Physicist”. act the natural philosopher White distinguishes the physicist (attending to sensible effects) from mathematicians/mechanicians who argue from smallest parts.
- ***modulus*** — rendered “little measure”. proportionate share, small scale Each smallest part acts 'pro suo modulo'.
- ***insuper habere*** — rendered “hold of no account”. disregard, ignore, pass over Classical idiom (Sallust, Livy); not 'have besides'.
- ***illotis manibus*** — rendered “with unwashed hands”. unprepared, irreverently Proverb (Gellius, Erasmus Adagia I.ix.55) for approaching sacred things unprepared; here Scripture interpretation needs the sciences.
- ***supellex*** — rendered “furniture”. equipment, stock, apparatus Intellectual equipment of the sciences.
- ***Enecatis me*** — rendered “You are killing me”. you weary me to death Comic exasperation (Plautus).
- ***repugnantia*** — rendered “repugnance”. resistance, opposition, incompatibility Andabata concedes the earth has no repugnance to rotation; cf. glossary 'repugnare'.
- ***tibi accedo*** — rendered “I side with you”. I agree with you, I join you Idiom of assent.
- ***ariolus*** — rendered “soothsayer”. diviner Plautine term.
- ***turbo / turben*** — rendered “top”. spinning top; whirlwind Classical (Virgil, Aen. VII.378 on the whipped top); White uses both forms.
- ***idem versus / eadem (via)*** — rendered “toward the same [way] / the same way”. in the same direction / in the same sense of rotation Hands on opposite sides of the top moved 'the same way' presumably means the same rotational sense; kept literal.
- ***scrinium; canthus*** — rendered “cabinet; rim”. box, case; iron tyre of a wheel Compartments on the rim of the self-moving wheel (cf. glossary scrinium).
- ***devexus / acclivis*** — rendered “down-sloping / up-sloping”. descending / ascending side The descending and ascending halves of the wheel.
- ***rota se movens*** — rendered “self-moving wheel”. overbalanced wheel; perpetuum mobile The classic overbalanced-wheel perpetual motion device, offered only as illustration.
- ***argentum vivum*** — rendered “quicksilver”. mercury Fluid 'silver' replacing sand in the wheel's compartments.
- ***paciscor*** — rendered “bargain for”. stipulate, vouch for 'praeter auditum nihil paciscor' = I vouch for nothing beyond hearsay.
- ***decido / allego*** — rendered “decide / allege”. settle; cite, bring forward Legal terms: White claims the example only for explanation, not proof.
- ***explicatio / apprehensio / probatio / confirmatio*** — rendered “explication / apprehension / proof / confirmation”. making plain / grasping / demonstration / corroboration Distinguishes illustrative from probative use of an example.
- ***circumitus*** — rendered “going-round”. circuit, revolution The circuit of ocean water round the globe described in knots ii–iii.
- ***principium motus*** — rendered “beginning of motion”. source, principle of motion Aristotelian definition of nature as principle of motion; here the whole earth's surface is pushed.
- ***Motor; perpes; assistere; afflare*** — rendered “Mover; perpetually; stand by; blow upon”. mover (as of the heavens); perpetual; assist/attend; breathe on The easterly wind (knot ii) as the earth's perpetual mover, echoing the scholastic 'assisting' movers of the spheres.
- ***adprimere*** — rendered “press on”. press upon, apply Of the wind driving the 'top'.
- ***articulatim*** — rendered “joint by joint”. point by point, distinctly Review the argument step by step.
- ***fides*** — rendered “credit”. belief, warrant Glossary rendering.
- ***praeacceptae phantasiae*** — rendered “preconceived phantasies”. prejudices of the imagination Cf. glossary 'phantasia'.
- ***caligare*** — rendered “be dim-sighted”. be in darkness, be blind 'ad apertam lucem caliges': blind in broad daylight.
- ***latet anguis in herba*** — rendered “a snake lies hidden in the grass”. a hidden danger Virgil, Eclogue 3.93.
- ***texta … telis aranearum*** — rendered “weavings … spiders' webs”. fabrics, constructions Proverbial flimsiness of arguments.
- ***supernatare / pertundere*** — rendered “swim over / beat through”. float above / bore through, perforate Whether the faster upper layer slides over or drives into the layer below.
- ***fors ita sit*** — rendered “be it perchance so”. perhaps, as may be Cf. glossary 'fors'.
- ***flumen*** — rendered “stream”. river; current, flow Used both of the ocean current and of the Thames.
- ***portus viridis*** — rendered “green harbour [Greenwich]”. Greenwich (Old English 'green trading place') A playful Latinization; Greenwich, with the royal palace, upstream route to London Bridge.
- ***Pons; arcus; interstitia*** — rendered “the Bridge; arches; interstices”. London Bridge; its arches; the piers between The famous rapids and back-eddies of old London Bridge, used as an observed case of contrary currents.
- ***remisso aestu*** — rendered “the tide being slackened”. at ebb, at slack water When the Thames falls toward the sea.
- ***exercitum*** — rendered “practised”. exercised, put into practice 'ipsa re … exercitum ostendam': shown at work in reality.
- ***aequor pacificum*** — rendered “pacific sea-plain”. the Pacific Ocean 'pacificum' left lower-case as printed; cf. glossary 'aequor'.
- ***ostia*** — rendered “mouths”. straits, outlets Probably the Strait of Magellan as the one narrow channel where the deep eastward current shows.
- ***deprehendi*** — rendered “be detected”. be caught, be discovered Of the Ocean's undercurrent being observed.
- ***Ain vero?*** — rendered “Do you say so indeed?”. Really? Colloquial (ain = aisne).
