# De Mundo — Dialogue III (De causis mundi), Knot xix

*Latin title:* Nodus XIX. Quando finis mundi sit futurus?  
*English title:* Knot XIX. When the end of the world will be?  
*Pages:* PDF pp. 454–457 = printed pp. 443–446 (from Ereunius' 'Quando inquies?' on p. 443 to 'FINIS.' on p. 446).

## Dialogue III, Knot xix (printed pp. 443–446)

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| [Ereu., continuing] Quando inquies? Nescio cum enim ex animorum ratione e corporibus assumptorum hoc pendeat (Horum autem quis sit in regno intelligibili ordo seu Hierarchia, quis eorum numerus sit ille, qui supremi conditoris opera quasi annulo perfecto ad palam reducat, ad Theologiam spectet) Andabatae nostro meditandum relinquo. [Margin: Nodus XIX. Quando finis mundi sit futurus?] | [Ereu., continuing] When, you will say? I do not know; for since this depends on the account [ratio] of the souls [animorum] taken up [assumptorum] out of bodies (but what the order or Hierarchy of these is in the intelligible kingdom, what that number of them is which would bring the works of the supreme founder [conditoris], as with a ring [annulo] made perfect, into the open [ad palam], belongs to [spectet] Theology) I leave [it] to our Andabata to be meditated. [Margin: Knot XIX. When the end of the world will be?] |
| Andab. Hic Theologiam agnosce Magistram, cum enim corporibus resurrectio debeatur, tunc orbis exitium expectandum erit, cum corporibus humanis materia commode distributioni in corpora & corporum loca adaequabitur. | Andab. Here acknowledge Theology [as] Mistress [Magistram]; for since resurrection is owed to bodies, then the destruction [exitium] of the orb will have to be awaited, when, for human bodies, the matter will be made adequate [adaequabitur] conveniently [commode] to the distribution into bodies and into the places of bodies. |
| Ereu. Mirifice congruunt quae ais: sed quoniam ad materiam digressus es, ego quoque ex materia augurium sumo a tuis oraculis non alienum. Orbem nostrum a sole pendere qui non videt, caecus est: Solem non nisi ingentem ignem esse supra exposuimus; ignem vero continuum esse flumen e corpore quo alitur vastato nutritum & sparsum. Necessum itaque est corpus hoc semper minus & siccius evadere, neque tantae ruinae in infinitum sufficere. Cum itaque naturae vires non ultra sufficiendo alimento erunt, necessum est ut cum sole pereat hic etiam noster orbis. Quod non dubito tunc futurum, cum Andabatae prophetia effectum acceperit. Sic enim omnipotentis opera consona & sibi erunt adaequata. Neque est quod reparari speres solem, cum nihil crassi ad ipsum accedere possit. Aer autem sine densiori substantia brevis est vitae, praesertim in summo ardore; & ubi necesse est ex ipsa concoctione exarescere partes & inutiles igni reddi. Ex quo etiam arguit Galenus non posse corpus humanum ad iuventutem reparari, quod ossium siccitas nullo modo possit remedium accipere. Habemus & ex Alchemia omen in quo corpora paulatim in igneos spiritus & salem & caput mortuum intractabilia igni corpora abeunt, sed & circulatione (quae naturae operationi est parallella) densari & minus dissolubilia composita evadere experimur. | Ereu. Wonderfully agree [congruunt] the things which you say: but since you have digressed to matter, I too take from matter an augury [augurium] not foreign to your oracles. That our orb hangs [pendere] from the sun, [he] who does not see [it] is blind: that the Sun is nothing but an enormous fire we have set out above; and that fire is a continuous stream [flumen], nourished and scattered out of the body by which it is fed [alitur], [that body being] laid waste [vastato]. It is necessary [necessum] therefore that this body come out [evadere] always smaller and drier, and not suffice for so great a ruin into infinity. When therefore the forces of nature will no further be for supplying [sufficiendo] nourishment, it is necessary that with the sun this our orb too should perish. Which I do not doubt will then be, when Andabata's prophecy shall have received [its] effect. For thus the works of the omnipotent [one] will be consonant and adequate [adaequata] to themselves. Nor is there [reason] why you should hope that the sun is repaired, since nothing gross [crassi] can approach [accedere] to it. And air without a denser substance is of short life, especially in the highest heat [ardore]; and where it is necessary that from the very concoction [concoctione] the parts dry out and are rendered useless to the fire. From which also Galen argues that the human body cannot be repaired to youth, because the dryness of the bones can in no way receive a remedy. We have also from Alchemy an omen, in which bodies little by little go off [abeunt] into fiery spirits and salt and caput mortuum [caput mortuum], bodies intractable to fire; but also by circulation [circulatione] (which is parallel to the operation of nature) we experience [experimur] compounds to be condensed and to come out [evadere] less dissoluble. |
| Asphal. Me quoque vatem pastores dicunt, liceat itaque etiam mihi vaticinari, si non valeam de longinquis tempore, certe loco distantibus & occultis, videtur mihi quod [Margin: Quid sit mundus.] quoniam conclusum est mundum non esse perpetuum, & esse instrumentum ad determinatum finem factum, & hunc finem aliquid permanens esse, & aeternum; & inter corpora nihil tale existere; neque ex nostris elementis neque ex quantitate compingi posse; ex necessitate fieri, totum hoc universum non esse creatum, nisi propter spiritus corporibus incarceratos, quos (quia unam tantum speciem agnovimus) homines appellamus. Quot itaque nobis apparent lucida corpora, si ex sese lucent, ut sol ignobilissima esse & magis instrumentalia illis quae illuminantur, haec vero sedes esse variarum specierum hominum, atque adeo & universi quantitatem & modum inde accipiendum esse, ut quot sint hominum possibiles diversae species, tot sint illis diversa a magno numine constructa habitacula: & iuxta propagationis singulorum leges, datam molem singulis eorum tentoriis: temporis quoque proportionatos fines, ex iis momentis quae vos contemplati estis. Quandoquidem itaque omnes vaticinari, quod idem est apud antiquos quod furere, coepimus, credo aptissimum nobis omnibus esse somni remedium. | Asphal. Me too the shepherds call a bard [vatem]; let it therefore be permitted to me also to prophesy [vaticinari] — if I am not able [to] about things far off in time, certainly about things distant in place and hidden: it seems to me that [Margin: What the world is.], since it has been concluded that the world is not perpetual, and is an instrument made for a determinate end, and that this end is something permanent, and eternal; and that among bodies nothing such exists; [and that it] can be compacted neither from our elements nor from quantity; it comes about of necessity [ex necessitate fieri] that this whole universe has not been created except for the sake of spirits incarcerated in bodies, whom (because we have recognized only one species) we call men. As many lucid bodies, therefore, as appear to us, if they shine of themselves, as the sun [does], [it seems to me] that [they] are most ignoble and more instrumental to those which are illuminated, but that these [latter] are seats of various species of men; and so that both the quantity and the mode [modum] of the universe are to be taken from thence, so that as many as are the possible diverse species of men, so many are the diverse dwellings [habitacula] constructed for them by the great divinity [numine]: and [that], according to the laws of the propagation of each, a bulk [molem] [is] given to each of their tents [tentoriis]: also proportioned ends of time [are given], out of those moments [momentis] which you have contemplated. Since therefore we have all begun to prophesy [vaticinari], which among the ancients is the same as to rave [furere], I believe the remedy of sleep to be most apt for us all. |
| His dictis ridentibus omnibus & sibi invicem gratulantibus, consurrectum est, & cubitum discessum; Neque postera lux Andabatam ad vada Rumi invenit, cum primulis enim solis afflatibus iam suos salutabat. | These things having been said, all laughing and congratulating one another [sibi invicem], there was a rising together [consurrectum est], and a departing to bed [cubitum discessum]; Nor did the following light [lux] find Andabata at the shallows [vada] of Rumus [Rumi], for with the very first [primulis] breathings [afflatibus] of the sun he was already greeting his own [suos]. |
| FINIS. | THE END. |

## Words and phrases with alternative or special meanings

- ***Quando inquies?*** — rendered “When, you will say?”. inquies = future 'you will say' (anticipated objection). Opens the question of the margin; the Knot begins inside Ereunius' speech.
- ***animi e corporibus assumpti*** — rendered “souls taken up out of bodies”. assumere = take up, receive (into heaven); also 'adopt'. The end of the world depends on the full number of the blessed taken from mankind — the Augustinian idea (De civ. Dei XXII.1; Enchiridion 29) that the elect complete the heavenly city.
- ***regnum intelligibile / Hierarchia*** — rendered “intelligible kingdom / Hierarchy”. the realm of intellectual beings; the angelic orders. Dionysian celestial hierarchy; White defers the number of the elect to Theology.
- ***quasi annulo perfecto*** — rendered “as with a ring made perfect”. annulus = ring, circle; perfectus = completed, closed. Image of the closed circle of creation: the number of the blessed 'completes the ring' of God's works. Possibly also a seal-ring (annulus signatorius).
- ***ad palam reducat*** — rendered “bring into the open”. palam is an adverb ('openly'); used here as a noun after ad. Unusual construction; = manifest, display (the works of God).
- ***Theologiam agnosce Magistram*** — rendered “acknowledge Theology [as] Mistress”. magistra = teacher, mistress, guide. White's standing order of disciplines: philosophy reasons, theology settles revealed matters (cf. D3-N18 margin on Theology).
- ***exitium orbis*** — rendered “destruction of the orb”. orbis = the world, the earth (orbis terrarum), or a celestial orb. Here = this world/earth; kept 'orb' as in earlier units.
- ***materia ... distributioni adaequabitur*** — rendered “the matter will be made adequate to the distribution”. adaequare = make equal, commensurate. Andabata: the world ends when the available matter just suffices to furnish all resurrected human bodies and their places — a quantitative reading of the resurrection.
- ***augurium / oracula / omen / prophetia*** — rendered “augury / oracles / omen / prophecy”. classical divinatory vocabulary used playfully. Sets up the closing play on vates and vaticinari.
- ***Solem non nisi ingentem ignem*** — rendered “the Sun nothing but an enormous fire”.  Refers to White's earlier argument (Dialogue II) that the sun is a fire fed by matter; its depletion is the natural cause of the world's end.
- ***flumen*** — rendered “stream”. river, flux, flowing. Fire as a continual flux of matter (a Heraclitean/Digbean conception of flame).
- ***necessum*** — rendered “necessary”. archaic = necesse. As in D3-N18.
- ***non ultra sufficiendo alimento erunt*** — rendered “will no further be for supplying nourishment”. dative of purpose with gerundive: 'will not suffice to supply food'.
- ***crassi (nihil)*** — rendered “nothing gross”. crassus = thick, dense, gross. Nothing dense can reach the sun to refuel it.
- ***concoctio / exarescere*** — rendered “concoction / dry out”. concoctio = cooking, digestion, ripening. Galenic physiology: heat 'concocts' and dries the parts.
- ***Galenus ... ossium siccitas*** — rendered “Galen ... the dryness of the bones”.  Galen's account of ageing as progressive drying (De marcore; De sanitate tuenda); White uses it as an analogy for the sun.
- ***caput mortuum*** — rendered “caput mortuum”. 'dead head': the inert residue left after distillation or sublimation. Alchemical technical term; kept in Latin.
- ***igneos spiritus & salem*** — rendered “fiery spirits and salt”. spiritus = volatile distillate. Paracelsian triad (spirit/salt/residue) of chemical analysis.
- ***circulatio*** — rendered “circulation”. repeated distillation in a closed vessel (pelican), refluxing. Alchemical operation said to imitate nature (quae naturae operationi est parallella).
- ***experimur*** — rendered “we experience”. find by experience, find by trial. Empirical/chemical observation.
- ***Me quoque vatem pastores dicunt*** — rendered “Me too the shepherds call a bard”. vates = bard, poet, prophet. Virgil, Eclogue 9.33–34 ('me quoque dicunt / vatem pastores; sed non ego credulus illis'), with the word order adjusted; the pun on vates/vaticinari introduces Asphalius' 'prophecy'.
- ***vaticinari ... quod idem est apud antiquos quod furere*** — rendered “prophesy ... the same among the ancients as to rave”. vaticinari = prophesy, also talk wildly; furere = rave, be in frenzy. Classical commonplace of prophetic furor (Plato, Phaedrus 244; Cicero, De div. I.66: vaticinari furor vera solet).
- ***videtur mihi quod ... esse / fieri*** — rendered “it seems to me that ... is / comes about”. quod-clause mixed with accusative-and-infinitive. Non-classical construction; all the following infinitives (fieri, esse, accipiendum esse) depend on it.
- ***spiritus corporibus incarcerati*** — rendered “spirits incarcerated in bodies”.  Platonic image of the body as prison (Phaedo 62b, 82e); here the final cause of the universe.
- ***sedes variarum specierum hominum*** — rendered “seats of various species of men”. homo extended to any embodied rational spirit. Speculation on plurality of inhabited worlds, current after Galileo and Kepler (cf. Wilkins, Discovery of a World in the Moone, 1638); Asphalius offers it as playful 'prophecy'.
- ***habitacula / tentoria*** — rendered “dwellings / tents”.  Tent image for the body/abode: 2 Cor. 5:1–4; 2 Pet. 1:13–14.
- ***moles*** — rendered “bulk”. mass, size. As in glossary.
- ***momenta*** — rendered “moments”. instants of time, or weighty considerations/causes. Ambiguous: either the moments (measures) of time the speakers considered, or the weighty points (momenta) they discussed.
- ***consurrectum est / cubitum discessum*** — rendered “there was a rising together / a departing to bed”. impersonal passives. Formal close of the dialogue frame.
- ***ad vada Rumi*** — rendered “at the shallows of Rumus”. vada = shallows, fords; Rumus unidentified. Returns to the setting of D1-N01 (Asphalius' lodging 'ad Rumi vada'). The glossary also lists Rumus = Rumon (old name of the Tiber); D1-N01 left the place unidentified; rendering kept consistent with D1-N01.
- ***primulis solis afflatibus*** — rendered “with the very first breathings of the sun”. primulus = diminutive of primus; afflatus = breath, blast, inspiration. Poetic dawn; Andabata had already left at daybreak to greet his own [people].

## Translator's notes

- Unit: Dialogue III, Knot xix (the last Knot), complete. PDF pp. 454–457 = printed pp. 443–446, from Ereunius' 'Quando inquies?' (p. 443, beside the margin 'Nodus XIX. Quando finis mundi sit futurus?') to 'FINIS.' on p. 446. Offset +11 holds (running heads 443–446; signatures Kkk ij, Kkk iij).
- End of the book: the dialogue ends on printed p. 446 (PDF 457) with 'FINIS.' PDF pp. 458–475 are an alphabetical 'Index rerum' (subject index with page references), ending with its own 'FINIS.' and the Bayerische Staatsbibliothek stamp. The Index is not one of the units in the plan and has not been translated; no errata or privilege leaf is present in this scan.
- Inner marginal heading: 'Quid sit mundus.' (p. 445) placed in its row beside 'videtur mihi quod'.
- Uncertain or noteworthy readings: (1) p. 443 'Nescio cum enim…' — the print has no stop after Nescio; punctuated in the English as 'I do not know; for since…'. (2) p. 443 'ad palam reducat' — palam as noun, odd; rendered 'into the open'. (3) p. 443 '(… ad Theologiam spectet)' — subjunctive in the parenthesis taken as potential/indirect, rendered 'belongs to'. (4) p. 444 'Necessum' twice, as printed. (5) p. 444 'parallella' (sic, for parallela) kept as printed. (6) p. 445 'videtur mihi quod … fieri … esse' — quod followed by infinitives; translated with 'that' throughout. (7) p. 445 'momentis' ambiguous (time-moments or weighty points). (8) 'Rumi vada' still unidentified (see terms list).
- Verification pass: the Latin was re-read sentence by sentence against the page images (150 dpi; 300 dpi crops of p. 443 margin/parenthesis, p. 444 alchemy sentence, p. 445 opening) and against the English. Corrections made: 2 — (a) p. 445 'iuxta propagationis singulorum leges, datam molem singulis eorum tentoriis' first rendered 'a given bulk [belongs] to each of their tents'; corrected to 'a bulk [is] given to each of their tents', keeping datam as a participle in the accusative-infinitive series (and '[are given]' supplied with proportionatos fines); (b) p. 446 'credo aptissimum nobis omnibus esse somni remedium' first rendered 'the most apt [thing] for us all is the remedy of sleep'; corrected to the Latin order 'the remedy of sleep to be most apt for us all'. No omissions or additions were found; negatives (non ultra, neque … neque, non nisi, nullo modo) and comparatives (minus, siccius, densiori, minus dissolubilia) re-checked. Also checked: 'intractabilia igni corpora' is in apposition to salem & caput mortuum (accusative), not a second subject; 'Quod non dubito tunc futurum' — futurum [esse] with tunc ... cum.
- Conventions as in earlier units: long s, ligatures and accents normalized; u/v normalized; supplied words in [square brackets]; Latin of technical or ambiguous words in brackets after the English; nodus = Knot. This completes the translation of the three dialogues.
