## Words and phrases with alternative or special meanings

- ***Syllogus nodorum … integrantur*** — rendered “Collection of the Knots … are made whole”. syllogus (Gk syllogos) = gathering, collection, not 'syllogism'; integrare = to make whole, complete, constitute. The table presents the nodi as the parts that together compose (integrate) each dialogue — an 'integral parts' notion from scholastic logic.
- ***nodus / nodi*** — rendered “Knot / Knots”. knot, joint, node; difficulty to be untied (cf. 'nodum solvere'). White's own unit of division; each nodus is a knotty question to be untied.
- ***Tabula figurarum*** — rendered “Table of figures”. tabula = plate, board, list. Here a plate of the geometrical diagrams referred to in the text.
- ***plures / plura*** — rendered “more [than one]”. more, several, many. In 'Mundos non esse plures' the sense is that there is not a plurality of worlds — the scholastic/Brunian question 'an sint plures mundi'.
- ***locus (de loci immobilitate)*** — rendered “place”. place as Aristotelian category (Physics IV: the innermost immobile boundary of the containing body). The immobility of place is the Aristotelian definition's crux; relevant to a moving earth.
- ***orbes coelestes*** — rendered “heavenly orbs”. solid celestial spheres carrying the planets. White, with Tycho and Galileo, rejects solid orbs for a fluid heaven (cf. nodus v).
- ***inolescere (inoleverit)*** — rendered “grow in”. to grow into, take root, become ingrained. Implies the opinion is an ingrained habit rather than a demonstrated truth.
- ***demonstratio*** — rendered “demonstration”. proof; strictly, Aristotelian demonstrative syllogism yielding scientia. Aristotle's 'demonstration' of the incorruptibility of the heaven (De caelo I).
- ***analoga*** — rendered “analogous”. proportionate, of like kind. Thesis that celestial bodies are of the same kind as terrestrial ones — against Aristotle's fifth essence.
- ***per se lucentia*** — rendered “things shining of themselves”. self-luminous bodies, i.e. suns/fixed stars vs. bodies lit by another. Scholastic 'per se' = by its own nature, not by another.
- ***tubus astrisequus / in summo esse*** — rendered “star-following tube / to be at the highest”. the telescope ('tube that follows the stars'); 'in summo esse' = to be at its peak/perfection, or to be at the top (located highest). Uncertain sense: probably that the telescope has reached its highest perfection (or limit). Galilean instrument.
- ***Magnes*** — rendered “Magnet”. lodestone. Against William Gilbert's De magnete (1600), which made the earth a great magnet.
- ***individuum*** — rendered “individual”. a single numerically one thing (scholastic individuum). Nodus xiv concerns the numerical identity of things (no natural return of the same individual).
- ***aestus maris*** — rendered “the tide of the sea”. aestus = heat, surge, swell, tide. Galileo had made the tides an effect of the earth's motion (Dialogo, Day 4); the printed title denies this — see notes.
- ***praeventio Aequinoctiorum*** — rendered “anticipation of the Equinoxes”. the precession of the equinoxes (usual Latin praecessio). Technical astronomy; 'praeventio' = coming before.
- ***motus localis*** — rendered “local motion”. motion with respect to place (Aristotle's phora), as opposed to alteration, growth. Aristotelian kinds of change.
- ***insidentia*** — rendered “things sitting upon [it]”. bodies resting on (the earth's surface). The classic objection that a rotating earth would fling off bodies on it.
- ***angulus contingentiae*** — rendered “angle of contact”. the 'horn angle' between a circle and its tangent. Long-running mathematical controversy (Peletier, Clavius, Wallis) on whether it is a quantity smaller than any rectilinear angle (Euclid III.16).
- ***suppositio*** — rendered “supposition”. hypothesis, assumption. Astronomical hypotheses (circular motions) treated as calculation devices.
- ***principium*** — rendered “beginning / principle”. principle (source of being or motion) vs. temporal beginning. Both senses occur: 'de Principio … mundi' (beginning) and 'a principio externo' (principle).
- ***ens a se*** — rendered “being from itself”. aseity: being that has existence of itself, not from another. Scholastic designation of God.
- ***creabilia*** — rendered “things creatable”. possible creatures. Leibnizian-sounding 'best of possible worlds' thesis, here in 1642.
- ***condere*** — rendered “found”. to found, establish, create, build. Used of God's creating the world; kept as 'found' for consistency.
- ***fatum Stoicorum*** — rendered “the fate of the Stoics”. Stoic heimarmenê, the necessary chain of causes. Classical philosophical doctrine opposed to free will.
- ***varia*** — rendered “various”. varied, diverse; changeable, inconstant; possibly misprint for 'vana' (vain). See notes.
- ***liberum arbitrium*** — rendered “free choice”. free will; freedom of decision. Scholastic term (arbitrium = judgement, decision).
- ***repugnare*** — rendered “be repugnant to”. to conflict with, be logically incompatible with. Scholastic 'repugnantia' = logical contradiction.
- ***beatitudo*** — rendered “beatitude”. happiness, blessedness. Theological end of man.
- ***finiendus*** — rendered “to be ended”. to be brought to an end / to be limited. Gerundive of necessity or futurity.

## Translator's notes

- Conventions: as in FM1 — long s → s; æ/œ → ae/oe; abbreviations expanded (nō → non; motū, Mundū, creabiliū, hominū, conditā → motum, Mundum, creabilium, hominum, conditam); consonantal u normalised to v; i/j as printed except 'tertij' → tertii; the page numbers printed in the table are kept and set after a dash; ornaments and the Google Books watermark omitted. Figures are described in brackets in both columns (Latin bracket = the lettering only).
- Page-range correction (following the book): FM2 occupies PDF pp. 7–11, not 8–11. PDF p. 7 carries Figures I–II, p. 8 Figures III–V; the Syllogus nodorum runs PDF pp. 9–11 (Dialogue I list on p. 9, Dialogue I xv–xvi and Dialogue II on p. 10, Dialogue III on p. 11, ending with the catchword 'DIA-'). Dialogue I begins on PDF p. 12 = printed p. 1 (offset +11, confirmed by the running page number '2' on PDF p. 13).
- Title of the table: printed 'SYLLOGUS NODORUM', not 'Syllogismus' as in the task description.
- Uncertain readings: (1) Figure II: the letters at the left end of the upper line and of the base line are blurred, read {?C} and {?F}. (2) Dialogue III xii 'esse varia' is clearly printed, but the sense may be 'vana' (vain), a common misprint (n/ri); translated as printed. (3) Dialogue III ix 'asse' is printed for 'esse' (misprint), normalised. (4) Dialogue II iv: printed 'nō nasci' (= non); this denies Galileo's tide theory, which should be checked against the nodus itself when reached. (5) Italic numerals with long-s-like 5 read as 5: 55, 133(3), 173, 285, 315, 335, 435. (6) Dialogue I i 'Pag. 2', though the marginal 'Nodus I. Introductio' stands on printed p. 1 and 'Occasio sermonis' in the margin of p. 2. (7) Dialogue I xi 'in summo esse' — sense (perfection vs. location) uncertain.
- Verification pass: every title was re-read against the page images (zoomed at 300 dpi) and against the English. 5 corrections: (1) Dialogue III ix page number corrected 333 → 335 (italic 5 misread); (2) Dialogue III xiii 'esse conditam' rendered 'was founded' (perfect passive infinitive) instead of 'is founded'; (3) Dialogue II x — 'ex vi sua' kept as 'out of its own force' rather than 'by'; (4) Figure III — letter read as L (not E) at the lower intersection; (5) Dialogue I xv 'fore' (future infinitive) rendered 'will be', not 'would be'.
