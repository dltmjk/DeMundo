# De Mundo — Dialogue III (De causis mundi), Knot v

*English title:* Knot V. That the being from itself is one only and the cause of the rest

## Dialogue III, Knot v (printed pp. 306–315)

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| [Margin, p. 306: Nodus V. Ens a se unicum esse & caeterorum causam.] | [Margin, p. 306: Knot V. That the being from itself [ens a se] is one only [unicum] and the cause of the rest.] |
| Ereu. Ergo si poneretur existentiae subsistentis seu existentis esse aliqua ratio, nullas posset habere differentias: quandoquidem differentiae rationi distinguendae superveniunt. | Ereu. Therefore if there were posited [poneretur] to be some notion [ratio] of subsistent existence [existentiae subsistentis], or of [a thing] existing, it could have no differences: since [quandoquidem] differences supervene upon a notion [rationi] that is to be distinguished. |
| Asphal. Vide ergo quo nos perduxeris. | Asphal. See then whither you have led us [perduxeris]. |
| Andab. Non video salebras quas exhorrescam. | Andab. I do not see rough places [salebras] at which I should shudder. |
| Asphal. At non vides non posse plura entia a se esse, quin ratio existentiae subsistentis generica sit, & per singulorum differentias distrahatur. Quod cum concluseris non fieri pote, gratiae tibi referendae sunt, quod ens a se unicum posse esse effeceris clarissimum. Et, quod ex hoc patescit, caetera omnia entia, quaecumque sint, ab ipso esse: Sed & ab ipso ex nihilo esse condita, ac proinde conditorem nobis mundi aperuisti. Nam contra haec nulla potest esse contradictio. | Asphal. But you do not see that more [than one] beings cannot be from themselves [a se] without [quin] the notion [ratio] of subsistent existence being generic, and being drawn apart [distrahatur] through the differences of the singulars. Which since you have concluded cannot be done [non fieri pote], thanks must be returned to you, because you have made [effeceris] [it] most clear that the being from itself [ens a se] can be one only [unicum]. And, what is laid open from this, that all the other beings, whatsoever they be, are from it: But also that they were founded [condita] by it out of nothing; and accordingly you have opened to us the founder [conditorem] of the world. For against these things there can be no contradiction. |
| Andab. Perpende quaeso, Asphali, calliditatem & astutiam nostri Ereunii, vult nobis Deum stipitem, caecum & bliteum obtrudere. Erras profecto si dimitti speras, priusquam intelligentem & ex consilio suae voluntatis agentem (prout Philosophum Christianum decet) demonstraveris. | Andab. Weigh, I pray, Asphalius, the cunning and astuteness of our Ereunius: he wishes to thrust upon us a God [who is] a log [stipitem], blind and insipid [bliteum]. You err assuredly if you hope to be dismissed before you have demonstrated [him] understanding [intelligentem] and acting from the counsel of his own will (as befits a Christian Philosopher). |
| Ereu. Dic itaque mihi quid vocas intelligere? | Ereu. Tell me therefore what do you call to understand [intelligere]? |
| Andab. Neque hoc facio, nosti nos homines solos intelligentes existimare: Interea itaque quae hominibus propria sunt, inquirito. Ego enim si quid dixero, statim infers quod vis: & colloquii & quasi itineris fructu nos privas. | Andab. Neither do I do this; you know that we reckon [existimare] men alone [to be] understanding: Meanwhile therefore, inquire [inquirito] what things are proper to men. For I, if I shall have said anything, at once you infer what you wish: and you deprive us of the fruit both of the colloquy and as it were of the journey. |
| [Margin, p. 307: Deum esse intelligentem.] | [Margin, p. 307: That God is understanding [intelligentem].] |
| Ereu. Durum imperium! sed omnia ab Andabata sunt sustinenda. Video itaque alia Animalia, neque orationes & colloquia habere, neque respublicas vel exercitus conficere, neque machinas ad omnem usum instruere, neque scientias docere vel discere: & plura huiusmodi hominibus esse propria cerno: & cum attentius rimor: qua potestate vel methodo haec praestent homines, video ex collatione unius rei cum alia, tertium aliquid educere & sic paulatim in infinitum procedere. Rursus collationem unius cum alio video fieri nequire, nisi consideranti utraque praesto sint. Video itaque plurium notiones vel similitudines vel naturas in homine simul consistere; quod an in reliquis fiat non satis dispicio. | Ereu. A hard command [imperium]! but all things must be borne from Andabata. I see therefore that other Animals neither have speeches [orationes] and colloquies, nor make up [conficere] commonwealths or armies, nor fit out [instruere] machines for every use, nor teach or learn sciences: and more [things] of this kind I perceive [cerno] to be proper to men: and when I search more attentively by what power or method men perform [praestent] these things, I see [them] from the comparing [collatione] of one thing with another to draw out some third thing, and so little by little to proceed into infinity. Again, I see that the comparing of one with another cannot be done [fieri nequire] unless both are at hand [praesto] to the one considering. I see therefore that the notions or likenesses [similitudines] or natures of more [things] stand together [consistere] at once in man; which, whether it happens in the rest, I do not sufficiently discern [dispicio]. |
| Certe plura corpora sese locis excludunt, color unus alteri cedit, calor frigori, humor siccitati: & in iis quae propinquiora nobis sunt, & in ipso corpore nostro, succedere sibi imaginationes & unam cum alia non consistere clarum est; & aliquando cogitatum tam cito oblivioni commendatum quam ab obtutu remotum experimur. | Certainly more [than one] bodies exclude one another from places, one colour yields to another, heat to cold, moisture [humor] to dryness: and in those things which are nearer to us, and in our very body, it is clear that imaginations [imaginationes] succeed one another and one does not stand together [consistere] with another; and sometimes we experience [experimur] a thing thought [cogitatum] committed to oblivion as quickly as [it is] removed from view [obtutu]. |
| Et e diverso si quid plura simul intuetur, quomodo unum eorum se habeat ad aliud eo ipso videtur agnoscere, & quod sequitur, unum alteri accommodare, unde cum tertium sequatur, porro pergere posse perspicuum est. Hoc itaque quod est plura posse simul praesentia habere video vel esse ipsum intelligere vel certe rei intelligenti ut tali connexum. Hoc itaque quaerendum mihi video an plura, ut plura, id est, secundum suas distinctiones & proprietates enti a se adesse possint, nisi quid vobis erratum esse in hoc discursu apparuit. | And on the contrary [e diverso], if anything beholds [intuetur] more [things] at once, by that very [fact] it seems to recognise how one of them holds itself [se habeat] to another, and, what follows, to accommodate one to the other; whence, since a third follows, it is plain that it can go on further. This therefore, which is to be able to have more [things] present at once, I see to be either to understand [intelligere] itself, or certainly [something] connected with an understanding thing as such. This therefore I see must be sought by me: whether more [things], as more, that is, according to their distinctions and properties, can be present [adesse] to the being from itself [enti a se]; unless anything has appeared to you to have been erred [erratum esse] in this discourse [discursu]. |
| Andab. Nihil calumnior, nisi forte quid officiunt quae ab animalibus quasi cum ratione perfici videmus, ut avium nidos & quarumdam exercitus, Apum Remp. Simiorum & Elephantorum meditationem, Vulpium astutiam, & aliorum machinamenta quaedam humanis coniuga. Sed etiam adeo imperfecta haec & aliis quae ab hominibus perficiuntur, remota esse video, ut per seriem causarum vel organorum recte dispositorum perfici posse crediderim, sicut quae per horologia & alias machinas effici miramur. Porro itaque abire permissum est. | Andab. I make no false charge [Nihil calumnior], unless perhaps [those things] stand somewhat [quid] in the way [officiunt] which we see accomplished by animals as if with reason: as the nests of birds and the armies of certain [kinds], the Commonwealth of Bees, the meditation [meditationem] of Apes and of Elephants, the astuteness of Foxes, and certain contrivances [machinamenta] of others yoked [coniuga] to human [ones]. But I also see these things to be so imperfect, and removed from those which are accomplished by men, that I should believe they can be accomplished through a series of causes or of organs rightly disposed, just as [those things] which we wonder at being effected through clocks [horologia] and other machines. Further therefore it is permitted to go off [abire]. |
| Ereu. Tum hoc quoque num necessarium putas? postquam tot corpora quae admirandam hanc fabricam componunt, ab hoc ente aeterno esse statutum est: vel ab ipso omnia vel certe plura immediate producta esse, vel unum aliquod. | Ereu. Then this also, do you think it necessary? since it has been established [statutum est] that so many bodies, which compose this admirable fabric, are from this eternal being: that by it either all, or certainly more [than one], were produced immediately, or some one. |
| Andab. Necessum est. | Andab. It is necessary [Necessum est]. |
| Ereu. At si unicum processit, nulla eiusdem a se immutatio cum esse possit, unicum mansisset. | Ereu. But if a single [one] proceeded, since there can be no change [immutatio] of the same from itself, it would have remained single. |
| Andab. Non ex necessitate, posset enim variis constare particulis, quarum cum non foret eadem indoles, ex ea diversitate nasci potuit alteratio & novorum corporum procreatio. | Andab. Not of necessity; for it could consist of various particles, of which, since there would not be the same disposition [indoles], from that diversity alteration and the procreation of new bodies could have been born. |
| Ereu. Dixisses itaque prius plura corpora immediate fuisse producta, quid enim interest plurane dicas corpora an unius corporis diversas partes? hoc enim tantumdem est ac si dixisses plura corpora coniuncta immediate orta a Deo esse? | Ereu. You would therefore have said before [prius] that more [than one] bodies were produced immediately; for what does it matter whether you say more bodies or diverse parts of one body? for this is just as much as if you had said that more bodies, conjoined, arose immediately from God? |
| Asphal. Si unum & homogeneum fuisset, tamen, cum iam duo entia existerent Deus & corpus, tertium ex iis procedere nihil incommodi est: Non enim idem diversa faceret, sed a diversis procederent diversa. Corpus scilicet a Deo, tertium autem a Deo simul & corpore. Ponatur itaque Deus corpus a se conditum movere, facile fit totam, quae universi est, varietatem inde proficisci. | Asphal. If it had been one and homogeneous, yet, since now two beings existed [existerent], God and body, there is nothing inconvenient [nihil incommodi] in a third proceeding from them: For the same [thing] would not make diverse [things], but diverse [things] would proceed from diverse. Body, namely, from God, but the third from God and body together. Let it be posited therefore that God moves the body founded by himself: it easily comes about that the whole variety which is of the universe sets out [proficisci] thence. |
| Ereu. Neque succurrit tibi quod in hac ipsa dissertatione supra conclusum est, principium tale esse debere ut plura simul complectatur: sive ergo unum corpus immediate ab ente a se progressum existimemus, idque ipsum a Deo motum, sive plura immediate a Deo esse facta, ex utroque sequitur, plura ut plura a Deo processisse, & quod ex hoc conficitur, plura secundum suam proprietatem & distinctionem in Deo esse: quod est eum intelligentem esse, vel id ex quo sequitur Deum esse intelligentem, postquam hoc in eo deprehensum est quod cum intelligentia unice connexum est. Adde quod neque ipsa coporis ratio immediate procedere potest ab aliquo in quo non ipsius partes aequaliter insunt: quod ipsum arguit plura immediate ab ipso fluere. | Ereu. Nor does it occur [succurrit] to you what was concluded above in this very discussion [dissertatione], that the principle must be such that it embraces more [things] at once: whether therefore we think one body to have gone forth immediately from the being from itself, and that very [body] to have been moved by God, or more [bodies] to have been made immediately by God, from either it follows that more [things] as more have proceeded from God, and, what is made out [conficitur] from this, that more [things] according to their property and distinction are in God: which is [to say] him to be understanding, or that from which it follows that God is understanding, since this has been detected [deprehensum est] in him which is connected uniquely [unice] with intelligence. Add that neither can the very notion [ratio] of body [coporis, sic] proceed immediately from something in which its parts are not equally in [insunt]: which very [thing] shows [arguit] that more [things] flow immediately from it. |
| Andab. Gratulor tibi, Asphali, quod tu etiam in casses Ereunii tam feliciter incideris; Neque ultra peto ab Ereunio ut voluntatem nobis in Deo demonstret: nihil enim minus securus sim voluntatem cum intellectu coniunctam esse, quam hanc plurium inexistendi potestatem, ex qua intellectualem esse Deum confecit. | Andab. I congratulate you, Asphalius, that you too have fallen so happily into the nets [casses] of Ereunius. Nor do I ask further from Ereunius that he demonstrate to us will in God: for I should be in nothing less secure [nihil … minus securus sim] that will is conjoined with intellect than [of] this power of in-existing [inexistendi] of more [things], from which he has made out [confecit] God to be intellectual. |
| Asphal. Tu rides, Andabata, neque respicis syrtes inter quas laboramus. Ecce confectum est Deum esse intelligentem & mundum creavisse & non ab aeterno, quid respondebimus Philosophis irridentibus & quaerentibus quid aeternum Deum pepulerit, ut post tot saecula mundum condiderit infinito prius tempore desidiosus. | Asphal. You laugh, Andabata, and do not look back at the Syrtes [syrtes] among which we labour. Behold, it has been made out [confectum est] that God is understanding, and created the world, and not from eternity: what shall we answer to the Philosophers deriding and asking what drove [pepulerit] the eternal God, that after so many ages he founded the world, having been idle [desidiosus] through infinite time before? |
| Andab. Facile perturbaris, quanta enim perspicuitate hoc in liberum voluntatis ipsius motum refertur? Cur per tot saecula desedit? quia voluit. Cur in eo instanti coepit? quia voluit. Cur hunc orbem creavit cum alium posset? Itidem quia voluit. Vide ingens negotium quod te sollicitum habet. | Andab. You are easily disturbed; for with how great perspicuity is this referred to the free motion of his will? Why through so many ages did he sit idle [desedit]? because he willed. Why at that instant did he begin? because he willed. Why did he create this world [orbem] when he could [have created] another? Likewise because he willed. See the huge business which holds you anxious. |
| Asphal. Siccine credis? Neque fluxum temporis imaginari sublatum meministi, ut non sint puncta & saecula inter quae eligat. | Asphal. Do you believe so? Nor have you remembered that the flux of imaginary time has been taken away [sublatum], so that there are not points and ages among which he might choose. |
| Ereu. Et si qua forent, indifferentia certe forent; nec ullum eorum aliis potius foret, ut in eo motus inciperet: quare electionis nulla copia aeterno numini foret. | Ereu. And if there were any, certainly they would be indifferent; nor would any one of them be [so] rather than the others, that the motion should begin in it: wherefore there would be no store [copia] of election for the eternal divinity [numini]. |
| [Margin, p. 312: Voluntatem rectam nihil sine ratione velle.] | [Margin, p. 312: That right will wills nothing without reason [ratione].] |
| Andab. Immo tunc electionis optima seges, & maxime propria voluntatis materia, cum perfecta est aequalitas, tum enim maxime libertatem suam exercet. | Andab. Nay rather [Immo], then [is] the best crop [seges] of election, and the most proper matter of will, when there is perfect equality; for then most of all it exercises its liberty. |
| Ereu. Voluntatem itaque ponis esse motum vel principium motus cum ratione vel non? | Ereu. Do you posit therefore that will is a motion or principle of motion with reason [ratione], or not? |
| Andab. Cum ratione extra omnem ambiguitatem, maxime vero in Deo aliquid aliter quam cum ratione ponere blasphemiae crimine arcesseretur. | Andab. With reason, beyond all ambiguity; but most of all in God, to posit anything otherwise than with reason would be arraigned [arcesseretur] on the charge of blasphemy. |
| Ereu. Ergo, quod hoc illi praeeligat Deus, ratione facit. | Ereu. Therefore, that God prefers [praeeligat] this to that, he does by reason. |
| Andab. Plane. | Andab. Plainly. |
| Ereu. Sed si ratio aliqua est cur hoc illi praeeligat, non prorsus erant indifferentia. | Ereu. But if there is some reason why he prefers this to that, they were not altogether indifferent. |
| Andab. Immo, quia erant absolute indifferentia, propterea ratio suasit unum ipso voluntatis motu praeferendum. Voluntas vero hoc, in quo incepit universum, caeteris praetulit. | Andab. Nay rather, because they were absolutely indifferent, for that reason reason advised [suasit] that one was to be preferred by the very motion of the will. But the will preferred this [one], in which the universe began, to the rest. |
| Ereu. Dato quod ratio suasit alterum alteri praeferendum (quod tamen ipsum contra rationem est, cum ratio aequalia aequalibus debeat) hoc tamen permisso, num & ratio hoc unum reliquis praeferendum suasit? si enim suasit, necessum est rationem aliquam fuisse praelationis, & non omnino indifferentia fuisse, si non suasit, tum haec electio irrationalis fuit, & sine ratione seu causa. | Ereu. Granted that reason advised one to be preferred to the other (which yet itself is against reason, since reason owes equal [things] to equals), this nevertheless being permitted, did reason also advise this one to be preferred to the rest? for if it advised, it is necessary that there was some reason of preference [praelationis], and that they were not altogether indifferent; if it did not advise, then this election was irrational, and without reason or cause. |
| Andab. Haec est voluntatis indoles. | Andab. This is the disposition [indoles] of will. |
| Ereu. Quapropter & hominum naturam dicito esse irrationalem. Naturam enim ex actione habemus. Actio autem ex voluntate & electione sequitur: si igitur voluntatis & electionis indoles est absque ratione agere, etiam actiones necessum est irrationales esse, & hominis denique naturam. Ne credas itaque voluntatem irrationalem sua indole esse, sed eos qui hoc asserunt a ratione deficere, & certum habeto nihil rationi indifferens esse: & vel hoc satis praeiudicii esse contra tempus vel spatium imaginarium, quod omnes ipsius partes indifferentes forent, cum natura certis omnia gradibus & proprietatibus distincta producat. | Ereu. Wherefore say [dicito] also that the nature of men is irrational. For we have nature from action. But action follows from will and election: if therefore the disposition of will and election is to act without reason, it is necessary also that the actions are irrational, and finally the nature of man. Do not therefore believe that will is irrational by its disposition, but that those who assert this fall short [deficere] of reason; and hold it certain that nothing is indifferent to reason: and that even this is enough of prejudgement [praeiudicii] against imaginary time or space, that all its parts would be indifferent, since nature produces all things distinct by certain grades and properties. |
| Asphal. Sed ad ipsam quaestionem quid affers, Ereuni? Cur Deus post semetipsum & non primo secum mundum procreaverit. | Asphal. But to the question itself what do you bring, Ereunius? Why God procreated the world after himself, and not first [primo] with himself. |
| [Margin, p. 314: Dei praeexistentiam esse in necessitate existendi sitam.] | [Margin, p. 314: That the pre-existence of God is situated in the necessity of existing.] |
| Ereu. Haec est illa ipsa quaestio quam toties deprecor; cum enim tres fere abstractiones Metaphysicam difficilem reddant: Ea quae est a loco, quae est a partibus continuis, & quae est a successione seu tempore, si me arbitrum eligas, hanc ultimam in difficultate contemplationi afferenda longe caeteris anteponam. Nunc autem in familiari colloquio sumus: ad haec vero cogitanda, secessus & tenebrae & contractio animae in semetipsam necessaria sunt. Unde colligis quam iniquum sit inter Physicas contemplationes talia miscere. | Ereu. This is that very question which so often I beg off [deprecor]; for since about [fere] three abstractions make Metaphysics difficult: That which is from place, that which is from continuous parts, and that which is from succession or time; if you choose me [as] arbiter, I would put this last far before the rest in the difficulty to be brought [afferenda] to contemplation. But now we are in a familiar colloquy: but for thinking these things, withdrawal [secessus] and darkness and contraction of the soul into itself are necessary. Whence you gather how unfair it is to mix such things among Physical contemplations. |
| Vide quomodo pictura naturam imitatur, in ea quam prospectivam dicimus arte, quomodo quadrata Rhombis, circulos ovatis, distantia elevatis, proxima depressis exprimat. Et scito talem vel magis defectilem esse nostram apprehensionum & discursuum legem, ad insensilem naturae partem; maxime ad aeternam naturam, maximoque conatu & attentissimo animi nisu laborandum, ne corporum umbrae nobis offuscent contemplationem. | See how painting imitates nature, in that art which we call perspective [prospectivam]: how it expresses squares by Rhombi, circles by ovals [ovatis], distant [things] by raised [ones], nearest [things] by depressed [ones]. And know that such, or more deficient [defectilem], is the law of our apprehensions and discourses towards the insensible part of nature; most of all towards the eternal nature; and with greatest endeavour and most attentive striving [nisu] of the mind one must labour, lest the shadows of bodies darken [offuscent] for us the contemplation. |
| Satis esto nobis in Deo omnimodam non existendi impotentiam & existendi necessitatem inesse pernovisse. Creaturarum si quae immortales sunt, proximam illi sed magis defectivam esse existentiae legem; Corporum absolute debitam esse interitui naturam, motus tandem quasi ex ortu & interitu compositam indolem. Horum quatuor graduum supparia ingenia inter sese conferre velle, vel summa infimorum legibus coarctare, hoc est, nostris apprehensionibus quae ex infima desumuntur, vanum & ἀφιλόσοφον ἔστω. | Let it be enough for us to have thoroughly known [pernovisse] that in God there is an every-way [omnimodam] impotence of not existing and a necessity of existing; [that] of creatures, if any are immortal, the law of existence is next to that [illi], but more defective; [that] the nature of bodies is absolutely owed [debitam] to perishing [interitui]; [that] the disposition [indolem] of motion, finally, is as if composed of rising [ortu] and perishing. To wish to compare among themselves the nearly-equal [supparia] natures [ingenia] of these four grades, or to constrain the highest [things] by the laws of the lowest, that is, by our apprehensions which are taken from the lowest, let it be vain and unphilosophical [ἀφιλόσοφον ἔστω]. |
| Sciamus igitur non inanium saeculorum effusa quantitate, sed existendi necessitate Deum mundo praeextitisse; quibus autem apprehensionibus, & qua loquendi figura linguis nostris hoc accommodandum sit, eo usque dimittamus, donec per longam meditationis assuetudinem & contemplationis fixionem, oculum in puri luminis caliginem intendere eruditi simus. | Let us know therefore that God pre-existed the world not by an effused [effusa] quantity of empty ages, but by necessity of existing; but by what apprehensions, and by what figure of speaking, this is to be accommodated to our tongues, let us dismiss [it] until then [eo usque], until through long habituation [assuetudinem] of meditation and fixing [fixionem] of contemplation we shall have been trained [eruditi simus] to direct [intendere] the eye into the darkness [caliginem] of pure light. |
| Asphal. Quoniam ita vis, huic decedamus Theoriae, sed ea conditione ut quam iniecit Andabata quaestionem agitemus. Cur scilicet hunc mundum tot ab eo creabilibus praetulerit. | Asphal. Since you so wish, let us withdraw from [decedamus] this Theory, but on that condition, that we discuss [agitemus] the question which Andabata threw in: Why, namely, he preferred this world to so many [worlds] creatable [creabilibus] by him. |

## Words and phrases with alternative or special meanings

- ***ratio (existentiae subsistentis)*** — rendered “notion [ratio]”. reason, account, formal character, definition; here the common concept or formality under which several things fall. Scholastic ‘ratio’ as formal concept: if several self-existents shared one ratio of subsistent existence, it would be generic and need differences, which existence itself cannot admit.
- ***differentiae … superveniunt*** — rendered “differences supervene”. the specific differences ‘come upon’ a genus to divide it (Porphyrian tree). Logical vocabulary: a genus is contracted by differences extrinsic to it; White argues pure existence admits no such contraction, so the ens a se is unique (cf. Aquinas, SCG I.42).
- ***ens a se / unicum*** — rendered “the being from itself / one only”. aseity; ‘unicum’ = sole, unique, not merely ‘one’. Title of the Knot: the uniqueness of the self-existent being (aseitas).
- ***non fieri pote*** — rendered “cannot be done”. archaic ‘pote’ = potest (also potis, pote); classical in Plautus, Catullus, Lucretius. White’s deliberate archaism; kept as printed.
- ***stipes, caecus, bliteus*** — rendered “a log, blind, insipid”. stipes = log/stump, proverbial for a blockhead; bliteus = insipid, stupid (from blitum, a tasteless herb: Plautus). Andabata’s jibe: a merely necessary first being without intellect would be a God like a log (cf. Lucretian or Spinozist objections to a non-intelligent first cause).
- ***intelligere / intelligens*** — rendered “to understand / understanding”. to know intellectually; intelligens as ‘intelligent’. Kept as ‘understanding’ (participle) to show White’s argument that God is ‘intelligens’.
- ***collatio*** — rendered “comparing [collatione]”. bringing together, comparison, collation. White’s definition of human reasoning as comparing two things to draw out a third (syllogistic middle term); grounds his account of intellect as holding many things present at once.
- ***plura ut plura*** — rendered “more [things] as more”. many things precisely as many (as distinct). Thomist and Digbean thesis: intellect is the capacity to have many forms present without their excluding one another (contrast bodies, which ‘exclude one another from places’).
- ***consistere*** — rendered “stand together”. coexist, subsist, stand firm. Used of notions coexisting in the mind vs. bodies and contraries that cannot coexist.
- ***e diverso*** — rendered “on the contrary”. conversely (late Latin). Scholastic idiom.
- ***calumnior*** — rendered “I make a false charge”. raise a quibble, cavil, accuse falsely. Disputation idiom: Andabata says he has no cavil to raise.
- ***coniuga (humanis)*** — rendered “yoked [coniuga] to human [ones]”. rare adjective coniugus ‘joined, conjoined’; perhaps a misprint for ‘coniuncta’ or ‘congenera’. Animal ‘reasoning’ (bees’ commonwealth, nests, armies of ants) as a standard objection; White, like Digby (Two Treatises, 1644) and Descartes, explains it by mechanism ‘like clocks’.
- ***horologia & aliae machinae*** — rendered “clocks and other machines”. horologium = any timepiece. The mechanist answer to animal intelligence (Descartes, Discours V; Digby).
- ***immutatio a se*** — rendered “change from itself”. self-alteration. A single homogeneous body cannot alter itself, so diversity needs diverse immediate effects or an extrinsic mover.
- ***indoles*** — rendered “disposition [indoles]”. native character, nature, temper. Used of particles, of will, and of motion; kept constant.
- ***insunt / inexistendi potestas*** — rendered “are in / power of in-existing”. inesse, inexistere = to be present in. The ‘power of many things in-existing’ is White’s mark of intellect.
- ***casses*** — rendered “nets”. hunting nets, snares. Andabata’s jest that Asphalius has been caught as well.
- ***syrtes*** — rendered “Syrtes”. the shoals off Libya; proverbial for dangers. Classical allusion (Virgil, Aen. I.111; Horace).
- ***quid aeternum Deum pepulerit … desidiosus*** — rendered “what drove the eternal God … idle”. the ancient objection ‘why did God create then and not before?’ Epicurean objection (Cicero, De nat. deor. I.21–22; Lucretius V.168–73), answered by Augustine (Conf. XI.10–13; Civ. Dei XI.4–5).
- ***quia voluit*** — rendered “because he willed”. voluntarist answer: the divine will as ultimate reason. Andabata gives the voluntarist reply (Scotist/nominalist; cf. Augustine’s ‘causa voluntatis Dei’); White/Ereunius rejects any choice without reason (Leibniz’s later principle of sufficient reason).
- ***indifferentia / electio*** — rendered “indifferent / election”. liberty of indifference; election = choice. The debate over libertas indifferentiae: Andabata says equality is the will’s best field; Ereunius that ‘nothing is indifferent to reason’.
- ***seges (electionis)*** — rendered “crop”. field of corn, harvest; ‘materia’ for an activity. Metaphor: the richest ground for choice.
- ***praelatio*** — rendered “preference [praelationis]”. a putting-before. Rare late-Latin noun from praefero.
- ***praeiudicium*** — rendered “prejudgement [praeiudicii]”. precedent, prejudice, presumptive argument. Legal: a presumption counting against imaginary time and space.
- ***tempus / spatium imaginarium*** — rendered “imaginary time / space”. the scholastic ‘imaginary’ extension before or outside the world. White denies real imaginary time (see D3-N03); a uniform imaginary time would offer no reason for a beginning at one point rather than another.
- ***tres abstractiones*** — rendered “three abstractions”. from place, from continuous parts (quantity), from succession (time). Metaphysics requires abstracting from place, quantity and time; the last is hardest (cf. Boethius’ eternity ‘tota simul’).
- ***deprecor*** — rendered “beg off”. pray to avert, decline. He declines to answer the question about God’s ‘before’ the world.
- ***prospectiva (ars)*** — rendered “perspective”. optics/linear perspective. Analogy: our concepts of eternity represent it as painting represents squares by rhombi; images of time distort the eternal.
- ***defectilis / defectivus*** — rendered “deficient / defective”. liable to fail. Rare words; kept distinct.
- ***supparia ingenia*** — rendered “nearly-equal natures”. suppar = nearly equal, almost contemporary; perhaps misprint for ‘disparia’ (unequal). As printed the sense would be ‘to compare as if near-equal’; the context (four very different grades) favours ‘disparia’.
- ***ἀφιλόσοφον ἔστω*** — rendered “let it be unphilosophical”. Greek: unphilosophical, unworthy of a philosopher. Greek tag closing the argument.
- ***existendi necessitate praeextitisse*** — rendered “pre-existed by necessity of existing”. priority of nature/necessity, not of time. God’s ‘before’ the world is priority of necessity, not of empty ages (cf. Augustine, Conf. XI.13; Aquinas ST I.46.1 ad 8).
- ***puri luminis caligo*** — rendered “the darkness of pure light”. dazzling darkness. Mystical topos (Pseudo-Dionysius, ‘divine darkness’; Aristotle Metaph. II.1: like bats’ eyes to daylight).
- ***creabilia*** — rendered “creatable [things]”. possible creatures. Leads into Knot VI on the best of creatable worlds.
