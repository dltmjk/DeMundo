# De Mundo — Dialogue III (De causis mundi), Knot ii, part 1 of 2

*English title:* Knot II. That the motion of the universe is from an external principle

## Dialogue III, Knot ii, part 1 (printed pp. 269–278)

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| [p. 269] Ereu. Nosti me interrogationibus procedere solitum. [Margin: Nodus II. Motum universi a principio externo esse.] Age itaque si ab aeterno vel certe a media hora vel quantumvis exiguo temporis spatio omnia corpora quievissent, putasne inceptum iri postea motum ab ipsorum quoquam? | [p. 269] Ereu. You know me [to be] accustomed to proceed by questions. [Margin: Knot II. That the motion of the universe is from an external principle [principio].] Come then: if from eternity, or at least [certe] from half an hour [ago], or for however small a space of time, all bodies had rested [quievissent], do you think that motion would afterwards be begun [inceptum iri] by any one [quoquam] of them? |
| Andab. Quid ni? gravitas enim vel alia qualitas motiva cessante illo tempore suum effectum sortiretur. | Andab. Why not? For gravity [gravitas] or another motive quality [qualitas motiva], that time ceasing, would obtain [sortiretur] its effect. |
| Ereu. Et quare toto illo tempore nihil fecit, & postmodum incipit operari? | Ereu. And why during that whole time did it do nothing, and afterwards begins to operate? |
| Andab. Aliqua causa ponenda est. Vel itaque deerat concursus divinus quem novisti esse necessarium, vel quoniam effectus corporei non fiunt nisi in tempore, dicendum erit hoc tempus exiguum necessarium fuisse ut actio exiret, sive ut praepararet sese ad operandum caussa. | Andab. Some cause is to be posited. Either, then, the divine concurrence [concursus divinus] was lacking, which you know to be necessary; or, since bodily effects do not come about except in time, it will have to be said that this small time was necessary in order that the action might go forth [exiret], or that the cause [caussa] might prepare itself for operating. |
| Ereu. Nimium Theologus es: ego quid natura fieret peto, tu Deum supra naturam sese exercentem introducis. Misso itaque facto naturae conditore, quomodo ponis tempus operationi necessarium? vel enim ponis aliquid interea circa corpora fieri vel nihil: si hoc posterius, quomodo vocas illud necessarium quod ad nihil necessarium est? prius autem [p. 270] contra suppositionem est, qua quievisse corpora, hoc est in nullo mutata esse ponebatur. | Ereu. Too much a Theologian you are: I ask what would come about by nature; you bring in God exercising himself above nature. The founder [conditore] of nature therefore being dismissed [misso … facto], how do you posit a time necessary for the operation? For either you posit that something meanwhile comes about concerning the bodies, or nothing: if this latter, how do you call that necessary which is necessary for nothing? but the former is [p. 270] against the supposition [suppositionem], by which it was being posited that the bodies had rested, that is, had been changed in nothing. |
| Andab. Sic ergo dictum oportuit: ego enim non nisi locali motu immutata suscipiebam. Si itaque sic intelligis, fateor si omnia quiescere ponatur, ab iis motum exordiri non posse. | Andab. So then it ought to have been said: for I was taking [suscipiebam] [them as] unchanged only in local motion. If therefore you understand [it] so, I confess, if all things be posited to rest, that motion cannot begin [exordiri] from them. |
| Ereu. Et unde hoc accipis? | Ereu. And whence do you take this? |
| Andab. Quia effectum aliquem de novo esse sine causa non est possibile. Causa autem motus seu primae mutationis nulla potest esse. Si enim causae existentes talis motus causa esse possent, iam tum a principio quietis fuissent, quod cum non fuerint, clarum est neque esse posse sine aliqua mutatione, quae ab ipsis procedere non potest, cum omnes quiescant: quare necessum est aliquam causam extrinsecam invenire, unde prima sit mutationis origo. | Andab. Because it is not possible that some effect be anew [de novo] without a cause. But a cause of the motion, or of the first change [mutationis], there can be none. For if the existing causes could be the cause of such a motion, they would have been [so] already then, from the beginning [principio] of the rest; and since they were not, it is clear that neither can they be [so] without some change, which cannot proceed from them themselves, since all are at rest: wherefore it is necessary [necessum est] to find some extrinsic cause, whence may be the first origin of the change. |
| Idem enim manens idem semper facit idem, si itaque omnes hae causae semel quietem faciunt, semper quietem facient. | For the same, remaining the same, always makes the same; if therefore all these causes once make rest, always will they make rest. |
| Ereu. Firmiter & constanter haec asseveras? | Ereu. Firmly and constantly do you assert these things? |
| Andab. Ut nihil certius existimem, non totum sua parte maius. | Andab. So [firmly] that I should reckon nothing more certain, not [even that] the whole [is] greater than its part. |
| Ereu. Ulterius itaque interrogo. Si ponatur corpus aliquod in motu esse, & ponantur [p. 271] omnes causae motus in uno signo temporis desinere moveri, putasne corpus adhuc moveri? | Ereu. Further, then, I ask. If some body be posited to be in motion, and all the causes of the motion be posited [p. 271] in one sign [signo] of time to cease to be moved, do you think that the body is still moved? |
| Andab. Impossibile puto, postquam ipsa interrogatione omnes causas motus exclusisti; non enim effectus absque causa in rerum natura collocari potest. | Andab. I think [it] impossible, since [postquam] by the question itself you have shut out all the causes of the motion; for an effect cannot be placed in the nature of things without a cause. |
| Ereu. Non cogitas fortassis, Andabata, non de effectu novo, sed de continuatione eiusdem esse sermonem: posui enim motum prius fuisse, & peto dumtaxat, an cessante causa, possit perpetuari, sicut cessante patre operari, non moritur filius, & cessante aedificatore, manet aedificium? | Ereu. You are not considering, perhaps, Andabata, that the discourse [sermonem] is not about a new effect, but about the continuation of the same: for I posited that the motion had been before, and I ask only whether, the cause ceasing, it can be perpetuated, just as, the father ceasing to operate, the son does not die, and, the builder ceasing, the building remains? |
| Andab. Haec quidem exempla in opere perfecto recte exhibentur, sed in his ipsis cernere est veritatem quam asserui: aedificator enim ante aedificationem perfectam si cesset, non procedet aedificatio. | Andab. These examples indeed are rightly displayed in a finished [perfecto] work, but in these very ones one may discern [cernere est] the truth which I asserted: for the builder, if he should cease before the building [aedificationem] [is] finished, the building [aedificatio] will not proceed. |
| Hanc ergo video inter motum & res differentiam, quod in motu, quamdiu est, semper intelligitur aliqua novitas: in rebus autem, eiusdem totius constantia. Unde si in medio motu omnes causae cessent, sequentem motus partem (si qua sequatur) sine causa incipere necessum est; hoc est, effectum (ut prius dixi) sine causa existentiam arripere, neque de hoc quicquam ambigo magis quam de priori, sed pari evidentia constat. | This difference, therefore, I see between motion and things [res]: that in motion, as long as it is, some newness [novitas] is always understood; but in things, the constancy of the same whole. Whence, if in the middle of the motion all the causes cease, it is necessary that the following part of the motion (if any follow) begin without a cause; that is, that an effect (as I said before) seize [arripere] existence without a cause; nor do I doubt anything about this more than about the former, but it stands [constat] with equal evidence. |
| [p. 272] Ereu. Hoc rursus expedi, corpus quod agit in aliud quando in illud agit? num tunc cum effectus incipit esse vel ante? | [p. 272] Ereu. Unravel [expedi] this again: a body which acts on another, when does it act on it? Is it then, when the effect begins to be, or before? |
| Andab. Hoc etiam clarissimum est, causam actu agere, cum effectus actu est, & simul ex necessitate componi causam actu & effectum actu. Neque potest effectus ab eo sperari quod iam non est. Unde somniare causam in aliquo tempore priori agere, & effectum immediatum in tempore posteriori primum existentiam concipere, prorsus a ratione est alienum. | Andab. This too is most clear: that the cause acts in act [actu] when the effect is in act, and that together [simul], out of necessity, the cause in act and the effect in act are put together [componi]. Nor can an effect be hoped for from that which now is not. Whence to dream that the cause acts in some prior time, and that the immediate effect first conceives existence in a posterior time, is utterly foreign to reason [ratione]. |
| Ereu. Nunc non superest nisi ut per te colligas quid ex concessis sequatur, omnibus ut vides, evidentibus. Et quoniam neque ipse referre putes quo numero corpora sumantur, modo sit finitus: Esto ternarius corporum universi numerus. A, B, & C, & sit tempus D, E, F, divisum in E, & moveatur C, in tempore D, E, & in signo E, quiescat: quid inde sequetur? | Ereu. Now nothing remains except that you gather [colligas] by yourself what follows from the [things] granted, all of them, as you see, evident. And since you yourself do not think either that it matters [referre] in what number the bodies are taken, provided it be finite: let the number of the bodies of the universe be three [ternarius]: A, B and C; and let there be the time D, E, F, divided at E; and let C be moved in the time D, E, and in the sign [signo] E let it rest: what will follow thence? |
| Andab. Si suppositionem ita facias, ut solum C, velis esse motum, video quidem sequi quod, nisi vel A, vel B, vel C, mutetur extrinsecus, semper quieturum sit C, cum enim positis causis iisdem, necesse est effectum manere eundem, positum autem sit causas non esse alias corporeas quam A, B, & C: his autem positis positum etiam est quiescere C, [p. 273] clarum est, nisi aliqua harum mutetur, semper quieturum: Sed quid inde ad intentum tuum colligere valeas, nondum dispicio. | Andab. If you make the supposition thus, that you wish C alone to have been moved, I see indeed that it follows that, unless either A or B or C be changed extrinsically, C will always rest [quieturum sit]; for since, the same causes being posited, it is necessary that the effect remain the same, and it has been posited that there are no other bodily causes than A, B and C — and these being posited, it has also been posited that C rests — [p. 273] it is clear that, unless some one of these be changed, it will always rest: But what you may be able to gather thence towards your intent [intentum], I do not yet discern [dispicio]. |
| Ereu. Et cum in signo E, est, quae est causa ut ibi sit? | Ereu. And when it is in the sign E, what is the cause that it is there? |
| Andab. Non aliae certe corporeae quam A, B, & C. | Andab. No other bodily [causes], certainly, than A, B and C. |
| Ereu. Fac itaque eas immutabiles manere, quid fiet? | Ereu. Make them, then, remain unchangeable: what will come about? |
| Andab. Manebit & quiescet, hoc enim ipsum positum est, non immutari. | Andab. It will remain and rest; for this very thing has been posited: [that they] are not changed. |
| Ereu. Recte advertisti: sed si ponatur C, esse & causa & subiectum suscipiens effectum, vides duplicem in eo mutationem accipi posse, unam ut novum quidpiam agat, alteram ut novum quidpiam recipiat. Omissa igitur hac mutatione posteriori qua mutatur ut subiectum, & posito dumtaxat eam priori non moveri, quid sequitur? | Ereu. You have noticed rightly: but if C be posited to be both cause and subject [subiectum] receiving [suscipiens] the effect, you see that a double change can be taken [accipi] in it: one, that it act something new; the other, that it receive something new. This latter change, therefore, by which it is changed as subject, being left aside, and it being posited only that it [eam] is not moved by [or: in] the former, what follows? |
| Andab. Video adhuc quieturum, abstulisti enim omnes causas novitatis. Quandoquidem enim A, B, & C, faciunt C, esse in signo E, ubi est: & oportet C, mutari ut alibi sit, neque mutatio possit esse, nisi sit aliqua mutationis causa; neque causa mutationis ulla esse possit; nisi vel A, vel B, vel C, ut causa sit mutata; fit nullam esse mutationis causam, &, quod protinus nascitur, nullam esse muta-[p. 274]tionem, quod ipsum est C, quiescere. | Andab. I see [it] still going to rest [quieturum], for you have taken away all the causes of newness [novitatis]. For since A, B and C make C to be in the sign E, where it is; and C must be changed in order that it be elsewhere; and there cannot be a change unless there be some cause of the change; nor can there be any cause of change unless either A or B or C have been changed as cause: it comes about [fit] that there is no cause of change, and — what is born [from it] straightway — that there is no cha-[p. 274]nge, which itself is [this]: that C rests. |
| Ereu. Et cur non colligis ut motus in rerum natura vel incipiat vel continuetur necessarium esse principium extrinsecum a quo primo oriatur? Quandoquidem corpus non agit ultra instans in quo est, & in singulis instantibus ex conspiratione omnium corporum quaelibet res est in aliquo determinato loco, & per consequens semper ibi erit dum omnia corpora sunt quod sunt: ut autem aliquid incipiat esse aliud quam quod est, necessum est ab aliquo alio motum seu mutationem primordium accipere. | Ereu. And why do you not gather that, in order that motion in the nature of things either begin or be continued, there is necessary an extrinsic principle [principium] from which it first arises? Since a body does not act beyond the instant in which it is, and in the single instants, out of the conspiring [conspiratione] of all bodies, each thing is in some determinate place, and consequently will always be there while all bodies are what they are: but in order that something begin to be other than what it is, it is necessary that the motion or change receive [its] first beginning [primordium] from some other. |
| Sunto enim omnia corpora A, B, C, si C, mutandum est, vel a se mutandum est, vel ab uno reliquorum. Non a se: Clarum enim est C, stantibus A, & B, quomodo sunt, facere quod facit; facit autem se esse in signo E. Neque a B, clarum enim est B, stantibus A, & C, quomodo sunt facere C, esse in E: & eadem est ratio de A; & de omnibus simul. Vides itaque nisi a principio non corporeo incipiatur motus, & quasi singulae motus particulae non omnino ullum futurum. | For let all bodies be A, B, C: if C is to be changed, either it is to be changed by itself, or by one of the rest. Not by itself: for it is clear that C, A and B standing as they are, does what it does; but it makes itself to be in the sign E. Nor by B: for it is clear that B, A and C standing as they are, makes C to be in E: and the same is the reasoning [ratio] concerning A, and concerning all together. You see, then, that unless motion be begun — and, as it were, [each of] the single particles of motion — from a non-bodily principle, there will not be any [motion] at all [non omnino ullum futurum]. |
| [Margin: Cessante primo mobili, omnia quietura.] Andab. Equidem saepius mirari mihi contigit Aristotelem & Peripateticos & etiam S. Thomam & Theologos eius sequaces tam constanter asseverasse cessante motu primi mobilis, omnem inferiorum cessaturam mu-[p. 275]tationem, & visi sunt mihi somniare, potius quam Philosophari. Nunc vero video altiorem latere sensum quam unquam suspicatus fuissem. | [Margin: The first mobile [primo mobili] ceasing, all things will rest.] Andab. For my part, it has happened to me rather often to wonder that Aristotle and the Peripatetics, and also St Thomas and the Theologians his followers, so constantly asserted that, the motion of the first mobile [primi mobilis] ceasing, all cha-[p. 275]nge of the lower [things] would cease; and they seemed to me to dream rather than to Philosophise. But now I see that a higher sense lies hidden [there] than I had ever suspected. |
| Ereu. Non novi equidem eos hoc argumento uti, sed ex duobus principiis inductione notis tantumdem colligunt. Quorum unum est, quicquid movetur ab alio moveri, reliquum vero corpus non movere, nisi ipsum motum sit. Ex quibus inducunt hanc ipsam veritatem. | Ereu. I have not known them, indeed, to use this argument; but from two principles known by induction they gather just as much [tantumdem]. Of which one is: whatever is moved is moved by another; but the remaining [one]: a body does not move unless it itself has been moved. From which they induce [inducunt] this very truth. |
| [Margin: Unde hoc Aristoteli constiterit.] Cum enim in mundo finito neque moventia, neque mota possint esse infinita; a moto accepto ad immediatum movens, & sic per gradus ad ultimum sive primum progrediuntur, quod cum motum esse ex secundo axiomate illis constet, necessario ad motorem incorporeum derivatur speculatio. | [Margin: Whence this was established [constiterit] for Aristotle.] For since in a finite world neither the movers nor the moved [things] can be infinite, from a moved [thing] taken they proceed to the immediate mover, and so by steps to the last or first; and since it is established for them from the second axiom that this [would have] been moved [motum esse], the speculation [speculatio] is necessarily diverted [derivatur] to an incorporeal mover. |
| Andab. At hoc primum principium omne quod movetur ab alio moveri, quomodo inductione fulciri potest? Cum gravia & levia, hoc est omnia quae tractamus, experiamur a seipsis moveri? Sed & animalium motus ab ipsismet esse, omni experientiae conforme est. | Andab. But this first principle — everything which is moved is moved by another — how can it be propped up [fulciri] by induction? When we experience heavy and light [things], that is, all the things which we handle, to be moved by themselves? And also, that the motions of animals are from their very selves is conformable to all experience. |
| Ereu. Spectarunt hi Philosophi omnium harum rerum generationem, & invenerunt motus hos cum ipsa generatione ortum sumpsisse, verbi gratia, cum aquam cernerent [p. 276] descendere, eandem calefactam ita ascendere perceperunt, ut refrigerata paululum, iterum recideret. Collegerunt proinde hos motus a calefaciente & infrigidante in aqua fieri, ac per consequens ab extrinseco agente ea mutante. | Ereu. These Philosophers looked at the generation [generationem] of all these things, and found that these motions had taken [their] rise with the generation itself; for example [verbi gratia], when they discerned water [p. 276] descend, they perceived the same, heated, so to ascend, that, cooled a little, it fell back again. They gathered accordingly that these motions come about from the [agent] heating and cooling in the water, and consequently from an extrinsic agent changing it. |
| Notarunt etiam motus hos non accidere, nisi cum res naturales extra loca propria reperiuntur: Unde non actionem, sed passionem naturalem eis esse agnoverunt, motu recto ferri: Agunt enim res naturales, cum perfectae sunt; ut autem perficiantur, patiuntur. Actionem itaque collegerunt alterius esse. | They noted also that these motions do not happen except when natural things are found outside [their] proper places: whence they recognised that to be borne with a straight motion is for them not an action but a natural passion [passionem]: for natural things act when they are perfect [perfectae]; but in order that they may be perfected, they suffer [patiuntur]. The action, therefore, they gathered to be of another. |
| Animalibus vero partes alias motas, alias motrices esse distinxerunt, quoad causas motus earum investigare potuerunt. Unde non dubitarunt idem etiam in caeteris esse, quo scientia ipsorum non pertingebat. | But in animals they distinguished some parts to be moved, others moving [motrices], as far as they were able to track down the causes of their motion. Whence they did not doubt that the same was also in the rest, whither their science [scientia] did not reach. |
| Andab. Non minor est de altero etiam principio dubitatio, cum magnetem & succinum movere ferrum & paleas absque eo quod ipsa moveantur, apertissimum sit: Quid dicam quomodo esca trahat animalia? spectabile pueros? haec tamen omnia immota esse ipsius Peripatetici calculo signatum est: docente Aristotele bonum & finem immota movere. | Andab. Not less is the doubt about the other principle too, since it is most open that the magnet and amber move iron and straws without their being moved themselves [absque eo quod ipsa moveantur]: What shall I say of how bait [esca] draws animals? a spectacle [spectabile], boys? Yet that all these are unmoved has been sealed by the vote [calculo] of the Peripatetic himself: Aristotle teaching that the good and the end move [while] unmoved. |
| Ereu. Non sufficiebat viris illis magnis ut externa species alicuius naturae opposita uni-[p. 277]versali regulae videretur, nisi causa apparens fidem ei faceret. Hinc se nescire quomodo succinum paleas attraheret fateri maluerunt, quam noto experimento ignotam causam temerario attribuere. | Ereu. It did not suffice for those great men that the external appearance [species] of some nature should seem opposed to a uni-[p. 277]versal rule, unless an apparent cause made faith [fidem … faceret] for it. Hence they preferred to confess that they did not know how amber attracted straws, rather than rashly to attribute an unknown cause to a known experiment [experimento]. |
| Sed post eorum tempora diluxit tam magnetem moveri ad ferrum, quam ferrum ad magnetem, & non sine motu magnetis & succini eas attractiones fieri. | But after their times it has dawned [diluxit] that the magnet is moved to the iron as much as the iron to the magnet, and that those attractions do not come about without motion of the magnet and of the amber. |
| De animalibus quoque non ignorabant a sensibus, hoc est, ab obiectorum actione omnem eorum motionem oriri: & quamvis finis quatenus talis motio sit, absque eo quod moveatur, quatenus tamen actio ipsius efficienti communis est, movetur in agendo suo modo ipse finis & Boni natura. | About animals also they were not ignorant that from the senses, that is, from the action of the objects [obiectorum], all their motion [motionem] arises: and although the end, in so far as [it is] such, be a moving [motio] without its being moved, yet in so far as its action is common to the efficient [cause], the end itself and the nature of the Good is moved, in its own way, in acting. |
| Asphal. Subtilia sunt quae commemoras, Ereuni, & parentum nostrorum gloriae idonea: sed & acutissime propositam ab Andabata quaestionem demonstravisti, nisi tamen nimium non properasti. | Asphal. Subtle are the [things] which you recount, Ereunius, and fit for the glory of our forefathers [parentum]: but also you have most acutely demonstrated the question proposed by Andabata — unless, however, you have not hurried too much [nisi tamen nimium non properasti]. |
| Dum enim te sequerer, ego in ingens praecipitium offendi, e quo me extricare tua dignum est opera. Videor autem mihi omnem motum ex universo sublatum ire. | For while I was following you, I stumbled into a huge precipice, from which to extricate me is worthy of your labour. For [autem] it seems to me that I am going to take [sublatum ire] all motion out of the universe. |
| Si enim verum est, agens non agere, nisi in instanti in quo est, quaeque sunt omnia in illo instanti non aliud facere, nisi ut haec res in hoc loco sit, puta C, ibi in signo E, sive agentia sint corporea [p. 278] sive incorporea, nihil usquam aliud agent. | For if it is true that an agent does not act except in the instant in which it is, and that all the things which are, in that instant do nothing else except that this thing be in this place — say C, there in the sign E — whether the agents be bodily [p. 278] or incorporeal, they will act nothing else anywhere. |
| Quare non extrinseco principio opus erit, ut motus exerceatur, sed aliquo quod simul in pluribus instantibus esse possit, quod an possit fieri nisi duo instantia simul sint, ignoro. Hoc certe clare impossibile est, unde multum vereor ne si huic argumento nimium tribuamus, omnem prorsus motum exterminemus. | Wherefore there will be need not of an extrinsic principle in order that motion be exercised, but of something which can be at once [simul] in several instants; which, whether it can come about unless two instants be at once, I do not know. This certainly is clearly impossible; whence I much fear lest, if we attribute too much to this argument, we banish [exterminemus] all motion altogether. |
| Quare rogandus es mihi ut vel differentiam inter materiatum agens & spirituale, quae ad rem sit, ostendas; aut quomodo nodus hic extricandus sit, aperias. | Wherefore you are to be asked by me that either you show a difference between a materiate [materiatum] agent and a spiritual [one] which is to the point [ad rem]; or that you open how this knot [nodus] is to be untied [extricandus]. |

## Words and phrases with alternative or special meanings

- ***signum (temporis); in signo E*** — rendered “sign [signo] (of time); in the sign E”. sign, mark, point; in scholastic usage an ‘instant of nature’ or indivisible point of time (signum = instans). Earlier units render signum of the turning-point of a motion as ‘mark’; here it is a point of time (‘in uno signo temporis’), used interchangeably with instans, so ‘sign’ is kept with the Latin bracketed.
- ***instans, instantia*** — rendered “instant, instants”. the indivisible now; Aristotelian nun (Phys. IV.11–13). The whole argument turns on the claim that a body ‘does not act beyond the instant in which it is’ — White’s denial that a body’s action can carry over from one instant to the next.
- ***concursus divinus*** — rendered “divine concurrence”. general concurrence of God with every created action. Standard scholastic doctrine (Suárez, Molina); Ereunius sets it aside as ‘too theological’, asking only what happens by nature.
- ***Misso … facto … conditore*** — rendered “the founder being dismissed [misso … facto]”. ablative absolute of the idiom missum facere = to dismiss, let go; facto could also be read as a noun (‘the deed’). Taken as the idiom (Terence, Cicero): ‘letting the founder of nature go’.
- ***de novo; novitas*** — rendered “anew; newness”. novitas = novelty, newness, the coming-to-be of something new. White’s key distinction: motion always involves ‘some newness’ in each part, things (res) a constancy of the whole — hence motion, unlike a finished building, needs a cause at every moment.
- ***Idem manens idem semper facit idem*** — rendered “the same, remaining the same, always makes the same”. scholastic axiom (idem manens idem semper facit idem). Aristotle, De gen. et corr. II.10, 336a27–28; used in Thomist arguments about change.
- ***totum sua parte maius*** — rendered “the whole greater than its part”. Euclid, Elements I, Common Notion 5. Andabata’s benchmark of certainty.
- ***actu agere; componi causam actu & effectum actu*** — rendered “acts in act; the cause in act and the effect in act are put together”. componi = be combined, be co-posited, be compossible. Aristotle, Phys. II.3 195b16–21: causes in act are simultaneous with their effects in act.
- ***conspiratio (omnium corporum)*** — rendered “conspiring [conspiratione]”. concord, joint working, ‘breathing together’. White’s picture: the combined state of all bodies fixes each thing’s place at each instant.
- ***primordium*** — rendered “first beginning [primordium]”. origin, first principle; Lucretian primordia = atoms. Here simply the first beginning of a change.
- ***primum mobile (primi mobilis)*** — rendered “the first mobile”. the outermost sphere whose daily motion carries the heavens. Aristotelian–Thomist doctrine (Aquinas, In De caelo II; ST I q.115 a.3) that if the first heaven stopped, all generation and change below would cease — White reinterprets it as a cosmological argument for an incorporeal first mover.
- ***omne quod movetur ab alio movetur; corpus non movet nisi motum*** — rendered “whatever is moved is moved by another; a body does not move unless it has been moved”. the two Aristotelian axioms of Phys. VII.1 and VIII.4–5. With the impossibility of an infinite regress they yield the unmoved mover (Phys. VIII; Metaph. XII.7); White calls them principles ‘known by induction’.
- ***inducunt / inductione*** — rendered “induce / by induction”. induco = lead to, infer; inductio = epagoge. Kept literal; ‘induce’ here means ‘infer’.
- ***passio naturalis (motu recto ferri)*** — rendered “natural passion”. passio = being acted upon, undergoing; not ‘emotion’. Aristotle, Phys. VIII.4 255b: the natural motion of heavy and light bodies is from the generator and the remover of obstacles, not from the body itself.
- ***perfectae / perficiantur*** — rendered “perfect / be perfected”. complete, finished, in their full form. Things act when complete, undergo when being completed (Aristotelian act/potency).
- ***magnes & succinum*** — rendered “magnet and amber”. lodestone; amber (electrum). The standard counter-examples to ‘every mover is moved’; White follows Gilbert (De magnete, 1600) and Digby in holding that the magnet also moves to the iron.
- ***spectabile pueros?*** — rendered “a spectacle, boys?”. spectabile = something worth seeing; the verb trahat is understood from the previous clause. Elliptical: ‘[how] a show [draws] boys’ — a further case of a mover that seems unmoved.
- ***calculo signatum*** — rendered “sealed by the vote [calculo]”. calculus = pebble used for voting or reckoning; signare = seal, mark. Figure from voting with pebbles (white/black calculi).
- ***bonum & finem immota movere*** — rendered “the good and the end move [while] unmoved”. Aristotle, Metaph. XII.7 1072a26–b3: the final cause moves as the object of love (kinei hōs erōmenon). White claims the end is ‘moved in its own way’ in so far as its action is shared with the efficient cause.
- ***fidem facere*** — rendered “make faith”. establish credibility, convince. Idiom kept literal.
- ***diluxit*** — rendered “it has dawned”. become clear, come to light. Points to recent magnetic philosophy (Gilbert).
- ***motio / motus*** — rendered “a moving [motio] / motion”. motio = the act of moving another (active); motus = motion. Cf. D2-N05: White separates active moving from the motion received.
- ***nisi tamen nimium non properasti*** — rendered “unless, however, you have not hurried too much”. With pleonastic non the sense is ‘unless perhaps you have hurried too much’; or read ‘if only you have not hurried too much’. Asphalius’ polite objection; the doubled negative is kept as printed.
- ***materiatum agens & spirituale*** — rendered “materiate agent and spiritual [agent]”. materiatum = made of matter, embodied. Scholastic: a material agent is bound to its instant and place; the question whether a spiritual one is not leads into Ereunius’ answer in part 2.
- ***nodus … extricandus*** — rendered “knot … to be untied”. nodus also = White’s section unit (‘Knot’). Pun: Asphalius asks for the ‘knot’ to be untied in a section that is itself a nodus.
