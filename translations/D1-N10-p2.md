## Dialogue I, Knot x, part 2 (printed pp. 92–96)

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| [p. 92] Andab. Attamen si perpetuum sit lumen, implebit lineam C, O, et si impleta est, quid addi potest? | [p. 92] Andab. Yet if the light be perpetual, it will fill the line C, O; and if it has been filled, what can be added? |
| [p. 93] Ereu. Non advertis recta cadentem pluviam pavimentum toto corpore, parallela vero casui moenia levi fortassis aspergine, et quasi contagio, non proposito, irrorare. Sic et lumen cogita, etsi lineam C, O, quoniam respective ad agens, longitudinem et latitudinem participat, participata quoque percussione infundere. | [p. 93] Ereu. Do you not advert [that] rain falling straight bedews [irrorare] the pavement with [its] whole body, but the walls parallel to the fall with a light sprinkling perhaps, and as if by contagion, not by purpose [proposito]? So think also [that] light, although [etsi] [it strikes] the line C, O [only obliquely], since, relatively [respective] to the agent, [that line] partakes of length and breadth, pours in [infundere] with a partaken [participata] percussion also. |
| Asphal. Si Andabatae inquietudinem ademisti, et meam lenito. Expecto siquidem cur neque soles illi supercoelestes tales nobis affundant Cometas, quales hic noster familiaris. | Asphal. If you have taken away Andabata's disquiet, soothe [lenito] mine also. For indeed [siquidem] I wait [to hear] why those supercelestial suns too do not pour upon us such Comets as this familiar [sun] of ours [does]. |
| Ereu. Non dixim hoc, Asphali, neque enim scio, neque necessitas ulla me astringit ut hoc credam. Fateor tamen difficilis esse me fidei, ut factum credam, ut Cometa quispiam nobis iubas ex tam alto quassaverit. Dum enim ingentem Polaris Phari altitudinem prospicio, et ingentia illa vaporum aequora quae ad stellam formandam desiderantur, perpendo. Cum adactum per nebulas immanes lumen quantum hebetetur attendo: non sine ratione, actuosam me probationem facti postulare confido. Quid si pensitem distantiam Cometae a sole suo neque posse non esse magnam, cum quaelibet minima vel unius dumtaxat gradus excedat longe distantiam Cometae a sole nostro, quantumvis integri quadrantis esset. | Ereu. I would not say [dixim] this, Asphalius, for neither do I know [it], nor does any necessity bind me that I should believe this. I confess, however, that I am of difficult faith [difficilis … fidei], so as to believe [it] done [factum], that any Comet has shaken [quassaverit] [its] manes [iubas] at us from so high. For while I look forth at the huge height of the Polar Pharos [Polaris Phari], and weigh those huge seas [aequora] of vapours which are required for forming a star. When I attend how much the light driven [adactum] through immense mists [nebulas] is dulled: not without reason, I trust, do I demand an effectual [actuosam] proof of the fact. What if I should ponder [pensitem] that the distance of the Comet from its sun cannot not be great, since any [distance] whatever, the least, even [vel] of one degree only, far exceeds the distance of a Comet from our sun, however much [quantumvis] it were of an entire quadrant. |
| [p. 94] Cum enim circuli sint in proportionibus diametrorum, diameter autem orbis solaris ad diametrum firmamenti quod unum ad 2160. gradus autem ad quadrantem sui orbis ut unum ad 90. 90. vero 24. in 2160. contineantur: fit quod uno gradu in coelo octavo distat 24. quadrantes solaris periodi abesse. | [p. 94] For since circles are in the proportions of [their] diameters, and [autem] the diameter of the solar orb [is] to the diameter of the firmament as [quod] one to 2160, and a degree to the quadrant of its orb as one to 90, and 90 is contained 24 [times] in 2160: it comes about [fit] that what is distant by one degree in the eighth heaven is away 24 quadrants of the solar circuit [periodi]. |
| Vides itaque nisi novus sol nostro sole maior sit, vix illuminabilem esse materiam Cometicam, nedum ut moriens lumen per tantarum nubium voragines nos adusque prodat. Adiunge his quantulum lumen pleraque Astra affluant nobis, quod puta neque umbram in obscurissima nocte reddat: Et caudam, quoniam a sole aversa est, vel nos versum extendit, vel aversam esse vel in eodem cum suo sole circulo: sed hoc quidem tertio modo non erit in eadem recta: sin acclivis aut declivis est cauda, necesse erit tanto breviorem esse, et in ipsum fere caput reciduam resorberi. | You see therefore [that] unless the new sun be greater than our sun, the Cometic matter is scarcely illuminable, much less [nedum] that [its] dying light should go forth [prodat] through the whirlpools [voragines] of such great clouds [nubium] as far as us. Add to these how small a light most Stars flow upon [affluant] us, [a light] which, suppose [puta], does not even render a shadow in the darkest night: And [add that] the tail, since it is turned away from the sun, either extends toward us, or [is] turned away, or [lies] in the same circle with its sun: but in this third mode indeed it will not be in the same straight [line] [recta]: but if [sin] the tail is upward-sloping [acclivis] or downward-sloping [declivis], it will be necessary [that it] be so much the shorter, and, falling back [reciduam], be reabsorbed into almost the head itself. |
| Hoc reddo non assertionis sed cautelae argumentum. Scio enim fingenti suppetere ex quantitatis natura unde quidlibet asseratur. Potentiam autem illorum corporum numeris et rationibus comprehensam ego necdum habeo. | This I render [as] an argument not of assertion but of caution [cautelae]. For I know [that] for one who feigns [fingenti] there is at hand, from the nature of quantity, [that] whence anything whatever may be asserted. But the power of those bodies comprehended by numbers and reasons [rationibus] I do not yet have. |
| Asphal. Si igitur alias quidem stellas, alios Cometas affirmas, quis erit hos ab iis distinguendi modus? Quos enim videre datur, eos [p. 95] etiam internoscere par est. | Asphal. If therefore you affirm some [to be] stars indeed, others Comets, what will be the mode of distinguishing these from those? For those which it is given to see, it is fitting [par] also [p. 95] to discern [internoscere]. |
| Ereu. Sed neque ego Cometas aliquos assero; mortales enim quas videmus stellas non ausim Cometas affirmare. Fortassis enim stellae erunt de sese nobis invisibiles quibus affusus undique corporatior aer per reflexum lumen augustiorem speciem dederit, quo postea fatiscente in suam ignobilitatem rursus desciscant. | Ereu. But neither do I assert any [to be] Comets; for the mortal stars which we see I would not dare to affirm [to be] Comets. For perhaps they will be stars of themselves invisible to us, to which the more corporeal [corporatior] air, poured round on all sides, will have given through reflected light a more august appearance [speciem]; which [air] afterwards growing faint [fatiscente], they may fall away [desciscant] again into their ignobility. |
| Deinde cui constat eas quae nobis apparent stellulae non omnes soles esse, quibus singulis sint sua regna Planetarum nobis incognita? Quo enim sydereo nuncio haec referente discere possumus? | Next, to whom is it established [constat] [that] those little stars which appear to us are not all suns, to each of which [there] are their own kingdoms of Planets unknown to us? For by what starry messenger [sydereo nuncio] reporting these [things] can we learn [it]? |
| Cum neque magnitudo neque vigor luminis quicquam nobis auxilii conferre possit, dispungente omnia meditata tanta horum corporum distantia: prae qua, num ea corpora in una superficie sint, an vero ad diversas a nobis longitudines nihil affirmate scire conceditur: hoc autem ignoto, magnitudinum ratio nulla iniri possit, quae enim a multis inita est, eam iacit hypothesim in una eademque superficie tot lumina circumvehi: Neque sublata magnitudinum notitia, ex luminis vigore certi aliquid componere permissum est. | Since neither the magnitude nor the vigour of the light can confer anything of help on us, the so great distance of these bodies cancelling [dispungente] all [things] thought out: before which [prae qua] it is granted to know nothing affirmatively [affirmate], whether those bodies are in one surface, or truly at diverse lengths [longitudines] from us: and this being unknown, no reckoning [ratio] of the magnitudes can be entered upon [iniri]; for that which has been entered upon by many, lays down [iacit] [as its] hypothesis that so many lights are carried round in one and the same surface: Nor, the notice of the magnitudes being taken away, is it permitted to compose anything certain out of the vigour of the light. |
| Vigor enim luminis ex concursu seu constipatione plurium radiorum est, quae celerem videtur luminis agitationem efficere: plaeraeque enim voces quibus hanc luminis affectionem exprimimus a vita motuque tradu-[p. 96]ctae sunt, quasi ipsa re nobis indicante non nisi velocitatem motus esse quam sentimus. | For the vigour of light is from the concourse or crowding-together [constipatione] of more rays, which seems to effect a swift agitation of the light: for most words [voces] by which we express this affection of light have been trans-[p. 96]ferred [traductae] from life and motion, as if, the thing itself indicating [it] to us, [it were] nothing but speed of motion that we sense. |
| Constipatio modo radiorum vel ex magnitudine obiecti a quo remittitur lumen, vel ex laevore eiusdem a quo fit ut plures in unam partem emittantur, et tandem ex eo quod per subortum novorum progressi impellantur, existere potest. At ex quanam harum trium caussarum procedat, difficilis (mihi certe) est coniectura. | The crowding-together of the rays, however [modo], can exist either from the magnitude of the object from which the light is sent back, or from the smoothness [laevore] of the same, from which it comes about that more [rays] are emitted into one part, and lastly from this, that [rays] having advanced [progressi] are driven on through the up-springing [subortum] of new ones. But from which of these three causes it proceeds, the conjecture is difficult (to me certainly). |

## Words and phrases with alternative or special meanings

- ***irrorare / aspergo*** — rendered “bedew / sprinkling”. wet with dew or spray Rain image: light falling obliquely on a surface strikes it 'by contagion, not by purpose', i.e. with diminished intensity (cosine law in qualitative form).
- ***respective ad agens*** — rendered “relatively [respective] to the agent”. in respect of, with relation to Scholastic adverb from respectus (relation); the line C, O counts as having breadth only relative to the light source.
- ***participata percussio*** — rendered “partaken [participata] percussion”. shared, partial, diminished stroke Participation language (more and less) applied to the optical 'stroke' of light.
- ***dixim*** — rendered “I would not say [dixim]”. archaic perfect subjunctive = dixerim Plautine form; potential subjunctive of modest assertion.
- ***difficilis fidei*** — rendered “of difficult faith”. hard to convince, slow to believe Genitive of quality.
- ***iubae*** — rendered “manes [iubas]”. crests, hair; comets as 'hairy stars' Classical comet imagery (κομήτης, crinitus).
- ***Polaris Pharus*** — rendered “the Polar Pharos [Polaris Phari]”. the Pole-star as lighthouse Pharos of Alexandria; the fixed star as a distant beacon/sun.
- ***actuosa probatio facti*** — rendered “effectual [actuosam] proof of the fact”. active, actual, working proof; proof from observation Demand for factual evidence before admitting supercelestial comets.
- ***quod unum ad 2160*** — rendered “as [quod] one to 2160”. quod used for ut in a proportion Same 1:2160 ratio of solar orb to firmament as in part 1; one degree of the firmament = 2160/360 = 6 solar circles = 24 quadrants.
- ***periodus (solaris)*** — rendered “circuit [periodi]”. period, revolution; here the solar orb's circumference Spatial rather than temporal sense.
- ***nedum ut*** — rendered “much less that”. still less
- ***vorago*** — rendered “whirlpool [voragines]”. abyss, gulf Light lost in vast depths of cloud.
- ***acclivis / declivis*** — rendered “upward-sloping / downward-sloping”. inclined toward or away Geometry of the comet's tail relative to the line of sight.
- ***cautela*** — rendered “caution [cautelae]”. precaution; legal 'cautel' White marks his calculation as a cautionary, not assertive, argument.
- ***fingere / fingens*** — rendered “feign / one who feigns [fingenti]”. suppose, frame hypotheses Contempt for hypothesis-making cf. Galileo/Kepler; cf. 'hypotheses non fingo' (later).
- ***internoscere*** — rendered “discern [internoscere]”. distinguish between
- ***corporatior aer*** — rendered “more corporeal [corporatior] air”. denser, more bodily Explains the novae as faint stars temporarily magnified by dense air.
- ***fatiscere*** — rendered “grow faint [fatiscente]”. crack, flag, become exhausted
- ***desciscere*** — rendered “fall away [desciscant]”. revert, defect
- ***sydereus nuncius*** — rendered “starry messenger [sydereo nuncio]”. starry message Allusion to Galileo's Sidereus nuncius (1610), which could be read as 'messenger' or 'message'.
- ***dispungere*** — rendered “cancel [dispungente]”. check off, strike out of accounts Accounting metaphor: distance cancels all calculations.
- ***affirmate*** — rendered “affirmatively [affirmate]”. with assurance Rare adverb.
- ***vigor luminis*** — rendered “vigour of the light”. brightness, intensity Kept apart from intensio (degree).
- ***constipatio*** — rendered “crowding-together [constipatione]”. compression, density Brightness explained as density of rays — corpuscular/Digbean optics.
- ***traducere (voces)*** — rendered “transfer [traductae]”. carry over (metaphorically) Cf. translatio vocis; words for brightness (vivid, lively, flashing) drawn from life and motion.
- ***laevor*** — rendered “smoothness [laevore]”. polish Smooth surfaces reflect more rays one way (specular reflection).
- ***subortus*** — rendered “up-springing [subortum]”. arising afresh Successive emission of rays pushing earlier ones forward.

## Translator's notes

- Unit: Dialogue I, Knot x, part 2 of 2 (end of Knot x). PDF pp. 103–107 = printed pp. 92–96, from 'Andab. Attamen si perpetuum sit lumen' (p. 92) to '…difficilis (mihi certe) est coniectura.' (p. 96). Knot xi begins on p. 96 (PDF 107) at 'Asphal. Ergo ad tubos coelispices', margin 'Nodus XI. Tubum opticum in summo esse'; a further margin on p. 96 reads 'Liber ingeniosissimus Renati de Cartes' (belongs to Knot xi). Offset +11 holds (p. 96 = PDF 107).
- Readings and syntax: (a) p. 93 'etsi lineam C, O … participata quoque percussione infundere' is elliptical; 'lineam' is construed as the object of 'infundere' (light pours into the line C, O with a partial stroke); the bracketed '[it strikes] … [only obliquely]' is interpretive. (b) p. 93 'Dum enim … prospicio, et … perpendo.' is a dum-clause without main verb, kept as a fragment as printed. (c) p. 94 'Et caudam … vel nos versum extendit, vel aversam esse' mixes indicative and accusative-infinitive; rendered literally. (d) p. 95 'eas quae nobis apparent stellulae' — accusative 'eas' with nominative 'stellulae' as printed (attraction or misprint for 'stellulas'). (e) p. 95 'sydereo' and p. 95 'plaeraeque' (printed 'plæræque') kept in printed spelling. (f) p. 96 'laevore' from printed 'lęuore'.
- Arithmetic: 2160 / 90 = 24, so one degree of the eighth sphere = 24 quadrants (6 full circumferences) of the solar orb — consistent with the 1:2160 ratio used in part 1.
- Verification pass: Latin re-read against the 150 dpi images and a 300 dpi zoom of p. 94; English checked sentence by sentence. 4 corrections: (1) 'Dum … prospicio, et … perpendo' — both verbs inside the dum-clause (first draft made 'perpendo' a main verb); (2) 'vel unius dumtaxat gradus' — 'vel' = 'even', not 'or'; (3) 'quo postea fatiscente' — ablative absolute referring to the air, not to the stars; (4) 'prae qua' rendered 'before which' rather than 'because of which' to keep the literal sense (bracketed).
- Conventions as in glossary.md: long s → s, ę → ae, accents dropped, '&' → et, u/v normalised; speaker abbreviations kept (Ereu., Asphal., Andab.).
