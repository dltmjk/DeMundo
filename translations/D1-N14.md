## Dialogue I, Knot xiv (printed pp. 108–112)

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| [p. 108] [Margin: Nodus XIV. Idem individuum non posse naturaliter redire.] Asphal. Dum tu ad Andabatam disputas, obversata est animo meo cogitatio ab ipsa tua dissertatione experrecta, quam communem facere aveo. | [p. 108] [Margin: Knot XIV. That the same individual [individuum] cannot naturally return.] Asphal. While you dispute against [ad] Andabata, there has hovered before [obversata est] my mind a thought awakened [experrecta] by your dissertation itself, which I long [aveo] to make common. |
| Videatur enim mihi ex illa per minima mixtione quam commemorasti, cum eam quam multiplex sit mecum penso, illud confieri. | For it would seem [Videatur] to me [that] out of that mixtion [mixtione] through [their] least [parts] [per minima] which you mentioned, when I weigh [penso] with myself how manifold it is, this [illud] is brought about [confieri]: |
| Idem singulare seu individuum, ut vocant, naturae vi repeti nunquam posse. | [that] the same singular, or individual [individuum] as they call [it], can never be repeated by the force of nature. |
| Et quod me movet est. | And [here] is what moves me. |
| Video in iis ludis qui plurium casu subeuntium vicissitudine constant, quo plura sunt quae miscentur, seu quo plures sunt vices aequaliter combinatae, eo rarius unam aliquam unam accidere posse. | I see in those games which consist in the alternation [vicissitudine] of many [things] coming up [subeuntium] by chance [casu], [that] the more [quo plura] are the things which are mixed, or the more [quo plures] are the turns [vices] equally combined, by so much the more rarely [eo rarius] can some one one [unam aliquam unam] happen. |
| Ut si tribus aleis ludatur, tres seniones certo quodam numero incerte mixto obviaturos. | As, if one plays [ludatur] with three dice, [I see that] three sixes [seniones] will turn up [obviaturos] in a certain fixed number [of throws] [certo quodam numero] uncertainly mixed. |
| Idem futurum si a sex aleis, sex seniones poscantur, sed numerum longe minorem repetitae senionum collectionis futurum, et sic certa lege augeri iactus raritatem. | The same will be if from six dice six sixes are demanded, but [that] the number of the repeated collection of sixes will be far smaller, and thus [that] by a certain law the rarity of the throw is increased. |
| Video modo in natura caussarum concurrentium absolutam et perfectam infinitatem. | I see now [modo] in nature an absolute and perfect infinity [infinitatem] of concurring causes. |
| Etsi enim caussae numeratae, definita et facili satis multitudine comprehendantur, tamen per applicationem unius ad aliam video in infinitas dehiscere. | For although the causes, [when] numbered, are comprehended in a defined and easy enough multitude, yet through the application of one to another I see [them] gape open [dehiscere] into infinite [ones]. |
| Esto enim dividendum lignum securi, vel vinum fusione in vitrum, sive materiam, sive agens respicio, video neutrum compos certi termini, sed per utriusve divisibilitatem fieri, ut vinum vel lignum ex divisione nasciturum ex [p. 109] infinitis sorte eligatur. | For let there be [Esto] wood to be divided by an axe, or wine [to be divided] by pouring into a glass: whether I look at the matter or the agent, I see neither [to be] master [compos] of a certain term [termini], but [that] through the divisibility of either [utriusve] it comes about that the wine or wood that is to be born from the division is chosen by lot [sorte] out of [p. 109] infinite [possibilities]. |
| Si itaque necessarium est ut quo maior est accidentium varietas eo rarius idem effectum occurrat: Pariter necesse est ubi multiplicitas omnem modum excedit, neque secundo redire posse. | If therefore it is necessary that the greater [quo maior] is the variety of accidents [accidentium], the more rarely [eo rarius] the same effect occurs: Equally it is necessary [that], where the multiplicity exceeds every measure [modum], [the same] cannot return even a second time [neque secundo]. |
| Ereu. Subtiliter, Asphali, et pro tuo ingenio: at si quis tibi reponat nihil mutatas esse caussas, ex eo quod semel effectus hic produxerit, Esse proinde eandem in causis aequabilitatem quae prius, cur itaque non eandem prorsus divisionem secundo fecerit, quam prius fecit? | Ereu. Subtly, Asphalius, and in keeping with [pro] your wit [ingenio]: but if someone should reply [reponat] to you that the causes have been changed in nothing, from this, that [it] once produced the effects here [effectus hic], [that] there is accordingly the same evenness [aequabilitatem] in the causes as before: why then would [it] not have made [fecerit] a second time exactly the same division as [it] made before? |
| Et si reponas infinitatem causarum, reddat tibi eandem esse et valere respectu illius effecti quodcumque eventurum est, et tamen unum designato exiturum non diffiteberis. | And if you reply [with] the infinity of the causes, he may return to you that it is the same and is equivalent [valere] in respect of that effect [effecti], whatever is going to result [eventurum est]; and yet you will not deny that one [outcome] will come out designated [designato]. |
| Asphal. Rependam ego mutatas esse caussas, neque iam in potentia earum esse quod semel redactum est in exercitium, et infinitatem quae respectu multorum talis est, respectu illius unius exituri ex totali complexione causarum esse determinatam. | Asphal. I will pay back [Rependam] that the causes have been changed, and that what has once been reduced into exercise [exercitium] is no longer in their potency [potentia], and that the infinity which is such in respect of many [things] is determinate in respect of that one [thing] going to come out of the total complexion [complexione] of the causes. |
| Andab. Siccine, Asphali, aleas aut manum, aut fritillum conficies esse mutatum uno iactu, ut eandem repetere non possit? atqui nonnunquam repetit. | Andab. Is it so [Siccine], Asphalius: will you make out [conficies] the dice or the hand or the dice-box [fritillum] to have been changed by one throw, so that it cannot repeat the same [throw]? and yet [atqui] sometimes it repeats [it]. |
| Neque infinitatem determinatam negaverim, sed hoc quaeritur cur non possit esse aeque determinata ad idem atque fuit ad diversum? | Nor would I deny the infinity [to be] determinate; but this is asked: why can it not be equally determined to the same as it was to a different [thing]? |
| [p. 110] Asphal. Intuere ergo, Andabata, rerum speciem, et vide impositas fritillo aleas certo situ iacere a modo quo indebantur et laterum fritilli excussu cum in ipso fritillo iactatione permiscentur; hanc permixtionem ex dicto situ, forma fritilli et motu pendere; motum et formam fritilli ex aliis prioribus, et motibus et instrumentis. | [p. 110] Asphal. Look then, Andabata, at the appearance [speciem] of things, and see [that] the dice put into the dice-box lie in a certain site [situ] from the mode in which they were put in [indebantur] and from the shaking [excussu] of the sides of the dice-box, when in the dice-box itself they are mixed through [permiscentur] by tossing [iactatione]; [that] this mixing-through [permixtionem] depends on the said site, the form of the dice-box and the motion; [that] the motion and form of the dice-box [depend] on other prior [things], both motions and instruments. |
| Non itaque (quod tu videris existimare) ex solis proximis causis pendet haec determinatio, sed ab aliis remotioribus quamvis et has mutatas quis negare possit? | Not therefore (as [quod] you seem to think) does this determination depend on the next [proximis] causes alone, but on other more remote [ones]; although [quamvis] who could deny that these too [are] changed? |
| Certe situs alearum in fritillo vel maxime facit ad futurum iactum, et plerumque esse mutatus negari non potest. | Certainly the site of the dice in the dice-box makes [facit ad] even most of all [vel maxime] for the future throw, and it cannot be denied [that it] for the most part [plerumque] is changed. |
| Fritillum an sit sic mutatum ut ad rem nostram faciat, nihil produco. | Whether the dice-box is so changed that it makes [faciat] for our matter, I bring forward [produco] nothing. |
| Certe manum, et quod magis est iactantis phantasiam quae manum dirigit in momenta, esse mobilem, nemo inficias eat, et tamen ceteris omnibus positis, hac una errabit perpetuo iactus. | Certainly, that the hand, and what is more, the phantasy [phantasiam] of the thrower, which directs the hand, is mobile from moment to moment [in momenta], no one would go to deny [inficias eat]; and yet, all the others being posited, by this one [cause] the throw will err perpetually. |
| Quid itaque miraris si causas esse mutatas dixi. | Why then do you wonder if I said the causes have been changed. |
| Et cum his causis aliae plures coniunctae sunt quarum, si una aliqua desit hae non satis erunt effecto ostendendo. | And with these causes more others are conjoined, of which, if some one be lacking [desit], these will not be sufficient for showing forth [ostendendo] the effect [effecto]. |
| Et hae ipse iterum ab aliis pendent, quarum si quae alia sit quam fuerit, harum aliqua defuat. | And these themselves in turn depend on others, of which, if any be other than it was [fuerit], some one of these would be lacking [defuat]. |
| Et quo plures causas ad examen evocas, eo eas a pluribus pendere invenis, et incertior magisque errori obnoxius fit effectus. | And the more [quo plures] causes you call out to examination, by so much [eo] you find them to depend on more, and more uncertain and more liable [obnoxius] to error becomes the effect. |
| Et harum causarum singulae infinitatem illam sortiuntur [p. 111] quam expressimus. | And of these causes each one [singulae] obtains [sortiuntur] that infinity [p. 111] which we expressed. |
| Ne vero exemplo producto obtundaris, adverte iactum senionum cum numerabilem admodum sortem subeat, necessum esse, etsi mille varietates in causis sint, has omnes ad eundem numerum revocari, neque maiorem facere diversitatem quam si sola iactuum varietas foret. | But lest you be blunted [obtundaris] by the example brought forward, notice [that], the throw of sixes, since it undergoes a quite numerable lot [sortem], it is necessary [necessum], even if there be a thousand varieties in the causes, [that] all these are called back to the same number, and do not make a greater diversity than if there were only the variety of the throws. |
| Individuum autem cum a Mathematica unitate circumstantiarum pendeat, perfecte infinitatem varietatis sentire. | But [that] the individual [individuum], since it depends on a Mathematical unity of circumstances, perfectly feels [sentire] the infinity of variety. |
| Iactus itaque quem urges non individuo sed specie et fortasse non infima unus est, et numeratarum causarum effectus, cum ea de quibus quaerimus, verae infinitati quae potentiam circumstat, subiiciatur. | The throw therefore which you press [urges] is one not in individual [individuo] but in species [specie], and perhaps not the lowest [infima] [species], and [is] the effect of numbered causes, whereas [cum] those [things] about which we inquire are subjected [subiiciatur] to the true infinity which stands around [circumstat] the potency [potentiam]. |
| Neque quod de infinitate asseruimus, ratione vacat: si enim multitudo causarum est, et uni servitum est, non ipsa multitudo cogit ne eidem rursus pareatur? | Nor is what we asserted about infinity void of reason [ratione]: for if there is a multitude of causes, and service has been done [servitum est] to one, does not the multitude itself compel that the same not be obeyed [pareatur] again? |
| alioquin ipsa multitudinis ratio intereat, si non praevaleat uni. | otherwise the very reason [ratio] of multitude would perish, if it should not prevail over [praevaleat] one. |
| Quod enim in multitudine, ordine sese protrudente, facit successionis ordo, hoc in confusione facit multitudinis quantitas. | For what in a multitude, with the order thrusting itself forward, the order of succession does, this in confusion the quantity of the multitude does. |
| Sed in ordine vides donec redeatur, unum uno trudi in orbem, neque posse idem nisi ipso ordine perfecto redire. | But in an order you see [that], until there is a return [redeatur], one is thrust by one into a circle [in orbem], and [that] the same cannot return unless the order itself is completed [perfecto]. |
| Erit igitur in confusione multitudinis ratio huic aequivalens, maxime si infinita sit, semper enim cum quae acta sunt, finita sint, quae agenda infinita; resistant praeteritis futura in ea proportione qua finito comparatur infinitu- [p. 112] do neque redeundi cuiquam illorum potestatem ullam faciet. | There will therefore be in confusion a reason [ratio] of multitude equivalent to this, most of all if it be infinite; for always, since the things which have been done are finite, the things to be done infinite, the future [things] would resist [resistant] the past in that proportion in which infinitude is compared to the finite, [p. 112] nor will it make [faciet] any power of returning for any one of those. |

## Words and phrases with alternative or special meanings

- ***individuum / singulare*** — rendered “individual / singular”. a numerically one thing (scholastic individuum, Porphyrian 'individual' under the lowest species); 'singulare' the logical singular The knot's thesis: nature never reproduces the numerically same individual. White contrasts 'individuo … specie' (one in number vs one in kind) in his reply about the dice.
- ***per minima mixtio*** — rendered “mixtion through [their] least [parts]”. minima naturalia; atomist 'smallest bodies' Refers back to Ereunius' account of mixture per minima in the preceding knots (cf. glossary 'per minima'); the Digbean/Whitean corpuscular account of mixts.
- ***infinitas*** — rendered “infinity”. endlessness; indeterminacy Here an 'infinity' of concurring causes arising from the continuum's divisibility, not an actual infinite multitude; cf. D1-N02.
- ***senio, seniones*** — rendered “six, sixes”. the six-spot on a die (Martial, Suetonius); 'iactus senionum' = a throw of all sixes Classical dicing vocabulary, with alea (die), fritillus (dice-box), iactus (throw); an early probabilistic argument about rarer combinations as the number of dice grows.
- ***fritillus*** — rendered “dice-box”. cup for shaking dice Classical (Juvenal, Martial).
- ***vices (aequaliter combinatae)*** — rendered “turns (equally combined)”. alternations, chances, 'cases' In effect equiprobable combinations.
- ***sors / sortiri*** — rendered “lot / obtain”. chance; share, fate 'sorte eligatur' = chosen by lot; 'numerabilem sortem subeat' = undergoes a numerable chance (finite number of outcomes); 'sortiuntur' = obtain as their share (cf. glossary).
- ***compos certi termini*** — rendered “master of a certain term”. possessed of a fixed end/limit Neither the matter nor the agent has a fixed limit at which division must stop: continuum divisibility.
- ***dehiscere (in infinitas)*** — rendered “gape open (into infinite [ones])”. split, yawn Vivid image of finite causes opening into infinite possibilities.
- ***designato (exire)*** — rendered “come out designated”. determinately, as a designated one Cf. glossary designare/designabilis (to mark off as 'this').
- ***redigere in exercitium*** — rendered “reduce into exercise”. bring into act Scholastic 'reductio de potentia ad actum'; exercitium = actual exercise of a power (actus secundus).
- ***complexio (causarum)*** — rendered “complexion (of causes)”. combination, totality Cf. glossary complexio; here the total concourse of causes.
- ***phantasia*** — rendered “phantasy”. imagination (Aristotelian φαντασία) The thrower's imagination directs the hand; the inner sense of scholastic psychology.
- ***inficias ire*** — rendered “go to deny”. deny Classical idiom (Plautus, Livy).
- ***effectum / effectus*** — rendered “effect”. thing produced 'effecto ostendendo' (dative gerundive): for the effect to be shown forth.
- ***defuat*** — rendered “would be lacking”. archaic subjunctive of desum (= desit) Old Latin form (Plautus); White likes archaisms (cf. dixim, autumare).
- ***necessum*** — rendered “necessary”. archaic = necesse Lucretian/Plautine form.
- ***species (infima)*** — rendered “species (lowest)”. infima species = the lowest species above individuals Porphyrian tree; the throw 'three sixes' is one only in kind, perhaps not even in the lowest kind.
- ***circumstare potentiam*** — rendered “stand around the potency”. surround the power The infinity of possible outcomes belonging to a potency.
- ***in orbem trudi*** — rendered “be thrust into a circle”. in rotation, in turn Image of an ordered series that only repeats when the cycle is complete (a Great Year?).
- ***ratio (multitudinis)*** — rendered “reason (of multitude)”. nature, account, principle Bracket Latin per glossary.

## Translator's notes

- Unit: Dialogue I, Knot xiv, complete in one part. PDF pp. 119–123 = printed pp. 108–112, from 'Asphal. Dum tu ad Andabatam disputas' (top of p. 108, margin 'Nodus XIV. Idem individuum non posse naturaliter redire') to '…potestatem ullam faciet.' (line 2 of p. 112). Knot xv begins on p. 112 (PDF 123) with 'Ereu. Strenuete gessisti, Asphali', margin 'Nodus XV. Universum aliud futurum, si quodlibet singulare esset mutatum'. Offset +11 holds (p. 112 = PDF 123).
- Title: margin 'Idem individuum non posse naturaliter redire'; table of nodi (FM2) 'Idem individuum naturaliter non redire'. The margin title is used.
- Readings: (a) p. 108 'unam aliquam unam accidere' — 'unam' printed twice; possibly a misprint for 'unam aliquam vicem' {?vicem}; translated as printed ('some one one'). (b) p. 110 'indebantur' read as imperfect passive of indo ('were put in'), not a misprint. (c) p. 109 'effectus hic produxerit': 'hic' taken as adverb ('here') and 'effectus' as acc. pl.; subject of 'produxerit/fecerit' unexpressed, taken as the causes collectively ('[it]'). (d) p. 110 'quamvis et has mutatas quis negare possit?' is elliptical; construed as a concessive rhetorical question. (e) p. 110 'hae ipse' printed (ipse for ipsae); kept as printed. (f) p. 111 'subiiciatur' singular with neuter plural 'ea'; translated as plural. (g) p. 111 'multitunis' misprint for 'multitudinis', normalised in transcription. (h) p. 112 'resistant' (pres. subj.) rendered potential 'would resist'; subject of 'faciet' taken as the infinitude/infinity. (i) Speaker mark on p. 108 has a stray dot after 'Andabatam' — printer's mark, ignored.
- Verification pass (sentence-by-sentence against the Latin and 300 dpi crops of pp. 108, 110, 111): 2 corrections — (1) transcription 'seniorum' (p. 108, line-break 'senio-num') corrected to 'senionum' on the zoomed image; (2) 'ex eo quod semel effectus hic produxerit' first construed with 'effectus hic' as nominative ('this effect'), corrected to 'hic' adverb + 'effectus' acc. pl., since an effect cannot be the producer. Moods/tenses re-checked (videatur, reponat, fecerit, negaverim, eat, defuat, resistant all subjunctive; faciet future); comparatives (quo … eo), negatives (neque secundo, nihil mutatas, non diffiteberis) and 'plures' = 'more' confirmed. No omissions or additions found.
