## Dialogue III, Knot xii, part 1 (printed pp. 370–377)

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| [Margin, p. 370: Nodus XII. Iudicia Astrologica esse vana.] | [Margin, p. 370: Knot XII. That Astrological Judgements are vain.] |
| Andab. Non est tuum invehi, Ereuni, sed ratione pugnare. Illa litem cernet, si eam consules. Illam adi. | Andab. It is not yours to inveigh, Ereunius, but to fight with reason [ratione]. She will decide [cernet] the suit, if you consult her. Go to her. |
| Ereu. Adeo, & ante caetera ipsas stellas intuere, quas effectuum in terris causas esse docent tam inevitabiles: & oculi tibi testabuntur tot esse, ut comprehendi ab artificibus non possint. Unde longe maior earum pars negligitur. Centesima pars neque attingitur quidem. Reliquae itaque nihil mutabunt, vel manifeste vana est ars. | Ereu. I go to [her] [Adeo]; and before other [things] look at the stars themselves, which they teach to be such inevitable causes of effects on earth [in terris]: and [your] eyes will testify to you that there are so many, that they cannot be comprehended by the artificers [artificibus]. Whence by far the greater part of them is neglected. The hundredth part is not even touched. The rest therefore will change nothing, or the art is manifestly vain. |
| Rursus earum quae considerantur cursus, hactenus ignoti fuerunt, & tamen supposititiorum cursuum leges censentur valere. Nihilne Iupiter per quatuor suas invisas tot saeculis lunulas patietur? Nihil Saturnus a duabus? Nihil omnium caeterorum circa solem iter, quod circa tellurem existimabatur, in effectis immutabit? | Again, the courses of those which are considered have hitherto been unknown, and yet the laws of supposititious [supposititiorum] courses are reckoned to be valid. Will Jupiter suffer nothing through his four little moons [lunulas], unseen for so many ages? Saturn nothing from [his] two? Will the journey of all the others around the sun, which was thought [to be] around the earth [tellurem], change nothing in the effects [effectis]? |
| Deinde quanta mysteria in stationibus, retrogradationibus & similibus passionibus, quae deprehensae sunt nullae esse, latitare putabantur? Conditiones quoque causandi, quam vanae? quod in oppositione quadrata, vel sextili, vel coniunctione sunt. Quod ascendentes vel descendentes, quod adustae sint a radiis solaribus vel non? Quod in tali Zodiaci signo, quam domum earum vocant: quas omnes futiles esse & ad effectum nihil spectantes, quivis acri iudicio sine duce vel lumine videre potest. | Then how great mysteries were thought to lie hidden in stations, retrogradations and similar passions [passionibus], which have been detected to be none [nullae]? The conditions also of causing, how vain [are they]? that they are in quadrate opposition [oppositione quadrata], or sextile, or conjunction. That [they are] ascending or descending, that they are burnt [adustae] by the solar rays or not? That [they are] in such a sign of the Zodiac, which they call their house [domum]: all which [conditions] anyone of sharp judgement can see without a guide or light to be futile and looking to nothing as regards the effect. |
| Sed & virtutes quae eis tribuuntur vel leviter, vel ficte eis attribuuntur. Ut Marti quia ruber est, calor; frigus Saturno, quia pallidus; Veneri vero, Marti, Iovi, Mercurio & caeteris ex fictitiorum Deorum qualitatibus, pronos ad Generationem, Bellum, Eloquentiam, Imperium facere. | But also the virtues [virtutes] which are assigned to them are attributed to them either lightly or fictitiously. As to Mars, because he is red, heat; cold to Saturn, because [he is] pale; but to Venus, Mars, Jupiter [Iovi], Mercury and the rest, out of the qualities of feigned Gods, to make [men] prone to Generation, War, Eloquence, Empire [Imperium]. |
| Tandem qui quicquam vel minimum sapit, videt eas causas tantummodo communes esse posse; Communes autem causas unica actione omnes materias perfundere. Unde ergo effectus ex multis compositi iis attribui possunt? ut facere eloquentes, fortunatos, religiosos, Imperio dignos, &c. quae ex plurium qualitatum compositione integrantur? | Finally, he who has savour [sapit] of anything even the least, sees that those causes can be only common [causes]; but [that] common causes pour over all matters [materias] with a single action. Whence then can effects compounded out of many [things] be attributed to them? as to make [men] eloquent, fortunate, religious, worthy of Empire, &c., which are made whole [integrantur] out of the composition of several qualities? |
| Coniiciamus modo oculos ad effecta, & quaeramus an inde laetior sit iis spes aut fortuna, & obvium est e vestigio effecta quibus stellas donant, alias causas in inferioribus habere; in quas plene & per singulos gradus referantur: & earum rursus causarum alias causas, ut nusquam occasio sit, in ignotas quicquam referendi. Quas omnes inanes esse oportet, si stellae propriae sint eorum effectorum origines. | Let us but cast [our] eyes on the effects [effecta], and let us ask whether thence their hope or fortune is more joyful, and it is obvious straightway [e vestigio] that the effects with which they endow the stars have other causes among the inferior [things]; to which they are referred fully and through single degrees: and of those causes again other causes, so that nowhere is there occasion of referring anything to unknown [causes]. All which [causes] must be empty, if the stars are the proper origins of those effects. |
| Rursus clarum est effectus complures eius esse indolis, ut ab influxu quocumque circa infantem natum originem trahere non possint, nisi ex aliqua minima parte; ut ea quae a fortuna & extrinsecis accidentibus pendent. Unde & ipsi aliquando in ea respiciunt, ut aliquid propius coniicere possint, &c. Esto infans moriturus sit per ruinam tecti. Per quem motum in sanguinem matris vel corpus infantile potest effici, ut tectum collabatur? Quod tamen necessarium est, ut effectus consequatur. | Again it is clear that very many effects are of that disposition [indolis], that they cannot draw [their] origin from any influx [influxu] whatever about the infant [when] born, except from some least part; as those [things] which depend on fortune and extrinsic accidents. Whence they themselves too sometimes look to those [things], so that they may conjecture something more nearly, &c. Let it be [Esto] that the infant is going to die through the fall [ruinam] of a roof. Through what motion into the blood of the mother or the infantile body can it be effected that the roof collapse? Which [collapse] nevertheless is necessary, that the effect may follow. |
| Deinde effectus plerumque sunt tales ut determinatam causarum collectionem & ordinem requirant: Clarum autem est causam per se talis effectus non posse esse corpus aliquod mortuum; Sed ex necessitate referendum esse in corpus aliquod, vel causam intelligentem. Unde etiam hinc apparet hanc artem ex iis Idolomaniae insomniis ortum trahere, quae animata Astra, animabus ratione & intelligentia plenis, hoc est, divina quaedam numina constituebant, & sine his principiis esse mancam & absurdam. | Then effects for the most part are such that they require a determinate collection and order of causes: but it is clear that the cause per se [per se] of such an effect cannot be some dead body; but [that it is] of necessity to be referred to some body, or [rather] an intelligent cause. Whence also hence it appears that this art draws [its] origin from those dreams of Idolomania, which made [constituebant] the Stars animated, with souls full of reason and intelligence, that is, certain divine divinities [numina], and [that] without these principles it is maimed and absurd. |
| Attendatur etiam quod saepe effectus a pluribus hominibus dependet, quorum horoscopi sunt diversi, & stellarum virtutes non sociae neque contextae. Unde vanum est credere ex uno horoscopo possibile esse de toto effecto iudicare. Adde etiam pendere saepe ab hominibus natis in regionibus longinquissimis, ut secundum regulas Astronomicas, stellae quae inspiciuntur, nullam virtutem habere valeant circa instrumenta, quibus perficiendus est effectus. | Let it be attended to also that often an effect depends on several men, whose horoscopes are diverse, and the virtues of the stars not allied nor woven together [contextae]. Whence it is vain to believe that it is possible from one horoscope to judge of the whole effect [effecto]. Add also that it depends often on men born in most far-off regions, so that according to the Astronomical rules, the stars which are inspected can have no virtue about the instruments by which the effect is to be accomplished. |
| Tandem cum Astra communiter luceant in caetera animalia, cur nullos effectus in ea habent? Cur non impediunt ne plerumque porci, oves & boves omnes occidantur? Equi vero & asini nimia fatigatione pereant? Imo plerique homines naturali morte defunguntur, qui tamen diversissimis stellarum virtutibus subditi nascuntur. Cur mores omnium eiusdem patriae similes? diversarum regionum semper faciant diversos, cum eadem Zodiaci signa omnes supervolitent? | Finally, since the Stars shine in common upon the other animals, why have they no effects on them? Why do they not hinder that for the most part pigs, sheep and oxen are all slaughtered? but [vero] that horses and asses perish by too much fatigue? Nay, most men discharge [defunguntur] [their life] by a natural death, who nevertheless are born subject to most diverse virtues of the stars. Why [do they make] the manners of all of the same country similar? [and] always make [those] of diverse regions diverse, when the same signs of the Zodiac fly over all? |
| Si respondeant inferiores causas diversificare motum superiorum; tum tota ars inutilis est. Patet enim inferiorum tantam esse copiam, ut nihil intra aestimationis limites certum relinquant: Sed & tam varii tantique effectus quomodo a minima lucula, quam a stellis advenire videmus (sentire enim non possumus) proficisci credibile est? | If they should answer that the inferior causes diversify the motion of the superior [ones]; then the whole art is useless. For it is plain that there is so great a store of inferior [causes], that they leave nothing certain within the limits of estimation: but also how is it credible that such various and such great effects proceed from a very small little light [lucula], which we see to arrive from the stars (for we cannot feel [it])? |
| Et si ad qualitates absconditas recurras, hoc ipso te risui & ludibrio exponis, tum quod ad commune ignorantiae asylum te reducis, tum quod haec latebra omni dolo nequitiaeque obtendi potest: tum quod saltem esse tales, licet quales sint nescias, probandum est. Et haec quidem argumenta abstracte ab ipsis stellarum naturis evincunt ignorantiae & levitatis sectatores huius disciplinae. | And if you run back to hidden qualities [qualitates absconditas], by this very [thing] you expose yourself to laughter and mockery, both because you lead yourself back to the common asylum of ignorance, and because this lurking-place can be held out [as a screen, obtendi] for every guile and worthlessness [nequitiae]: and because at least that there are such [qualities], although you know not of what sort they are, must be proved. And these arguments indeed, abstractly from the very natures of the stars, convict [evincunt] the followers of this discipline of ignorance and levity. |
| Sed si accipiamus, quod heri disseruimus, omnino constare stellas ex nostris elementis conflatas esse, & vel esse ignes, vel igneam naturam aliunde aspersam ad nos remittere; neque alium effectum habere: quam ridenda & explodenda manet haec sententia, ultra eorum dementiam qui se Reges & Imperatores mente capti per vigiliam somniant? Et tamen quanta hominum copia, & quanta ingenia sub hac fatuitate, cum nominis Christiani dedecore laborant? Cum Ethnicorum prudentiores hoc hominum genus e rebuspublicis suis eliminaverint. | But if we accept, what we discoursed yesterday, that it is altogether established that the stars are blown together [conflatas] out of our elements, and either are fires, or send back to us a fiery nature sprinkled [on them] from elsewhere; and have no other effect: how [much] to be laughed at and hissed off [explodenda] does this opinion [sententia] remain, beyond the madness of those who, captive in mind [mente capti], dream through waking that they are Kings and Emperors? And yet how great a store of men, and how great wits [ingenia], labour under this fatuity, with the disgrace of the Christian name? Since the more prudent of the Heathens [Ethnicorum] have banished [eliminaverint] this kind of men from their commonwealths. |
| Asphal. Verum habent hi homines quod tuis argumentis reponant, & libris etiam diffusis hanc artem propugnaverunt, & propugnare sunt parati. | Asphal. But these men have [something] which they may reply [reponant] to your arguments, and in diffuse books too they have championed [propugnaverunt] this art, and are ready to champion [it]. |
| Ereu. Neque ego ad illos disputo, sed ad te & Andabatam, viros, ut credo, moderatiores; Novi enim nihil adeo rationi consentaneum esse, nihil tam demonstrative verum, ut eos convincere possit qui vel intelligere, vel cum veritatis desiderio audire nolunt: sed hoc unum festinant sive praeiudicio detenti, sive honoris alicuius vel lucri cupiditate praeventi, ut sustineant id cui semel sese dediderunt. | Ereu. Nor do I dispute toward those [men], but toward you and Andabata, men, as I believe, more moderate; for I have known that nothing is so consonant to reason, nothing so demonstratively true, that it could convince those who are unwilling either to understand, or to hear with desire of truth: but this one [thing] they hasten [to do], whether held back by prejudice [praeiudicio], or forestalled [praeventi] by desire of some honour or gain, that they may sustain that to which they have once given themselves up. |
| Qui nesciunt propria a communibus, necessaria a probabilibus separare (quorum non est modica hominum portio) nunquam convinci possunt, nisi primo voluntati eorum aliquae insidiae tendantur. Quae si semel captiva sit, intellectus postmodum facillime dirigitur. | Those who know not [how] to separate proper [things] from common, necessary from probable [probabilibus] (of whom the portion of men is not moderate) can never be convinced, unless first some snares be stretched for their will. Which if once it be captive, the intellect afterwards is most easily directed. |
| Andab. Ergo nullam virtutem Astris in haec inferiora concedis? | Andab. Then do you concede no virtue to the Stars upon these inferior [things]? |
| [Margin, p. 375: Astra quos effectus habeant.] | [Margin, p. 375: What effects the Stars have.] |
| Ereu. Primo Astra divido: Soli enim & Lunae vel maximos effectus concedo, sed hos tamen vel universales, quales sunt Aestatem & Ver & Hyemem & Autumnum constituere, aliquam mensis stationem humidiorem vel sicciorem efficere, & similia: vel si particulares poscis, ex his fluentes, ut in conceptione huius animalis aliqua primarum qualitatum magis abundet, alia deficiat & effectuum qui ex contemperatione sequuntur proportionalem quandam partem; non tamen tantam, ut iudicium de Physica animalis temperie ex his ferri possit. | Ereu. First I divide the Stars: for to the Sun and the Moon I concede even the greatest effects, but these nevertheless either universal, such as are to constitute Summer and Spring and Winter and Autumn, to make some station [stationem] of a month moister or drier, and similar [things]: or if you demand particular [effects], [then] flowing from these, as that in the conception of this animal some one of the first qualities should abound more, another be lacking, and a certain proportional part of the effects which follow from the tempering-together [contemperatione]; not however so great, that a judgement concerning the Physical temperament [temperie] of the animal could be passed [ferri] from these. |
| Cum parentum tum habitualis constitutio, tum accidentalis longe maiorem vim habeant; ut si plus solito biberint, ambulaverint, riserint, aut e contra torpuerint, sederint, abstemii fuerint, plus operatum sit quam quod hyeme, vel aestate, plenilunio, vel novilunio natus, vel conceptus sit infans. | Since the constitution of the parents, both the habitual and the accidental, have far greater force; so that if they have drunk, walked, laughed more than usual, or on the contrary have been torpid, sat, been abstemious, more has been wrought [operatum sit] than by [the fact] that the infant was born or conceived in winter or in summer, at full moon or at new moon. |
| Quoad reliquas vero stellas; quicquam quam minimum eas efficere mihi persuadeo, quod neque sensibile sit, neque aestimabile. Et plus interesse crediderim duae num sint an una tantum in cubiculo lucernae, quam in qua coniunctione sit Venus cum Iove, vel Mercurio: unde ex hoc nihili effecto velle tam grandia inducere, tale est, ac si quis ex eo quod habeat canem qui currere possit, velit inferre eum etiam aves volantes capere posse vel Balaenas in Oceano venari, vel aliud quidpiam magis inopinabile. | But as to the remaining stars; I persuade myself that they effect something as small as possible, which is neither sensible nor estimable. And I would believe that it makes more difference [interesse] whether there are two lamps in the bedchamber or only one, than in what conjunction Venus is with Jupiter [Iove], or [with] Mercury: whence to wish to bring in such great [things] from this effect of nothing [nihili effecto], is such [a thing] as if someone, from this, that he has a dog which can run, should wish to infer that it can also catch flying birds or hunt Whales in the Ocean, or some other [thing] more unthinkable [inopinabile]. |
| Andab. Negas itaque Medicorum & Chirurgorum disciplinam, tam accurate lunae motus ad morborum crises & phlebotomiae effectus notantium. | Andab. You deny therefore the discipline of Physicians and Surgeons, [who] note so accurately the motions of the moon with respect to the crises of diseases and the effects of phlebotomy. |

## Words and phrases with alternative or special meanings

- ***Illa litem cernet … Illam adi / Adeo*** — rendered “She will decide the suit … Go to her / I go to [her]”. adeo is here the verb adire (‘I approach’), not the adverb ‘so much’; litem cernere is the legal ‘decide a suit’. Andabata personifies ratio as a judge; Ereunius answers with the one-word legal formula, a play on adi/adeo.
- ***artifices*** — rendered “artificers”. Practitioners of an ars; here the astrologers. Ars = astrology as a craft; White repeatedly contrasts ars with scientia.
- ***supposititii cursus*** — rendered “supposititious courses”. Substituted, spurious, assumed (suppositicius) — not merely ‘supposed’ hypotheses. The astrologers’ tables of planetary courses are false (Ptolemaic) ones; cf. Galileo’s discoveries.
- ***quatuor lunulae (Iovis) / Saturnus a duabus*** — rendered “four little moons / Saturn from two”. lunula = little moon, satellite. Galileo’s Medicean stars (1610) and his ‘tricorporeal’ Saturn (the ring seen as two companions): new bodies unknown to astrology.
- ***circa solem iter*** — rendered “journey around the sun”.  Copernican heliocentrism granted as fact for the argument.
- ***stationes, retrogradationes, passiones*** — rendered “stations, retrogradations, passions”. passio = property, affection (Aristotelian proprium). Planetary stations and retrogradations, which the heliocentric system shows to be appearances only (‘nullae’).
- ***oppositio quadrata, sextilis, coniunctio*** — rendered “quadrate opposition, sextile, conjunction”. ‘quadrata’ is the square aspect (90°); White runs it with ‘oppositio’. Astrological aspects.
- ***adustae (a radiis solaribus)*** — rendered “burnt”. Combust: a planet too near the sun. Technical astrological term (combustio).
- ***domus*** — rendered “house”. Astrological domicile of a planet in a sign.
- ***sapere*** — rendered “have savour [sapit]”. ‘be wise’ / ‘taste of’. qui quicquam vel minimum sapit: idiom ‘who has any sense at all’.
- ***e vestigio*** — rendered “straightway”. Literally ‘from the footprint’ = immediately.
- ***indoles*** — rendered “disposition [indolis]”. nature, character. As in glossary (bracket to keep from dispositio).
- ***influxus*** — rendered “influx”. Astrological ‘influence’ (cf. influentia).
- ***Idolomania*** — rendered “Idolomania”. Mad idol-worship. Rare coinage; pagan star-worship (stars as animated numina) as the origin of astrology.
- ***numina*** — rendered “divinities”.  Glossary: numen = divinity.
- ***horoscopus*** — rendered “horoscope”. The ascendant degree / nativity chart.
- ***qualitates absconditae*** — rendered “hidden qualities”. = qualitates occultae. Scholastic ‘occult qualities’, the target of mechanist critics (Digby, White).
- ***asylum ignorantiae*** — rendered “asylum of ignorance”. sanctuary. Cf. Spinoza’s later asylum ignorantiae; here of occult qualities.
- ***obtendi*** — rendered “be held out [as a screen]”. obtendere = stretch before, use as pretext.
- ***evincere*** — rendered “convict [evincunt]”. prove, overcome. Forensic: prove someone guilty (cf. coarguere).
- ***conflatae*** — rendered “blown together [conflatas]”. forged, compounded. Stars made of terrestrial elements — the previous day’s (Dialogue I) argument.
- ***explodere*** — rendered “hiss off [explodenda]”. drive off the stage by clapping/hissing; reject. Classical theatrical sense, not ‘explode’.
- ***mente capti*** — rendered “captive in mind”. = insane.
- ***Ethnici / respublicae*** — rendered “Heathens / commonwealths”.  Expulsion of astrologers (Chaldaei, mathematici) from Rome, e.g. Tacitus, Ann. II.32, XII.52.
- ***propugnare*** — rendered “champion [propugnaverunt]”. fight in defence of.
- ***disputare ad*** — rendered “dispute toward”. address an argument to.
- ***probabilia*** — rendered “probable [things]”. approvable, plausible (dialectical), not statistical. Necessaria vs probabilia: demonstration vs dialectic.
- ***contemperatio*** — rendered “tempering-together [contemperatione]”.  Glossary (D1-N15): concurrence of causes.
- ***temperies*** — rendered “temperament [temperie]”. crasis of the four qualities. Galenic medicine.
- ***primae qualitates*** — rendered “first qualities”. hot, cold, moist, dry. Aristotelian elementary qualities.
- ***nihili effectum*** — rendered “effect of nothing”. worthless, negligible effect.
- ***inopinabilis*** — rendered “unthinkable [inopinabile]”. incredible, beyond expectation.
- ***crises morborum / phlebotomia*** — rendered “crises of diseases / phlebotomy”.  Galenic critical days tied to lunar phases; astrological bloodletting calendars.

## Translator's notes

- Unit: Dialogue III, Knot xii, part 1 of 2. PDF pp. 381–388 = printed pp. 370–377, from Andabata’s ‘Non est tuum invehi, Ereuni, sed ratione pugnare’ (p. 370, beside the margin ‘Nodus XII. Terram in usus hominum esse conditam’ stands beside Asphalius’ ‘Te latet itaque…’ (the table gives xiii at p. 382: confirmed). Offset +11 holds (running heads 370–377). Signatures Aaa ij (p. 371), Aaa iij (p. 373), Bbb (p. 377).
- Uncertain readings and constructions: (1) p. 373 ‘Sed ex necessitate referendum esse in corpus aliquod, vel causam intelligentem’ — clearly printed thus; after ‘non posse esse corpus aliquod mortuum’ one expects ‘corpus aliquod [animatum]’; taken as a correction (‘some body, or [rather] an intelligent cause’). (2) p. 371 ‘supposititiorum’ (= suppositiciorum, ‘supposititious’, i.e. substituted/spurious) — clear in the scan; the sense ‘assumed [hypothetical]’ is also possible. (3) p. 371 ‘quod in oppositione quadrata, vel sextili, vel coniunctione sunt’ — ‘quadrata’ stands where one expects a separate aspect (quadratura); rendered literally ‘quadrate opposition’. (4) p. 373–374 ‘Cur mores omnium eiusdem patriae similes? diversarum regionum semper faciant diversos’ — elliptical; the verb ‘faciant’ is supplied in the first clause. (5) p. 374 ‘quam a stellis advenire videmus’ — quam taken as the relative (agreeing with lucula), not ‘than’. No illegible readings.
- Verification pass: the Latin was re-read against the page images (p. 371 and p. 373 doubtful lines zoomed at 300 dpi), then each Latin sentence against the English for omissions, additions, case, tense, mood, agreement, negatives and comparatives. The check produced 5 corrections: (i) p. 371 the draft transcription ‘suppositiorum’ corrected to the printed ‘supposititiorum’; (ii) p. 374 ‘Si respondeant’ — subjunctive, set to ‘If they should answer’ (first drafted ‘If they answer’); (iii) p. 376 ‘plus operatum sit quam quod … natus … sit’ — rendered ‘than by [the fact] that’ (first drafted ‘than that’), so the comparison is between causes; (iv) p. 376 ‘crediderim’ — potential perfect subjunctive, ‘I would believe’ (first drafted ‘I believe’); (v) p. 373 ‘Equi vero & asini’ — vero first left untranslated, now ‘but [vero]’. Negatives (‘neque attingitur quidem’, ‘non sociae neque contextae’, ‘neque sensibile … neque aestimabile’, ‘nihil … nisi’, ‘nunquam … nisi’) and comparatives (‘longe maior’, ‘laetior’, ‘propius’, ‘moderatiores’, ‘plus solito’, ‘plus … quam’, ‘magis inopinabile’) were checked and found correctly rendered.
- Conventions: as in previous units (see glossary.md). ‘rebuspub.’ expanded to rebuspublicis. Long speeches split at sense-breaks (continuation rows carry no speaker head). virtus = virtue (operative power); Astra (capitalised as printed) = Stars; effectum/effecta = effect(s) [effecta] where printed in the neuter.
