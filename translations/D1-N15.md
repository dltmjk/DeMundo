# De Mundo — Dialogue I (De materia mundi), Knot xv

*Latin title:* Nodus XV. Universum aliud futurum, si quodlibet singulare esset mutatum (table: Universum aliud fore ad mutationem cuiuslibet individui)  
*English title:* Knot XV. That the universe would be other [aliud futurum], if any singular whatever were changed (table: That the universe will be other upon the change of any individual whatever)  
*Pages:* PDF pp. 123–127 = printed pp. 112–116 (from 'Ereu. Strenue te gessisti, Asphali' on p. 112 to '…flabrorum fragilitati subiicere.' on p. 116)

## Dialogue I, Knot xv (printed pp. 112–116)

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| [p. 112] [Margin: Nodus XV. Universum aliud futurum, si quodlibet singulare esset mutatum.] Ereu. Strenue te gessisti, Asphali, iam enim te devoraverat Andabata. | [p. 112] [Margin: Knot XV. That the universe would be other [aliud futurum], if any singular whatever were changed.] Ereu. Vigorously have you borne yourself, Asphalius; for already Andabata had devoured you. |
| Sed ut vicem tibi reddam, speculatio tua ad magna quaedam nos ducere videtur. | But that I may return to you the turn [vicem], your speculation seems to lead us to certain great things. |
| Apparet enim mihi quasi per sublustria, alliciens quidam splendor, insolitum aliquid de se promittens; sequamur itaque si libet: | For there appears to me, as if through dim-lit [places] [sublustria], a certain enticing splendour, promising something unwonted of itself; let us follow, therefore, if it pleases: |
| Effectum ex infinita indeterminatione, per praesentes causas elicuisti, sed omnes; harum itaque si quaelibet absit, non erit hic effectus | the effect, out of an infinite indetermination [indeterminatione], you have elicited through the present causes, but [through] all [of them]; of these, therefore, if any whatever be absent, this effect will not be. |
| Asphal. Sic est. | Asphal. So it is. |
| Ereu. Sed et haec causa ex infinitis, eadem omnium suarum causarum contemperatione nata est; quare si una quaepiam etiam inde desit, neque haec causa neque prior effectus futura sint. | Ereu. But this cause too, out of infinite [things], has been born by the same tempering-together [contemperatione] of all its causes; wherefore if some one [una quaepiam] also be lacking thence, neither this cause nor the prior effect would be [futura sint]. |
| Et si eodem instituto progrediamur, nullus erit finis, donec ad causas primas, i. prima corpora (si quae a Deo immediate sint condita) filum hoc Ariadnaeum productum sit. | And if we proceed by the same purpose [instituto], there will be no end, until to the first causes, i.e. the first bodies (if any were founded [sint condita] by God immediately), this Ariadnaean thread shall have been drawn out [productum sit]. |
| Asphal. Sic arbitror. | Asphal. So I judge. |
| Andab. At non ego. Quid enim vetat aliam causam eandem vim habere et cum caeteris causis conspirantem hos effectus educere in lucem. | Andab. But not I. For what forbids another cause to have the same force [vim] and, conspiring with the other causes, to lead out these effects into light. |
| Ereu. Quia eandem virtutem in alio ente reperiri non est pote. | Ereu. Because the same virtue [virtutem] cannot [non est pote] be found in another being [ente]. |
| [p. 113] Hoc enim ipso quod virtus quaelibet suo subiecto propria est, iam aliquo distat a virtute quae in alio est, et cum numeralis unitas omnium quae insunt, complicatione conficiatur, quaelibet diversitas numerum immutat. | [p. 113] For by this very [fact] that any virtue whatever is proper to its own subject, already it is distant [distat] by something [aliquo] from the virtue which is in another; and since numerical unity is made up [conficiatur] by the complication [complicatione] of all [the things] which are in [it] [insunt], any diversity whatever changes the number. |
| Unde et in effectum eodem iure refunditur. | Whence also it is poured back [refunditur] into the effect by the same right [iure]. |
| Sed si quae corpora immediate a Deo producta sint, dubitare non possumus, quin ita sint producta, ut contigua sibi conveniant, et eiusmodi sint qualia aequum est contigua esse, ut contiguorum operibus suam opellam conferant: | But if any bodies were produced immediately by God, we cannot doubt but that [quin] they were so produced that, [being] contiguous, they agree [conveniant] with each other [sibi], and are of such a kind as it is fair [aequum] that contiguous [things] be, so that they contribute their own little work [opellam] to the works of the contiguous [things]: |
| nec enim in corporibus fas est existimare unum contiguorum integro se ad operationem sic accingi, ut contiguo neque inferat, neque ab ipso quicquam patiatur, et nihil a contiguo in effectum redundet. | for neither in bodies is it right [fas] to esteem that one of the contiguous [things], itself [remaining] entire [integro se], girds itself for operation in such a way that it neither inflicts [inferat] [anything] on the contiguous, nor suffers anything from it, and [that] nothing overflows [redundet] from the contiguous into the effect. |
| Hoc igitur ulterius habetur, contiguorum si quod aliud sit, in effectis varietatem facere. | This therefore is had further: [that], if any [one] of the contiguous [things] be other, [it] makes a variety in the effects. |
| Contiguorum modo rationem ad sphaeram quae de universo nihil demat, pertinere (cum ipsum ex contiguis constet) nihil attinet deducere. | That the account [rationem] of contiguous [things] merely [modo] reaches [pertinere] to a sphere which takes away nothing from the universe (since it [ipsum, the universe] consists of contiguous [things]), it is nothing to the purpose [nihil attinet] to deduce. |
| Habemus ergo nullum effectum tam exiguum in rebus esse, non volitationem plumae, non pulvisculi divisionem, quin ab universae huius corporeae machinae singulis corporibus dependeat, tanquam vel a causis, vel a consequentibus: et ea pariter omnia ab ipso, sic ut ipso non existente omnia et singula alia esse, et mutata oporteret. | We have, therefore, [that] there is no effect in things so slight — not the flitting [volitationem] of a feather, not the division of a little dust [pulvisculi] — but [quin] it depends on the single bodies of this whole [universae] corporeal machine, as either on causes or on consequents: and all those [things] equally on it [ab ipso], so that, it not existing, it would behove [oporteret] all and each [thing] to be other, and changed. |
| Et quod ex his consequitur nullum esse adeo [p. 114] exiguum corpusculum, ex perfecta cuius comprehensione non in omnes omnium naturalium causarum effectus et Entium comprehensionem non possit transiri. | And [there is] what follows from these: [that] there is no little body [corpusculum] so [p. 114] slight, out of the perfect comprehension of which there could not [non … non possit] be a passing-over [transiri] into all the effects of all natural causes and [into] the comprehension of Beings [Entium]. |
| Scimus enim necessitatem esse illum pontem, quo ignorantiae volumina superat scientia. | For we know necessity to be that bridge by which science surmounts the rolling floods [volumina] of ignorance. |
| Videtis igitur quo nos ducit speculatio haec tua, ad stupendam totius mundi unitatem, et connexionem, et in admirandum et inopinatum scientiae campum si, necessariis insistere curemus, omissa inani illa gloria multa insipienter dicendi. | You [pl.] see therefore whither this speculation of yours leads us: to a stupendous unity of the whole world, and connexion, and into an admirable and unexpected field of science, if we take care [curemus] to stand upon [insistere] necessary [things], that vain glory of saying many [things] unwisely being omitted. |
| Neque dubitemus si assuetudine hac semel facilitatem nobis comparaverimus dulcissimo et iucundissimo nos in aeternum usuros sapientiae contubernio. | Nor let us doubt [that], if by this accustoming [assuetudine] we shall once have procured facility for ourselves, we shall enjoy [usuros] for ever the sweetest and most pleasant companionship [contubernio] of wisdom. |
| Asphal. Videor et alia via ad idem pervenire posse, si tamen alia est: adverto enim causam aliquam si mutata est, quantum effectorum variorum secum syrma trahat. | Asphal. I seem to be able to arrive at the same [thing] by another way too, if yet it is another: for I notice, if some cause has been changed, how great a train [syrma] of various effects it drags with itself. |
| Unde in singulis causis quas mutari oportebit novas effectorum in infinitum prosapias video proseminari, unde rursus surgit nihil unum posse mutari absque immensa aliorum immutatione, quae (ut credo) ad fontem quo prius multis sit e vivis derivanda. | Whence in the single causes which it will behove to be changed I see new lineages [prosapias] of effects sown forth [proseminari] to infinity; whence again arises [this]: [that] nothing one can be changed without an immense changing [immutatione] of others; which (as I believe) is to be channelled [sit derivanda] out of many living [springs] [multis … e vivis] to the fountain whither [quo] [we came] earlier. |
| Sed indulgete mihi, si hinc quoque aliquid expiscer ad priorem meam propositionem declarandam. | But indulge [pl.] me, if from here too I fish out [expiscer] something for declaring my prior proposition. |
| Nostis enim quam blandi et arridentes nobis sint proprii partus, sic ergo accipite; postquam [p. 115] prima corpora constituta sunt, video plura ex iis mixtione composita; Et ex his rursus alia et alia, nusquam autem video unde in eadem quae primo fuerunt corpora, regressus fiat; | For you know how flattering [blandi] and smiling upon us are our own offspring [partus]; thus, therefore, receive [it]: after [p. 115] the first bodies were constituted, I see more [bodies] [plura] composed out of them by mixtion; and out of these again other and other [ones]; but nowhere do I see whence a regress may come about [fiat] into the same bodies which were at first; |
| mixtione enim hoc fieri non contingit: separationem autem horum mixtorum in elementa facere nullam video potentiam; necesse est ergo nunquam redeant. | for it does not happen [contingit] that this come about by mixtion: but I see no power [potentiam] to make a separation of these mixts into elements; it is necessary therefore [that] they never return. |
| Sed si non redeant, neque aliquod individuum fas est redire, cum non nisi ex iisdem causis confici possit. Nihil itaque horum exspectandum est. | But if they do not return, neither is it right [fas] [that] any individual return, since it cannot be made up except out of the same causes. Nothing therefore of these [things] is to be expected. |
| Ereu. Euge vero; exemisti nobis Platonici anni metum, et tumultuum horum quos in corpore sentimus, aeternam pacem confecisti. | Ereu. Bravo indeed! You have taken away from us the fear of the Platonic year, and of these tumults which we feel in the body you have made [confecisti] an eternal peace. |
| Andab. Pacem vero egregiam attulistis qui ipsum pacis tabernaculum sustulistis: si enim mus micam arroserit praeter fixas a vobis tabulas, ipsum Coelum Empyreum, et flammantia moenia mundi subito sunt ruitura. | Andab. A peace indeed excellent you [pl.] have brought, [you] who have taken away the very tabernacle of peace: for if a mouse shall have gnawed at [arroserit] a crumb beyond the tables fixed [fixas … tabulas] by you, the very Empyrean Heaven and the flaming walls of the world are suddenly about to fall in ruin [ruitura]. |
| Asphal. Murem igitur illum adamantinis catenis ligabimus, ne quid praeter ea quae natura apposuerit, corrodat. | Asphal. That mouse therefore we will bind with adamantine chains, lest it gnaw away [corrodat] anything beyond those [things] which nature shall have set before [it] [apposuerit]. |
| Coelum autem Empyreum Theologis dimittemus, qui si opus sit, Angelicas custodias adhibebunt, et infusas qualitates quibus ab hoc malo muniatur: | But the Empyrean Heaven we will leave [dimittemus] to the Theologians, who, if there be need, will apply Angelic guards, and infused qualities by which it may be fortified against this evil: |
| mundi vero flammantia moenia, quoniam [p. 116] omnium Entium defectibilissimus est ignis, perpetuoque suggesti alimenti indigus, reliquae naturae fata subire non aegre est ferendum. | but [as for] the flaming walls of the world, since [p. 116] fire is the most defectible [defectibilissimus] of all Beings, and needy [indigus] of perpetually supplied nourishment, [that they] undergo the fates of the rest of nature is not to be borne ill [aegre]. |
| Andab. Nescio quomodo voluptuarius ille Poeta mihi flammam pro aheno suggessit, debuissem enim ahena vel adamantina, vel si quid fortius est, indigitavisse. | Andab. I know not how that pleasure-loving [voluptuarius] Poet suggested to me 'flame' instead of 'brazen' [pro aheno]; for I ought to have named [indigitavisse] [them] brazen or adamantine, or, if anything is stronger, [that]. |
| Postquam ad hoc posita sunt, ut tantam tam diversorum existentium, et molem, et numerum cohibeant inque ordinem redigant, cuius soliditatis corpus mirum est quomodo velitis flammae et flabrorum fragilitati subiicere. | Since [Postquam] they have been placed for this, that they should restrain both the so great bulk and the number of so diverse existents and bring [them] back into order, a body of which solidity [cuius soliditatis] — it is wonderful how you [pl.] are willing to subject [it] to the fragility of flame and of blasts [flabrorum]. |

## Words and phrases with alternative or special meanings

- ***Universum aliud futurum*** — rendered “the universe would be other”. futurum (fut. inf., esse omitted) with past-contrary 'esset mutatum'; the FM2 table has 'Universum aliud fore ad mutationem cuiuslibet individui' The knot's thesis of universal interconnexion: change any singular and the whole universe is numerically other.
- ***indeterminatio*** — rendered “indetermination”. indefiniteness; the unbounded range of possible outcomes Picks up the dice/combination argument of Knot xiv: an effect is 'elicited' from infinite possibility by the concurrence of all present causes.
- ***contemperatio*** — rendered “tempering-together”. blending, adjustment; mixture in due proportion Medical/physical term for proportioned mixture; used of causes concurring.
- ***filum Ariadnaeum*** — rendered “Ariadnaean thread”. clue, guiding thread Classical allusion (Theseus in the Labyrinth): the chain of causes traced back to the first bodies.
- ***virtus*** — rendered “virtue”. power, force, active quality Scholastic 'virtus' = operative power inhering in a subject; distinct from vis = force.
- ***non est pote*** — rendered “cannot [non est pote]”. archaic 'pote est' = potest (Plautine, Lucretian) Archaism; rendered by sense.
- ***complicatio / numeralis unitas*** — rendered “complication / numerical unity”. folding-together; one-in-number Cusan/scholastic: numerical identity results from the folding-together of everything in the thing, so any difference changes the number (individual identity).
- ***refundere*** — rendered “pour back”. redound, transfer The diversity of causes is 'poured back' into the effect.
- ***contiguus*** — rendered “contiguous”. touching, adjacent Aristotelian contiguity (Phys. V.3); the universe as a plenum of contiguous bodies each acting on its neighbour.
- ***integro se*** — rendered “itself [remaining] entire”. with its whole self; undiminished Ablative absolute-like phrase: a body cannot act without being acted upon (action-reaction in Aristotelian physics, De gen. I.7).
- ***nihil attinet*** — rendered “it is nothing to the purpose”. it does not matter / there is no need Introduces a step White thinks needless to prove.
- ***machina (corporea)*** — rendered “machine”. fabric, structure Mechanical image of the world (machina mundi, Lucretius V.96), congenial to Digbean corpuscular physics.
- ***corpusculum*** — rendered “little body”. corpuscle Corpuscular vocabulary of White and Digby.
- ***Ens / Entia*** — rendered “Being / Beings”. things that are Kept capitalised as printed.
- ***volumina (ignorantiae)*** — rendered “rolling floods”. rolls, coils, volumes (books) Image of a river crossed by the bridge of necessity; a pun on 'volumes' of books of ignorance is possible.
- ***contubernium*** — rendered “companionship”. sharing of a tent; cohabitation Military/domestic term for intimate living-together, here with wisdom.
- ***syrma*** — rendered “train”. trailing robe (tragic costume); a comet's train Greek σύρμα; glossary already 'Syrma = Train' (D1-N09).
- ***prosapia*** — rendered “lineage”. stock, race Archaic (Sallust, Cicero notes it as old-fashioned).
- ***proseminare*** — rendered “sow forth”. propagate Agricultural image.
- ***derivare (ad fontem … e vivis)*** — rendered “channel”. draw off water; derive Hydraulic image: many living springs lead to the same fountain — i.e. Asphalius' route reaches the earlier conclusion.
- ***expiscari*** — rendered “fish out”. search out Glossary term (D1-N09 p2).
- ***partus (proprii)*** — rendered “own offspring”. births; productions of the mind Proverbial: authors love their own brain-children.
- ***annus Platonicus*** — rendered “Platonic year”. Great Year Plato, Timaeus 39d: when all heavenly bodies return to the same configuration; linked to the doctrine of eternal recurrence, which Knots xiv–xv deny.
- ***tabernaculum (pacis)*** — rendered “tabernacle”. tent, dwelling Andabata's quip: the 'peace' destroys its own dwelling (the stable world).
- ***fixae tabulae*** — rendered “tables fixed”. laws posted up (tabulae = law-tablets, e.g. Twelve Tables); also astronomical tables Ironic: the interlocutors' rigid 'laws' of connexion.
- ***Coelum Empyreum*** — rendered “Empyrean Heaven”. the immobile heaven of the blessed Theological, not astronomical; hence left 'to the Theologians'.
- ***flammantia moenia mundi*** — rendered “the flaming walls of the world”. fiery ramparts Lucretius, De rerum natura I.73 ('extra / processit longe flammantia moenia mundi'); the 'pleasure-loving Poet' (voluptuarius, an Epicurean) is Lucretius.
- ***qualitates infusae*** — rendered “infused qualities”. supernaturally given qualities Scholastic theology (infused habits/qualities vs acquired).
- ***defectibilis*** — rendered “defectible”. liable to fail or perish Scholastic Latin; fire needs constant fuel (Aristotle, De gen. II.8).
- ***indigitare*** — rendered “name”. invoke by name, call Glossary 'indigitatio' = name-invocation (D1-N09 p2); here plainly 'to name'.
- ***ahenus*** — rendered “brazen”. of bronze Horace's 'murus aeneus' (Ep. I.1.60); Andabata would rather have bronze or adamantine walls.
- ***flabra*** — rendered “blasts”. gusts of wind Poetic (Lucretius, Virgil).

## Translator's notes

- Unit: Dialogue I, Knot xv, complete in one part. PDF pp. 123–127 = printed pp. 112–116, from 'Ereu. Strenue te gessisti, Asphali' (p. 112, line 3; margin 'Nodus XV. Universum aliud futurum, si quodlibet singulare esset mutatum') to '…flabrorum fragilitati subiicere.' (p. 116, mid-page). Knot xvi begins on p. 116 (PDF 127) with 'Ereu. Nequaquam ego, Andabata', margin 'Nodus XVI. Mundo non esse opus corpore caetera continente'. Offset +11 holds (p. 116 = PDF 127).
- Title: margin 'Universum aliud futurum, si quodlibet singulare esset mutatum'; the table of nodi (FM2) gives 'Universum aliud fore ad mutationem cuiuslibet individui'. The margin title is used.
- Readings: (a) p. 112 'Strenuète' printed as one word = 'Strenue te'. (b) p. 112 'hic effectus' lacks a final stop; supplied. (c) p. 112 Andabata's 'Quid enim vetat …' is a question printed with a full stop; translated as printed. (d) p. 114 'non in omnes … non possit transiri': 'non' appears twice after 'nullum'; the evident sense requires a single negation (no body so small that one could not pass from it to all effects), so the first 'non' is taken as pleonastic {?non}; a strict reading with three negations would invert the sense. (e) p. 113 'Contiguorum modo rationem … pertinere … nihil attinet deducere' is compressed; 'modo' read as 'merely' (alternatively 'now'). (f) p. 114 'quae … ad fontem quo prius multis sit e vivis derivanda': construed 'multis … e vivis' = 'from many living [springs]' and 'quo prius' = 'whither [we came] earlier'; uncertain. (g) p. 116 'cuius soliditatis corpus' is an anacoluthon (relative of quality with no main verb of its own); rendered literally with a dash. (h) p. 116 'indigitauisse' has a stray mark between g and t on the scan; reading certain.
- Verification pass (sentence-by-sentence against the Latin and 300 dpi crops of pp. 112, 113, 116): 3 corrections — (1) 'donec … productum sit' first rendered 'has been drawn out', corrected to future-perfect sense 'shall have been drawn out' (perfect subjunctive in a donec clause of expectation); (2) 'integro se' first rendered 'with its whole self', corrected to 'itself [remaining] entire' (the point is that it acts without being affected); (3) plural 'you [pl.]' marked for attulistis/sustulistis/velitis/videtis/indulgete, where the address shifts from Andabata/Ereunius to the group. Moods re-checked (absit, desit, futura sint, progrediamur, conficiatur, dependeat, oporteret, possit, curemus, dubitemus, fiat, redeant, muniatur all subjunctive; superat, refunditur indicative); comparatives/superlatives (defectibilissimus, dulcissimo, iucundissimo, fortius) and negatives (non est pote, nullus, nihil, non nisi, non aegre, Nequaquam) confirmed. No omissions or additions found.
- Conventions as in glossary.md: long s normalised; ę/æ → ae; '&' → et; consonantal u → v; supplied words in [ ]; Latin of key words in [ ] after the English; [p. n] marks printed page starts.
