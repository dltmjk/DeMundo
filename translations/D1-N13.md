## Dialogue I, Knot xiii (printed pp. 106–107)

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| [p. 106] [Margin: Nodus XIII. Singulis magnis solidis suum liquidum esse.] [Ereu., continuing] Et quod in terra usuvenit, idem in globis illis coelestibus, si telluri pares sunt, ut diximus, expectandum est. | [p. 106] [Margin: Knot XIII. That to each great solid [body] there is its own liquid.] [Ereu., continuing] And what happens [usuvenit] in the earth [terra], the same is to be expected in those celestial globes, if they are equal [pares] to the earth [telluri], as we said. |
| Nusquam itaque aer nisi quo vapores ex aliquo globo non pertingunt, securus et impollutus habitabit. | Nowhere therefore will air, except where [quo] vapours from some globe do not reach, dwell secure and unpolluted. |
| Verum num et illic solis radios effugiet? at illi si flamma sunt, sicci quoque et calidi et aeris amantes. | But will it escape the rays of the sun even there? but those [rays], if they are flame, [are] also dry and hot and lovers of air. |
| His itaque miscebitur, per hos mutabitur, et sua puritate excidet. | With these therefore it will be mixed, through these it will be changed, and it will fall away [excidet] from its purity. |
| Sed fac manere incorruptum, cui usui in tam dissitis interstitiis est? quibus viventibus famulatur? | But suppose [fac] [it] to remain incorrupt: for what use is it in interstices [interstitiis] so set apart [dissitis]? what living [things] does it serve [famulatur]? |
| Rursus putasne ex no- [p. 107] stris corporibus si caussae propriae assint, confici posse talem aerem necne? | Again, do you think that out of our [p. 107] bodies, if [their] proper causes should be present [assint], such air can be made up [confici] or not? |
| si negas, iram libelli toties nominati extimesce, qui unam esse quantitatem, et quodcumque ex corpore fieri potest, ex quolibet fieri posse coarguit, sin ais: vide num non eo ipso aliquod ex nostris sit? | If you deny [it], dread [extimesce] the wrath of the little book [libelli] so often named, which proves [coarguit] that quantity is one, and that whatever can be made out of [a] body can be made out of any [body] whatever; but if you affirm [it]: see whether it is not by that very [fact] something out of ours? |
| Certe cuicumque famulatui obstringatur, ex nostris elementis, eundem subministrare potuisset natura. | Certainly, to whatever service [famulatui] it may be bound, out of our elements nature could have supplied the same. |
| Cur itaque alienum et cuius caussae nullae in natura sint, introduxit? | Why therefore did she introduce [something] foreign, and of which there are no causes in nature? |
| non iam vides naturae ipsi hoc repugnare, ut non suis causis res agat quas posset suis? naturam ceterorum dissimilem, destructioni subiectam, reparatui impossibilem inter repugnantia collocaverit? | Do you not now see that this is repugnant [repugnare] to nature herself, that she should do things not by her own causes which she could [do] by her own? [that] she should have placed a nature unlike [those] of the rest, subject to destruction, impossible for repair [reparatui], among repugnant [things] [repugnantia]? |
| Nullum itaque talem credito, Andabata. | Therefore believe [credito] [there is] none such, Andabata. |
| Sed expressam {?expressum}, ex terra et a sole decoctum, et subtiliorem redditum humorem omnia quae circa terram sunt, occupavisse, usque dum haec effluvia proximorum ex aliis globis advenarum incursu retundantur: | But [believe] that the humour [humorem] squeezed out [expressam] from the earth [terra], and boiled down [decoctum] by the sun, and rendered subtler, has occupied all [things] which are around the earth [terram], until these effluvia are blunted [retundantur] by the onrush [incursu] of the nearest newcomers [advenarum] from other globes; |
| gradus subtilitatis pro caussarum eos inferentium inaequalitate sortiri: | [that they] obtain [sortiri] degrees of subtlety according to the inequality of the causes bringing them in [inferentium]; |
| Misceri fortassis in his intervallis duorum corporum spiramenta: | that perhaps in these intervals the breathings-out [spiramenta] of two bodies are mixed; |
| nonnunquam tam copiosa esse unius corporis recrementa, ut aliud intra suos fines contineat, quandoque aliter accidere. | that sometimes the refuse [recrementa] of one body is so copious that it contains the other within its own bounds [fines]; sometimes [that it] happens otherwise. |
| Horum legem non aliam esse, quam corporum ipsorum molem, et qualitatem quae in singulis variatur. | That the law of these [things] is no other than the bulk [molem] of the bodies themselves, and the quality which is varied in each [singulis]. |

## Words and phrases with alternative or special meanings

- ***solidum / liquidum (margin); solidum corpus / fluidum (table)*** — rendered “solid / liquid; solid body / fluid”. The margin title 'Singulis magnis solidis suum liquidum esse' differs from the table of nodi 'Corporibus solidis sua esse fluida'. Both mean that each great body (globe) is surrounded by its own fluid envelope (air/aether), a Digbean-Gilbertian picture of planetary 'atmospheres' (Gilbert's orbis virtutis, Kepler) against a single uniform celestial aether.
- ***usuvenit (usu venire)*** — rendered “happens”. comes about in practice, by experience Printed as one word; cf. glossary 'usu venire'.
- ***pares (telluri)*** — rendered “equal”. like, of the same kind, matching in size Refers back to the argument (Knot x–xi) that the planets are earths like ours.
- ***quo (nisi quo)*** — rendered “where [quo]”. literally 'whither' Adverb of motion-towards, natural with 'pertingunt'.
- ***flamma (radii solis)*** — rendered “flame”. fire Aristotelian fire is hot and dry; hence the rays 'love' air (fire's neighbouring element) and mix with it.
- ***excidere (sua puritate)*** — rendered “fall away from”. lose, forfeit
- ***dissitus / interstitia*** — rendered “set apart / interstices”. far-separated / intervals, spaces between The vast spaces between the celestial globes.
- ***famulari / famulatus*** — rendered “serve / service”. minister to / servitude Teleological argument: a body must serve some living thing (final cause).
- ***libellus toties nominatus*** — rendered “the little book so often named”. Digby's treatise Kenelm Digby's work (published 1644 as Two Treatises), cited in the dialogues as 'libellus'; its doctrine that all body is one quantity, differing by rare and dense, so that anything can be made from anything.
- ***unam esse quantitatem*** — rendered “that quantity is one”. that body/matter is homogeneous Digbean thesis: quantity (body) is of a single kind; qualities come from degrees of rarity and density.
- ***coarguere*** — rendered “prove”. convict, demonstrate against Forensic verb: the book 'convicts' the opponent; rendered 'proves' in the sense of conclusive demonstration.
- ***confici*** — rendered “be made up”. be produced, be compounded
- ***credito*** — rendered “believe”. future imperative ('you shall believe') Formal, legal-sounding imperative; it governs the following accusative-and-infinitive clauses (occupavisse, sortiri, misceri, esse, accidere, esse).
- ***humor (expressus, decoctus)*** — rendered “humour”. moisture, fluid, vapour Air as the earth's exhaled moisture, refined ('boiled down', concoction) by the sun: cf. Knot xii, 'Aerem esse telluris effluvium'.
- ***advenae*** — rendered “newcomers”. strangers, immigrants The effluvia arriving from neighbouring globes, personified.
- ***retundere*** — rendered “blunt”. beat back, repel, check
- ***spiramenta*** — rendered “breathings-out”. exhalations, vents Kept distinct from effluvia (effluvium) and vapores.
- ***recrementa*** — rendered “refuse”. dross, excrement, residue Medical/alchemical term for what a body throws off.
- ***moles*** — rendered “bulk”. mass Size of the globe as one factor of the 'law' of these atmospheres.
- ***repugnare / repugnantia*** — rendered “be repugnant / repugnant [things]”. be contrary, conflict / contraries Nature would act against herself if she set an incorruptible body among corruptible, mutually opposed (repugnant) elements.
- ***reparatus*** — rendered “repair”. restoration, renewal A body that can be destroyed but not regenerated would break the cycle of generation.

## Translator's notes

- Unit: Dialogue I, Knot xiii, complete in one part. PDF pp. 117–118 = printed pp. 106–107, from 'Et quod in terra usuvenit' (p. 106, Ereunius' speech continuing, beside the margin 'Nodus XIII') to '…quae in singulis variatur.' (end of p. 107). Knot xiv begins at the top of p. 108 (PDF 119) with 'Asphal. Dum tu ad Andabatam disputas', margin 'Nodus XIV. Idem individuum non posse naturaliter redire'. Offset +11 holds (p. 107 = PDF 118). The knot is thus two pages, not three as the table's page numbers (106, 108) suggest.
- Title: the margin reads 'Singulis magnis solidis suum liquidum esse' ('that to each great solid [body] there is its own liquid'); the table of nodi (FM2) reads 'Corporibus solidis sua esse fluida'. The margin title is used here.
- Readings: (a) p. 107 'expressam' printed (fem.), but it must agree with 'humorem' (masc.) like 'decoctum' and 'redditum': taken as a misprint for 'expressum' {?expressum}; translation unaffected. (b) 'causis' (single s) printed beside 'caussae/caussarum' in the same passage; kept as printed. (c) 'naturam … collocaverit?' is construed as a second question loosely dependent on 'hoc repugnare, ut…' (understood 'ut'); it might also be an independent rhetorical question ('would she have placed…?'). (d) 'eos inferentium': 'eos' (masc.) has no expressed masculine antecedent other than 'gradus'; taken as 'the causes bringing them [the degrees / the effluvia] in'. (e) The subject of 'sortiri' is unexpressed — taken as the effluvia/humours. (f) 'aliud intra suos fines contineat': subject taken as 'the one body' (singular verb after neuter plural 'recrementa').
- Verification pass (sentence-by-sentence against the Latin and 300 dpi crops of pp. 106–107): 3 corrections — (1) 'si caussae propriae assint' first rendered 'if proper causes are present', corrected to subjunctive 'should be present'; (2) 'potuisset' corrected from 'could supply' to pluperfect subjunctive 'could have supplied'; (3) 'introduxit' kept as perfect indicative 'did she introduce' (first draft had 'would she introduce'). No omissions or additions found; transcription confirmed (incl. 'extimesce', 'coarguit', 'reparatui', 'retundantur', 'recrementa').
- Conventions as in glossary.md: long s normalised; ę/æ → ae; '&' → et; consonantal u → v; supplied words in [ ]; Latin of key words in [ ] after the English; [p. n] marks printed page starts.
