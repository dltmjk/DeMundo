# De Mundo — Dialogue II (De forma mundi, seu de motu magnorum corporum), Knot xi

*Latin title:* Nodus XI. Angulum contingentiae non esse quolibet rectilineo minorem (table of knots: … non esse minorem omni rectilineo)  
*English title:* Knot XI. That the angle of contact [angulum contingentiae] is not less than any rectilinear [angle] whatever  
*Pages:* PDF pp. 247–250 = printed pp. 236–239 (from ‘Ereu. Doctissima prorsus & subtilissima est haec dissertatio’ on p. 236 to Asphalius’ ‘…Accinge itaque te, si libet, ad alia.’ on p. 239; Knot xii follows on p. 239).

## Dialogue II, Knot xi (printed pp. 236–239)

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| [p. 236] [Margin: Nodus XI. Angulum contingentiae non esse quolibet rectilineo minorem.] Ereu. Doctissima prorsus & subtilissima est haec dissertatio, sed utinam hanc admonitionem aliis locis fecisse satis habuisset. Nisi enim fallor, hic intempestiva fuit. Cum hoc exemplo magis pateat Geometris opus esse Logica quam Logicis Geometria. Vere enim Geometrae circa hunc angulum contingentiae in gravissimos errores incidunt: & (si quid ego intelligo) Logicae defectu, quod aequivocationem latentem extricare nesciant. | [p. 236] [Margin: Knot XI. That the angle of contact [angulum contingentiae] is not less than any rectilinear [angle] whatever.] Ereu. Most learned altogether [prorsus] and most subtle is this dissertation [dissertatio]; but would that he [Galileo] had held it enough to have made this admonition [admonitionem] in other places. For, unless I am deceived, here it was untimely [intempestiva]. Since [cum] by this example it is more evident that Geometers have need of Logic than Logicians of Geometry. For truly Geometers, about this angle of contact, fall into most grave errors: and (if I understand anything) by a defect of Logic, because they do not know how to disentangle [extricare] a hidden equivocation [aequivocationem]. |
| Andab. Fac me huius veritatis participem ut insultantibus sua valeam verba reddere. | Andab. Make me a sharer [participem] of this truth, that I may be able to give back to those insulting [us] their own words. |
| Ereu. Ne faxis, Andabata, nimium enim reprehensionis rei sumus qui tantopere Geometriam negligimus. Etsi Pythagoreos & Platonem etiam merito ab Aristotele redargutos existimem, quod minus recte aliquando numerorum scientia in Physicis usi sint. Quod etiam interdum aliquibus Medicis contingere non abnuam, qui tertio, septimo, nono, & similibus numeris nescio quam mysticam vim in effectus naturales inesse cogitant. | Ereu. Do not do [it] [ne faxis], Andabata; for we are guilty [rei] of too much reproach, who so greatly neglect Geometry. Although I should think the Pythagoreans and Plato also [to have been] deservedly refuted [redargutos] by Aristotle, because they sometimes used the science of numbers less rightly in Physics [in Physicis]. Which also I would not deny [abnuam] happens at times to some Physicians [Medicis], who think that in the third, seventh, ninth and similar numbers there is [inesse] I know not what mystical force [vim] upon [in] natural effects. |
| Ergo tuo ut satis agam desiderio, vide hunc angulum contingentiae. [Margin: Quaere in tabula Figuram 5.] Demonstravit Euclides nullam lineam rectam posse interiici inter circulum & tangentem. Ex hoc Theoremate sive Euclides sive (ut [p. 237] potius reor) aliquis eius Scholiastes hoc corollarium adiecit. Semicirculi angulum quovis angulo acuto rectilineo maiorem esse, reliquum vero minorem. Quisquis autem hoc adiecit, non ex arte fecit: Non definivit enim alicubi Euclides, quid sit angulum unum esse altero maiorem, quod praemissum oportuit. | Therefore, that I may satisfy [satis agam] your desire, see this angle of contact. [Margin: Look in the table [for] Figure 5.] Euclid demonstrated that no straight line can be put in between [interiici] the circle and the tangent. From this Theorem either Euclid or (as [p. 237] I rather think) some Scholiast [Scholiastes] of his added this corollary: That the angle of the semicircle is greater than any acute rectilinear angle whatever, but the remaining [one] [reliquum] less. But whoever added this did not do [it] according to art [ex arte]: for Euclid did not anywhere define what it is for one angle to be greater than another, which ought to have been premised [praemissum]. |
| Utcumque sit, hinc lis inter Geometras hactenus indecisa orsa est, omnibus communiter hoc corollarium acceptantibus; sed aliis angulo contingentiae nomen anguli invidentibus (neque enim rem ipsam eos negare crediderim, nisi simul nobis & mentem & oculos eripere velint, ut neque videamus, neque anguli definitionem competere ei mente comprehendamus.) Aliis e diverso angulos mixtos cum rectilineis comparari tanquam quantitates diversorum generum existimantibus, ut inde evadatur quomodo diminutio in infinitum determinatam diminutionem non excedat. | However that may be, from here a dispute [lis] among Geometers, up to now undecided, has arisen [orsa est], all commonly accepting [acceptantibus] this corollary; but some begrudging [invidentibus] the angle of contact the name of angle (for I would not believe that they deny the thing [rem] itself, unless they wish to snatch from us at once both mind and eyes, so that we neither see [it], nor comprehend with the mind that the definition of angle belongs [competere] to it). Others, on the contrary [e diverso], thinking that mixed angles [angulos mixtos] are compared with rectilinear [ones] as quantities of diverse kinds [generum], so that thence an escape may be made [evadatur] [as to] how a diminution to infinity [in infinitum] does not exceed a determinate diminution. |
| Debuerant vero meminisse in similibus non esse aliud maius, aliud minus, superficiem enim linea maiorem non dicimus, vel minorem; sed angulos angulis dicimus. Cogitemus modo in quo consistat quantitas realis & vera anguli; & videbimus non esse aliam ipsius quantitatem quam superficiem inter latera interiectam. Unde manifestum est eum angulum esse maiorem [p. 238] qui inter lineas paris longitudinis debite acceptas maiorem superficiem contineat. Quo posito manifestum erit corollarium inductum falsum esse, cum propositio verissima sit. Et illatum ex eo quod definitio prius non sit statuta. Ex hac autem nostra definitione de se patet nihil in Geometria incommodi evenire, cum ex priori inevitabile sit alterum duorum explicatorum, videlicet ut vel determinata proportio diminutione non exhauriatur, vel anguli mixti non sint anguli. | But they ought to have remembered that in similar [things] [in similibus] there is not one greater, another less; for we do not say a surface [superficiem] [is] greater than a line, or less; but we say angles [are greater or less] than angles. Let us only think in what consists the real and true quantity of an angle; and we shall see that there is no other quantity of it than the surface put between [interiectam] the sides. Whence it is manifest that that angle is greater [p. 238] which, between lines of equal length duly taken, contains a greater surface. Which being posited, it will be manifest that the corollary brought in [inductum] is false, although [cum] the proposition is most true; and [that it was] inferred [illatum] from this, that the definition was not established beforehand. But from this our definition it is evident of itself [de se] that nothing of inconvenience [incommodi] comes about in Geometry, whereas [cum] from the former [ex priori] one or other of the two [things] set forth [explicatorum] is inevitable: namely, that either a determinate proportion is not exhausted by diminution, or mixed angles are not angles. |
| Asphal. Immo vero hoc incommodi sequitur, minorem angulum continere maiorem. Necessum enim erit aliquem angulum rectilineum aliquo angulo contingentiae esse minorem & tamen eum continebit: cum angulus contingentiae ad communem terminum inter tangentem & reliquam rectam pertingat. | Asphal. Nay rather [immo vero], this of inconvenience [incommodi] follows: that a lesser angle contains a greater. For it will be necessary that some rectilinear angle be less than some angle of contact, and yet it will contain it: since the angle of contact reaches to the common term [terminum] between the tangent and the remaining straight [line] [reliquam rectam]. |
| Ereu. Subtiliter reposuisti; sed hoc attende. Non per hoc dici unum ab alio contineri, quod aliqua pars contineatur; nisi ipsum totum ponatur contineri, non ergo sufficit quod angulus hic quomodocumque contineatur, nisi etiam ponatur, quod quatenus & ubi maior contineatur; alioquin enim non angulus sed anguli pars erit contenta. | Ereu. You have replied [reposuisti] subtly; but attend to this. [I say that] one is not said to be contained by another through this, that some part [of it] is contained, unless the whole itself be posited to be contained; it does not therefore suffice that this angle be contained in whatever way [quomodocumque], unless it be also posited that, in so far as [quatenus] and where it is greater, it is contained; for otherwise not the angle but a part of the angle will be contained. |
| Prius itaque duobus angulis determinata la[p. 239]terum magnitudo, qua pares superficies contineant, vel certe proportionatas, est assignanda, & tunc videndum an inter ea latera anguli quem dicis minorem, ea rursus latera anguli maioris contineantur: si enim secetur maior angulus, aut latus ipsius a latere minoris anguli, antequam ad praefinitum signum perveniat, vides minorem angulum partem maioris non totum continuisse. Et aequi{?vo}catione quantitatis anguli delusos Mathematicos in errores & ἄτοπα nihil nostris debentia incidisse; neque tot litibus & etiam libellis hinc inde conscriptis sese expediuisse. Nunc itaque ad reliqua te contra telluris motum congesta argumenta converte, Asphali. | First, therefore, for the two angles a determinate magnitude of the si[p. 239]des, by which they may contain equal surfaces, or at least proportioned [ones] [proportionatas], is to be assigned; and then it is to be seen whether between those sides of the angle which you call lesser, those sides, in turn, of the greater angle are contained: for if the greater angle be cut, or its side [be cut] by the side of the lesser angle, before it reaches the predetermined mark [signum], you see that the lesser angle has contained a part of the greater, not the whole. And [you see] that the Mathematicians, deluded by the equivocation [aequi{?vo}catione] of the quantity of the angle, have fallen into errors and absurdities [ἄτοπα] owing nothing to ours [nihil nostris debentia], and have not extricated [expediuisse] themselves from so many disputes and even little books [libellis] written on this side and that [hinc inde]. Now therefore turn yourself to the remaining arguments heaped up [congesta] against the motion of the earth [telluris], Asphalius. |
| Asphal. Caetera leviora sunt quam ut te iam fatigatum iis impedire operae pretium existimem, praeter Mathematica in quibus te ab ipso nihil dissentire crediderim. Accinge itaque te, si libet, ad alia. | Asphal. The rest are lighter than that I should think it worth the trouble [operae pretium] to hinder you, already wearied, with them, except the Mathematical [things], in which I would believe you dissent in nothing from him [ab ipso] [Galileo]. Gird yourself therefore, if it pleases, to other [things]. [End of Knot xi; Knot xii begins on p. 239 with Ereunius’ question ‘Si tu quoque, Andabata…’.] |

## Words and phrases with alternative or special meanings

- ***angulus contingentiae*** — rendered “angle of contact”. horn angle; angle between a circle and its tangent. Euclid, Elements III.16 and its corollary; the 16th-century dispute between Peletier (horn angle is no angle, 'nullus') and Clavius (it is an angle, but heterogeneous to rectilinear angles). White sides with neither, defining angle-quantity as enclosed surface.
- ***semicirculi angulus / reliquus*** — rendered “angle of the semicircle / the remaining [one]”. the mixed angle between diameter and arc / the horn angle between arc and tangent. The wording of Elements III.16: the angle of the semicircle exceeds every acute rectilinear angle, the remaining one is less.
- ***Scholiastes*** — rendered “Scholiast”. commentator, annotator. White suspects the corollary to III.16 is a scholiast's addition (it is in fact transmitted as a porism).
- ***corollarium*** — rendered “corollary”. porism, consequence. Euclidean technical term.
- ***ex arte*** — rendered “according to art”. methodically, by the rules of the discipline. 'Ars' as a technical discipline (geometry/logic).
- ***aequivocatio*** — rendered “equivocation”. ambiguity of a term. Scholastic logic: the hidden equivocation is in 'quantity of an angle' (p. 239, printed 'aequicatione', read aequivocatione).
- ***anguli mixti*** — rendered “mixed angles”. curvilinear-rectilinear angles. Clavius' term for angles bounded by a straight and a curved line.
- ***quantitates diversorum generum*** — rendered “quantities of diverse kinds”. heterogeneous magnitudes. Clavius' answer: horn and rectilinear angles are not of one genus, so no ratio holds between them (cf. Elements V def. 4).
- ***superficies*** — rendered “surface”. area, plane region. White's definition: an angle's 'real and true quantity' is the surface enclosed between its sides taken at equal length.
- ***incommodum*** — rendered “inconvenience”. absurd consequence. Scholastic disputation term for an untoward consequence of a position (glossary).
- ***continere / contineri*** — rendered “contain / be contained”. enclose; hold within. Asphalius' objection: the rectilinear angle 'contains' the larger horn angle; Ereunius distinguishes containing a part from containing the whole.
- ***quatenus & ubi*** — rendered “in so far as and where”. in the respect and at the place in which. Scholastic qualification (quatenus = qua).
- ***ἄτοπα*** — rendered “absurdities”. 'out-of-place' things; absurd consequences. Aristotelian Greek technical word for absurd conclusions.
- ***nihil nostris debentia*** — rendered “owing nothing to ours”. no less than ours, not inferior to ours. Idiom 'nihil debere' = to fall short in nothing; i.e. errors as bad as the logicians' own.
- ***ne faxis*** — rendered “do not do [it]”. archaic perfect subjunctive of facio. Comic/legal archaism (Plautus, Livy).
- ***rei (reprehensionis)*** — rendered “guilty (of reproach)”. accused, liable. Reus with genitive of the charge.
- ***numeri tertius, septimus, nonus*** — rendered “the third, seventh, ninth numbers”. the critical days. Hippocratic-Galenic doctrine of critical days in fevers; White links it with Pythagorean number mysticism criticised by Aristotle (Metaphysics I, XIII–XIV).
- ***accingere se*** — rendered “gird oneself”. prepare oneself. Classical idiom.
- ***operae pretium*** — rendered “worth the trouble”. worthwhile. Classical idiom.

## Translator's notes

- Unit: Dialogue II, Knot xi, complete in one unit. PDF pp. 247–250 = printed pp. 236–239, from ‘Ereu. Doctissima prorsus’ (p. 236, beside margin ‘Nodus XI’) to Asphalius’ ‘Accinge itaque te, si libet, ad alia.’ (p. 239). The margin ‘Nodus XII. Lunam non moveri immediate a sole’ stands beside Andabata’s ‘Video (si vera sunt…)’ lower on p. 239, but Ereunius’ question just before it (‘Si tu quoque, Andabata… num ex luna putes vapores tolli sicut ex tellure?’) opens the new topic, so it is assigned to Knot xii.
- Offset +11 holds (running heads 236–239; signature Gg iij on p. 237). Marginal notes: p. 236 knot title; p. 236 ‘Quaere in tabula Figuram 5’ (printed ‘Figu-ram 5.’; the table of figures’ fig. 5 is the angle of contact, see FM2).
- Uncertain or difficult readings: (1) p. 239 printed ‘æquicatione’, almost certainly a misprint for ‘aequivocatione’ (cf. ‘aequivocationem latentem’ on p. 236); transcribed aequi{?vo}catione. (2) p. 236 ‘Cum hoc exemplo magis pateat…’ stands as a sentence with no main clause; rendered literally as ‘Since…’. (3) p. 237 ‘in similibus non esse aliud maius, aliud minus’: the argument (surface vs line are not compared) requires ‘only in like things is one greater, another less’; perhaps ‘nisi’ has dropped out or ‘dissimilibus’ was meant; translated as printed. (4) p. 238 ‘Non per hoc dici unum ab alio contineri’: accusative-and-infinitive with no governing verb; ‘[I say that]’ supplied. (5) p. 239 ‘ab ipso’: taken as Galileo.
- Verification pass: each Latin sentence re-read against the page images (with 300-dpi crops of the p. 236 margin and p. 239 lines 9–13) and against the English, checking omissions, cases, tenses, moods, negatives and comparatives. 1 correction: the p. 236 margin, recorded in the previous run's progress note as ‘tabula Figurarum 5’, reads on the zoom ‘Quaere in tabula Figuram 5’ (‘Figu-ram’ divided across lines); transcription and English set accordingly. Also re-checked and confirmed: comparative ‘minus recte’ (‘less rightly’), not a simple negative; ‘neque … sese expediuisse’ depends, like ‘incidisse’, on ‘vides’; ‘Debuerant’ (pluperfect) rendered ‘ought to have’; ‘continebit’ has the rectilinear angle as subject.
- Conventions as in the glossary: long s → s, æ → ae, macrons expanded (angulū → angulum, autē → autem, minorē → minorem), ę → ae (praemissum); u/v normalised as in earlier units; Greek ἄτοπα kept as printed; words supplied for sense in square brackets.
