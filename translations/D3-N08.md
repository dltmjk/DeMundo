# De Mundo — Dialogue III (De causis mundi), Knot viii

*Latin title:* Nodus VIII. Deum liberum mundum condidisse. (Table: Deum libere mundum condidisse.)  
*English title:* Knot VIII. That God [as] free founded the world. (Table: That God freely founded the world.)  
*Pages:* PDF pp. 337–344 = printed pp. 326–333 (from Ereunius’ ‘Quod ad Theologiam & fidem spectat’ on p. 326 to ‘…ut apertam reddamus nodi secturam.’ on p. 333, where Knot ix begins).

## Dialogue III, Knot viii (printed pp. 326–333)

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| [Margin, p. 326: Nodus VIII. Deum liberum mundum condidisse.] | [Margin, p. 326: Knot VIII. That God [as] free [liberum] founded [condidisse] the world.] |
| Ereu. Quod ad Theologiam & fidem spectat, Ego non Ecclesiae solum defero, sed & tibi, Andabata, & cuicumque Theologo liberum & non praeiudiciis illigatum intellectum tenenti; sed iustum etiam videor mihi petere, si audiri desidero. Et quoniam obiecisti & ad libertatis notionem pertinuit obiectio, permitte mihi ut de oppositis libertatis aliquid a te discam. Hoc igitur imprimis peto, an aliud libertati officiat, quam vel coactio vel natura? | Ereu. As to what regards [spectat] Theology and faith, I defer [defero] not to the Church only, but also to you, Andabata, and to whatever Theologian holding an understanding [intellectum] free and not bound up [illigatum] by prejudices [praeiudiciis]; but I seem to myself also to ask what is just, if I desire to be heard. And since you have objected, and the objection pertained to the notion [notionem] of liberty, permit me that I learn something from you about the opposites of liberty. This therefore I ask first of all [imprimis]: whether anything else hinders [officiat] liberty than either coercion [coactio] or nature? |
| Andab. Nihil plane. | Andab. Nothing at all [plane]. |
| Ereu. Coactio vero numquid esse potest, si nullum sit externum principium impediens internum agentis impetum? Quod enim secundum naturam & inclinationem suam movetur, quantum ego hactenus didici, eatenus non dicitur coactum. | Ereu. But can coercion [coactio] be at all [numquid], if there be no external principle impeding the internal impetus [impetum] of the agent? For that which is moved according to its nature and inclination, as far as I have hitherto learned, so far [eatenus] is not called coerced. |
| Andab. Veritatem loqueris. | Andab. You speak the truth. |
| Ereu. Quoniam itaque nullum principium extrinsecum est quod in Deum agat, non est a coactione timendum, ne divinae officiat libertati: Naturam autem, si officit libertati, inde credo quia tollit considerationem multorum & electionem ex pluribus. Num minus rem attingo? | Ereu. Since therefore there is no extrinsic principle which may act upon [in] God, it is not to be feared from coercion that it hinder the divine liberty: but nature, if it hinders liberty, [does so] thence, I believe, because it takes away the consideration of many [things] and the choice [electionem] out of several. Do I touch the matter [rem attingo] any less [than rightly] [minus]? |
| Andab. Immo optime. | Andab. Nay [immo], most excellently. |
| Ereu. Si itaque natura non tollat quo minus ex consideratione multorum & electione inter ea procedat ad conditionem mundi Deus, libere condiderit universum. | Ereu. If therefore nature does not take away [non tollat quo minus] that out of the consideration of many [things] and choice among them God proceed to the founding [conditionem] of the world, freely will He have founded [condiderit] the universe. |
| Andab. Neque hoc inficior. | Andab. Nor do I deny [inficior] this. |
| Ereu. Age igitur: naturale sit Deo quod posuisti a rectitudine non deficere: sit autem rectitudo in hoc posita, ut ex infinitis mundis, quos condere potest, ex eorum consideratione eligat meliorem, ita ut si non eligat meliorem, deficiat a rectitudine; putasne quod natura vel impotentia deficiendi tollat consilium & electionem? vel ea solidet & confirmet, faciat consilium certum, electionem perfectam & optimam. | Ereu. Come then: let it be natural to God — what you have posited [posuisti] — not to fall short [deficere] from rectitude [rectitudine]; and let rectitude be placed in this, that out of the infinite worlds which He can found, out of the consideration of them He choose the better, so that if He do not choose the better, He fall short from rectitude: do you think that nature, or impotence [impotentia] of falling short, takes away counsel [consilium] and choice? or [that] it makes them solid [solidet] and confirms them, makes counsel certain, choice perfect and best. |
| Andab. Video equidem quod considerationem non tollat; non possum enim negare quin omnia videat, & ex visione optimi ad illud determinetur, sed quod electionem non tollat caecutio, vel magis aperte video electionem tollere. | Andab. I see indeed that it does not take away consideration; for I cannot deny that He sees all things, and out of the vision of the best is determined to it; but that it does not take away choice, I am blind [caecutio] — or rather [vel magis] I see openly [aperte] that it takes away choice. |
| Ereu. At ipsa determinatio ad melius, quia melius non est electio? non unum accipit, reliqua reiicit? | Ereu. But the very determination to the better, because [it is] better, is it not choice? does it not accept one, reject the rest? |
| Andab. Ita est, si hoc libertate fieret; sed natura & necessitate fit, & idcirco libertas tollitur. Est enim libertas quando omnibus positis praerequisitis ad actionem, voluntas ex suo motu sese determinat ad unam partem, non autem quoties vel ex natura vel ab intellectu praeviam habet determinationem. | Andab. So it is, if this were done by liberty; but it is done by nature and necessity, and therefore liberty is taken away. For there is liberty when, all the prerequisites [praerequisitis] to action being posited, the will out of its own motion determines itself to one part [partem], but not whenever it has a prior [praeviam] determination either from nature or from the intellect. |
| Ereu. Haec tua quam affers libertatis sive definitionem sive descriptionem, estne de fide? | Ereu. This your — whether definition or description — of liberty which you bring forward, is it of faith [de fide]? |
| Andab. Non hoc dixerim. | Andab. I would not say [dixerim] this. |
| Ereu. Ergo neque ad Theologiam spectat; haec enim quae cum iis quae ad fidem spectant, committit, a Philosophia haurit: tota itaque iam quaestio Philosophica est, & quoniam periculum in fidem vel Theologiam peccandi nullum est, maneat quod diximus, liberam esse Dei de creatione mundi dispositionem, quantumvis a rectitudine rationis deviaret si eum non condidisset, quod impossibile esse pariter concedimus, & a naturae determinatione ortum habere; | Ereu. Therefore neither does it regard Theology; for this [Theology] draws [haurit] from Philosophy those things which it joins [committit] with those which regard faith: the whole question therefore is now Philosophical; and since there is no danger of sinning against [in] faith or Theology, let what we have said remain: that the disposition [dispositionem] of God concerning the creation of the world is free, however much He would deviate from the rectitude of reason if He had not founded it — which we equally concede to be impossible, and to have [its] origin from the determination of nature; |
| Naturamque, non quamlibet, sed eam quae ex sese determinata est ad unum solitarium opponi libertati, determinationem vero ad unum ex multis, etsi a natura fiat, ipsam & perfectissimam quidem esse libertatem. | and that nature — not any whatever, but that which of itself is determined to one solitary [thing] [ad unum solitarium] — is opposed to liberty, but that determination to one out of many, even if it be done by nature, is liberty itself, and indeed the most perfect. |
| Asphal. Diluenda adhuc manet quam impegit, Andabata, ex societate ad Philosophos aspersio. Novimus enim ab Ecclesia reprobatam eorum de positura mundi ex necessitate a Deo sententiam: quam si secteris, nequaquam te liberum omni periculo fas est credere, Ereuni. | Asphal. There still remains to be washed away [diluenda] the aspersion [aspersio] which Andabata has thrust [impegit] [on you] out of [your] fellowship [societate] with the Philosophers. For we know their opinion [sententiam] about the setting [positura] of the world by God out of necessity [to have been] reprobated by the Church: which if you follow, by no means is it right [fas] to believe you free from all danger, Ereunius. |
| Ereu. Id ipsum fateor, habuit enim Philosophos is error, ut crederent mundum adaequatum esse divinae potestatis partum: non ex multis electum optimum, sed qui posset fieri unicum: ut natum potius quam factum ex eorum sermone crederes, aeternum quoque & connaturalem Deo: quae vides immane quantum a nostris dictatis recedant & ipsa re & appellationibus. Cum ipsi libertatem, electionem, temporalitatem mundi irrideant. Nos eorum inscitiam finitum infinito comparantium. | Ereu. This very thing I confess; for this error held [habuit] the Philosophers, that they believed the world to be the adequate [adaequatum] offspring [partum] of the divine power: not the best chosen out of many, but the only one [unicum] that could be made: so that from their discourse [sermone] you would believe [it] born rather than made, eternal also and connatural to God: which [things] you see how monstrously far [immane quantum] they recede from our dictates [dictatis], both in the thing [re] itself and in appellations. Since they themselves mock [irrideant] the liberty, choice, temporality of the world. We [mock] the ignorance [inscitiam] of them comparing the finite to the infinite. |
| Asphal. Sed vide quam consequenter. Si enim nisi Deus mundum condat, a rectitudine deficit, & rursus a rectitudine deficere impossibile sit. Iam impossibile est mundum non condere. Ubi nunc libertas & electio? Fortassis enim hunc mundum aliis praeelegit, sed certe mundum non praeelegit condere, nihil enim oppositum habet. | Asphal. But see how consistently [consequenter]. For if, unless God found the world, He falls short from rectitude, and again to fall short from rectitude be impossible. Now it is impossible not to found the world. Where now [are] liberty and choice? For perhaps He preferred [praeelegit] this world to others, but certainly He did not prefer to found a world, for it has nothing opposite. |
| Quod si mundum ex necessitate condidit: vide quomodo temporarius esse possit, & non coaeternus Deo: Adiice si elegit hunc mundum ex multis, ergo alium potuisset creare & sine hoc esse & cum illo esse potuit, itaque voluntatem aliam habuisse, hanc non habuisse, mutabilis ergo est: & ut Deum liberum faciamus, ex alia parte sacrilegi evadimus. Si qui enim sunt in his gradus, constantius profecto erit Deum immutabilem esse, quod Philosophi dicunt, quam esse liberum quod nos Christiani. | But if He founded the world out of necessity: see how it can be temporary [temporarius], and not coeternal with God. Add [adiice]: if He chose this world out of many, then He could have created another, and He could both be without this and be with that; therefore [He could] have had another will, not have had this one: therefore He is mutable: and so that we may make God free, on the other side we turn out [evadimus] sacrilegious. For if there are any degrees [gradus] in these [matters], it will surely be more consistent [constantius] that God is immutable, which the Philosophers say, than that [He] is free, which we Christians [say]. |
| Ereu. Ardua sunt quae opponis, & quae Philosophos Christianos quibus Christi doctrina rationibus humanis antiquior semper fuit, tenuerunt multum anxios: sed audendum est & quod Poeta ait: fata viam invenient. Veritas enim laetior per medias angustias emicat & post Magellanicos tumultus aequor pacificum nobis apertura est. | Ereu. Arduous are the things which you oppose, and which have held [tenuerunt] much anxious the Christian Philosophers, to whom the doctrine of Christ was always older [antiquior] than human reasons: but one must dare, and [say] what the Poet says: the fates will find a way. For Truth flashes forth [emicat] more joyful through the midst of straits [angustias], and after the Magellanic tumults is about to open [apertura est] to us the pacific [pacificum] sea [aequor]. |
| Cum primis enim de hoc quod dicis impossibile est mundum non condere, quaerendum est, unde sit haec impossibilitas? Et clarum est ex eo proficisci quod videat optimum esse mundum condere, hoc est, ex electione vel determinatione iam facta, hoc vero quod est a rectitudine deficere non posse, ex ipsa communi & prima Dei ratione procedit, quod scilicet ipsa bonitas seu perfectio seu existentia existens sit. | For first of all [cum primis], concerning this which you say, 'it is impossible not to found the world', it must be asked: whence is this impossibility? And it is clear that it proceeds from this, that He sees it to be best to found the world, that is, from a choice or determination already made; but this, which is 'not to be able to fall short from rectitude', proceeds from the very common and first notion [ratione] of God, namely that He is goodness itself, or perfection, or existing existence [existentia existens]. |
| Vides itaque unum ex primo principio, alterum ex ultimo seu resolutione ipsa capta non posse fieri. Nemo autem quod sciam de ratione libertatis ponit esse ut post resolutionem factam mutari possit liber: & non facere, quod decrevit, pertinet enim hoc ad inconstantiam, sed ais, nisi deviet a rectitudine, non posse non mundum facere: cur hoc? quia scilicet non potest in consulendo deficere. τὸ itaque non posse mundum condere mediante resolutione pendet a τῷ non posse deficere. | You see therefore [that] the one 'cannot be done' [non posse fieri] [is] from the first principle, the other from the last [ultimo], or [seu] from the resolution [resolutione] itself [once] taken. But no one, that I know of, posits it to be of the notion [ratione] of liberty that after a resolution made the free [agent] [liber] can be changed, and not do what he decreed — for this pertains to inconstancy; but you say, unless He deviate from rectitude, He cannot not make the world: why this? because, namely, He cannot fall short in counselling [consulendo]. The [τὸ] 'not to be able [not] to found the world', therefore, by means of [mediante] the resolution depends on the [τῷ] 'not to be able to fall short'. |
| Fateor tamen non talem esse gradationem, ut de mundo in communi condendo resolutio fiat: sed eum immediate & sine consideratione plurium ab ea voluntate pendere, qua ad benefaciendum Deus propendet, quae essentialis est. Sed ea resolutio cum universalis sit, neque exeat ad opus, nisi in hoc mundo non impedit quominus resolutio de mundo condendo sit libera. | I confess, however, that there is not such a gradation [gradationem] that a resolution is made concerning founding the world in common [in communi]; but that it [eum, the world in common] depends immediately and without consideration of several [things] on that will by which God inclines [propendet] to doing good [benefaciendum], which is essential. But that resolution, since it is universal, and does not go out to the work [opus] except in this world, does not hinder [non impedit quominus] the resolution concerning founding the world from being free. |
| Andab. Quid ita? Tandem enim ad hoc pervenitur ut unicum sit medium quo rectitudini conformetur divina voluntas, nempe ut hunc singularem mundum extruat. | Andab. Why so? For at length one arrives at this, that there is a single [unicum] means [medium] by which the divine will is conformed to rectitude, namely that it build [extruat] this singular world. |
| Ereu. Non advertis nomina ista a nobis ad Deum transferri, in nobis autem naturam dici primum motus principium, & ea natura fieri quae absque intellectus consilio perficiuntur: quae autem per gradus & descensionem consilii ad particularia a nostra determinatione & actione nostra in nosmetipsos proficisci dicimus, quantumvis evidens & ex evidentibus sit progressio quam facimus; | Ereu. You do not notice [non advertis] that those names are transferred by us to God; but [autem] in us nature is called the first principle of motion, and those things [are said] to be done by nature which are accomplished without the counsel of the intellect: but those which [go] through degrees and a descent of counsel to particulars, we say proceed from our determination and our action upon [in] ourselves, however evident, and out of evident [things], be the progression which we make; |
| cum proportione proinde haec nomina ad Deum sunt transferenda, & naturaliter ea dicenda sunt fieri, quae immediate & sine multiplicitate proveniunt; ea vero quae parallelam humanis discursibus patiuntur varietatem, proportionato libertatis insignienda sunt vocabulo. | with proportion [cum proportione] accordingly are these names to be transferred to God, and those things are to be said to be done naturally which come forth immediately and without multiplicity; but those which undergo [patiuntur] a variety parallel to human discourses [discursibus], are to be marked [insignienda] with the proportioned word [vocabulo] of liberty. |
| His sic edoctis, non video quid ab aeternitate quam mundo minantur Philosophi, pertimescendum sit; Esto enim, Deo necessarium sit mundum condere, non ex alia profecto radice haec necessitas ortum habet, quam quia voluntas eius recta est. Si itaque condendus est orbis, ut in ipsius voluntate decisum est, condendus est; si itaque voluntas quae ratione regitur, hanc ei conditionem affinxerit, ut cum initio temporis incipiat, sic ex necessitate futurus est condi. | These things being thus thoroughly taught [edoctis], I do not see what is to be dreaded from the eternity which the Philosophers threaten to [minantur] the world. For be it [esto] [that] it is necessary for God to found the world: surely this necessity has its origin from no other root than because His will is right. If therefore the orb [orbis] is to be founded, as it has been decided in His will, so it is to be founded; if therefore the will which is ruled by reason has attached [affinxerit] to it this condition [conditionem], that it begin with the beginning of time, so out of necessity it is going to be founded. |
| Necessitas itaque qua Deus mundum creat, eadem adigit ut cum tempore eum efficiat. Tantum itaque abest ut ex necessitate fiat coaeternum Deo universum, ut temporaneum ipsa ab necessitate prodeat. Super adhuc est ultima & eadem difficillima obiectorum pars qua immobilitas primi entis tangitur, & ad vivum quidem, si Philosophis credimus, {?se} eo nobis enixius conandum est, ut apertam reddamus nodi secturam. | The necessity therefore by which God creates the world, the same drives [adigit] [Him] to effect it with time. So far therefore is it [tantum abest] that out of necessity the universe should become coeternal with God, that [it] comes forth temporal [temporaneum] from necessity itself. There is still over [super … est] the last and likewise the most difficult part of the objections, by which the immobility [immobilitas] of the first being is touched — and to the quick [ad vivum] indeed, if we believe the Philosophers; {?se} therefore [eo] we must strive the more strenuously [enixius] that we render open [apertam] the cutting [secturam] of the knot. |

## Words and phrases with alternative or special meanings

- ***liber / libertas / libere*** — rendered “free / liberty / freely”. free from coercion; free from natural determination; free choice (liberum arbitrium). The whole Knot turns on which opposite of liberty is meant: coercion (coactio) or nature; White argues only determination ‘ad unum solitarium’ is opposed to liberty.
- ***coactio / coactum*** — rendered “coercion / coerced”. compulsion, force, violence (Aristotle’s βία). Scholastic: the violent is what is moved by an external principle against the internal inclination (Aristotle, EN III.1; Aquinas ST I-II q.6 a.4–5).
- ***natura (as opposed to libertas)*** — rendered “nature”. natural agency, acting of necessity; essence. In the natural/free division of agents (Aristotle, Phys. II; Scotus’ natura vs voluntas); Ereunius later defines nature in us as ‘primum motus principium’ (Phys. II.1).
- ***electio / eligere / praeeligere*** — rendered “choice / choose / prefer”. election; selection; deliberate preference (προαίρεσις). Aristotelian prohairesis (EN III.2–3): choice following deliberation (consilium).
- ***consilium / consulere*** — rendered “counsel / counselling”. deliberation, plan, advice. Deliberation preceding choice (βούλευσις); ‘descensio consilii ad particularia’ = the practical syllogism descending to particulars.
- ***rectitudo (voluntatis / rationis)*** — rendered “rectitude”. uprightness, rightness; moral correctness. Anselmian ‘rectitudo voluntatis’; here the necessity of God’s choosing the best (cf. Leibnizian optimism avant la lettre).
- ***deficere a rectitudine*** — rendered “fall short from rectitude”. fail, defect, desert. Scholastic language of sin as defect; impotentia deficiendi = impeccability.
- ***libertas … quando omnibus positis praerequisitis ad actionem, voluntas ex suo motu sese determinat*** — rendered “liberty is when, all the prerequisites to action being posited, the will … determines itself”. liberty of indifference. The Molinist/Jesuit definition (Molina, Concordia q.14 a.13 d.2), set against the Thomist view; Ereunius denies it is ‘de fide’ and so treats the question as merely philosophical.
- ***praevia determinatio*** — rendered “prior determination”. predetermination; physical premotion. Echoes the Dominican ‘praedeterminatio physica’ (Báñez) debated in the De auxiliis controversy.
- ***de fide*** — rendered “of faith”. defined doctrine, binding on belief. Technical theological note: a proposition belonging to the deposit of faith, as opposed to theological opinion.
- ***determinatio ad unum (solitarium) / ad unum ex multis*** — rendered “determination to one (solitary) [thing] / to one out of many”.  Scholastic criterion: natural agents are ‘determinata ad unum’; White distinguishes determination to a lone possibility from determination to the best among many, which he calls the most perfect liberty.
- ***conditio (mundi)*** — rendered “founding / condition”. conditio from condere = founding, creation; conditio from condicio = condition, stipulation. Both senses occur in this Knot: ‘ad conditionem mundi’ (founding) and ‘hanc ei conditionem affinxerit’ (condition).
- ***caecutio*** — rendered “I am blind”. I see dimly, I am purblind (caecutire). Andabata professes he cannot see how determination leaves choice intact.
- ***positura (mundi)*** — rendered “setting”. positing, placing, arrangement, establishment. Rare noun; here the Philosophers’ view that the world is posited by God of necessity (necessary emanation).
- ***adaequatum … partum*** — rendered “adequate offspring”. equal-to, exhaustive product, ‘birth’. The world as the full and only possible expression of divine power (Neoplatonic/Avicennian emanation), condemned e.g. in the 1277 Paris articles.
- ***natum potius quam factum*** — rendered “born rather than made”. begotten vs made. Allusion to the Nicene Creed ‘genitum, non factum’, said of the Son: the Philosophers in effect make the world a second Son.
- ***fata viam invenient*** — rendered “the fates will find a way”.  Virgil, Aeneid III.395 and X.113.
- ***Magellanicos tumultus … aequor pacificum*** — rendered “Magellanic tumults … the pacific sea”. pacificum = peaceful / the Pacific. Pun on Magellan’s passage of the stormy Strait (1520) into the ocean he named Mar Pacifico; the ‘straits’ (angustias) of the argument.
- ***resolutio*** — rendered “resolution”. decision; also analysis (resolution into principles). Here a decision of the will; contrasted with the ‘first principle’ (God’s essence) as the source of a necessity.
- ***τὸ / τῷ (non posse …)*** — rendered “the [τὸ] / the [τῷ]”.  Greek article used to nominalise an infinitive phrase, a scholastic-humanist device (nominative τὸ, dative τῷ after ‘a’ for ablative).
- ***in communi*** — rendered “in common”. in general, generically. Logical/scholastic: the world taken generically, as opposed to ‘hunc singularem mundum’.
- ***nomina … ad Deum transferri … cum proportione*** — rendered “names … transferred to God … with proportion”. by analogy. Doctrine of analogical predication of divine names (Aquinas, ST I q.13 a.5); ‘proportionato vocabulo’ = analogical term.
- ***discursus*** — rendered “discourse”. discursive reasoning, step-by-step thought. Human reasoning proceeds discursively; God’s knowledge does not, hence ‘parallel variety’.
- ***existentia existens*** — rendered “existing existence”.  White’s Digbean-Thomist formula for God as ipsum esse subsistens (cf. D3-N06).
- ***immobilitas primi entis*** — rendered “immobility of the first being”. immutability; unmoved mover. Aristotle, Metaph. Λ 7: the first unmoved mover; the objection that free choice implies change in God.
- ***nodus Gordianus / nodi sectura*** — rendered “Gordian knot / cutting of the knot”.  Alexander cutting the Gordian knot; a pun on White’s own ‘nodi’ (Knots) as units of the work.
- ***ad vivum*** — rendered “to the quick”.  Idiom: to the living flesh, i.e. touching the vital point.
- ***temporarius / temporaneus / temporalitas*** — rendered “temporary / temporal / temporality”. having a beginning in time. Against Aristotelian eternity of the world (Phys. VIII; De caelo I).
