## Dialogue III, Knot i (printed pp. 265–269)

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| [p. 265] DIALOGUS TERTIUS, De Duratione & Gubernatione Mundi. | [p. 265] THIRD DIALOGUE, On the Duration [Duratione] and Government [Gubernatione] of the World. |
| Etiam te & horum meminisse aequum est, [Margin: Nodus I. Introductio.] Ereunium in hortum concessisse, ut leni ad solem hyemalem blandulum iam & gratum, deambulatione motum animi sedaret & famem obsonaretur, ut dicebat ille. | It is fair [aequum est] that you also remember these [things] too, [Margin: Knot I. Introduction.] that Ereunius withdrew [concessisse] into the garden, so that with a gentle [leni], at [ad] the winter sun, now coaxing [blandulum] and welcome, walk [deambulatione] he might settle the motion of [his] mind [motum animi] and cater for [obsonaretur] [his] hunger, as that one [ille] used to say. |
| Tu interea cum Andabata, inter vestiendum, quae dicta erant repetebas, & cum pridiano colloquio conferebas. | You meanwhile with Andabata, during dressing [inter vestiendum], were going over again [repetebas] the things which had been said, and were comparing [them] with the colloquy of the day before [pridiano]. |
| Et evenit ut videretis una dissertatione quasi rotas & membra huius ingentis Machinae, hoc est, quibus constaret elementis delineatum, proxima vero etiam motus seu motuum rationes expressas: Et invicem fabulabamini alia dissertatione quae caussas mundi complecteretur, perfectam hanc contemplationem exituram. | And it came about that you [pl.] saw, in one discourse [dissertatione], as it were the wheels and members of this huge Machine [Machinae], that is, of what elements it consisted, delineated [delineatum], but in the next [proxima] also the reasons [rationes] of the motion or of the motions expressed: And you were chatting [fabulabamini] with one another [invicem] that with another discourse, which should embrace the causes of the world, this contemplation would come out [exituram] perfect. |
| Urgebat proinde Andabata, ut post remissam famem, solli-[p. 266]citaretur ad tertiam disputationem Ereunius. | Andabata was pressing accordingly [proinde], that after [his] hunger [had been] relaxed [remissam famem], Ereunius should be solli-[p. 266]cited [sollicitaretur] to a third disputation. |
| Tu negabas viro facili & ad obsequendum prono stimulos addendos esse, satis uno die esse actum. | You were denying that goads [stimulos] were to be added to a man easy and prone to complying [ad obsequendum]; [saying that] enough had been done [esse actum] in one day. |
| Si manere etiam hac nocte apud te vellet Andabata, postridie cum gratia impetrari posse, quod modo futurum esset oneri. | If Andabata wished to stay also this night at your [house] [apud te], [it] could be obtained on the next day with favour [cum gratia], which now [modo] would be [futurum esset] for a burden [oneri]. |
| Post plures ultro citroque altercationes, conventum est Andabatam etiam hac nocte mansurum, sed disputationem ipsa nocte post coenam fore, si modo Ereunio sine nimia vi persuaderi posset. | After several [plures] altercations to and fro [ultro citroque], it was agreed [conventum est] that Andabata would stay also this night, but that the disputation would be on the night itself [ipsa nocte] after supper, if only Ereunius could be persuaded [Ereunio … persuaderi] without too much force [vi]. |
| Successit prandium in quo Andabata, salibus iocisque omnia perspergebat; multa ab Ereunio quaeritans, quis Planetarum piscosissimus foret, quae acetaria crederet esse lunaribus illis hominibus, & plura alia quae argutulis hinc inde diverbiis epulas condiebant. | There followed [Successit] the midday meal [prandium], in which Andabata was sprinkling all things with salts [salibus] and jokes; asking [quaeritans] many things of Ereunius: which of the Planets would be the most fish-abounding [piscosissimus], what salads [acetaria] he believed there were for those lunar men, and several other things which, with sharp little [argutulis] dialogues [diverbiis] from this side and that [hinc inde], seasoned [condiebant] the feast [epulas]. |
| Post epulas ad focum, hyemali more, consessum est. | After the feast, at the hearth, by winter custom, [they] sat together [consessum est]. |
| Ubi cum multa etiam Andabata interrogaret: coepit Ereunius eum serio adhortari ut animum & etiam calamum ad aliquid de his scribendum accommodaret. | Where, when Andabata was also asking many things: Ereunius began seriously to exhort him that he should apply [accommodaret] [his] mind and also [his] pen [calamum] to writing something about these [matters]. |
| Ille vero qua demum utilitate hic labos sibi compensaretur, quaerebat; cum inutili tantum delectatione curiosis ista auribus affunderentur. | But he was asking by what utility, in the end [demum], this labour [labos] would be compensated to him; since those things [ista] were poured into [affunderentur] curious ears with only a useless delight. |
| Tum Ereunius exponere. Quomodo nihil humanae menti contemplatione naturalius & convenientius foret: quomodo sicut oculis videre, auribus audire, tactui sentire dulce foret, sic menti scibilia com-[p. 267]prehendere. | Then Ereunius [began] to set forth [exponere — historic infinitive]: How nothing would be more natural and more fitting [convenientius] to the human mind than contemplation: how, just as for the eyes to see, for the ears to hear, for the touch to feel would be sweet, so for the mind to com-[p. 267]prehend [comprehendere] the knowables [scibilia]. |
| Caetera autem quae volupe sunt a natura tributa, ut actiones naturales ad mentis usum perficiant. | But the other things which are pleasurable [volupe] [are] bestowed [tributa] by nature so that they may perfect [perficiant] the natural actions for the use of the mind. |
| Hanc unam operationem quasi caeterorum omnium finem & fruitionem quandam esse, de qua nihil rogandum esset quo fine fieret, cum ipsemet finis foret. | [That] this one operation is as it were the end [finem] of all the others and a certain fruition [fruitionem], about which nothing would have to be asked [as to] for what end it was done, since it itself would be the end. |
| Quod si fortassis obiectum reprehenderet, cogitaret ipsum universum esse, quantum creatura capere possit perfectam Dei imaginem. | But if perhaps he should find fault with [reprehenderet] the object [obiectum], let him consider [cogitaret] that the universe itself is, as far as a creature can hold [capere], the perfect image of God. |
| Quod itaque obiectum par vel simile cogitari posset? | What object, therefore, could be thought equal or similar? |
| Quin & si utilitate omnia metiretur, non inutilem esse quae non solatium modo vitae humanae conferret, sed etiam lucem, tum ad hanc, tum ad futuram vitam moderandam & adipiscendam. | Nay, and if he measured all things by utility, [that] she [inutilem — fem., sc. contemplation] was not useless, who conferred on human life not solace only, but also light, both for this [life] to be governed [moderandam], and for the future life to be attained [adipiscendam]. |
| Et quam (inquit) Andabata? Et ille. | And which [light] [quam] (says Andabata)? And he: |
| Ex instrumenti inquit natura finis ad quem factus est, colligitur, & artificis ingenium a quo processit, & etiam organorum ratio quibus usus est. | From the nature of an instrument, he says, is gathered [colligitur] the end to which it was made [factus est], and the wit [ingenium] of the craftsman [artificis] from whom it proceeded, and also the reason [ratio] of the instruments [organorum] which he used. |
| Quae putasne (inquit) ad vitae gubernationem recte instituendam inania esse? | Which [things] do you think (he says) are empty [inania] for rightly ordering [instituendam] the government of life? |
| Tum Andabata. Ergo, inquit, ex his quae dixisti Dei existentiam, & quod mundum condiderit, & gubernet, & quo fine, elici posse putas? | Then Andabata: Therefore, he says, from these things which you have said, you think that the existence of God, and that he founded [condiderit] the world, and governs [it], and for what end, can be elicited [elici]? |
| Quae enim organa ipsius commemores non satis dilucet: postquam creandi instrumenta plerique Theologi renuant. | For what instruments [organa] of his you mean to mention [commemores] is not sufficiently clear [dilucet]: since [postquam] most Theologians refuse instruments of creating. |
| Ad quae Ereunius non ex his solis fortassis (inquit) sed haec adminiculo in hac specula-[p. 268]tione futura confidam. | To which Ereunius: not from these alone perhaps (he says), but I shall trust [confidam] that these will be for a prop [adminiculo] in this specula-[p. 268]tion. |
| Instrumenta vero dicebam quae Angelos Theologi vocant quorum opera in formando & gubernando mundo usum esse constat. | But I was calling instruments [those] which the Theologians call Angels, whose work [opera] it is established [constat] [that God] used [usum esse] in forming and governing the world. |
| Reposuit hic Andabata. Haec si serio pollicearis me cogitabundum reddideris, sed tot, similia mihi ex Aristotele pollicitantes habui, qui me tamen frustrati sunt, ut amplius illudi mihi non patiar. | Here Andabata replied [Reposuit]: If you promise these things in earnest [serio], you will have rendered me thoughtful [cogitabundum]; but I have had so many promising [pollicitantes] me similar things out of Aristotle, who nevertheless have frustrated [frustrati sunt] me, that I do not suffer myself to be mocked [illudi mihi] any further. |
| Et Ereunius non ludo, & nisi antemeridiana disputatione caput mihi exarsisset, e vestigio experimentum fecisses. | And Ereunius: I am not playing [ludo], and unless my head had become inflamed [exarsisset] in the forenoon [antemeridiana] disputation, you would have made the trial [experimentum] on the spot [e vestigio]. |
| Sed modo debilitas capitis absterret, ne ausim me huiusmodi speculationi immiscere | But now [modo] the weakness of [my] head frightens [me] off [absterret], so that I should not dare [ne ausim] to mix myself into a speculation of this kind |
| Hic ansam tu tibi nactus videbare deprecandi Andabatam, ut hac nocte quietem & restaurationem Ereunii praestolaretur; quod ille renuere simulabat, donec utrimque conventum esset nocturnam fore dissertationem. | Here you seemed to have got for yourself a handle [ansam] for entreating [deprecandi] Andabata that this night he should wait for [praestolaretur] the rest and restoration of Ereunius; which he was pretending [simulabat] to refuse, until it had been agreed on both sides that the discourse [dissertationem] would be nocturnal. |
| Hisque firmatis, Andabata servulum qui equos adduxerat vocatum suis nunciare mandat sese ab Ereunio dementatum, & ab Asphalio constrictum non posse etiam hac nocte eos visere, sed cum philtrum edormisset, summo mane eos salutaturum, Equum sibi suum relinquat, ipsemet discedat, se cum prima Aurora Equitem futurum. | And these things being made firm [firmatis], Andabata orders the little servant [servulum] who had brought the horses, [having been] called, to announce to his [people] [suis] that he [sese], driven out of his mind [dementatum] by Ereunius and bound fast [constrictum] by Asphalius, could not visit them also this night, but when he had slept off [edormisset] the love-potion [philtrum], he would greet them at the very top of the morning [summo mane]; [that] he should leave for him his [own] Horse, [that he] himself should depart; [that] he [Andabata] would be a Horseman [Equitem] with the first Dawn. |
| Post haec secutae sunt variae fabulae, & consueta exercitia, tum coena quae cerebro non foret gravis: & post coenam occlusis foribus ad focum accensis animis con-[p. 269]sessum est. | After these [things] there followed various talks [fabulae], and the accustomed exercises, then a supper which would not be heavy for the brain: and after supper, the doors having been shut, at the hearth, with minds kindled [accensis animis], [they] sat [p. 269] together [consessum est]. |
| Neque piger Andabata, promissum repetiit ab Ereunio, ad quem | Nor [was] Andabata slow [piger]: he demanded back [repetiit] the promise from Ereunius, to whom [Ereunius replied — the speech opens Knot ii]: |

## Words and phrases with alternative or special meanings

- ***De Duratione & Gubernatione Mundi (heading) / de caussis Mundi (running head)*** — rendered “On the Duration and Government of the World”. The FM2 table calls Dialogue III ‘de causis Mundi seu de Principio Gubernatione et interitu ipsius’; the running heads on pp. 267 and 269 read ‘de caussis Mundi’. Three titles for one dialogue: duratio (beginning and end in time: knots ii–iii, xviii–xix) and gubernatio (providence) are the two halves of the ‘causes’ (efficient/governing and final) announced at the end of Dialogue II.
- ***famem obsonari (ut dicebat ille)*** — rendered “cater for [his] hunger (as that one used to say)”. obsonare/obsonari = buy provisions, cater; here ‘work up an appetite’. ille = ‘he’ (Ereunius) or ‘that famous man’. Classical allusion: Cicero, Tusc. V.97 — Socrates, walking hard till evening, said he did it ‘obsonare ambulando famem’, to cater for his supper by walking. ‘ille’ most probably points to Socrates, not Ereunius.
- ***motum animi sedaret*** — rendered “settle the motion of [his] mind”. motus animi = emotion, agitation, passion; literally ‘motion’. Rendered literally to keep the play on motus, the subject of Dialogue II, from which Ereunius’ head is still ‘inflamed’.
- ***Machina (huius ingentis Machinae)*** — rendered “Machine”. fabric, structure, engine; machina mundi. Lucretian ‘moles et machina mundi’ (V.96); White’s mechanical image of the world with ‘wheels and members’ (rotas & membra).
- ***dissertatio / disputatio / colloquium*** — rendered “discourse / disputation / colloquy”. dissertatio = discussion, dissertation; disputatio = formal academic debate. Kept distinct: the three days’ sessions are called all three.
- ***fabulari / fabulae*** — rendered “chat / talks”. fabula = story, fable, conversation, talk. Here everyday conversation, not ‘fables’.
- ***sales (salibus … perspergebat … condiebant)*** — rendered “salts … seasoned”. sal = salt and wit (Attic salt). Culinary metaphor sustained through the meal: salt, seasoning, salads.
- ***acetaria … lunaribus illis hominibus*** — rendered “salads … for those lunar men”. acetaria = vinegar-dressed vegetables, salads (Pliny NH XIX). Jesting at the Selenites of Dialogue I (heavenly bodies analogous to ours; cf. Kepler’s Somnium, Wilkins’ Discovery of a World in the Moone, 1638).
- ***diverbium*** — rendered “dialogue”. the spoken (as opposed to sung) part of Roman comedy (Livy VII.2). Implies comic repartee.
- ***prandium / coena*** — rendered “midday meal / supper”. prandium = late-morning or midday meal; coena = main evening meal. Time-scheme of the day: morning disputation, garden walk, prandium, fireside, supper, night disputation.
- ***labos*** — rendered “labour”. archaic nominative of labor. Archaism.
- ***scibilia*** — rendered “the knowables”. things capable of being known (scientia). Scholastic scibile = object of science.
- ***comprehendere*** — rendered “comprehend”. grasp, understand; in scholastic usage know exhaustively. Here the general ‘grasp’ of the knowable; rendered consistently.
- ***volupe*** — rendered “pleasurable”. indeclinable archaic adjective (Plautus). Archaism.
- ***fruitio*** — rendered “fruition”. enjoyment, the use of a thing as an end. Augustinian uti/frui (De doctrina christiana I.3–4): frui = to rest in a thing for its own sake; contemplation as the end not asked ‘for what end’ — Aristotle, NE X.7.
- ***obiectum*** — rendered “object”. the object of a power or act. Scholastic: an operation is specified by its object; contemplation is as noble as its object.
- ***perfectam Dei imaginem (universum)*** — rendered “the perfect image of God”. image, likeness, representation. Cf. Plato, Timaeus 92c (the cosmos as εἰκών of the intelligible god); scholastic ‘vestigium’ vs ‘imago’ — White pointedly calls the universe ‘image’, qualified ‘as far as a creature can hold’.
- ***instrumentum / organum*** — rendered “instrument / instruments [organa]”. organum = tool, organ. Glossary keeps organum as ‘instrument [organum]’ with the Latin bracketed to distinguish it.
- ***finis ad quem factus est*** — rendered “the end to which it was made”. factus masc. though instrumentum is neuter. Perhaps attracted to finis or a slip for factum; sense unaffected.
- ***artificis ingenium*** — rendered “the wit of the craftsman”. ingenium = wit, skill, character. Argument from design: from the instrument (the world) to the maker.
- ***creandi instrumenta … Theologi renuant*** — rendered “most Theologians refuse instruments of creating”. renuere = nod refusal, deny. Scholastic thesis: creation cannot be done by means of an instrumental cause (Aquinas, ST I q.45 a.5; against Peter Lombard, Sent. IV d.5).
- ***Angeli (instrumenta)*** — rendered “Angels (as instruments)”. angels as agents moving and governing the world. Cf. the ‘intelligences’ of Dialogue I; White and Digby reinterpret the scholastic movers of the spheres as created agents in the order of second causes.
- ***postquam (causal)*** — rendered “since”. normally ‘after’; causal in late and Neo-Latin.
- ***dilucet*** — rendered “is clear”. impersonal ‘it becomes light’, ‘it is evident’ (Livy).
- ***cogitabundum*** — rendered “thoughtful”. pensive, deep in thought (Gellius).
- ***ludo / illudi*** — rendered “play / be mocked”. illudere = mock, deceive. Wordplay: ‘I do not suffer to be played upon’ — ‘I am not playing’.
- ***e vestigio*** — rendered “on the spot”. instantly; lit. ‘from the footprint’. Idiom.
- ***ansa (ansam nactus)*** — rendered “handle”. occasion, pretext. Classical idiom ansam dare/habere (Cicero).
- ***deprecari*** — rendered “entreat”. beg off, pray to avert; intercede. Asphalius begs Andabata to desist from pressing for the night session.
- ***dementatum / constrictum / philtrum edormisset*** — rendered “driven out of his mind / bound fast / slept off the love-potion”. philtrum = love-potion; edormire = sleep off (crapulam edormire, Cic. Phil. II.30). Mock-erotic jest: Andabata is ‘bewitched’ by philosophy.
- ***summo mane*** — rendered “at the very top of the morning”. first thing in the morning. Idiom (Cicero).
- ***Eques*** — rendered “Horseman”. rider; also knight. Andabata (a blindfolded gladiator on horseback) jokes that he will be in the saddle at dawn — perhaps a play on his own name.
- ***accensis animis*** — rendered “with minds kindled”. with spirits fired. Placed beside ‘ad focum’: the hearth is lit, and so are the minds.

## Translator's notes

- Unit: Dialogue III, Knot i (Introduction), complete in one part. PDF pp. 276–280 = printed pp. 265–269, from the Dialogue III heading and ‘Etiam te & horum meminisse’ (margin ‘Nodus I. Introductio.’, p. 265) to ‘ad quem’ at the top of p. 269; Knot ii begins with Ereunius’ speech ‘Nosti me interrogationibus procedere solitum’ (margin ‘Nodus II. Motum universi a principio externo esse’, p. 269). Offset +11 holds (running heads 266–269; signatures Ll, Ll ij, Ll iij).
- Headings: the dialogue heading reads ‘De Duratione & Gubernatione Mundi’; the FM2 table reads ‘de causis Mundi seu de Principio Gubernatione et interitu ipsius’; the running heads (pp. 267, 269) read ‘de caussis Mundi’.
- Narrative frame: as in Dialogues I and II, the narrator addresses Asphalius in the second person (‘Tu … repetebas’, ‘apud te’); Asphalius is the host. The whole of pp. 265–269 is a single printed paragraph; it is split into rows at sentence breaks so Latin and English align.
- Uncertain readings / constructions: (1) ‘ut dicebat ille’ — ‘ille’ may be Ereunius or ‘that famous man’ (Socrates, Cic. Tusc. V.97); rendered ‘that one’. (2) ‘delineatum’ (neuter sg.) does not agree with ‘rotas & membra’; construed with the indirect question ‘quibus constaret elementis’. (3) ‘finis ad quem factus est’ — masculine ‘factus’ for neuter instrumentum. (4) ‘ut hac nocte quietem … praestolaretur’ — ‘hac nocte’ may go with ‘praestolaretur’ (wait this night) or describe the rest; since the disputation is in fact held that night after supper, the request seems to be to let Ereunius rest first. (5) ‘Equum sibi suum relinquat’ — ‘sibi’/‘suum’ refer to Andabata (subject of ‘mandat’): the servant should leave Andabata his horse and go home himself. (6) No full stop is printed after ‘immiscere’ (p. 268); ‘tot, similia’ has a comma as printed. (7) ‘Equum’ (p. 268) has a damaged initial E; reading certain from the 300-dpi crop.
- Verification pass: sentence-by-sentence re-check of the Latin against the English and of the transcription against the page images (with 300-dpi crops of pp. 265 and 268). 4 corrections: (1) ‘leni’ construed with ‘deambulatione’ (‘with a gentle … walk’), not with ‘solem’; (2) ‘accensis animis’ construed together (‘with minds kindled’), not ‘at the kindled hearth’; (3) ‘inutilem … quae … conferret’ recognised as feminine (contemplation understood) and rendered ‘she … who’; (4) ‘satis uno die esse actum’ rendered as perfect (‘enough had been done’), not present.
- Conventions: as in earlier units — long s normalised, æ/œ/ę → ae/oe, accents dropped (interea, e vestigio, utrimque), u/v normalised (ut, evenit, invicem, Urgebat, servulum, diverbiis), ampersand kept as &; caussas kept as printed; supplied words in [square brackets]; Latin of technical or ambiguous words in [brackets]; printed page breaks marked [p. n] in both columns.
