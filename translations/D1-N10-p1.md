## Dialogue I, Knot x, part 1 (printed pp. 84–92)

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| [p. 84] [Margin: Nodus X. Plura esse in universo per se lucentia.] | [p. 84] [Margin: Knot X. That things shining of themselves [per se lucentia] are in the universe more [than one] [plura].] |
| Ereu. Accipe igitur quid mediter: a tellure distare solem aiunt Astronomi una ex iis partibus quarum 2160 habet semidiameter octavae [p. 85] sphaerae. Perpende modo lumen quod nobis impendit sol etiam summa aestate ubi perpendiculariter imminet. Cogita dum in Cancro se ostendit Syenen habitari, Permiam rigere, et hoc sole excedente terram. Hic folium verte, et quid post peractum millies tantumdem itineris, in Astrum ipso maius acturus sit pensita, rigebunt procul dubio cuncta. Aude totum iter, et refer tecum ab astris ad nos lumen. | Ereu. Receive then what I meditate: that the sun is distant from the earth [tellure], say the Astronomers, by one of those parts of which the semidiameter of the eighth [p. 85] sphere has 2160. Weigh now the light which the sun spends [impendit] upon us even at the height of summer, when it hangs over [us] perpendicularly. Think [that], while it shows itself in Cancer, Syene is inhabited, Permia is stiff [with cold] [rigere], and this with the sun exceeding the earth [in size]. Here turn the leaf, and weigh what, after a thousand times as much of journey has been completed, it will do upon a Star greater than itself: all things will be stiff without doubt. Dare the whole journey, and bring back with you from the stars to us [the] light. |
| Addo quoque cometicae materiei tenuitatem, quam tantum mole oportet crescere, quantum soliditate deficitur, ut lumen aequale remandet. | I add also the thinness [tenuitatem] of the cometic matter [materiei], which must grow in bulk [mole] as much as it falls short [deficitur] in solidity, that it may send back [remandet] equal light. |
| Rursus rationes ad caput institue, positoque solem liberaliter cum Procyone et lyra egisse, adeo ut per tantum spatii nos ex eorum rivulis bibamus; expende tantum lumen quem calorem tostis a vertice infliget Aethiopibus? Neque si quid sapias plus Ereunio negotii facessas. | Again set up the reckonings [rationes] to the head [ad caput], and, it being supposed that the sun has dealt liberally with Procyon and the Lyre, so far that across so much of space we drink from their rivulets; weigh out: so much light, what heat will it inflict on the Ethiopians roasted from the vertex? Nor, if you are at all wise [si quid sapias], would you make more trouble [negotii facessas] for Ereunius. |
| Sed ego ultro indulgeo, et peto si sol tantum a nobis abesset ut vix, et non nisi refractionis auxilio videri posset, quid putas habiturum in nos virium, cum facilior sit visui quam tactui excitando? at si abesset quantum stellae fixae non maior appareret stella sextae magnitudinis quae absque capillitio videri vix aut ne vix posset. | But I of my own accord indulge [you], and ask: if the sun were away from us so much that scarcely, and not except by the help of refraction, could it be seen, what of strength [virium] do you think it would have upon us, since it is easier for sight to be excited than touch [facilior sit visui quam tactui excitando]? but if it were away as much as the fixed stars, it would not appear greater than a star of the sixth magnitude, which without [its] head of hair [capillitio] could be seen scarcely or not even scarcely [vix aut ne vix]. |
| Cum itaque stellae fixae paulo minus ab eo distent, talem iis apparere necesse est, et per consequens longe ab hoc abesse ut lumen ab iis ad nos re-[p. 86]mittat, cum vel inde difficulter spectaretur. | Since therefore the fixed stars are distant a little less from it, it is necessary that it appear such to them, and consequently [that it be] far away from this, that it should send back [re-[p. 86]mittat] light from them to us, since even from there it would be beheld with difficulty. |
| Adde (quod caput est) unde hoc sit, ut et in pari aestimare valeas. Lumen ab igne esse et supra declaratum est, et si indiges authore, de Lycaeo proferam: Ignem vero e materia undequaque spargi qua obex non interiicitur, omnis experientia clamat: vides igitur in orbem (quod in sole quoque experimur) et ex angusto in amplum contendere lucem. | Add (which is the head [caput] [of the matter]) whence this is, that you may be able to judge also in a like [case] [in pari]. That light is from fire both has been declared above, and, if you need an author, I will bring [one] forth from the Lyceum: but that fire is scattered from [its] matter on every side where an obstacle [obex] is not thrown between, all experience cries out: you see therefore that light strains [contendere] in a circle [in orbem] (which we experience also in the sun) and from narrow into wide. |
| Cum itaque omnis ignis et lux eadem in angusto fuit quae postea amplo dominatur aequore, tantum necesse est viribus et intensione deficiat quantum mole et orbe superat. | Since therefore all fire and light was in narrow [space] the same which afterwards lords it over [dominatur] a wide expanse [aequore], it is necessary that it fall short in strength [viribus] and intension [intensione] as much as it surpasses in bulk [mole] and circuit [orbe]. |
| Age nunc, conum cuius in sole sit vertex, expendamus, cuius cum ad tellurem pervenerit par circulo magno terrae sit basis, quantam putamus futuram cum ad fixas pertigerit? Nihil dubium quin in dupla ratione axium futurae sint bases: Sed axes ex supradictis sunt in ratione unius ad 2160. | Come now, let us weigh out a cone whose vertex is in the sun, whose base, when it has come to the earth [tellurem], is equal to the great circle of the earth [terrae]: how great do we think [it] will be when it has reached to the fixed [stars]? [There is] no doubt but that the bases will be in the duplicate ratio [dupla ratione] of the axes: but the axes from the things said above are in the ratio of one to 2160. |
| Lumen itaque et calor solis cum ad Firmamentum delatum fuerit per terram, erit sparsum in eam latitudinem quae illic habebit 4685600 partes qualem unam circulus maximus terrae constituet, et pars Firmamenti aequalis dicto circulo maximo habebit luminis et caloris unam partem quarum circulus maximus terrae habebit 4685600. Aestima modo utrum legere, vel videre tali lumine daretur. | The light therefore and heat of the sun, when it has been carried to the Firmament by way of the earth [per terram], will be scattered into that breadth [latitudinem] which there will have 4,685,600 parts such as the greatest circle of the earth will constitute one [of], and a part of the Firmament equal to the said greatest circle will have of light and heat one part of which [parts] the greatest circle of the earth will have 4,685,600. Judge now whether to read, or to see, would be granted by such light. |
| Andabatae quoniam nihil [p. 87] sine experimentis vult, commendo primum Galilaei de systemate dialogum plenum ingeniosissimis, et iisdem confirmatissimis de luminis quantitate experimentis, quae referre neque angustia temporis patitur, neque si liberet, ea facilitate et evidentia et suavitate qua illic leguntur, exhibere possem. | To Andabata, since he wants nothing [p. 87] without experiments [experimentis], I commend the first dialogue of Galileo on the system, full of most ingenious, and the same [iisdem] most confirmed, experiments on the quantity of light, which neither does the narrowness of time allow [me] to relate, nor, if it pleased [me], could I exhibit [them] with that facility and evidence [evidentia] and sweetness with which they are read there. |
| Asphal. Movisti me fateor neque leviter, aperta enim sunt et fortia quae proposuisti: sed quod me conturbet et anxium teneat hoc est: lumen per conos et conorum bases metiris, fortassis minus accurate. | Asphal. You have moved me, I confess, and not lightly, for open and strong are the things which you have proposed: but what disturbs me and holds [me] anxious is this: you measure light through cones and the bases of cones, perhaps less accurately. |
| Cum enim sol, lunam, vel terram radiis suis percutit, non iis solis ferit qui ab uno puncto, vel mediocri aliqua parte manant, sed iis quae a toto sole spectabili et per lineas rectas agente protruduntur: qui cum a singulis punctis solis in singula puncta hemisphaerii illuminati procedunt, vide quomodo nihil coni habeant, vel si habeant, ii solem pro basi, pro vertice umbrae terrenae verticem, ubi rursus coeunt solares radii habento. | For when the sun strikes the moon or the earth with its rays, it smites [them] not with those alone which flow from one point, or from some middling part, but with those which are thrust forth from the whole sun [as] visible [spectabili] and acting through straight lines: which [rays], since they proceed from single points of the sun into single points of the illuminated hemisphere, see how they have nothing of a cone, or if they have, let them have [habento] the sun for base, for vertex the vertex of the earthly shadow, where the solar rays come together again. |
| Aspice itaque ne alius aliquis metiendi luminis non sit investigandus modus, si tamen aliquis sit. Video enim tam implexum hunc flexum radiorum, ut num in ordines digeri possint, plurimum ambigam. | Look therefore whether some other mode of measuring light is not to be investigated, if yet there be any. For I see this bending [flexum] of the rays [to be] so entangled [implexum], that I very greatly waver [ambigam] whether they can be digested into orders. |
| Ereu. Singulos ergo mihi conos largiris, Asphali, recte subiectos metationi. Vide num et de partibus conorum non idem dicendum [p. 88] sit: nam et ipsi coni sunt si non recti certe scaleni, quorum eadem vel omnino vel quod nos attinet, usque ad insensilem differentiam est ratio, et quoniam omnis illa lux solis, vel integris conis, vel partibus conorum eandem semper rationem retinentibus constet, vide non esse peccatum, et si sit, inde nihil nobis decedere. | Ereu. Single cones, then, you bestow on me, Asphalius, rightly subjected to measuring-out [metationi]. See whether the same is not to be said [p. 88] of the parts of cones too: for they themselves also are cones, if not right [recti] certainly scalene, of which the ratio [ratio] is the same either altogether or, as regards us, up to an insensible difference; and since all that light of the sun consists either of whole cones, or of parts of cones always retaining the same ratio [rationem], see that there is no fault [peccatum], and, if there be, that nothing departs [decedere] from us on that account. |
| [Margin: Lumen cur perpendiculariter plus illuminet.] | [Margin: Why light illuminates more perpendicularly.] |
| Andab. Neque mihi de dialogis illis paradoxis quicquam magni sperandum est, praeterquam enim quod ridiculam illam hypothesim de motu terrae sustinet, audio etiam alios illis inesse turpissimos errores. Et unum quidem insignis quidam et Philosophus et Mathematicus mihi demonstravit, et a Galilaeo equidem pro demonstratione venditum quem ipse coram et demonstratione, et irrefragabilibus experimentis confutavit. Neque miser Simplicius impune tulit assensisse tam apertae fallaciae, quantumvis aliena culpa id fecerit. | Andab. Nor is anything great to be hoped by me from those paradoxical dialogues; for besides that it maintains that ridiculous hypothesis on the motion of the earth, I hear also that other most foul errors are in them. And one [error] indeed a certain distinguished [man], both Philosopher and Mathematician, demonstrated to me, and [one] sold [venditum] by Galileo indeed for a demonstration, which he himself in [my] presence [coram] confuted both by demonstration and by irrefragable experiments. Nor did wretched Simplicius bear [it] unpunished to have assented to so open a fallacy, however much he did it by another's fault. |
| Ereu. Mira narras, Andabata, sed ecce Rhodus, ubi saltus? forsitan enim et ipse in eandem lapsus sim foveam, et bis debitorem me feceris si et meae et ipsius quem honoro ignorantiae consulueris. | Ereu. You tell wonders, Andabata, but behold Rhodes: where [is] the leap? for perhaps I too myself have slipped into the same pit, and you will have made me twice a debtor if you take counsel for [consulueris] both my ignorance and [that] of him whom I honour. |
| Andab. Quoniam ipsa erroris materia non multum extra oleas est, dicto tuo ero audiens. Quaestio erat cur lumen ad perpendiculum [p. 89] incidens plus haberet virium, quam cum idem oblique accideret. | Andab. Since the very matter of the error is not much outside the olives [extra oleas], I will be obedient to your word [dicto tuo ero audiens]. The question was why light falling in to the perpendicular [ad perpendiculum] [p. 89] had more of strength [virium] than when the same fell upon [accideret] [a thing] obliquely. |
| Expressit Galilaeus figuram plurium parallelarum, quas duae lineae secarent, altera ad perpendiculum, altera oblique. Et cum aequales essent, perpendicularis omnes secabat, obliqua paulo plus dimidiam partem. Hinc collegit Galilaeus super lineam obliquam non tantum luminis cadere quantum super perpendicularem. | Galileo expressed a figure of several parallels, which two lines cut, the one to the perpendicular, the other obliquely. And since they were equal, the perpendicular cut all, the oblique a little more [than] the half part. Hence Galileo gathered that upon the oblique line not as much of light falls as upon the perpendicular. |
| [Margin: Quaere in tabula figuram secundam.] | [Margin: Seek in the table the second figure.] |
| Fallaciae hic Galilaeum arguit noster Philosophus, quam ut melius explicem; ecce tibi figuram Galilaei. Addo nunc litterulam E, in intersectione C, D, cum C, E. | Here our Philosopher accuses Galileo of fallacy [Fallaciae … arguit], which that I may better explicate: behold for you the figure of Galileo. I add now the little letter E, at the intersection of C, D with C, E. |
| Imprimis enim dictitabat Philosophus se demonstraturum C, O, esse aeque illuminatam quam est D, C. Cum enim C, D, non sit magis illuminata quam C, E. Et C, I, aequaliter sit illuminata cum C, E, aequaliter toti C, D, illuminata erit. Quod autem sit illuminata quantum C, E, inde constat quod omnes radii qui in C, E, incidunt, iidem pertranseunt C, O. | For first of all the Philosopher kept saying that he would demonstrate C, O to be equally illuminated as D, C is. For since C, D is not more illuminated than C, E. And C, {?I} is illuminated equally with C, E, it will be illuminated equally to the whole C, D. But that it is illuminated as much as C, E, is established [constat] from this, that all the rays which fall in upon C, E, the same pass through C, O. |
| Sic ergo arguit. Radii C, E, implent spatium C, O, tam perfecte quam spatium C, E, sed illuminari aequaliter non est aliud quam radiis aeque impleri: aeque itaque illuminatur spatium C, O, et spatium C, E, et haec quidem est demonstratio apertissima. | Thus therefore he argues. The rays of C, E fill the space C, O as perfectly as the space C, E; but to be illuminated equally is not other than to be equally filled with rays: equally therefore is illuminated the space C, O and the space C, E; and this indeed is a most open demonstration. |
| Porro experientias trahebat ab iis qui montibus inaedificant: si enim lateritio muro vel vallis complanatis muniatur hortus vel vi-[p. 90]varium, sive per campestria, sive per montuosa dirigatur murus, eundem numerum vallorum et laterum poscunt operarii, et ecce ductis his lineis F, G, H, I, K, L, manifestum est cuilibet intuenti. | Further, he drew experiences [experientias] from those who build upon mountains: for if a garden or a vi-[p. 90]varium [vivarium] be fortified with a brick wall or with smoothed stakes [vallis complanatis], whether the wall be directed through flat lands or through mountainous [places], the workmen demand the same number of stakes and of bricks; and behold, these lines F, G, H, I, K, L being drawn, it is manifest to anyone looking on. |
| [Margin: Solutio argumenti contra demonstrationem {?facti}.] | [Margin: Solution of the argument against the demonstration of the {?fact} [facti].] |
| Ereu. Non immerito abs te gratiam poposci, ipsa enim re me ipsum in eodem errore deprehendi. Quod enim de conis luminis proposui Theorema, eidem subiacet calumniae: quam ut diluamus: noveris, Andabata, vel nostri magis magnos esse Philosophos qui eadem argumentatione lineam C, E, linea C, O, non esse minorem efficacissime concludunt, et te quidem consentiente, Andabata, quamquam hanc ipsam lineam, C, O, citra omnem ambiguitatem maiorem credas linea C, E. | Ereu. Not undeservedly did I ask a favour [gratiam] of you, for by the thing itself I have caught myself in the same error. For the Theorem which I proposed about the cones of light lies under the same cavil [calumniae]: which that we may wash away: you should know, Andabata, that even of ours [vel nostri] more great Philosophers are [those] who by the same argumentation most efficaciously conclude that the line C, E is not less than the line C, O, and indeed with you consenting, Andabata, although this very line C, O you believe, short of [citra] all ambiguity, greater than the line C, E. |
| Andab. Ego vero Davus sum, non Oedipus, Aenigmata et Gryphos non satis coniectandi peritus. | Andab. But I am Davus, not Oedipus, not skilled enough in guessing Enigmas and Riddles [Gryphos]. |
| Ereu. Neque ego fallacias necto, sed puram putam rei nuditatem loquor. Quando contra eos disputas qui ex punctis continuum compingunt, non hoc ipso argumento doces quoniam non plura sunt puncta in linea C, O, quam quae descendunt ex C, E, non posse lineam C, O, esse maiorem, et si me consulis perfectissima est demonstratio, et ridiculum quicquid contra moliuntur erroris illius Patro-[p. 91]ni. Sed mutata materia non agnoscis argumentationem. | Ereu. Nor do I weave fallacies, but I speak the pure, mere [puram putam] nakedness of the thing. When you dispute against those who compact the continuum out of points, do you not teach by this very argument that, since there are not more points in the line C, O than those which descend from C, E, the line C, O cannot be greater? and, if you consult me, it is a most perfect demonstration, and ridiculous [is] whatever the Patro-[p. 91]ns of that error contrive against [it]. But with the matter [materia] changed you do not recognise the argumentation. |
| Age igitur hoc mihi expedi putasne lumen nisi totam lineam C, O, occupet, posse illuminare illam quantum C, E, illuminata est? | Come then, unfold [expedi] this for me: do you think that light, unless it occupies the whole line C, O, can illuminate it as much as C, E is illuminated? |
| Andab. Non potest. | Andab. It cannot. |
| Ereu. Sed valli quos formasti totamne lineam occupant? vel si dubitas lineas tuas F, G, H, I, K, L, cum subiectis partibus lineae C, O, confer et vide utrae longiores? | Ereu. But the stakes [valli] which you have formed, do they occupy the whole line? or, if you doubt, compare your lines F, G, H, I, K, L with the parts of the line C, O lying under [them], and see which [utrae] [are] longer? |
| Andab. Partes lineae C, O, sunt aperte longiores. | Andab. The parts of the line C, O are openly longer. |
| Ereu. Multiplica sine numero partes tuas F, G. Numquid longiores evadent, vel linea quae ex eis conflatur, quamdiu parallelae sunt lineae D, C. | Ereu. Multiply without number your parts F, G. Will they turn out [evadent] longer, or the line which is blown together [conflatur] out of them, as long as they are parallel to the line D, C. |
| Andab. Nunquam erunt, nisi absolute in infinitas abierint: et tunc tanta est confusio, ut nihil certi vel affirmari queat, vel deferri, et tu, ut video, inferes; ergo lumen nisi per infinita scissum sit, nunquam lineam {?D}, O, totam illuminabit; sed tantum illius quantum lineae C, E, erit aequale, reliquum vero interstitiis tenebrosis continebitur. | Andab. Never will they be, unless they have gone off absolutely into infinite [parts] [in infinitas]: and then so great is the confusion, that nothing certain can either be affirmed or be reported [deferri]; and you, as I see, will infer: therefore light, unless it be cut through infinite [parts], will never illuminate the whole line {?D}, O; but only so much of it as will be equal to the line C, E, while the remainder will be held in shadowy interstices. |
| Ereu. Quod res est dicis, et vallos tuos inaequalis longitudinis esse advertis, (ut lateribus occupent, qua basibus deficiunt) et vel ex hoc [p. 92] vide Metaphysicas considerationes alterius esse naturae quam Physicas et Mathematicas; quae testimonium quidem ingens perhibent Metaphysicis, sed ad Metaphysica pro dignitate tractanda ineptae sunt. | Ereu. You say what the thing is, and you notice that your stakes [vallos] are of unequal length (so that they occupy by [their] sides [lateribus] where they fall short by [their] bases), and even from this [p. 92] see that Metaphysical considerations are of another nature than Physical and Mathematical [ones]; which [latter] indeed furnish huge testimony to Metaphysical [things], but are unfit for treating Metaphysical [things] according to [their] dignity. |
| Est autem ipsa Quanti compositio considerationis trans naturam petendae, et quantum Mathematicae obiectum Physico obiecto abstractius; est itaque eius indoles ex praesuppositis utrique scientiae, et non ab iis seu eorum principiis petenda. Et eiusmodi sunt pleraque quae Aristoteles in 8. libris Physicorum docet. | But the very composition of the Quantum is of a consideration to be sought beyond nature [trans naturam], and [is] the quantum, the object of Mathematics, more abstract than the Physical object; its disposition [indoles] therefore is to be sought from the things presupposed to both sciences, and not from them or their principles. And of such sort are most of the things which Aristotle teaches in the 8 books of the Physics. |
| Unde est quod magni viri, sed Mathematicis et Physicis solummodo imbuti, cum ad tractandam continui, rari et densi, et infiniti, et loci temporisque naturam, et similia aggrediuntur, inextricandis sese laqueis implicant, et confundi rationis dictamina patiantur. | Whence it is that great men, but imbued only with Mathematical and Physical [things], when they approach to treating the nature of the continuum, of rare and dense, and of the infinite, and of place and time, and similar [things], entangle themselves in inextricable snares, and suffer the dictates [dictamina] of reason to be confounded. |
| Ut ergo ad demonstrationem tuam redeam, vides lineam C, O, aequali lumine vel aequali linea luminis cum linea C, E, non illuminari, demissionemque illam radiorum quam cogitat arguens nequaquam applicari posse. Asseres quoque seu vallos non longiorem lineam implere quam sit C, E, et exemplis illis disci aliquid ad architecturam non ineptum, sed Philosophiae impar. | That I may return therefore to your demonstration, you see that the line C, O is not illuminated with equal light, or with an equal line of light, with the line C, E, and that that letting-down [demissionem] of the rays which the arguer thinks of can by no means be applied. [You see] also [that] the poles [Asseres], or stakes, fill no longer a line than C, E is, and [that] by those examples something is learned not unfit for architecture, but unequal [impar] to Philosophy. |

## Words and phrases with alternative or special meanings

- ***capillitium*** — rendered “head of hair”. the 'hair' or fringe of rays round a star; cf. Galileo's 'adventitious rays' (irradiazione) Telescopic argument: stars seen without their rays are tiny (Sidereus nuncius 1610; Dialogo, Day 3).
- ***dupla ratio*** — rendered “duplicate ratio”. not 'double ratio' (2:1) but the ratio squared (Euclid V def. 9) Areas of cone bases are as the squares of the axes; hence 2160² parts.
- ***2160 / 4685600*** — rendered “2160 / 4,685,600”.  Sun–earth distance taken as 1/2160 of the radius of the eighth sphere. 2160² = 4,665,600; the printed 4685600 (twice) is an arithmetical or printing slip.
- ***semidiameter octavae sphaerae*** — rendered “semidiameter of the eighth sphere”. radius of the sphere of fixed stars Ptolemaic/Tychonic cosmography.
- ***Syene / Permia*** — rendered “Syene / Permia”. Aswan under the Tropic of Cancer; Perm region of northern Russia Classic examples of the hottest and coldest inhabited places.
- ***Procyon, lyra*** — rendered “Procyon, the Lyre”. Lyra = the bright star Vega Bright fixed stars taken as other suns.
- ***ad caput (rationes instituere)*** — rendered “to the head”. back to the beginning / to the main point / for the [comet's] head Accounting idiom 'rationes'; sense of 'ad caput' uncertain.
- ***quod caput est*** — rendered “which is the head”. the chief point Common Latin idiom.
- ***de Lycaeo*** — rendered “from the Lyceum”.  = an Aristotelian authority (Aristotle, De anima II.7 on light and fire).
- ***intensio*** — rendered “intension”. intensity, degree Scholastic intension and remission of forms.
- ***orbis*** — rendered “circuit / circle”. orb; extent Here the spreading circle of light, not the celestial orb.
- ***Galilaei de systemate dialogus primus*** — rendered “the first dialogue of Galileo on the system”. the First Day of the Dialogo sopra i due massimi sistemi (1632; Latin Systema cosmicum 1635) Day 1 discusses the light of the moon and reflection from rough surfaces.
- ***metatio*** — rendered “measuring-out”. laying out, surveying (of a camp) Rare word; cone-by-cone measurement.
- ***scalenus (conus)*** — rendered “scalene”. oblique cone vs rectus conus (right cone).
- ***habento*** — rendered “let them have”.  Archaic future imperative, legal style.
- ***dialogi paradoxi*** — rendered “paradoxical dialogues”.  Galileo's Dialogo; Andabata is the conservative Peripatetic.
- ***Simplicius*** — rendered “Simplicius”.  Galileo's Aristotelian interlocutor, shown assenting to the oblique-light argument.
- ***ecce Rhodus, ubi saltus?*** — rendered “behold Rhodes: where [is] the leap?”.  Aesop's boaster: 'Hic Rhodus, hic salta' = prove it here and now.
- ***extra oleas*** — rendered “outside the olives”. beyond bounds; off the subject Proverb from the olive trees marking the end of the racecourse.
- ***dicto audiens esse*** — rendered “be obedient to [your] word”.  Plautine/Ciceronian idiom.
- ***fallaciae arguere*** — rendered “accuse of fallacy”.  Genitive of the charge.
- ***vallus, valli / asser*** — rendered “stake / pole”. vallum = rampart; asser = beam, pole Fence-posts on a slope: horizontal projection argument.
- ***later, lateres / latera*** — rendered “bricks / sides”.  Pun: 'lateribus occupent' = they fill with their sides (or with bricks).
- ***vivarium*** — rendered “vivarium”. game park, fish-pond, enclosure for live animals
- ***Davus sum, non Oedipus*** — rendered “I am Davus, not Oedipus”.  Terence, Andria 194: the slave cannot solve riddles.
- ***gryphus*** — rendered “riddle”.  Greek γρῖφος, a riddle (not griffin).
- ***puram putam*** — rendered “pure, mere”.  Archaic doublet (as before).
