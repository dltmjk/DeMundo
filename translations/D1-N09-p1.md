## Dialogue I, Knot ix, part 1 (printed pp. 69–78)

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| [p. 69] [Margin: Nodus IX. De Cometarum generatione & natura.] | [p. 69] [Margin: Knot IX. On the generation [generatione] and nature of the Comets.] |
| Andab. Quid etiamne barbas Cometis et has a Sole aversas plerumque in recta linea, nonnunquam devias, motu neque linea, neque uno tenore constanti, et tot figuris variegatas? Sed neque eadem una figura contentas, cum interdum eadem recta, alias incurvata appareat? hascine tu credas in caussas reducere posse diversitates, si nostri similia patiantur Coeli? cum apud nos his paria nusquam contemplemur. | Andab. What, [do you say] even the beards [barbas] for the Comets, and these turned away from the Sun mostly in a straight line, sometimes devious, constant neither in motion nor in line nor in one tenor [tenore], and variegated with so many figures? But not content even with one same figure, since sometimes the same [beard] appears straight, at other times curved? these diversities, would you believe [yourself] able to reduce [them] into causes, if the Heavens suffer things similar to ours [nostri]? since among us we nowhere contemplate things equal to these. |
| Ereu. Non ego polliceor me cunctis phaenomenis satisfacere posse. Astronomorum enim haec opera sunt quorum ego discipulus non socius audire ambio. Illorum dictis fidum auctorem, inventis non incredulum praefero, ipse neque unquam Coelum intuitus neque Machinarum quibus utuntur vim callens, naturales tantummodo qualitates insectatus, ut hactenus vides. | Ereu. I do not promise that I can satisfy all the phenomena. For these are the works of the Astronomers, of whom I canvass [ambio] to be called [audire] a disciple, not a fellow [socius]. To their sayings I show [praefero] [myself] a faithful voucher [auctorem], to their findings not an unbeliever, [being] myself one who has neither ever gazed upon the Heaven nor is versed in the force of the Machines [Machinarum] which they use, having pursued [insectatus] only natural qualities, as you see thus far. |
| Quid autem eventurum sit, si Coeli nostris similia corpora habeant, mecum contemplare. Solem certe in Planetarum regno dominari vides, in tantum ut coniectis in Saturnum ipsum telis nos resultu feriat. Potens itaque toto illo regno est, et quoniam sic est ipsius indoles, ex his omnibus [p. 70] corporibus excitat Atomorum examina quae aliquando conglomerata, vastissimas quasdam nubes exhibent. | But what would come about, if the Heavens have bodies similar to ours, contemplate with me. The Sun certainly you see to be lord in the kingdom of the Planets, so far that, its darts [telis] having been thrown against Saturn itself, it strikes us by the rebound [resultu]. Powerful therefore it is in that whole kingdom, and since such is its disposition [indoles], out of all these [p. 70] bodies it stirs up swarms of Atoms which, sometimes conglomerated, exhibit certain most vast clouds. |
| Esto itaque ingens talis nebula (non enim hoc negabis non posse, non contingere) ingens nos versum latus extendens, alia vero quaqua versum, pro caussarum fortuito complexu. Feriat obiectum nobis latus Sol qua necesse est violentia. Dubitas an lumen in nos reflectat? | Let there be therefore a huge such mist [nebula] (for you will not deny this, that it cannot [be], [or] that it does not happen) stretching out a huge side toward us, but other [sides] whithersoever, according to the fortuitous embrace [complexu] of causes. Let the Sun strike the side thrown before [obiectum] us with the violence with which it is necessary. Do you doubt whether it reflects light onto us? |
| Interroga nubes candidas et depictas quas toties omnibus fere Coeli plagis volitantes aspicimus, quarum quamlibet unam fac ad perpendiculum a nobis discedere, non vides minorem semper et minorem evadere. | Question the white and painted clouds which so often we behold flying about in almost all the tracts [plagis] of the Heaven; of which make any one you please go away from us along the perpendicular [ad perpendiculum]: do you not see [it] turn out [evadere] smaller always and smaller. |
| Adde itaque mille irregularitates figurae; nonne eas necesse est ante medium corpus expirare? vehatur itaque donec Lunae vel dimidiae speciem non excedat, nonne clarum tibi est defectis omnibus figurae extantiis in rotundum nitidissimumque corpus evasurum? Habes itaque formatam Stellam. | Add therefore a thousand irregularities of figure; is it not necessary that these expire before the middle body? let it be carried, therefore, until it does not exceed the appearance [speciem] of the Moon or [of the Moon] halved, is it not clear to you that, all the standings-out [extantiis] of the figure having failed, it will turn out into a round and most shining body? You have therefore a formed Star. |
| Si exemplum poscis, a Venere mutuor quae nobis rotundula, per tubum collimantes mobili per varias species facie illudit. | If you demand an example, I borrow [it] from Venus, which, [appearing] roundish to us, mocks [illudit] us aiming [collimantes] through the tube with a face mobile through various appearances [species]. |
| Nunc quoniam nebulam hanc diximus Solis spiculis penetrabilem, cogita radios qui non reflectuntur ab ipsa superficie ipsi corpori nebulae insinuatos, etiam illinc ad nos reverberari; erit itaque luminis longus sulcus a corpore illo humido ad nos repulsus, quem vides ex figura et situ [p. 71] huiusmodi corporis tot modis variabili quamlibet fere speciem ad nos remandare posse: ut iam quaerendum non sit quare tot sint Cometarum species; sed quare non sint plures. | Now since we have said this mist [is] penetrable by the little darts [spiculis] of the Sun, think that the rays which are not reflected from the surface itself, [being] insinuated into the very body of the mist, are also from there beaten back [reverberari] to us; there will be therefore a long furrow [sulcus] of light driven back to us from that humid body, which you see can send back [remandare] to us almost any appearance [speciem] you please, from the figure and site [situ] [p. 71] of a body of this kind, variable in so many modes: so that now it is not to be asked why there are so many appearances [species] of Comets, but why there are not more. |
| Habes itaque cur stellatum caput? cur aversa barba, et latam varietatis concinnandae et Cinnos pro modo comendi ex opticis rationibus disciplinam. | You have therefore why the head [is] starred? why the beard [is] turned away, and a broad discipline, out of optical reasons [rationibus], of arranging [concinnandae] the variety and of dressing [comendi] the Curls [Cinnos] according to measure [pro modo]. |
| Andab. Vis ergo ut credam Solem a tam tenui corpore reflecti, quem video totum illud clarum et luminosum facere debere? Vis rursus a penetrabili corpore ad nos reiici lucida ut ille vocat tela diei? Neque nubes tuas flocci facio: eas enim nequaquam esse pellucidas obtenebratus Sol poene integras hebdomadas docet. [Margin: Lumen etiam a corpore penetrabili reflecti.] | Andab. Do you wish then that I believe the Sun to be reflected from so thin a body, [the Sun] which I see ought to make that whole [body] clear and luminous? Do you wish, again, that from a penetrable body there be thrown back to us the bright darts of day [lucida … tela diei], as that [poet] calls [them]? Nor do I value your clouds at a lock of wool [flocci facio]: for that these are by no means pellucid, the Sun darkened for almost entire weeks teaches. [Margin: That light is reflected even from a penetrable body.] |
| Ereu. Non tantus sum, Andabata, ut ad pileatos nubibus montes in ictu oculi te transferam; alioqui manibus tangeres quae dico, et in mediis nubibus existens sine pluvia madesceres, moxque paulo inferius cecidisse pluviam videns te nubibus immixtum et solem vidisse quem nubes vallibus occuluerant, intra ipsam nubem experireris. | Ereu. I am not so great, Andabata, that I might transfer you in the twinkling [ictu] of an eye to mountains capped [pileatos] with clouds; otherwise you would touch with [your] hands the things which I say, and, existing in the midst of clouds, you would grow wet without rain, and soon, seeing that rain had fallen a little lower down, you would experience [experireris] that you, mingled with the clouds, had also seen the sun which the clouds had hidden from the valleys, within the cloud itself. |
| Nunc itaque accipe vitrum planum duorum, vel trium digitorum crassitie latum, lateribus parallelis, et si inveneris, expolitis, certe quam potes aequatissimis. Huic aqua impleto obiice, vel Solem, vel candelam accensam, et nisi apparuerit tibi reper- [p. 72] cussus in oculos fulgor, mendacem me habeto. | Now therefore take a flat glass [vitrum], broad with a thickness of two or three fingers, with parallel sides, and, if you shall have found [one], polished, certainly as most even as you can. To this, filled with water, throw before [it] either the Sun or a lighted candle, and unless there shall have appeared to you a brightness [fulgor] beaten [p. 72] back into the eyes, hold me a liar. |
| Obscurato deinde caetera cubiculo, si non ipsum luminis tramitem per totam aquam tuis oculis notare valeas, maxime si eum vel angustaveris, vel interpolaveris, me habeto obnoxium. | Then, the chamber being darkened in other respects [caetera], if you be not able to mark with your eyes the very path [tramitem] of the light through the whole water, especially if you shall have either narrowed it or interpolated [interpolaveris] it, hold me liable [obnoxium]. |
| Nunquam in nebulosis his noctibus cum stantem quodammodo ingredientes secamus imbrem, occurrit tibi candelam vel ignem ex occluso radios suos in nebulam eiicientem conspicari? Et caput et caudam Cometae, sed hoc maxime ex penetrabili corpore reflexos radios ultra omnem disputationem haberes. | Has it never befallen you, on these misty nights, when, walking in, we cut through a rain that in a manner stands [still], to catch sight of a candle or a fire from an enclosed [place] [ex occluso] throwing its rays out into the mist? You would have both the head and the tail of a Comet, but this [i.e. the tail] especially, rays reflected from a penetrable body, beyond all disputation. |
| Tandem vel hoc cogita cum atomos in ipsis Solis radiis conspicaris ludentes, nisi ab iis oculi ferirentur, eos non fore conspicuos. Nunquid quia hi toto communes aere volitant aspectum nobis impediunt? vel non radios transmittit Sol? | Lastly, think at least this, when you catch sight of atoms playing in the very rays of the Sun, that unless the eyes were struck by them, they would not be conspicuous. Is it that, because these fly about common [communes] in the whole air, they hinder the sight [aspectum] for us? or does the Sun not send its rays across? |
| vide lucidissimam Crystallum quae in modica spissitudine fallit oculos neque permittit se trans lumen videri. Eadem si crassior reddatur, paulatim tenebras obiicit, et si augeatur crassities etiam opaca fit. | see the most lucid Crystal, which in a moderate thickness [spissitudine] deceives the eyes and does not permit itself to be seen across the light. The same, if it be rendered thicker, little by little throws darkness before [the eyes], and if the thickness [crassities] be increased, it becomes even opaque. |
| [Margin: Cometas non esse igneos.] Andab. In caeteris nihil moror. Sed in Cometa nova est quaerenda ratio, quem igneum esse hactenus sapientes existimaverunt. | [Margin: That Comets are not fiery.] Andab. In the other [points] I make no delay [nihil moror]. But in the Comet a new reason [ratio] is to be sought, which the wise have hitherto esteemed to be fiery. |
| Ereu. Coegit eos iam praeconcepta Coelorum Divinitas, cui quicquam mortale miscere profanum [p. 73] duxerunt Peripatetici. At si, quae ipsum confitetur coelum sereniori oculo fuissent intuiti, aliud profecto tradidissent. | Ereu. The already preconceived Divinity of the Heavens compelled them, with which to mix anything mortal the Peripatetics deemed profane. [p. 73] But if they had gazed with a more serene eye upon the things which the heaven itself confesses, they would assuredly have handed down something else. |
| Si ignem examinavissent, eum qui in tenui materia est subitaneum et brevissimae vitae comperissent, eum vero qui diuturnus est, in gravi aliqua materia, et quod inde fit, in terra continuo nixu decumbentem cogitavissent. | If they had examined fire, they would have found that [fire] which is in thin matter [to be] sudden and of the briefest life, but that which is long-lasting they would have thought [to be] in some heavy matter, and (what comes about from that) lying down on the earth [terra] with continual striving [nixu]. |
| Rursus cum stellas per ipsum caput Cometae transmicantes admirarentur, non iis in mentem venisset non alio magis luminosi conspectum, quam interposita flamma confundi? | Again, when they wondered at the stars twinkling through [transmicantes] the very head of the Comet, would it not have come into their mind that the sight [conspectum] of a luminous [thing] is confounded by nothing more than by a flame interposed? |
| Praeterea cum moveri flammam velocissimo raptu crederent non in oppositum motus iactari Pyramides expectarent? Non fumos e tanta flamma, et quidem nonnunquam non nimium clara, maiores quam a sole et flammam aliqua deferentes spectaremus? | Moreover, when they believed the flame to be moved with a most swift rapture [raptu], would they not expect the Pyramids [Pyramides] [of flame] to be tossed toward the opposite of the motion? Would we not behold fumes from so great a flame, and that sometimes not over-clear, greater than from the sun [i.e. than those the sun raises], and carrying down some flame? |
| Tandem in ea tenuitate materiei tantam accendi flammam posse, ab exemplis domi natis, credere est impossibile. | Lastly, that so great a flame can be kindled in that thinness of matter [materiei], it is impossible, from examples born at home [domi natis], to believe. |
| Motum modo Cometarum quod attinet: tres in medium propono, diurnum quem et raptus vocant, cuius speciales caussae frustra peterentur, cum eaedem futurae sint quae corporis in quo Cometa collocatus est. | Now as regards the motion of the Comets: I propose three into the middle [in medium]: the diurnal, which they also call [that] of the rapture [raptus], whose special causes would be sought in vain, since they will be the same as [those] of the body in which the Comet is placed. |
| [Margin: Cometis motum in oppositum Solis non esse necessarium.] Alius videtur in diversum Solis poni seu posse, seu debere. Clarum enim est si nascitur Cometa ex luce Solis nobis a vapore reddita, lex autem reflexionis petit angulos [p. 74] aequales ad reflectentem superficiem, alter autem terminus mutationem patiatur, locum in quo videtur etiam mutari debere. | [Margin: That a motion into the opposite of the Sun is not necessary for Comets.] Another seems to be placed, either able or obliged [seu posse, seu debere] [to be], toward the contrary [in diversum] of the Sun. For it is clear, if a Comet is born from the light of the Sun rendered to us by a vapour, and the law of reflexion seeks angles [p. 74] equal to the reflecting surface, and the other term [terminus] suffers a change, that the place in which it is seen ought also to be changed. |
| Ego tamen nihil necessitatis in hoc discursu sentio. Neque exempla reflexionum ex fluctibus et nubibus quae Irim formant quicquam cogunt. In eo itaque dissimilia contendo, quod mare et nubes Iridis genitrix campum latum habent per quem varietur terminatio visualium radiorum. Cometa vero brevissimum et qui notabili diversitati non sit satis. | I, however, feel nothing of necessity in this discourse. Nor do the examples of reflexions from the waves and from the clouds which form the Rainbow [Irim] compel anything. In this, therefore, I contend [them to be] dissimilar, that the sea and the cloud, mother of the Rainbow [Iridis], have a broad field through which the termination of the visual rays may be varied; but the Comet a most brief [field], and one which would not be enough for a notable diversity. |
| Ut itaque possibilitatem sortiatur haec machinatio, supponi deberet totum aetherem qua incedit vel incedere videtur Cometa, nube Cometica instratum esse, eam autem nobis tantummodo partem apparere qua Cometa proficiscitur. Ex utroque autem capite fidei incapax reddor. | In order, therefore, that this contrivance [machinatio] may obtain possibility, it ought to be supposed that the whole aether, where the Comet advances or seems to advance, is strewn over with a Cometic cloud, but that that part only appears to us where the Comet sets out. But on either head [capite] I am rendered incapable of belief. |
| Nam si tam immensa vaporum copia cogeretur, ut tantam Coeli partem obduceret, hoc imprimis sciscitandum est, Cur non frequentiores Cometae appareant? cum millesima pars talis materiei Cometae sufficiendo, si situm debitum habeat, abunde sit, situm autem levissimo motu habere contingat. | For if so immense a store of vapours were gathered as to cover over so great a part of the Heaven, this is first of all to be inquired: Why do Comets not appear more frequent? since the thousandth part of such matter [materiei] would be abundantly [enough] for supplying a Comet, if it have the due site [situm], and it may happen [contingat] that it have the site by a most light motion. |
| Reddenda rursus ratio est, cur Sol non totam illuminet vel certe tota lumen ad nos non reiiciat. Certe si non ad unam telluris plagam, saltem ad aliam, ut eodem tempore a pluribus in diversis locis spectetur, Iridis mo- [p. 75] do vel reflexionis ex aqua. | Again, a reason [ratio] is to be rendered why the Sun does not illuminate the whole [cloud], or certainly why the whole [cloud] does not throw back light to us. Certainly, if not to one tract [plagam] of the earth [telluris], at least to another, so that it might be beheld at the same time by many in diverse places, after the man- [p. 75] ner of the Rainbow or of reflexion from water. |
| Tandem nubi vel parieti illuminato propior est Cometa quam speculo. Illa vero quantumvis ex una parte illuminationem recipiant in omnem eandem amandant. Quod nisi Cometa faciat ex una tantum parte, sicut dicta Phaenomena, spectabitur. | Lastly, the Comet is nearer to an illuminated cloud or wall than to a mirror. But those [i.e. cloud and wall], however much they receive illumination from one part, send it away [amandant] into every [part]. Which unless the Comet does, it will be beheld from one part only, like the said Phenomena. |
| Praeterquam quod si verae sunt quae de Cometis extant historiae, nulla ad Solem lege, hic adversus solem motus cohibetur. Et hic itaque motus reliquum integrat. | Besides the fact that, if the histories which are extant about Comets are true, this motion against the sun is held in [cohibetur] by no law [relative] to the Sun. And so this motion also makes up [integrat] the remaining [one] [reliquum]. |
| Cuius cum nullus ordo nulla periodus existat in exleges etiam authores referendus est. Etenim appensos inani floccos quis iudicet alias subire vectationis suae regulas quam apud nos plumae, et festucae habent, vel etiam nubes, ventis videlicet obviis huc illuc differri sistive nisi fortassis ipse Cometa non aliud quam ventus coelestis sit motu proprio immania illa aequora percellens. | Of which, since no order, no period exists, it is to be referred also to lawless [exleges] authors. For who would judge that flocks [floccos] hung in the void [inani] undergo other rules of their carriage [vectationis] than feathers and straws have among us, or even clouds: namely, to be carried apart hither and thither, or stopped, by winds meeting [them]? unless perhaps the Comet itself be nothing other than a celestial wind, with its own motion smiting [percellens] those monstrous plains [aequora]. |
| Hinc alias in principio, alias in fine, alias in medio concitatior motus. Hinc flexuosus sine occasione. | Hence the motion [is] at times more roused [concitatior] in the beginning, at times in the end, at times in the middle. Hence [it is] winding without occasion. |
| Asphal. Monet hic quem meministi flexus de alio, de quo sensum tuum discupio. Is est curvatura quam caudae vocant, res inter Astrologos anceps, et quae labore tuo digna sit. [Margin: Unde flexura caudae Cometarum.] | Asphal. This bending [flexus] which you have mentioned admonishes [me] of another, about which I greatly desire [discupio] your sense [sensum]. It is the curvature which they call [that] of the tail, a thing doubtful [anceps] among the Astrologers, and one which would be worthy of your labour. [Margin: Whence the bending [flexura] of the tail of the Comets.] |
| Ereu. Meo Asphali? immo magnorum illorum Magistrorum qui ad hoc nati sunt, et principum thesauros ad experimenta facienda [p. 76] sibi apertos habent. | Ereu. Of my [labour], Asphalius? nay, of [the labour of] those great Masters who are born for this, and have the treasuries of princes [p. 76] open to them for making experiments [experimenta]. |
| Quid me alloqueris hominem qui nunquam vel quadrantem tractavi Geometrice, Coelum nunquam Mathematice inspexi, ad calculos Astronomicos, si quando occurrunt inhorresco. Nec oculos, nec otium, nec opes, nec operas, neque satis firmam voluntatem habeam, ad alia omnia usque a teneris assuetus. | Why do you address me, a man who never handled even a quadrant [quadrantem] Geometrically, never inspected the Heaven Mathematically, [who] shudder at Astronomical calculations, if ever they occur? I would have [habeam] neither eyes, nor leisure, nor wealth, nor labourers [operas], nor a will firm enough, [being] accustomed from [my] tender [years] to all other things. |
| Et de quaestione quidem quam proponis, mentem meam supra insinuavi, cum dicerem mirandum potius cur non plures essent Cometarum figurae quam cur tot existant. | And indeed about the question which you propose, I have insinuated [insinuavi] my mind above, when I said it was rather to be wondered why there were not more figures of the Comets than why so many exist. |
| Sed quoniam expressius desideras, prehende mente caudam esse radios per corpus diaphanum spissum perreptantes. Non statim apparet refundi ad nos, ita ut certe non omnes per perpendiculum? refringi itaque caeteros necessum esse? Vides iam flexum necessarium. | But since you desire [it] more expressly, grasp with the mind that the tail is rays creeping through [perreptantes] a thick [spissum] diaphanous body. Does it not at once appear that [they] are poured back [refundi] to us, in such a way that certainly not all [come] through the perpendicular? that therefore the others must necessarily be refracted [refringi]? You see now the bending [flexum] [is] necessary. |
| Cur hac vel illac, cur tantus, vel talis ab Opticorum scrupulosis regulis petendum erit, quas hic commemorare et in Astronomorum irrumpere scholam non est mihi propositum. | Why this way or that, why so great or of such a kind, will have to be sought from the scrupulous little rules [regulis] of the Opticians, which to recount here, and to burst into the school of the Astronomers, is not my purpose. |
| Andab. Ast ego quae dixisti, quibus experimentis dixeris postulo: sine his nihil ad me actum cogita. | Andab. But I demand by what experiments [experimentis] you have said the things which you have said: without these, think that nothing has been done as regards me [ad me]. |
| Ereu. Ferus es, Andabata, qui nihil ratione agi vis, omnia per lancem et libram. Verum si adeo certa crederem quam multa sunt quae de Cometis narrantur, abunde tibi satis dare [p. 77] valerem. | Ereu. You are savage [ferus], Andabata, who wish nothing to be done by reason [ratione], everything by the scale-pan and the balance [per lancem et libram]. But if I believed [them] as certain as the things related about Comets are many, I should [p. 77] be able abundantly to give you satisfaction [satis dare]. |
| Nunc negligentia observatorum, vel instrumentorum fallacia, vel consuetudine trahendi ad praeceptas rationes ipsa Phaenomena, anxium me habes quomodo petitioni tuae iustissimae respondeam. | Now, through the negligence of the observers, or the fallacy of the instruments, or the custom of dragging the Phenomena themselves to preconceived [praeceptas] reasons [rationes], you have me anxious how I may answer your most just petition. |
| Ferculum itaque unicum tibi offero, sed, nisi fallor, grati saporis. Nam neque instrumentis Astronomicis indiget, et tam notorium est ut nisi malam fidem imponere viro probo velimus, vix dubitari de eo possit. [Margin: Experimentum Cometae ex Fromondo.] | A single dish [ferculum], therefore, I offer you, but, unless I am deceived, of a pleasing savour. For it neither needs Astronomical instruments, and it is so notorious that, unless we wish to impute bad faith to an upright man, it can hardly be doubted about. [Margin: An experiment [experimentum] of a Comet out of Fromondus.] |
| Viderat duodecima Decembris, mane barbam Cometae sub plaustri rotis (erat annus 1618.) Libertus Fromondus: eandem noctu requirebat. Media nox erat et nihil comparebat. | Libertus Fromondus had seen, on the twelfth of December, in the morning, the beard of the Comet under the wheels of the Wain [plaustri] (the year was 1618): the same he was looking for again by night. It was midnight and nothing was to be seen [comparebat]. |
| Post mediam noctem cum Sol et Cometa qui Solem paululum antecedebat, reverti ad horizontem ortivum inciperent, Barba iuxta plaustrum paulatim redire et formari coepit, tenuissimo primum et albente tractu, ut plane matutina crepuscula solent, qui tamen sensim sumebat incrementum et incendebatur quo propius Cometae caput et Sol ad ortum accederent. | After midnight, when the Sun and the Comet, which a little preceded the Sun, began to turn back to the rising [ortivum] horizon, the Beard began little by little to return and be formed beside the Wain, at first with a most thin and whitening track [tractu], plainly as the morning twilights are wont, which [track] however was by degrees taking increase and was being kindled the nearer the head of the Comet and the Sun approached to the rising. |
| Habes quantum memoria patitur, ipsamet Authoris verba ex Meteorologica caussarum barbae speculatione. | You have, as far as memory suffers, the very words of the Author, out of the Meteorological speculation of the causes of the beard. |
| [Margin: Corollaria quaedam ex eo.] Colligit barbam e vaporibus qui spiculis Solaribus per caput Cometae transadactis confossi erant, esse procreatam. | [Margin: Certain corollaries out of it.] He gathers [colligit] that the beard was procreated out of vapours which had been pierced through by Solar little darts [spiculis] driven across through the head of the Comet. |
| Ego aliquid amplius, Cometam sub sole fuisse, barbam intra umbram terrae, et denique quomodo Cometae locus exa- [p. 78] cte haberi possit modo experimentum exacte capiatur. | I [gather] something more: that the Comet was under the sun, the beard within the shadow of the earth, and finally how the place of the Comet can be had exa- [p. 78] ctly, provided only the experiment [experimentum] be taken exactly. |
| Vides enim, si in linea recta Sol et Cometae caput et cauda stiterunt. Illa vero recta cum Sol primo caudam afflavit, radebat terram. i. contingens erat, et necdum caput super horisontem: erat itaque caput inter Solem et terram, postquam contingens linea eo altior est quo magis a contactu dissidens. | For you see whether the Sun and the head and the tail of the Comet stood in a straight line. But that straight [line], when the Sun first breathed upon [afflavit] the tail, was grazing the earth, i.e. it was touching [contingens], and the head [was] not yet above the horizon: the head therefore was between the Sun and the earth, since [postquam] the touching line [contingens linea] is the higher the more [it is] standing apart [dissidens] from the contact. |
| Deinde cum Sol per motum circa terram postmodum illustraverit Syrma quod prius erat tenebrosum, patet interiectam terram lumen Solis a Syrmate inhibuisse. | Then, since the Sun, through its motion around the earth, afterwards illumined the Train [Syrma] which before was dark, it is evident that the earth thrown between [interiectam] held back the light of the Sun from the Train. |
| Tandem si hora et locus caudae praecise annotata fuissent, in prima afflatione. Triangulum quod Solem, Stellam sub qua erat cauda, et oculum in punctis extremis habebat, vel certe triangulum ex centro terrae ad Solem et Stellam quam subibat cauda, notum fuisset; notis videlicet omnibus lineis. Quare et triangulum quod Solem centrum et caudam in extremis habet. | Lastly, if the hour and the place of the tail had been noted precisely, at the first breathing-upon [afflatione]. The triangle which had the Sun, the Star under which the tail was, and the eye in its extreme points, or certainly the triangle from the centre of the earth to the Sun and to the Star which the tail was going under, would have been known; namely, all the lines being known. Wherefore also the triangle which has the Sun, the centre and the tail at the extremes. |
| Cum enim notus sit angulus quem linea a Sole ad contactum ducta facit cum linea a centro ad Solem, patet lineam ex centro ad Solem cum utroque in extremis Angulo esse notam, et ex iis totum triangulum, et per consequens altitudinem caudae a centro quae una linearum est. | For since the angle is known which the line drawn from the Sun to the contact makes with the line from the centre to the Sun, it is evident that the line from the centre to the Sun, with each Angle at the extremes, is known, and from these the whole triangle, and in consequence the height of the tail from the centre, which is one of the lines. |

## Words and phrases with alternative or special meanings

- ***barba / cauda / caput (Cometae)*** — rendered “beard / tail / head”. Barba = the comet's beard (luminous appendage preceding or beside the head), cauda = tail (behind), coma = hair; White uses barba and cauda nearly interchangeably here for the train turned from the Sun. Traditional threefold classification of comets (crinitus, caudatus, barbatus: Pliny NH II.89).
- ***Syrma*** — rendered “Train [Syrma]”. Greek σύρμα: trailing robe of tragic actors (Juvenal VIII.229); hence a comet's train. Kept capitalised as printed.
- ***Cinnos … comendi*** — rendered “dressing the Curls”. cinnus = a mixed drink, or a grimace (Nonius); probably for cincinnus = curl of hair, matching comere 'to dress hair' and the hair imagery of coma/barba. Reading certain from 300 dpi image; sense uncertain; a pun on comet (coma, 'hair').
- ***concinnare*** — rendered “arrange”. fit together, make elegant; rhetorical concinnitas Of the 'variety' of comet shapes.
- ***lucida tela diei*** — rendered “the bright darts of day”.  Lucretius I.147, II.60, III.92, VI.40: 'non radii solis neque lucida tela diei'; 'ille' = the poet.
- ***flocci facere*** — rendered “value at a lock of wool”. = care at all (with neque: not care a straw) Colloquial idiom (Plautus, Cicero); rendered literally.
- ***telum / spiculum (Solis)*** — rendered “dart / little dart”. rays of the Sun Lucretian imagery of sunbeams as weapons.
- ***resultus*** — rendered “rebound”. reflexion Light reflected back from Saturn.
- ***indoles (Solis)*** — rendered “disposition [indoles]”. natural character See glossary.
- ***nebula*** — rendered “mist”. cloud, vapour, nebula Kept distinct from nubes = cloud.
- ***complexus (caussarum)*** — rendered “embrace [complexu]”. concourse, combination Atomist language: fortuitous concurrence of causes.
- ***ad perpendiculum*** — rendered “along the perpendicular”. straight away (from the eye) Optics: receding along the line of sight.
- ***extantia*** — rendered “standing-out [extantiis]”. projection, protuberance Rare noun (late Latin).
- ***tubus*** — rendered “the tube”. telescope Phases of Venus seen telescopically (Galileo, 1610).
- ***collimare*** — rendered “aim”. sight along a line (aim the telescope) Technical astronomer's verb (from a misreading of collineare).
- ***species*** — rendered “appearance [species]”. visible species, shape, phase Both optical and phase sense (Venus).
- ***remandare / amandare / reverberari / repulsus / reiicere*** — rendered “send back / send away / be beaten back / driven back / throw back”.  Kept distinct for White's varied vocabulary of reflexion.
- ***sulcus (luminis)*** — rendered “furrow”. track, streak Virgil, Aen. II.697 (the star's 'longo limite sulcus').
- ***pileatus*** — rendered “capped”. wearing the pileus (felt cap) Mountains capped with cloud.
- ***interpolare*** — rendered “interpolate”. to touch up, alter; here perhaps 'interrupt' the beam Sense uncertain; bracketed.
- ***obnoxius*** — rendered “liable”. bound, guilty, subject Pairs with 'mendacem me habeto'.
- ***ex occluso*** — rendered “from an enclosed [place]”. from a shuttered lantern / closed room
- ***communes (aere)*** — rendered “common”. shared, everywhere Motes present throughout the air.
- ***nihil moror*** — rendered “I make no delay”. I have no objection Formula of assent (legal/colloquial).
- ***Divinitas Coelorum*** — rendered “Divinity of the Heavens”.  Aristotle, De caelo I.3; Peripatetic doctrine of the incorruptible fifth body.
- ***raptus*** — rendered “rapture [raptu / raptus]”. hurrying-along; the diurnal motion imparted by the primum mobile Scholastic 'motus raptus'.
- ***Pyramides*** — rendered “Pyramids”. flame-shapes (Plato, Timaeus 56b: fire = pyramid) Flame streaming backward from motion.
- ***in medium proponere*** — rendered “propose into the middle”. set forth publicly See glossary 'in medium conferre'.
- ***seu posse, seu debere*** — rendered “either able or obliged”.  Peripatetic/Keplerian view that tails point away from the Sun.
- ***terminus*** — rendered “term”. end-point (of a line of reflexion) Geometric sense.
- ***Iris*** — rendered “Rainbow [Irim]”. the goddess Iris Personified 'nubes Iridis genitrix'.
- ***machinatio*** — rendered “contrivance”. device, machine Pejorative of a hypothesis.
- ***aequora (coelestia)*** — rendered “plains”. seas, level expanses Poetic for the fluid heavens.
- ***exleges authores*** — rendered “lawless authors”. causes not bound by law (author = cause, auctor) Wind-like, irregular causes.
- ***integrare*** — rendered “make up [integrat]”. make whole, complete The third of the three motions; glossary 'integrare = make whole'.
- ***quadrans*** — rendered “quadrant”. astronomical instrument; also a quarter-as coin Possible pun.
- ***operae*** — rendered “labourers [operas]”. workmen, assistants; or efforts
- ***per lancem et libram*** — rendered “by the scale-pan and the balance”. by weighing; legal per aes et libram Andabata's demand for experiment.
- ***satis dare*** — rendered “give satisfaction”. give security (legal)
- ***ferculum*** — rendered “dish”. course at table Banquet metaphor.
- ***plaustrum*** — rendered “Wain”. the Plough / Ursa Major The 'wheels' = the four stars of the body.
- ***Libertus Fromondus*** — rendered “Libertus Fromondus [Libert Froidmont]”.  Louvain philosopher (1587–1653); observed the comet of Nov.–Dec. 1618; Meteorologicorum libri sex (1627).
- ***horizon ortivus*** — rendered “rising horizon”. eastern horizon
- ***contingens (linea)*** — rendered “touching (line)”. tangent Geometry: line grazing the earth's surface.
- ***afflare / afflatio*** — rendered “breathe upon / breathing-upon”. first illumination by the Sun Metaphor.
- ***postquam*** — rendered “since”. after Causal use, uncommon.
