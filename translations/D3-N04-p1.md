# De Mundo — Dialogue III (De causis mundi), Knot iv, part 1 of 2

*Latin title:* Nodus IV. Dari aliquod ab aeterno existens  
*English title:* Knot IV. That there is given something existing from eternity  
*Pages:* PDF pp. 300–307 = printed pp. 289–296 (from Ereunius’ ‘Haec itaque mutatio qua non est alia prior’ on p. 289 to the end of Andabata’s ‘…satis novi te laboraturum.’ on p. 296; part 2 continues on p. 296 with Ereunius’ ‘Nunc itaque dimittamus’).

## Dialogue III, Knot iv, part 1 (printed pp. 289–296)

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| [Margin, p. 289: Nodus IV. Dari aliquod ab aeterno existens.] | [Margin, p. 289: Knot IV. That there is given something existing from eternity.] |
| Ereu. Haec itaque mutatio qua non est alia prior, a causa fuit quae nunquam non extitit. | Ereu. This change therefore, than which there is not another prior, was from a cause which never did not exist [nunquam non extitit]. |
| Andab. Et ipse aio. | Andab. I myself too say [it] [aio]. |
| Ereu. Hoc itaque confectum est, ab aeterno stetisse aliquid. Hoc itaque a seipso existere fateris, an ab alio? | Ereu. This therefore has been accomplished [confectum est]: that something has stood from eternity. This therefore, do you confess [it] to exist from itself [a seipso], or from another [ab alio]? |
| Andab. Implicata est haec petitio. Quantum enim adhuc dilucet, vel unum vel plura esse possunt: & si plura, finita, vel infinita, & horum vel aliquod aut aliqua a se, vel omnia ab alio. Quod ergo ex his responderi tibi vis? | Andab. Entangled [implicata] is this question [petitio]. For as far as it yet grows clear [dilucet], either one or several can be: and if several, finite or infinite; and of these either some one or some [ones] from themselves [a se], or all from another. Which therefore of these do you wish to be answered to you? |
| Ereu. Si unum tantum sit illud, ab alio factum esse non potuit: cum enim illud aliud iam non sit, extinctum esse necesse est: quare non eam mutationem quam posuimus fuisse primam, sed huius factionem vel alterius extinctionem, modo arcte quaestio sit proposita, de ipsa inceptione prima mutationis. Proxime deinde exhibe de pluribus finitis quid opineris, an omnia possint seu singula ab alio esse? | Ereu. If that be one only, it could not have been made by another: for since that other now is not, it is necessary [that it] has been extinguished: wherefore not that change which we posited to have been first, but the making [factionem] of this or the extinction of the other [would be first], provided the question be proposed strictly [arcte], about the very first inception of change. Next then set forth [exhibe] what you think about several finite [ones]: whether all can, or each singly [singula], be from another? |
| Andab. Non videtur. Sunto enim A, B, C, & C quidem a B, B rursus ab A: si ergo ponatur A a duorum quolibet esse, a seipso ponetur existere. | Andab. It does not seem [so]. For let there be A, B, C, and C indeed from B, B again from A: if therefore A be posited to be from whichever of the two [you will], it will be posited to exist from itself. |
| Ereu. Quid inde incommodi ostendis? non enim si mediate a seipso fit, propterea non immediate ab alio erit, novisti autem causam mediatam inter accidentales numerari, immediatam vero proprie & per se causam esse. | Ereu. What of inconvenience [incommodi] do you show from that? For not, if it is made mediately [mediate] from itself, will it on that account not be immediately [immediate] from another; but you know that a mediate cause is numbered among accidental [causes], but an immediate [one] to be properly and through itself [per se] a cause. |
| [Margin, p. 290: Unum vel plura aeterna finita non omnia esse facta.] Andab. Non me persuadent quae affers, etsi non satis suppetat quo ea reiiciam. Nescio enim quid aequivoci latere in iis videtur, quod non satis de repente evolvo. | [Margin, p. 290: That one or several eternal finite [beings] are not all made.] Andab. The things which you bring do not persuade me, although there is not sufficiently at hand [that] by which I may reject them. For I know not what of the equivocal [aequivoci] seems to lie hidden in them, which I do not sufficiently unroll [evolvo] on the sudden. |
| Ereu. Animum ad causae notionem applica, & cur a non existente nihil fiat, quod vides esse in hoc genere notissimum. | Ereu. Apply [your] mind to the notion of cause, and [to] why from a non-existing [thing] nothing is made, which you see to be in this kind most known. |
| Andab. Causam video dici ex quo aliquid sequitur. | Andab. I see a cause to be called [that] from which something follows. |
| Ereu. Hoc est, causam dici illud ex quo, quia ipsa est, quod est, aliud est. Unde apparet quod sicut in intellectu nostro positis quibusdam, puta duobus in Syllogismo, necessum est tertium esse ex eo quod haec sunt: sic in rebus esse. Quoniam enim intellectus noster ipsas res in sese habet, fit ut res observatis eius conditionibus id in ipso agant quod in rerum natura agere natae sunt. Sic itaque etiam in rerum natura causa est qua posita aliud est ex eo quod ipsa est, seu ex vi ipsius. Unde vides cur non ens non possit agere. Aliud enim non potest esse ex vi ipsius, seu ex eo quod ipsum est, quod revera non est, neque quod non habet esse, potest habere in se vim aliquam ex qua aliud sit. Vides eadem opera idem non posse esse sibi causam. Esse enim A, & esse A, unum sunt. Desit itaque natura alietatis quae inter causam & effectum essentialis est, ad hoc ut alteri desit, quod ab alio habeat. Quod enim ex sese habet, non accipit aliunde. Posito autem quod idem mediate a se sit, ponis minorem alietatem quam si immediate a se sit: cum media inter extrema, id est, unum & idem claudi necesse sit: Quare si maioritas aliqua & gradatio sit impossibilium, magis impossibile est ut mediate a se sit aliquid quam immediate. Cum itaque quod est non possit non esse, neque nisi non sit, accipere esse, id est, fieri; clarum itaque est idem non accipere esse seu fieri a seipso. Transeamus modo ad alteram partem, & petamus si infinita sint, an omnia possint ab alio pendere, an vero aliquod unum vel plura sint a se? Quid opinaris? | Ereu. That is, a cause to be called that from which, because it itself is, what is, is another [quod est, aliud est]. Whence it appears that, just as in our intellect, certain [things] being posited, for example [puta] two in a Syllogism, it is necessary [necessum est] that a third be from this, that these are: so [it is] in things. For since our intellect has the things themselves in itself, it comes about that things, their conditions being observed, act in it that which in the nature of things they are apt by nature [natae sunt] to act. Thus therefore also in the nature of things a cause is [that] which being posited, another is from this, that it itself is, or from the force [vi] of it. Whence you see why a non-being [non ens] cannot act. For another cannot be from the force of it, or from this, that it is, which in truth [revera] is not; nor can that which does not have being [esse] have in itself some force from which another may be. You see by the same work [eadem opera] that the same cannot be cause to itself. For to be A, and to be A, are one. Lacking therefore is [Desit] the nature of otherness [alietatis], which between cause and effect is essential, to this [end], that to the one [alteri] there be lacking what it has from the other [ab alio]. For what it has from itself, it does not receive from elsewhere. But it being posited that the same is mediately from itself, you posit a lesser otherness than if it is immediately from itself: since it is necessary that the middles [media] be enclosed between the extremes, that is, between one and the same: wherefore, if there be some greaterness [maioritas] and gradation of impossibles, it is more impossible that something be mediately from itself than immediately. Since therefore what is cannot not-be, nor, unless it is not, receive being, that is, be made; it is clear therefore that the same does not receive being, or come to be [fieri], from itself. Let us pass now to the other part, and let us ask, if they are infinite, whether all can depend [pendere] from another, or rather some one or several are from themselves? What do you think? |
| [Margin, p. 292: Aeterna etiam infinita non omnia esse facta.] Andab. Non posse omnia esse ab invicem, non enim video quid intersit ad hoc, finita sint an infinita, de quibus quaeritur. | [Margin, p. 292: That eternal [beings], even infinite, are not all made.] Andab. [I think] that all cannot be from one another [ab invicem], for I do not see what it matters [intersit] to this, whether they be finite or infinite, about which it is asked. |
| Ereu. Praeteritne te igitur infinita pertransiri non posse? Unde si ab uno aliquo numeres causas, si finitae sint, facile pervenitur ad unum de quo quaerendum sit a quonam sit & necesse est a seipso esse: quoniam in orbem restim ducere iam ex discursu habito factum est impossibile. Verum si infinitae sunt, nunquam ad hoc cogi poterimus. | Ereu. Has it then passed you by [Praeteritne te] that infinites cannot be passed through [pertransiri]? Whence, if you number the causes from some one, if they are finite, one easily arrives at one about which it must be asked from what it is, and it is necessary [that it] be from itself: since to lead the rope round in a circle [in orbem restim ducere] has already, from the discourse [discursu] held, been made impossible. But if they are infinite, we shall never be able to be forced to this. |
| Andab. Si ex nostra numeratione res penderet, haec recte dicerentur. At de illis sermo est quorum quantumvis infinitae sint operationes simul & quam cito unius, tam cito omnium sunt expeditae. Cum enim ab aeterno & ante omnem mutationem existere & efficere consensum sit, clarum est eorum omnium operationes aeternas esse. Si enim defecisset aliqua alicuius operatio, iam aliqua mutatio facta fuisset. Omnes itaque simul inceperunt, simul continuantur. Neque refert utrum a nobis numerari possint necne: de se enim usque ad ultimum effectum pertingunt. Video quoque de singulis dicendum esse quod pendeant, singula autem & omnia non differunt nisi in his quae ad numerum spectant, quale hoc non est. Immo etiam si de quolibet sit quaestio, illud ab omnibus superioribus pendere dicetur, & inferiores omnes ab eo: Clarum itaque est & omnes superiores dici debere pendere ab alio, & (quod hinc conficitur) esse a quo caetera pendent. Tandem si de quolibet numero complexe quaestio sit, idem reperitur, quemlibet numerum in hac infinitate conclusum pendere ab aliquo extra ipsum numerum. De ipso proinde infinito negari idem non poterit. | Andab. If the matter [res] depended on our numbering, these things would be rightly said. But the discourse [sermo] is about those whose operations, however infinite they be, are together [simul], and as soon as [those] of one, so soon [those] of all are dispatched [expeditae]. For since it has been agreed that [they] exist and effect from eternity and before every change, it is clear that the operations of them all are eternal. For if some operation of any one had failed [defecisset], already some change would have been made. All therefore began together, [and] are continued together. Nor does it matter whether they can be numbered by us or not: for of themselves [de se] they reach [pertingunt] as far as the last effect. I see also that it must be said of the singulars [singulis] that they depend; but singulars and all do not differ except in those things which regard number, of which sort this is not. Nay, even if the question be about any one whatever, that will be said to depend from all the superior [ones], and all the inferior [ones] from it: it is clear therefore both that all the superior [ones] ought to be said to depend from another, and (what is concluded [conficitur] hence) that there is [that] from which the rest depend. Finally, if the question be about any number whatever complexly [complexe], the same is found: that any number whatever enclosed in this infinity depends from something outside the number itself. Of the infinite itself, accordingly, the same will not be able to be denied. |
| Ereu. Facile eum pertinacem censeam qui non moveatur his quae dicis: sed quoniam haec infiniti indoles est ut primum in eo negetur, atque etiam a quibusdam omnium collective ratio, vel tollenda est infiniti secundum multitudinem ratio (quod ut posse fieri non dubito sic ex communi & non ex propriis ratiocinatio constaret) vel aliquid proprium afferendum est, cur necesse sit aliquod esse extrinsecum a quo pendeat tota multitudo. Vide igitur quid cogitaverim: sive numerus finitus sit sive infinitus, singula a singulis dependent. Si quis enim unum a pluribus vel plura ab uno immediate pendere subiecerit, plura illa pro uno ut talia sumantur. Hoc constituto aio necesse fore pendentium & eorum a quibus pendent, aequalem esse multitudinem. Cum itaque verum quoque & ex discursu immediate praecedente conclusum sit, nullum horum pendere ab aliquo quod pendet a se, concludo extraneum aliquid esse a quo aliquod pendeat, alioqui pendentium maiorem multitudinem fore quam eorum a quibus pendetur. Ablato enim ultimo seu immediato primae mutationi quod pendet ab aliis & nihil eorum pendet ab ipso; adhuc verum est quod singula reliquorum & omnia pendent. Rursus a singulis & omnibus aliquid pendet, puta hoc signatum de quo est quaestio prima. Addito itaque quod demi iussimus, pendens unum est ultra ea a quibus pendetur: Necessum itaque est ad hoc ut pendentia aequentur iis a quibus pendent, unum extraneum assumi a quo pendeatur, & ipsum non pendeat. | Ereu. Easily would I judge him obstinate [pertinacem] who should not be moved by these things which you say: but since this is the disposition [indoles] of the infinite, that a first be denied in it, and also, by some, the account [ratio] of all collectively [collective] [is denied], either the account [ratio] of the infinite according to multitude must be removed (which, as I do not doubt that it can be done, so the reasoning [ratiocinatio] would stand [constaret] from the common and not from the proper [ex propriis]), or something proper must be brought forward, why it is necessary that there be something extrinsic from which the whole multitude depends. See therefore what I have thought: whether the number be finite or infinite, singulars depend [dependent] from singulars. For if anyone should subjoin [subiecerit] that one depends immediately from several, or several from one, let those several be taken for one, as such [ut talia]. This being established, I say it will be necessary that the multitude of the depending [things] and of those from which they depend be equal. Since therefore it is both true and concluded from the immediately preceding discourse, that none of these depends from something which depends from it [a se], I conclude that there is something extraneous [extraneum] from which something depends; otherwise the multitude of the depending [things] would be greater than [that] of those from which there is depending [pendetur]. For, that last, or immediate to the first change, being removed, which depends from the others and none of them depends from it, still it is true that the singulars of the rest, and all, depend. Again, from the singulars and all something depends, for example [puta] this signate [one] [signatum] about which is the first question. Therefore, that which we ordered to be taken away being added [back], there is one depending [thing] beyond those from which there is depending: it is necessary [necessum] therefore, to this [end], that the depending [things] be equalled to those from which they depend, that one extraneous [thing] be assumed from which there is depending, and [that] it itself not depend. |
| Asphal. Hactenus egregie: sed credisne causationem quam affers ne infiniti multitudine rationem tollas, tibi excusando fore satis? Ego profecto non absisto donec hanc mihi gratiam concesseris. | Asphal. Thus far excellently: but do you believe that the plea [causationem] which you bring, lest you remove the account [rationem] of the infinite in multitude, will be enough for excusing you? I for my part [profecto] do not stand off [absisto] until you have granted me this favour [gratiam]. |
| Ereu. Voluntas tua mihi supra methodi leges observanda est. Accipe igitur. [Margin, p. 295: Infinitum multitudine esse impossibile.] Multitudinem essentialiter ex additione unitatis ad unitatem constare; nemo negat. Si itaque infinita est, ab indivisibili hoc modo excrescere necessum est. Erit itaque alicubi differentia finiti ad infinitum, eadem quae unius ad finitum praesuppositum. | Ereu. Your will is to be observed by me above the laws of method. Receive [it] therefore. [Margin, p. 295: That the infinite in multitude is impossible.] That multitude essentially consists [constare] from the addition of unity to unity, no one denies. If therefore it is infinite, it is necessary [necessum] that it grow out [excrescere] from the indivisible in this manner. There will be therefore somewhere a difference of the finite to the infinite, the same as [that] of one to the presupposed finite. |
| Andab. Expectabam a te, Ereuni, novum aliquid, & acri tuo ingenio dignum. Narras autem rem in scholis tritissimam, & nodum millies solutum. | Andab. I was expecting from you, Ereunius, something new, and worthy of your sharp wit [ingenio]. But you tell a thing most worn [tritissimam] in the schools, and a knot [nodum] a thousand times untied [solutum]. |
| Ereu. Ergo & millies nexum, Andabata, neque prorsus evanidum. Sed in omnibus hoc quaestionibus maxime vero in iis quae magna contentione ultro citroque agitantur, spectandum est, ut iudicium recte fiat, eligaturque quod ex propriis, & cum necessitate discurritur. Rarum enim est ut ad similia proposita nihil efficaciter dictum sit, ubi tam illustria ingenia tanto conatu sese impenderunt. Tu modo, Andabata, vide num non ex propriis multitudinis procedit haec ratiocinatio. Deinde considera num evidentia sint quae assumpta sunt, an aliquid dubitari possit? Essentialem esse multitudini compositionem per unitates num potes dubitare? | Ereu. Therefore also a thousand times tied [nexum], Andabata, and not altogether vanishing [evanidum]. But in all questions this, and most of all in those which are agitated with great contention to and fro [ultro citroque], is to be looked to, in order that judgement be made rightly, and that that be chosen which is discoursed [discurritur] from proper [principles] [ex propriis] and with necessity. For it is rare that to similar proposals [proposita] nothing has been said efficaciously, where such illustrious wits [ingenia] have spent themselves with so great effort. Do you only, Andabata, see whether this reasoning [ratiocinatio] does not proceed from the propers [ex propriis] of multitude. Then consider whether the things which have been assumed are evident, or whether something can be doubted? That composition through unities is essential to multitude, can you doubt? |
| Andab. Nequaquam. | Andab. By no means. |
| Ereu. Si non potes, vide num hoc redarguere possis hunc processum in infinita multitudine ab unitate usque ad infinitatem non interpolatam continuari? | Ereu. If you cannot, see whether you can refute this: that this progress [processum] in an infinite multitude is continued from unity up to infinity, uninterrupted [non interpolatam]? |
| Andab. Neque hoc. | Andab. Nor this. |
| Ereu. Num hoc itaque ex finito fieri infinitum, modo multitudo infinita sit? | Ereu. [Can you refute] this then: that from the finite the infinite comes to be [fieri], provided the multitude be infinite? |
| Andab. Non possum. | Andab. I cannot. |
| Ereu. Affer itaque sexcentas illas solutiones quas commemoravisti. | Ereu. Bring forward then those six hundred [sexcentas] solutions which you recalled [commemoravisti]. |
| Andab. Nunc oblita mihi tot carmina, neque de repente occurrit quod respondeam. Sed si penes me haberem quempiam ex iis qui infiniti possibilitatem tutantur, satis novi te laboraturum. | Andab. Now forgotten by me [are] so many songs [Nunc oblita mihi tot carmina], nor does [anything] occur on the sudden which I may answer. But if I had by me [penes me] anyone of those who defend [tutantur] the possibility of the infinite, I know well enough [satis] that you would be in labour [laboraturum]. |

## Words and phrases with alternative or special meanings

- ***nunquam non extitit*** — rendered “never did not exist [nunquam non extitit]”. ‘always existed’; litotes. Double negative kept to preserve the modal-temporal structure of the argument: the cause of the first change cannot itself have begun.
- ***a se / a seipso / ab alio*** — rendered “from itself / from another”. Aseity vs. dependence (ens a se vs. ens ab alio). Scholastic terminology for the self-existent; the whole knot argues that among eternal beings at least one must be a se. In p. 294 ‘ab aliquo quod pendet a se’ the reflexive refers back to ‘nullum horum’ (from something that depends on it), not to aseity.
- ***petitio*** — rendered “question [petitio]”. Request, demand, postulate; in disputation a petitio is a proposition demanded of the respondent. Andabata calls the demand ‘entangled’ because it presupposes one answer among several alternatives.
- ***factio / extinctio / inceptio*** — rendered “making / extinction / inception”. factio = the act of making (not ‘faction’). Ereunius’ point: if one eternal being were made by another now extinct, that extinction (a change) would precede the supposed first change.
- ***mediate / immediate; causa accidentalis / per se*** — rendered “mediately / immediately; accidental / through itself”. Mediate cause = remote cause in a chain; per se cause = proper, essential cause. Scholastic division (cf. Aquinas, ST I q.46 a.2 ad 7 on per se vs. per accidens ordered causes).
- ***aequivocum (aequivoci)*** — rendered “of the equivocal”. Equivocation, ambiguity of a term. Andabata suspects a fallacy of equivocation (cf. glossary ‘aequivocum’).
- ***evolvere*** — rendered “unroll [evolvo]”. Unfold, work out, explain. Image of unrolling a scroll; kept literal.
- ***causa … ex quo, quia ipsa est, … aliud est*** — rendered “a cause … that from which, because it itself is, another is”. Definition of cause by ‘from the being of which another being follows’. White grounds real causality on the model of syllogistic consequence: as two premises posited yield a third, so a cause posited yields another being ‘ex vi ipsius’.
- ***natae sunt (agere)*** — rendered “are apt by nature [natae sunt]”. ‘are born to’, ‘are naturally fitted to’. Scholastic aptum natum (glossary ‘natum esse’).
- ***vis*** — rendered “force [vi]”. Power, virtue, efficacy. ex vi ipsius = by its own power; used as synonym of ‘ex eo quod ipsa est’.
- ***non ens*** — rendered “non-being [non ens]”. Nothing, what does not exist. Ex nihilo nihil fit in its causal form.
- ***eadem opera*** — rendered “by the same work [eadem opera]”. Idiom: ‘by the same token’, ‘at the same time’. Kept literal per convention.
- ***alietas*** — rendered “otherness [alietatis]”. Distinctness, diversity. Rare scholastic coinage (alius → alietas), used e.g. in Scotist and Lullist writing; essential distinction of cause and effect.
- ***maioritas & gradatio impossibilium*** — rendered “greaterness [maioritas] and gradation of impossibles”. ‘degree of impossibility’; maioritas = a being-greater. Scholastic term (maioritas/minoritas) for comparative relations; White argues mediate self-causation is ‘more impossible’ because it posits less otherness.
- ***pendere / dependere / pendentia*** — rendered “depend [pendere] / depend [dependent] / the depending [things]”. Hang from, rely on. Key verb of the knot; ‘pendetur’ (impersonal passive) rendered ‘there is depending’.
- ***ab invicem*** — rendered “from one another [ab invicem]”. Mutually. Late-Latin idiom for mutual dependence (circular causation).
- ***pertransiri (infinita)*** — rendered “be passed through [pertransiri]”. Be traversed. Aristotelian axiom: an infinite cannot be traversed (Physics III; Post. An. I.22).
- ***in orbem restim ducere*** — rendered “to lead the rope round in a circle”. To argue/cause in a circle. Proverbial image (cf. Terence, Adelphoe 752 ‘restim ductans’, leading a rope-dance); White uses it for circular causation already refuted.
- ***expeditae (operationes)*** — rendered “dispatched [expeditae]”. Completed, performed, ready. All eternal operations are simultaneous; nothing awaits completion.
- ***de se*** — rendered “of themselves [de se]”. By their own nature. As opposed to ‘a nobis numerari’: dependence holds in reality, not in our counting.
- ***singula / omnia; complexe; collective*** — rendered “singulars / all; complexly; collectively”. Distributive vs. collective sense of ‘all’; complexe = taken as a whole, as a complex. Classic logical distinction (sensus divisus/compositus, distributive/collective) central to the medieval infinite-regress debate.
- ***ratio (infiniti; omnium collective)*** — rendered “account [ratio]”. Notion, nature, formal character. ‘Tollere rationem infiniti’ = to remove the very notion of an infinite multitude.
- ***ex communi / ex propriis*** — rendered “from the common / from the proper [ex propriis]”. From common principles vs. from proper principles. Aristotelian requirement that scientific demonstration proceed from principles proper to the subject (Post. An. I.2, I.9). White wants a proof proper to multitude.
- ***indoles*** — rendered “disposition [indoles]”. Nature, character. As glossary (D1).
- ***subiicere*** — rendered “subjoin [subiecerit]”. Add, object, suggest. Disputation sense: to add a rejoinder.
- ***signatum*** — rendered “signate [signatum]”. Designated, this particular one. Scholastic ‘hoc signatum’ = this designated individual (cf. materia signata).
- ***extraneus / extrinsecus*** — rendered “extraneous / extrinsic”. External, outside the series. Kept distinct: extraneum used of the term outside the dependent series, extrinsecum in Ereunius’ statement of the thesis.
- ***causatio*** — rendered “plea [causationem]”. Excuse, pretext; also ‘causation’ in scholastic Latin. Classical sense (excuse) fits: Asphalius rejects Ereunius’ excuse for avoiding the proof against an infinite multitude.
- ***excrescere ab indivisibili*** — rendered “grow out from the indivisible”. Grow, increase out of. Multitude built from the unit (unitas as indivisible principle of number, Euclid VII def. 1–2).
- ***nodum millies solutum / millies nexum*** — rendered “a knot a thousand times untied / a thousand times tied”.  Pun on nodus, the name of White’s own sections (‘Knot’).
- ***ultro citroque*** — rendered “to and fro [ultro citroque]”. Back and forth, on both sides. Classical idiom.
- ***evanidus*** — rendered “vanishing [evanidum]”. Fading, feeble. The knot, though often ‘untied’, has not vanished.
- ***non interpolatam*** — rendered “uninterrupted [non interpolatam]”. Not interpolated, not broken, unaltered. Agrees grammatically with ‘infinitatem’; sense is that the progress from unity is continuous.
- ***sexcentas*** — rendered “six hundred [sexcentas]”. Idiom: countless. Latin idiomatic large number, answering Andabata’s ‘millies’.
- ***Nunc oblita mihi tot carmina*** — rendered “Now forgotten by me [are] so many songs”.  Quotation of Virgil, Eclogues 9.53 (Moeris: ‘nunc oblita mihi tot carmina’).
- ***laboraturum*** — rendered “be in labour [laboraturum]”. Toil, be hard pressed, be in difficulty. Andabata: an advocate of the possible infinite would give Ereunius trouble.

## Translator's notes

- Uncertain readings and constructions: (1) p. 291 ‘illud ex quo, quia ipsa est, quod est, aliud est’ — punctuation as printed; construed ‘from which, because it [the cause] is, what is, is another’, i.e. another being exists; translated literally. (2) p. 291 ‘Desit itaque natura alietatis … ad hoc ut alteri desit, quod ab alio habeat’ — printed ‘Desit’ (subjunctive) where ‘Deest’ is expected; possibly a misprint; the purpose clause is obscure (sense: the otherness needed for one thing to receive from another what it lacks is absent). (3) p. 293 ‘& (quod hinc conficitur) esse a quo caetera pendent’ — subject of ‘esse’ may be ‘omnes superiores’ (all the higher ones are that on which the rest depend) or impersonal (there is something on which the rest depend); rendered with supplied ‘[that]’. (4) p. 293 parenthesis ‘quod ut posse fieri non dubito sic ex communi … constaret’ — ut…sic construction rendered literally (‘as … so’); sense is concessive. (5) p. 294 ‘ne infiniti multitudine rationem tollas’ — ‘multitudine’ printed (ablative), taken as ‘in multitude’. (6) p. 296 ‘non interpolatam’ agrees with ‘infinitatem’, not with ‘processum’. No illegible readings.
