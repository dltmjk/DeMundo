# De Mundo — Dialogue III (De causis mundi), Knot xii, part 2

*Latin title:* Nodus XII. Iudicia Astrologica esse vana.  
*English title:* Knot XII. That Astrological Judgements are vain.

## Dialogue III, Knot xii, part 2 (printed pp. 377–382)

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| [Margin, p. 377: Praedictionum Astrologicarum genera.] | [Margin, p. 377: Kinds of Astrological Predictions.] |
| Ereu. Immo video peritissimos eorum totum hunc locum contemnere; neque quicquam ab his metuere, & si quando utantur magis vel ad excusationem, vel ad celandas rationes suas, haec praetendere; quam quod effectui credant, quicquam conferre. Et tamen praeter haec ipsa non habent quae urgeant Astrologi, quorum aliqua ex credulitate imperitorum, alia vero ex levibus effectis & communibus ducuntur. | Ereu. Nay, I see the most skilled of them despise this whole topic [locum]; and fear nothing from these [things], and if ever they use [them], [that they] put these forward rather either for excuse, or for concealing their reasons [rationes], than because they believe [them] to contribute anything to the effect. And yet besides these very [things] the Astrologers do not have [anything] which they may urge; of which [things] some are drawn from the credulity of the unskilled [imperitorum], but others from light and common effects [effectis]. |
| Tota ergo quaestio ad experimenta reducitur, in quibus situm est si quid roboris habet hoc figmentum. Ea vero dividenda mihi {?video}. Alia enim sunt aequivoca; alia metaphorica, alia communia, vel partialia; alia perfecta: & haec certa vel incerta. | The whole question therefore is reduced to experiments [experimenta], in which it is placed [situm est] whether this figment [figmentum] has anything of strength [roboris]. But these I see are to be divided by me. For some are equivocal [aequivoca]; others metaphorical, others common, or partial; others perfect: and these certain or uncertain. |
| Aequivoca dico ea quorum historias aliquas habemus, ut de Imperatore quodam Germano, cui persuasum est se Romam subiugaturum, quia praedictum fuerat eum ad caput mundi perventurum. Contigit autem eum mori sub excelso monte quod mundi caput dicebatur. Alteri praedictum eum Hierosolymis moriturum, & obiit in cubiculo Hierusalem ab imagine civitatis ita dicto. | Equivocal [aequivoca] I call those of which we have some histories, as of a certain German Emperor, to whom it was persuaded that he would subjugate Rome, because it had been predicted that he would arrive at the head of the world [caput mundi]. But it befell that he died under a lofty mountain which was called the head of the world. To another [it was] predicted that he would die at Jerusalem [Hierosolymis], and he died in the bedchamber [called] Jerusalem, so called from an image of the city. |
| Alii ut caveret a leone, dumque pugno imaginem ferit, a clavo confixus rubiginoso periit. Alius cui idem praedictum erat, dum manum statuae leonis indit, morsus est a vipera. Alium homo cui nomen vulpes interfecit, cui praedictus fuerat a quadrupede interitus. Huiusmodi infinita sunt quae in Astra velle referre, insaniae potius est quam Philosophiae. | To another [it was predicted] that he should beware of a lion, and while he strikes the image with [his] fist, pierced by a rusty nail he perished. Another, to whom the same had been predicted, while he puts [his] hand into a statue of a lion, was bitten by a viper. Another a man whose name [was] Fox [vulpes] killed, to whom death [interitus] had been predicted from a quadruped. Of this kind there are infinite [instances], which to wish to refer to the Stars is [a mark] of madness rather than of Philosophy. |
| Neque minus de metaphoricis asserendum est, ut cum aquam calidam, vel ignem praedixerint, postea de febri vel cholera expediant, cum hominem nigris vestibus tibialibus rubris praedixerint, exeat avis loquax nigris plumis, pedibus rubicunda, & similes nugae. | Nor is less to be asserted concerning the metaphorical [ones], as when they have predicted hot water, or fire, [and] afterwards explain [expediant] [it] of a fever or of choler [cholera]; when they have predicted a man with black garments [and] red stockings [tibialibus], there comes out a talkative bird with black feathers, ruddy in the feet, and similar trifles [nugae]. |
| Communia & partialia sunt, ut cum brevi moriturum, longo tempore floriturum, magna facturum, &c. Partialia cum tali tempore, vel tali telo, vel mortis genere periturum affirmarint, & huius generis quam plurima sunt. | Common and partial [ones] are, as when [they say that one] will die shortly, will flourish for a long time, will do great [things], &c. Partial, when they have affirmed that [he] will perish at such a time, or by such a weapon, or kind of death; and of this kind there are very many. |
| Perfecta voco, cum tot fuerint circumstantiae praedictae, ut impossibile sit casu omnia evenisse, vel certe admodum difficile. Quae tum demum certa sunt, cum testimoniis supra exceptionem sancita fuerint, quorum neque fides esse suspecta potest, neque diligentia in debitis circumstantiis examinandis. | Perfect I call [them], when so many circumstances have been predicted, that it is impossible that all have come about by chance, or certainly very difficult. Which are then at last [tum demum] certain, when they have been ratified [sancita] by testimonies beyond exception, of which neither the credit [fides] can be suspect, nor the diligence in examining the due circumstances. |
| Ex his autem per se cuilibet patet tria genera ad experimenti fidem nihil habere ponderis. Et ex quarto genere incerta fidem non sufficientem, sed iuxta authorum aestimationem habere posse. Perfecta autem & certa tam pauca sunt, ut credam neque unum in omnibus saeculis nedum in praesenti possit exhiberi. | But from these it is plain of itself [per se] to anyone that three kinds have nothing of weight toward the credit [fidem] of experiment. And [that] of the fourth kind the uncertain [ones] can have a credit not sufficient, but according to the estimation of the authors. But the perfect and certain [ones] are so few, that I believe not even one in all ages, much less [nedum] in the present [age], can be exhibited. |
| Magna enim quae tam iactabunde ubique depraedicantur, ex tertio fere genere sunt, ut illud Tychonis de Sueonum Regis in Germaniam descensu, ex nova stella in Cassiopeiae signo visa, & aliud de Henrici IV. Gallorum Regis morte, quorum hoc dumtaxat tempus neque satis stricte, & veneno vel pugione interiturum exprimebat. Illud vero locum & tempus satis confuse, neque quicquam clare docet: adeo leviter absurda nobis ingeri permittimus, cum utiliter & ex ratione credenda impudenter reiiciamus. | For the great [ones] which are so boastfully [iactabunde] proclaimed everywhere, are for the most part [fere] of the third kind, as that [prediction] of Tycho concerning the descent of the King of the Swedes [Sueonum] into Germany, from the new star seen in the sign of Cassiopeia, and another concerning the death of Henry IV, King of the French [Gallorum], of which this [latter] expressed only the time, nor [that] strictly enough, and that he would perish by poison or dagger. But that [former] teaches place and time confusedly enough, nor [teaches] anything clearly: so lightly do we permit absurd [things] to be thrust upon us, while [cum] we impudently reject [things] usefully and with reason to be believed. |
| Andab. Negas itaque prorsus esse aliqua perfecta experimenta, cum tanta te testium nubes opprimat. | Andab. You deny therefore altogether that there are any perfect experiments, when [cum] so great a cloud of witnesses presses upon [opprimat] you. |
| Ereu. Testium multitudinem non vereor: nam unus idoneus me convicturus est. Sed fateor me fabulis audiendis adeo obduruisse, ut necessum sit eum mihi testem omnibus conditionibus probatum esse, ut nec fallere possit, neque falli, alioqui subveriturus sim. Sed si unus congruus afferatur, ego cedo dicto non sententia. | Ereu. The multitude of witnesses I do not fear: for one suitable [idoneus] [witness] is going to convince [convicturus] me. But I confess that I have so hardened [obduruisse] to hearing fables, that it is necessary that that witness be approved to me in all conditions, so that he can neither deceive, nor be deceived; otherwise I shall be somewhat afraid [subveriturus sim]. But if one congruous [congruus] [witness] be brought forward, I yield to the saying [dicto], not in opinion [sententia]. |
| Quid enim vetat hoc semel singulis saeculis accidere posse, ut cum effectus ipse casu factus sit, casu etiam incidat coniectorem in similem coniecturam lapsum esse. | For what forbids that this can happen once in single ages, that, since [cum] the effect itself has been made by chance, it should also fall out [incidat] by chance that the conjecturer [coniectorem] has slipped into a similar conjecture. |
| [Margin, p. 380: Praedictiones Astrologicas ex vi sortis interdum esse veras.] | [Margin, p. 380: That Astrological Predictions are sometimes true from the force of lot [sortis].] |
| Plura enim longe ad effectum in rerum natura prodendum petuntur, quam ad coniecturam verificandam. Coniectura enim ex paucis procedit, effectus ex multis. | For far more [things] are sought [petuntur] for bringing forth [prodendum] an effect in the nature of things, than for verifying a conjecture. For a conjecture proceeds from few [things], an effect from many. |
| Coniecturae tales sunt leges, ut circa certum numerum par sit eam veram evadere, effectui vero qui a longe pluribus pendet, non aeque facile est talem evadere. | The laws of conjecture are such, that about a certain number it is even [par] [odds] that it turns out true; but for an effect, which depends on far more [things], it is not equally easy to turn out such. |
| Verum ut ipsam rem ultra omnem ambiguitatem comprehendas, esto certus numerus hinc effectorum, quae Astris tribuuntur & inde combinationum ea effecta inferentium, & acceptis aleis quot satis erunt, singulis punctis effecta unius Astri applicentur; Et quoties Astrologus, & Horoscopum erigit, aliquis aleas iacito, & nisi toties successerit aleatori, quoties Astrologo convenire cum veritate effectus in natura, me mendacem habetote. Si vero toties convenerit, scitote Astrologum ex vi sortis, non ex arte praedixisse. | But that you may comprehend the thing itself beyond all ambiguity, let there be [esto] a certain number, on this side [hinc], of the effects which are attributed to the Stars, and on that side [inde] of the combinations inferring those effects; and, dice [aleis] having been taken, as many as will be enough, to the single points [punctis] let the effects of one Star be applied; and as often as the Astrologer also [&] erects a Horoscope, let someone throw [iacito] the dice; and unless it shall have succeeded for the dicer [aleatori] as often as [it succeeds] for the Astrologer that the effects agree with the truth in nature, hold [habetote] me a liar. But if it shall have agreed as often, know [scitote] that the Astrologer predicted from the force of lot [sortis], not from art. |
| Asphal. Verum Astrologus in ipso schemate ostendet quomodo non recte aliquid iudicaverit, cum debuerit aliud vidisse. | Asphal. But the Astrologer will show in the diagram [schemate] itself how he did not judge something rightly, since he ought to have seen another [thing]. |
| Ereu. Et tu, Asphali, nescis quam facile hoc factu sit. Habent siquidem regulas ad hoc factas, ut cur haec stella suum non sit sortita effectum (quoties fortuna defecerit) subiicere possint: hoc non viderant. Talis altera stella maligna est, & derogat ipsius viribus. | Ereu. And you, Asphalius, do not know how easy this is to do [factu]. For they have rules made for this, so that they can supply [subiicere] why this star has not obtained [sortita] its effect (as often as fortune has failed): this they had not seen; such another star is malign, and derogates from its forces. |
| Unde eos recte irridet Galilaeus, quod post rem peractam in schemate suo, eam perfectissime ostendere possint. Et in hoc quidem foeliciores sunt aleis quod in his simpliciter iactus quid sit & non sit, prima facie exhibet: illi vero omnia coloribus fucisque, ubi erraverint, inducere nihilominus possint. | Whence Galileo rightly mocks them, because after the thing [has been] accomplished they can show it most perfectly in their diagram [schemate]. And in this indeed they are more fortunate than dice, that in these the throw simply exhibits at first face [prima facie] what is and [what] is not: but they, where they have erred, can nonetheless overlay [inducere] all [things] with colours and paints [fucis]. |
| Unde qui experimenta ab iis facta probare velit, catus calidusque sit oportet: & ubi defecerint regulae, nihil correctionis admittere. Alioqui se in labyrinthum & meras tenebras condit, perpetuis erroribus mancipandus sine spe exitus. Ex effectu enim hanc stellam modo praevalere debuisse colligunt, non ex ratione aliqua fixa & perpetua. | Whence he who would test [probare] the experiments made by them must be shrewd [catus] and crafty [calidus]; and, where the rules have failed, admit nothing of correction. Otherwise he buries [condit] himself in a labyrinth and mere darkness, to be enslaved [mancipandus] to perpetual errors without hope of exit. For from the effect they gather that this star ought just then [modo] to have prevailed, not from some fixed and perpetual reason [ratione]. |
| Andab. Quae dixisti, video multa ratione subsistere. Sed terret me quod in usum hominum creata haec astra legimus: unde nullum in res humanas effectum, vel habere, vel certe non sensibilem prorsus a ratione alienum crediderim. | Andab. What you have said, I see stands firm [subsistere] by much reason. But it frightens me that we read these stars [to have been] created for the use of men: whence I would believe [it] altogether foreign to reason [that they] either have no effect upon human affairs, or certainly not a sensible [one]. |
| Asphal. Ego vero magnum eorum usum video, ut sint in signa temporum, & menses, & annos. Deinde in magnam ostentationem magnificentiae divinae ad erigendos animos ad naturae contemplationem. | Asphal. But I see a great use of them, that they may be for signs of times, and months, and years. Then for a great display [ostentationem] of the divine magnificence, for raising minds to the contemplation of nature. |
| Quam hebes enim est qui oculos in clara nocte (qualis hesterna fuit, quae te in admirationem perpulit, & originem huic contemplationi dedit) in coelos attollens, non Alexandros & Caesares omnes contemnit, qui per tot pericula & iniurias globi huius minimi particulas quasi ensibus dividunt, & propterea summos sese hominum existimant; Interea ignari quid in immensis illis spatiis mundisque immane quantum excedentibus, agatur. | For how dull is he who, lifting [his] eyes into the heavens on a clear night (such as yesterday’s was, which drove you into wonder [admirationem], and gave origin to this contemplation), does not despise all Alexanders and Caesars, who through so many dangers and injuries divide the little particles of this least globe as if with swords, and on that account esteem themselves the highest of men; meanwhile ignorant what is done in those immense spaces and worlds exceeding [it] monstrously how much [immane quantum]. |
| Ereu. Utilitatem quoque humanam alios fines excludere non est aequum. Esto igitur in humanos usus creata sint, num propterea elicere possumus hos solos vel certe praecipuos esse eorum fructus? sicubi legis in solam hominum servitutem factos coelos, hoc profer. | Ereu. It is also not fair that human utility exclude other ends. Let it be [Esto] then [that] they were created for human uses; can we on that account elicit [elicere] that these alone, or certainly [these as] the chief, are their fruits? If anywhere you read [that] the heavens [were] made for the sole servitude of men, bring this forward. |
| Alioquin hunc quem calcamus orbem, vel maxime si non unice in nostrum famulitium deditum accipiamus, reliquos ut ignotos praetereamus, donec aliunde nova nobis albescat aurora. | Otherwise let us accept this orb which we tread [calcamus] [as] given over [deditum], even most of all if not uniquely, to our service [famulitium]; let us pass by the rest as unknown, until from elsewhere a new dawn grow white [albescat] for us. |

## Words and phrases with alternative or special meanings

- ***locus (totum hunc locum)*** — rendered “topic [locum]”. place; commonplace, topic of discussion. Here the rhetorical/dialectical locus (the topic of lunar medicine), not Aristotelian place.
- ***figmentum*** — rendered “figment”. fiction, invention, contrivance. White's dismissive word for astrology as a whole.
- ***aequivoca / metaphorica / communia / partialia / perfecta*** — rendered “equivocal / metaphorical / common / partial / perfect”. A fivefold division of predictions (‘experiments’) by logical type. aequivocum in the logical sense (one word, several meanings), as in the scholastic fallacy of equivocation: the prophecy is fulfilled only by a pun on a name.
- ***caput mundi*** — rendered “head of the world”. Title of Rome; also a place-name. Equivocal prophecy-legends of this type (cf. ‘Florence’ in the death of Frederick II); White’s source is not identified.
- ***cubiculum Hierusalem*** — rendered “the bedchamber [called] Jerusalem”.  Henry IV of England died (1413) in the Jerusalem Chamber at Westminster, foretold to die ‘in Jerusalem’ (Holinshed, Shakespeare 2 Henry IV).
- ***leo / statua leonis / vipera*** — rendered “lion / statue of a lion / viper”.  Classical exempla: Aesop’s youth killed by a nail in a painted lion; Martial 3.19, the boy bitten by a viper hidden in a bronze bear’s mouth (here a lion).
- ***cholera*** — rendered “choler [cholera]”. bile; choleric disease; cholera. Humoral sense (yellow bile/choleric fever) intended.
- ***tibialia*** — rendered “stockings [tibialibus]”. leg-coverings, hose. The black-feathered, red-legged talkative bird is the chough or jackdaw.
- ***fides (testium, experimenti)*** — rendered “credit [fides / fidem]”. faith, trust, reliability. Juridical-epistemic: the credibility of a witness or report; glossary rendering ‘credit’.
- ***supra exceptionem*** — rendered “beyond exception”.  Legal: a witness against whom no exceptio (objection) can be raised.
- ***nedum*** — rendered “much less”.  After a negative: ‘not one …, much less …’.
- ***Tycho … de Sueonum Regis in Germaniam descensu*** — rendered “Tycho … concerning the descent of the King of the Swedes into Germany”.  Tycho Brahe’s reading of the nova of 1572 in Cassiopeia, later applied to Gustavus Adolphus’ landing in Germany (1630).
- ***Henricus IV. Gallorum Rex*** — rendered “Henry IV, King of the French”.  Assassinated by Ravaillac’s dagger, 1610; predictions of his death circulated widely.
- ***nubes testium*** — rendered “cloud of witnesses”.  Hebrews 12:1 (tantam habentes impositam nubem testium).
- ***subvereri*** — rendered “be somewhat afraid [subveriturus sim]”. to fear a little, be suspicious. Rare (Cicero). Not subire.
- ***cedo dicto non sententia*** — rendered “I yield to the saying, not in opinion”. dictum = what is said/reported; sententia (abl.) = in judgement. Ereunius would accept a reported fact without conceding the art.
- ***coniector / coniectura*** — rendered “conjecturer / conjecture”. diviner, interpreter (coniector of dreams). Classical coniector = soothsayer; White joins it to probabilistic ‘conjecture’.
- ***sors / ex vi sortis*** — rendered “lot / from the force of lot”. chance, lottery, fortune. Early probabilistic argument by simulation with dice.
- ***par sit*** — rendered “it is even [par] [odds]”. equal, an even chance. Gaming language: par/impar.
- ***alea, aleator, puncta*** — rendered “dice, dicer, points”.  puncta = the pips on the faces of a die.
- ***schema*** — rendered “diagram [schemate]”. the astrological chart (thema). Glossary rendering kept.
- ***Galilaeus irridet*** — rendered “Galileo … mocks”.  Galileo, Dialogo (1632), mocks astrologers who see after the event what their figures foretold.
- ***catus calidusque*** — rendered “shrewd and crafty”. calidus = hot-headed or cunning. Plautine/Terentian pairing; calidus here ‘cunning’.
- ***in signa temporum, & menses, & annos*** — rendered “for signs of times, and months, and years”.  Genesis 1:14 (sint in signa et tempora et dies et annos), adapted.
- ***immane quantum*** — rendered “monstrously how much”. enormously. Classical idiom (Horace, Sallust).
- ***famulitium*** — rendered “service [famulitium]”. household servitude, the body of servants. cf. glossary famulatus = service.
- ***albescat aurora*** — rendered “a new dawn grow white”.  Figurative: until new knowledge (e.g. revelation or observation) dawns.
