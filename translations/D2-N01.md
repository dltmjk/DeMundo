## Dialogue II, Knot i (printed pp. 127–133)

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| [p. 127] DIALOGUS SECUNDUS. DE FORMA MUNDI, seu motu magnorum Corporum. | [p. 127] DIALOGUE THE SECOND. OF THE FORM [forma] OF THE WORLD, or of the motion of the great Bodies [magnorum Corporum]. |
| [Margin: Nodus I. Introductio.] Postridie Bruma et Auster pluviae minax dubiam lucem usque in octavam protraxerant. | [Margin: Knot I. Introduction.] On the day after, the Winter-solstice [Bruma] and the South wind [Auster], threatening of rain, had drawn out the doubtful light right up to the eighth [hour] [in octavam]. |
| Sed Andabatam non detinebant tenebrae, quo minus non iam duas horas totam domum oberraret: | But the darkness did not hold back Andabata from wandering [quo minus … oberraret] over the whole house, now [non iam] these two hours: |
| Nunc horto inambulabat, sed obstipae pepulerunt nebulae: tum quae summis aedibus protendebatur stoa, spatiabatur; sed veritus ne minus matutinis foret incommodus, in conclave descendit: | Now he was walking up and down in the garden, but the stooping [obstipae] mists drove [him] off: then he was strolling [spatiabatur] in the portico [stoa] which was stretched out at the top of the house [summis aedibus]; but having feared lest he should be no less [minus] troublesome to those of the morning [matutinis], he went down into the chamber [conclave]: |
| Sed hinc etiam famularum operae domum aptantes exegerunt. | But from here also the labours [operae] of the maid-servants, setting the house in order, drove [him] out. |
| Cum primum itaque stridorem automati horarii octavam denumerantis persentisceret, ad tuas fores perrexit expertusque neque sera, neque pessulo munitas ingreditur, et heus te inquit, Asphali, nihil vereris iram solis hyemalis, si radios ipsius sic cubans [p. 128] asperneris. | As soon, therefore, as he perceived the grating [stridorem] of the self-moving [automati] clock [horarii] counting out the eighth [hour], he went on to your doors, and having found by trial [expertus] [them] fortified neither with lock-bar [sera] nor with bolt [pessulo], he goes in, and 'Ho there, you,' he says, 'Asphalius, you fear nothing the wrath of the winter sun, if lying so you [p. 128] spurn its rays.' |
| Me vero Ereunius ille tuus ita dementavit ut plane nihil, aut parum dormierim. | 'But me that Ereunius of yours has so driven out of my mind [dementavit] that I have slept plainly nothing, or little.' |
| Multi sunt iam anni quibus cognosco virum, et pluribus Scholasticis altercationibus cum eo interfui. | 'Many are now the years in which I know the man, and I have been present at several Scholastic altercations with him.' |
| Sed nihil simile hesternae collocutioni in eo vidi. | 'But nothing like yesterday's colloquy [collocutioni] have I seen in him.' |
| Adeo breviter et dilucide sua probabat, nostra reiiciebat: Adeo ex insitis et coniunctis locis arguebat: Neque dubia sed fere semper quae negari non possent, adducebat. | 'So briefly and clearly did he prove his own [things], reject ours: so from inbred [insitis] and conjoined places [locis] did he argue: and he brought forward not doubtful [things] but almost always [things] which could not be denied.' |
| Et tu excipiens, neque hoc advertebas, Andabata, perpetuo quasi filo totum ipsius discursum contextum? quomodo nihil nisi ex suppositis vel evidentibus, vel prius probatis, vel tandem quae ex alia scientia quasi ibi nota poscebat, concludebat; | And you, taking [him] up: 'And did you not notice this, Andabata, [that] his whole discourse [discursum] [was] woven together as if with a perpetual thread? how he concluded nothing except from [things] supposed [suppositis], or evident, or before proved, or finally [things] which he demanded from another science as if known there;' |
| ut videas in ipso exercitio, quod aliquando insinuavit, esse Physicarum rerum aeque ac Mathematicarum catenata theoremata; velint modo homines, (omissa disserendi vanitate) ad hoc conari, ut non nisi evidentibus assentiantur et quae ex iis sequantur, cum rigore meditari et inferre. | 'so that you may see in the exercise itself [that], which he once insinuated, that there are of Physical things equally as of Mathematical [ones] chained theorems [catenata theoremata]; if only men would be willing (the vanity of disputing [disserendi] being let go) to endeavour to this, that they may assent to nothing except evident [things], and with rigour [cum rigore] meditate and infer [the things] which follow from them.' |
| Tum Andabata. Tace quaeso, Asphali, nam et ipsa res mihi nescio quid huiusmodi insinuat: sed provectior iam aetas et inolita in utramque partem disputandi consuetudo facit, ut de me in illo genere parum sperare sustineam; ideoque neque libenter audiam. | Then Andabata: 'Be silent, I beg, Asphalius, for the thing itself too insinuates to me I know not what of this kind: but an age now more advanced and a custom, grown in [inolita], of disputing on either side [in utramque partem] makes [it] that I bring myself [sustineam] to hope little of myself in that kind; and therefore [that] I do not willingly hear [it] either.' |
| Potius Ereunium advocemus, ut pollicitationi [p. 129] hesternae satisfaciat; iam enim a duabus horis Cubiculi sui asseres obterentem audio. | 'Rather let us call Ereunius, that he may satisfy [his] yesterday's [p. 129] promise [pollicitationi]; for already, since two hours [a duabus horis], I hear [him] wearing down the planks [asseres] of his Chamber.' |
| Neque mora, dicto citius abiverat Andabata, et secum trahebat Ereunium, ad quem tu; siccine in molli culcitra Philosophandum arbitramini, ut nec surgendi mihi facultatem concedatis? | Nor [was there] delay; quicker than said [dicto citius] Andabata had gone off, and was drawing Ereunius with him, to whom you: 'Is it thus [siccine] that you judge one must Philosophize on a soft mattress [culcitra], so that you do not even grant me the faculty of getting up?' |
| Et Ereunius, Andabatam (inquit) accusa, qui me tantum non obtorto collo trahit. | And Ereunius: 'Accuse Andabata,' (he says) 'who all but [tantum non] drags me with twisted neck [obtorto collo].' |
| Sed te, Asphali, non tam iniquum expecto, ut quam semisomni ad redimendam, ut loquuntur, vexationem exprimi passus sum promissionem, tu velut aequam sponsionem exolvi postules. | 'But you, Asphalius, I do not expect [to be] so unfair [iniquum], that the promise which, half-asleep, I suffered to be pressed out [of me] for redeeming [ad redimendam], as they speak, a vexation, you should demand to be discharged [exolvi] as if a fair engagement [sponsionem].' |
| Cui tu. Tantum me ignoras, Ereuni? Nescis me iniurias dissimulatas odisse; sed si quae impudenter et gnaviter fiant, plurimum applaudere? | To whom you: 'So much do you not know me, Ereunius? Do you not know that I hate dissembled injuries [iniurias]; but if any are done shamelessly and briskly [gnaviter], [that I] applaud [them] very greatly?' |
| Non ipse tu commemoras plura me tibi scripta hoc iure surripuisse surreptaque retinere? Quid igitur inania causaris? Numquam stipulatione liberaberis. | 'Do not you yourself recall that I have filched [surripuisse] from you several writings by this right [hoc iure], and keep [them], filched? Why therefore do you plead empty [things] [inania causaris]? Never will you be freed from the stipulation [stipulatione].' |
| Tum Ereunius. Ast ego, nisi Andabatae huic meliorem inesse fidem novissem, tuo te iure abuterer. | Then Ereunius: 'But I, unless I had known [that] better faith [fidem] is in this Andabata, would use you up [abuterer] by your own right [tuo … iure].' |
| Tu ergo, Andabata, si quid postulas, voluntatem tibi gratum agendi libens exprimam; Quomodo effectum daturus sim homo qui coelorum motus prorsus ignoro, nescius sum: sed tu aequi bonique consules; hic iniuriarum amator quid agat, susdeque ferendum est. | 'You therefore, Andabata, if you demand anything, I will gladly express [exprimam] the will of doing [what is] pleasing to you; how I, a man who am wholly ignorant of the motions of the heavens, am to give [it] effect [effectum daturus sim], I am unknowing: but you will take [it] as fair and good [aequi bonique consules]; what this lover of injuries may do is to be borne up and down [susdeque]' [i.e. with indifference]. |
| His dictis consessum est, sitis ad lectum sedilibus, et iam [p. 130] vestras vices agetis, me repetendi nominum vestrorum labore exsolventes. | These things being said, there was a sitting-together [consessum est], seats being placed by the bed; and now [p. 130] you shall play your own parts [vestras vices agetis], releasing me from the labour of repeating your names. |
| [Margin: Quid nomine motus significetur.] Ereu. Institutum mihi est quantum imbecillitas mea patitur, ab iis incipere, quae adeo ex sese manifesta sunt, ut nulla probatione indigeant. | [Margin: What is signified by the name of motion [motus].] Ereu. It is my purpose [Institutum], as far as my weakness suffers, to begin from those [things] which are so manifest from themselves that they need no proof [probatione]. |
| Hoc itaque pro confesso a te postulo, Andabata, nisi quid reniteris; solem cottidiana semita circa terram ab Oriente in Occidentem moveri. | This therefore I demand [postulo] from you as confessed, Andabata, unless you resist somewhat: that the sun is moved by a daily path [semita] around the earth [terram] from the East into the West. |
| Andab. Ego vero pro certissimo hoc habeo. Sed non expectabam a te hoc postulari, quod tam ingentibus dissidiis hodie agitatur. | Andab. I indeed hold this for most certain. But I did not expect this to be demanded [postulari] by you, which today is agitated with such huge dissensions. |
| Ereu. Ignosce precor, nihil enim de tali quaestione assumenda cogito, hoc tantum postulo quod ipsi oculi testantur, perpetua vicissitudine solem cum terra permutari. | Ereu. Pardon, I pray, for I think nothing of assuming [assumenda] such a question; this only I demand, which the eyes themselves testify: that by a perpetual vicissitude [vicissitudine] the sun is exchanged [permutari] with the earth. |
| Andab. Cur itaque hoc loquendi modo uteris qui soli motum inesse prorsus exprimit? | Andab. Why therefore do you use this mode of speaking which expresses outright that motion is in the sun? |
| Ereu. Quia verus et proprius est, neque alium novi qui abstractius explicet rem, quam exprimere conor, absque eo quod utra ex parte causa lateat erueret. | Ereu. Because it is true and proper [proprius], nor have I known another which would explicate [explicet] more abstractly [abstractius] the thing which I try to express, without [absque eo quod] digging out on which side the cause lies hidden. |
| Andab. Siccine moveri et semita non satis exprimunt, ubi causa lateat? Quae enim demum est quaestio, nisi sol an terra moveatur; tu diserte solem moveri loqueris, et tamen [p. 131] te ab ipsa quaestione profiteris abstemium. | Andab. Is it thus [siccine] that 'to be moved' and 'path' do not express enough where the cause lies hidden? For what in the end is the question, except whether the sun or the earth is moved? You expressly [diserte] speak of the sun being moved, and yet [p. 131] you profess yourself abstinent [abstemium] from the question itself. |
| Ereu. Quod est, dicis, Andabata, sed vide num ratione extorris sim? nonne motum subiicis mutationi, cum hac differentia quod perpetua seu continua mutatio sit? si ergo in sole continuam mutationem invenio, non recte eum moveri dico. | Ereu. What is [so], you say, Andabata; but see whether I am an exile [extorris] from reason [ratione]? Do you not subject motion to change [mutationi], with this difference [differentia], that it is a perpetual or continual change? If therefore I find in the sun a continual change, [do] I not rightly say that it is moved? |
| Vide nunc quod sive sol, sive terra primo loco mutetur, hoc pro constanti est utriusque ad invicem continuam esse varietatem, ut aliud et aliud telluris signum centro solis ad rubricam occurrat. | See now that, whether the sun or the earth be changed in the first place [primo loco], this is for constant [pro constanti]: that there is a continual variety of each to the other [ad invicem], so that another and another mark [signum] of the earth [telluris] meets the centre of the sun at the red line [ad rubricam]. |
| Quod si verum est, utrumque verissime et propriissime dicitur, et terram respectu solis esse perpetuo motam et solem respectu telluris, Etsi causa ex una tantum modo parte haereat. | Which if it is true, each is said most truly and most properly: both that the earth [terram] in respect of the sun is perpetually moved, and the sun in respect of the earth [telluris], even if the cause cling [haereat] on one part only. |
| Sicut cum paries aliquis dealbatur, alius aliquis continuo similior et similior evadit, mutatione continua quam motum dicimus, etsi non moveatur. | Just as, when some wall is whitened, some other [wall] continually turns out [evadit] more like and more like, by a continual change which we call motion, even if it be not moved. |
| Sic nosti in hoc ipso situs genere de dextro in sinistrum, de longinquo in propinquum mutari aliquid, absque eo quod situ respectu universi mutent, quae omnia respective moveri eadem necessitate dicendum est, si motus definitionem et proprietatem vocis retinere volumus. | So you know [that] in this very kind [genere] of site [situs] something is changed from right into left, from far into near, without [absque eo quod] their changing in site in respect of the universe; all which [things] must be said, by the same necessity, to be moved relatively [respective], if we wish to retain the definition of motion and the propriety [proprietatem] of the word [vocis]. |
| Unde Copernici excusatio qua sic loquens, ad vulgus se loqui causatur, superflua et noxia mihi videtur. | Whence the excuse of Copernicus, by which, speaking thus, he pleads [causatur] that he speaks to the common people [ad vulgus], seems to me superfluous and harmful. |
| Andab. Novi ego magnos Theologos qui [p. 132] [Margin: De interpretatione Sacrarum literarum.] Copernici sententiam propter hanc sermonis improprietatem male excipiunt, neque putant satis tutam Christiano homini. | Andab. I have known great Theologians who [p. 132] [Margin: Of the interpretation of the Sacred letters.] receive ill the opinion [sententiam] of Copernicus on account of this impropriety of speech [sermonis], and do not think [it] safe enough for a Christian man. |
| Ereu. Ego Theologi nihil prae me fero. Miror tamen quomodo vocum improprietas in re huius conditionis possit Christianitati labem inferre? | Ereu. I make a show of [prae me fero] nothing of a Theologian. I wonder, however, how the impropriety of words [vocum] in a matter of this condition can bring a stain [labem] upon Christianity? |
| Andab. Facit hoc Sacrarum literarum reverentia, quas nefas est extra vocum proprietatem, sine urgenti causa interpretari? | Andab. Reverence for the Sacred letters does this, which it is unlawful [nefas] to interpret outside the propriety of the words [vocum] without an urgent cause. |
| Asphal. Ego Theologum quemdam amicum meum de hoc ipso aliquando consului, qui quae refero, mihi respondit. | Asphal. I once consulted a certain Theologian, a friend of mine, about this very [thing], who answered me [the things] which I relate. |
| Imprimis hanc esse Sacrarum literarum legem, ut etiam in iis quae necessitatem credendi secum trahant, si aliquatenus extra usum hominum posita sunt, debeant extra conspicuum et obvium vulgo sensum explicari; | First, that this is the law of the Sacred letters: that even in those [things] which draw with them a necessity of believing, if they are placed to some extent outside the use [usum] of men, they ought to be explicated outside the sense conspicuous and obvious to the common people [vulgo]; |
| et Theologiae rationem et utilitatem in eo positam, ut quomodo intelligendae sunt ex Ecclesiae traditione et scientiarum seu artium disciplinis edoceat; | and that the reason [rationem] and utility of Theology is placed in this, that it should teach thoroughly [edoceat] how they are to be understood, from the tradition of the Church and from the disciplines of the sciences or arts; |
| et breviter Sacram scripturam si nude et dogmatice sensa sua traderet, maiori hominum parti inutilem et fortasse noxiam futuram. | and, briefly, that the Sacred scripture, if it handed down its meanings [sensa] nakedly and dogmatically, would be useless, and perhaps harmful, to the greater part of men. |
| Adiungebat in iis quae ad fidelis multitudinis instructionem levius conducerent, et haberent veritatem abditam, hoc ipsum largiorem quamdam regulam sortiri. Quod [p. 133] ipse pluribus exemplis ostendebat. | He added that in those [things] which would conduce more lightly [levius] to the instruction of the faithful multitude, and had a hidden truth, this very [thing] obtains [sortiri] a certain larger [largiorem] rule. Which [p. 133] he himself showed by several examples. |
| Demum addebat, quantum linguarum scientia ad proprietatem vocum intelligendam adhiberi debet, tantum scientias naturales et Mathematicas ad ea loca quae ab his pendent; | At length he added that, as much as the science [scientia] of tongues ought to be applied to understanding the propriety of words [vocum], so much [ought] the natural and Mathematical sciences [to be applied] to those places [loca] which depend on these; |
| et quanta temeritas foret sine Grammatica velle nobis Idiotismos indicare, tantam esse ad haec loca sine idonea scientiarum supellectile se accingere. | and that as great a rashness as it would be to wish to point out Idioms [Idiotismos] to us without Grammar, so great [a rashness] is it to gird oneself for these places without a fit furniture [supellectile] of the sciences. |
| Tandem immanis esse sceleris quod tua inscitia vel barbaria hallucinaris, id velle in fidei regulam et legem doctrinae Christianae super verticem omnium fidelium erigere. | Finally, that it is [a thing] of monstrous crime [immanis … sceleris] to wish to erect that which you dream up [hallucinaris] by your own ignorance or barbarism into a rule of faith and a law of Christian doctrine over the head [verticem] of all the faithful. |
| Haec ille, quae tu, Andabata, qui harum rerum peritus es, quomodo se habeant, aestima. | These [things] he [said]; which [things], you, Andabata, who are skilled in these matters, judge [aestima] how they stand. |
| Andab. Ego vero intellectum captivare in obsequium fidei didici, et malo in secura ignorantia humi degere, quam in scientia periculosa super pennas ventorum volitare. | Andab. I indeed have learned to take captive [captivare] the understanding [intellectum] into the obedience [obsequium] of faith, and I prefer to live on the ground [humi] in a secure ignorance, rather than to flit [volitare] upon the wings of the winds in a dangerous science. |
| Sed quoniam nihil aliud postulas, Ereuni, quam vicissitudinariam hanc solis et telluris permutationem, ab eoque abstrahis an solis haec motu fiat an telluris, datam accipito et nos expectare puta quid inde educas; satis enim nos detinuit, haec de verbis inita contentio. | But since you demand nothing other, Ereunius, than this vicissitudinary [vicissitudinariam] exchange [permutationem] of the sun and the earth [telluris], and abstract [abstrahis] from this, whether it come about by the motion of the sun or of the earth, take it as granted [datam accipito], and reckon that we are waiting [to see] what you will draw out [educas] thence; for this contention entered upon about words has detained us enough. |

## Words and phrases with alternative or special meanings

- ***De forma mundi, seu motu magnorum Corporum*** — rendered “Of the form of the world, or of the motion of the great Bodies”. forma = Aristotelian form; here identified with the ordered motion of the great bodies White's Dialogue II treats the world's 'form' as its system of motions (heavens, earth, tides) — the Copernican question.
- ***Bruma*** — rendered “Winter-solstice”. bruma = shortest day; also winter generally Sets the dialogue at mid-winter; ties to the 'winter sun' joke below.
- ***usque in octavam*** — rendered “right up to the eighth [hour]”. octava (hora): 8 a.m. by the modern clock (cf. the clock striking 'octavam') Mechanical clock hours, not Roman temporal hours.
- ***quo minus non iam duas horas … oberraret*** — rendered “from wandering … now these two hours”. 'non iam' is pleonastic after 'quo minus' or means 'for no less than'; possibly 'nonnihil' Literal syntax kept; sense: he had already been roaming two hours.
- ***obstipae … nebulae*** — rendered “stooping mists”. obstipus = bent, bowed, hanging low; perhaps intended as 'thick, close' (cf. constipatus) Uncommon epithet; kept literal.
- ***matutinis*** — rendered “those of the morning”. dat. pl.: the morning-sleepers / people at their morning tasks; or 'morning hours' Elliptical; see notes.
- ***automatum horarium*** — rendered “self-moving clock”. automatum = automaton, any self-moving machine Striking mechanical clock.
- ***sera / pessulus*** — rendered “lock-bar / bolt”. sera = bar across a door; pessulus = bolt Classical pair (Plautus, Terence).
- ***insiti et coniuncti loci*** — rendered “inbred and conjoined places”. loci = topics, places of argument (Cicero, Topica: loci 'in ipso … haerent' vs 'coniuncti') Rhetorical topics: arguments from the thing itself and from things joined to it.
- ***catenata theoremata*** — rendered “chained theorems”. theorems linked in deductive chain White's programme: physics demonstrable 'more geometrico' like mathematics — the Digbean/Whitean ideal of a demonstrative physics.
- ***disserendi vanitas*** — rendered “vanity of disputing”. disserere = to discourse, argue dialectically; printed 'diſſerendi' (long s), not 'differendi' Attack on scholastic disputation in utramque partem.
- ***in utramque partem disputandi consuetudo*** — rendered “custom of disputing on either side”. Academic/Ciceronian and scholastic practice Andabata's sceptical, school-trained habit.
- ***sustinere (sperare)*** — rendered “bring oneself (to hope)”. sustineo + inf. = to endure to, have the heart to Classical idiom.
- ***obtorto collo*** — rendered “with twisted neck”. idiom: dragging someone by the neck, forcibly (Plautus, Cicero) Comic exaggeration.
- ***ad redimendam vexationem, ut loquuntur*** — rendered “for redeeming a vexation, as they speak”. legal: redemptio vexationis = paying/promising to be rid of a nuisance Canon/civil law phrase: a promise extorted to escape harassment is not binding — Ereunius' plea.
- ***sponsio / stipulatio*** — rendered “engagement / stipulation”. Roman-law forms of binding verbal promise Asphalius answers in legal terms: the stipulation stands.
- ***tuo te iure abuterer*** — rendered “I would use you up by your own right”. abuti = use fully, exploit, abuse; 'your own right' = the right of filching Asphalius has just claimed I.e. he would retaliate in kind.
- ***aequi bonique consulere*** — rendered “take as fair and good”. classical idiom: to accept in good part Common Ciceronian formula.
- ***susdeque ferendum*** — rendered “to be borne up and down”. susque deque ferre = to take with indifference Same idiom as D1-N16 'sus-deque'.
- ***vestras vices agetis*** — rendered “you shall play your own parts”. vices = turns, roles Narrative frame ends: from here the dialogue is set out dramatically with speaker names.
- ***motus / mutatio*** — rendered “motion / change”. Aristotle, Phys. III & V: motion (kinesis) as continuous change; mutatio = metabole Ereunius defines motion as continual change and so can apply it relatively to sun or earth.
- ***semita*** — rendered “path”. footpath, track Andabata objects that 'path' implies the sun itself moves.
- ***permutari / permutatio*** — rendered “be exchanged / exchange”. reciprocal change of position, 'interchange' White's neutral term for the relative sun–earth displacement.
- ***vicissitudo / vicissitudinarius*** — rendered “vicissitude / vicissitudinary”. alternation, succession by turns Kept as cognates.
- ***absque eo quod*** — rendered “without”. scholastic: 'without it being that …' Frequent scholastic connective.
- ***extorris ratione*** — rendered “an exile from reason”. extorris = banished Playful: 'am I out of my mind?'.
- ***ad rubricam*** — rendered “at the red line”. rubrica = red ochre, red line; perhaps the meridian line drawn in red on a sundial/instrument Likely the meridian: successive points of earth come under the sun's centre at the meridian.
- ***respective*** — rendered “relatively”. scholastic 'respectu', in relation Relativity of local motion: change of site with respect to another body.
- ***proprietas vocis / improprietas*** — rendered “propriety of the word / impropriety”. literal vs. figurative use of terms Central to the Copernican-exegesis debate (Galileo, Letter to Christina, 1615).
- ***Copernici excusatio … ad vulgus se loqui*** — rendered “Copernicus' excuse … that he speaks to the common people”. Copernicus (De revolutionibus I.10 etc.) and Copernicans say 'sun rises' in common usage White rejects the 'accommodation to common speech' defence as unnecessary since 'the sun moves' is literally true relatively.
- ***nefas*** — rendered “unlawful”. contrary to divine law Strong religious term.
- ***edocere*** — rendered “teach thoroughly”. e- intensive Theology's office as interpreter.
- ***largior regula*** — rendered “a larger rule”. broader, more lenient rule of interpretation Accommodation principle for non-saving truths (cf. Augustine, De Genesi ad litteram; Galileo's Letter).
- ***Idiotismi*** — rendered “Idioms”. Greek ἰδιωτισμός: idiomatic expression Not 'idiocies'.
- ***supellex scientiarum*** — rendered “furniture of the sciences”. supellex = household equipment Metaphor for intellectual equipment.
- ***intellectum captivare in obsequium fidei*** — rendered “to take captive the understanding into the obedience of faith”. 2 Cor. 10:5 (Vulgate: in captivitatem redigentes omnem intellectum in obsequium Christi) Andabata's fideist stance.
- ***super pennas ventorum volitare*** — rendered “flit upon the wings of the winds”. Ps. 17(18):11; 103(104):3 (Vulgate: super pennas ventorum) Scriptural image turned against speculative science.
- ***datam accipito*** — rendered “take it as granted”. future imperative; 'data' = a given (premise) Mathematical/dialectical 'datum'.

- Narrative frame: pp. 127–130 are narrated to Asphalius in the second person ('ad tuas fores', 'Et tu excipiens', 'Cui tu'); the narrator then hands over to the speakers ('vestras vices agetis'), and from p. 130 the text uses speaker labels.
- Uncertain readings / interpretations: (a) p. 127 'quo minus non iam duas horas' — 'non' apparently pleonastic; rendered 'now these two hours'. (b) p. 127 'obstipae … nebulae' — clear in the print; the epithet is odd (perhaps meant 'close, thick'). (c) p. 127 'ne minus matutinis foret incommodus' — 'minus' is hard; taken literally ('no less troublesome to those of the morning'); 'nimis' (too troublesome) would give easier sense. (d) p. 130 'non recte eum moveri dico.' is printed with a full stop but read as a rhetorical question, as the argument requires. (e) p. 131 'ad rubricam' taken as 'at the red [meridian] line'. (f) p. 132 Andabata's 'Facit hoc … interpretari?' is printed with a question mark; translated as a statement. (g) p. 128 'abiverat' printed thus (for abierat), kept.
- Verification pass (sentence-by-sentence against the Latin and 300 dpi crops of pp. 127–128): 3 corrections — (1) p. 128 'differendi' of the first transcription corrected to 'disserendi' (the glyph is long ſſ, identical to 'omiſſâ' in the same line); (2) p. 130 'non recte eum moveri dico' first rendered as a denial ('I do not rightly say'), corrected to the rhetorical question the argument requires; (3) p. 129 'tuo te iure abuterer' first rendered 'I would abuse your law', corrected to 'I would use you up by your own right' (te is the object).
