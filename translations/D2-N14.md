# De Mundo — Dialogue II (De forma mundi, seu de motu magnorum corporum), Knot xiv

*Latin title:* Nodus XIV. Falsam esse motus circularis suppositionem  
*English title:* Knot XIV. That the supposition [suppositionem] of circular motion is false  
*Pages:* PDF pp. 270–272 = printed pp. 259–261 (from Ereunius’ ‘Oblitus eram malorum…’ on p. 259 to Andabata’s ‘…supra invidiam collocatae?’ on p. 261, where Knot xv begins).

## Dialogue II, Knot xiv (printed pp. 259–261)

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| [p. 259] [Ereu., continuing] Oblitus eram malorum si non maximum, certe ingens ab ipsismet Astronomis sibi arcessitum: Bulimiam dico quandam omnia Phaenomena per circulos explicandi. [Margin: Nodus XIV. Falsam esse motus circularis suppositionem.] Quod opus cum rudis adhuc foret scientia, incepta est foeliciter, Anomaliis videlicet nondum detectis. Sed post eas detectas eandem velle continuare extremae est dementiae. | [p. 259] [Ereu., continuing] I had forgotten, of evils [malorum], [one] if not the greatest, certainly huge, fetched [arcessitum] upon themselves by the Astronomers themselves: I mean [dico] a certain Bulimia [Bulimiam] of explaining all Phaenomena through circles. [Margin: Knot XIV. That the supposition [suppositionem] of circular motion is false.] Which work [Quod opus], when the science was still rude, was begun [incepta est] fortunately [foeliciter], the Anomalies [Anomaliis], namely, not yet having been detected. But after these [were] detected, to want to continue the same [eandem] is [the part] of extreme madness [dementiae]. |
| Tum quia falsum apparenter assumitur sine probatione vel potius cum evidentia oppositae veritatis, tum quia impossibile factu est, & tandem quia Astronomiam difficilem, non intelligibilem, & intractabilem facit. Postquam enim Coelestes orbes esse nullos tot experimentis & tanta patuit evidentia. Celeriores etiam alibi syderum cursus, alibi tardiores esse demonstratum: alibi rursus a centro motus [p. 260] sui elatiores, alibi ad idem depressiores esse: quis ulterius locus superest credendi motus eorum esse uniformes & circulares? | Both [Tum] because a false [thing] is apparently [apparenter] assumed without proof, or rather with the evidence [evidentia] of the opposite truth; and [tum] because it is impossible to do [factu]; and finally because it makes Astronomy difficult, not intelligible, and intractable. For after [Postquam] it became plain [patuit] by so many experiments [experimentis] and so great evidence that the Celestial orbs [orbes] are none [esse nullos]; [after it was] demonstrated also that the courses of the stars [syderum] are in some places [alibi] swifter, in others slower; again, that in some places [they are] more raised [elatiores] from the centre of their [p. 260] motion, in others more depressed [depressiores] toward the same: what further place remains for believing that their motions are uniform and circular? |
| Argumenta vero quibus nixi ad hoc contenderunt, ex eo praecipue desumpta sunt, quod in corporibus tam nobilibus & aeternis nihil irregulariter, nihil praeter summam perfectionem expectari possit. Sed haec Idolomaniam sydera Deos existimantium vehementer olent. | But the arguments, leaning on which [quibus nixi] they contended for [ad] this, have been taken [desumpta sunt] chiefly from this, that in bodies so noble and eternal nothing irregularly [irregulariter], nothing beyond [praeter] the highest perfection, can be expected. But these [things] smell strongly [vehementer olent] of the Idolomania [Idolomaniam] of those reckoning [existimantium] the stars [to be] Gods. |
| Nullo praeterea cum robore fixum existat ea corpora quicquam nostro telluris globo nobilius antiquiusve obtinere: Immo nostrum si coniecturae valeant vel praestantissimum vel ex praestantissimis sit. Certe postquam hominum sedi destinatus est, quos plerique finem omnium existimant, ab iis omnium pretiosissimus, computari deberet. | Moreover with no strength [robore] would it stand fixed [fixum existat] that those bodies possess anything nobler or more ancient than our globe of earth [telluris]: nay, ours, if conjectures avail, would be [sit] either the most excellent or [one] of the most excellent. Certainly, since [postquam] it has been destined for the seat of men, whom most [people] reckon the end [finem] of all [things], by them [ab iis] [it] ought to be reckoned [computari] the most precious of all. |
| Rursus detur motus aliquis continua velocitatis mutatione perturbatus, quisquamne adeo demens est ut hunc pluribus circulis constanter latis imitabilem credat? Cum circuli numerati debeant esse, mutatio vero proportionis velocitatum infinite nova. | Again, let some motion be given [detur], disturbed [perturbatus] by a continual change of speed [velocitatis]: is anyone so mad that he should believe this imitable by several [pluribus] circles constantly carried [latis]? Since the circles must be numbered [numerati], but the change of the proportion of the speeds [is] infinitely new. |
| Astronomiam vero quibus computis comparationibusque repleat caeci vident. Praeter haec, syderis motus qui ex tot machinarum simultanea congressione pendet & non aliter explicatur, ignotus prorsus redditur: & res Astronomicae quasi barbara lingua descriptae latent: & audens dixerim hanc [p. 261] non levissimam esse causam cur non maioribus haec scientia profecerit augmentis. Sed quid tot & tantos Mathematicos induxerit, ut contra usum disciplinae rem non probatam pro constanti & certa supposuerint, ipsi edicant, mihi enim miraculi loco est. | But with what computations and comparisons it fills Astronomy, the blind see. Besides these [things], the motion of a star, which hangs on the simultaneous concourse [congressione] of so many machines [machinarum] and is not otherwise explicated, is rendered wholly unknown: and Astronomical matters [res] lie hidden as if described in a barbarous tongue: and daring [audens] I would say [dixerim] that this [p. 261] is not the lightest cause why this science has not advanced [profecerit] by greater increases [augmentis]. But what induced [induxerit] so many and so great Mathematicians that, against the usage of the discipline, they supposed [supposuerint] a thing not proved for constant and certain, let them themselves declare [edicant]; for to me it is in the place of a miracle. |
| Asphal. Suspicor te ieiunum, Ereuni, longa hac disputatione esse fatigatum, & ad descensum in terram ex longa navigatione properare. Quare si placet dimittamus impraesentiarum Concilium, & de reliquis Planetis post meridiem (si Andabatae licuerit) te rursus audiemus. | Asphal. I suspect that you, fasting [ieiunum], Ereunius, have been fatigued by this long disputation, and are hastening to the descent onto land [in terram] from a long voyage. Wherefore, if it pleases, let us dismiss at present [impraesentiarum] the Council [Concilium], and concerning the remaining Planets after midday (if it shall have been permitted [licuerit] to Andabata) we shall hear you again. |
| Andab. Si mihi licuerit, cur non aeque liceat ac comedere, dormire, vivere? his enim mihi charius omnibus est. Nescio enim in hac vita quid sit pretiosius quod in ea morari nos suadeat, quam huiusmodi contemplationes innocentes, dulces, supra invidiam collocatae? | Andab. If it shall have been permitted [licuerit] to me, why should it not be permitted equally as [aeque … ac] to eat, to sleep, to live? for to me [it] is dearer than all these. For I do not know what in this life is more precious, which should persuade us to tarry [morari] in it, than contemplations of this kind, innocent, sweet, placed above envy? |

## Words and phrases with alternative or special meanings

- ***Bulimia*** — rendered “Bulimia [Bulimiam]”. ox-hunger, morbid insatiable appetite (Greek βουλιμία); figuratively an uncontrollable craving A medical term (Galenic) used satirically: astronomers’ insatiable craving to explain every phenomenon by circles.
- ***Phaenomena*** — rendered “Phaenomena”. appearances; observed celestial data The ancient programme of ‘saving the phenomena’ (σῴζειν τὰ φαινόμενα) by uniform circular motions, attacked here.
- ***Anomalia*** — rendered “Anomaly [Anomaliis]”. irregularity; technically the angular inequality of a planet’s motion Technical astronomical term (Ptolemaic/Copernican first and second anomaly), not merely ‘oddity’.
- ***Quod opus … incepta est*** — rendered “Which work … was begun”. Which [thing], since it was needful (quod opus [erat]), … was begun Gender discord: incepta (fem.) agrees not with opus but with Bulimia (cf. eandem following). Rendered literally; see notes.
- ***apparenter*** — rendered “apparently [apparenter]”. manifestly, evidently; or ‘in appearance only’ Scholastic Latin often uses apparenter = ‘evidently’; here the sense is probably ‘a manifest falsehood is assumed’.
- ***factu (supine)*** — rendered “to do [factu]”. to be done, to perform impossibile factu: the hypothesis cannot physically be carried out (no solid orbs).
- ***orbes coelestes … nullos*** — rendered “the Celestial orbs are none”. there are no celestial spheres Tychonic/Galilean conclusion (from comets and novae) that solid spheres do not exist; cf. D1-N09–N10.
- ***elatiores / depressiores*** — rendered “more raised / more depressed”. farther from / nearer to the centre; higher / lower Variation of planetary distance from the centre of motion (apsides), against uniform circularity.
- ***Idolomania*** — rendered “Idolomania [Idolomaniam]”. idol-madness, idolatry White’s coinage-like Graecism: the perfection-of-the-heavens argument smells of pagan star-worship.
- ***nullo cum robore fixum existat*** — rendered “with no strength would it stand fixed”. it is established on no firm ground; it is in no way proved existat is subjunctive (potential); robur = firmness of proof. White denies the superiority of the heavens over the earth.
- ***finis omnium*** — rendered “the end of all [things]”. the purpose/final cause of all Anthropocentric final-cause doctrine (man as end of the corporeal world), cited as common opinion (plerique).
- ***circuli constanter lati*** — rendered “circles constantly carried”. circles uniformly moved; circles borne steadily latus from ferre: the uniform rotation of deferents and epicycles; a finite number cannot imitate an infinitely varying speed.
- ***numerati*** — rendered “numbered [numerati]”. finite in number, countable Contrast with infinite nova: a finite set of circles vs. continuous change.
- ***caeci vident*** — rendered “the blind see”. it is obvious even to the blind Proverbial (cf. ‘vel caeco appareat’).
- ***machinae / congressio*** — rendered “machines / concourse”. contraptions (the circle-devices); meeting, combination Mechanical image for compounded epicycles and deferents.
- ***audens dixerim*** — rendered “daring I would say”. I would venture to say Potential perfect subjunctive, common modest formula.
- ***miraculi loco est*** — rendered “it is in the place of a miracle”. it is a marvel to me Idiom: loco + gen. = ‘serves as’, ‘counts as’.
- ***Concilium*** — rendered “Council [Concilium]”. assembly, gathering, session Playful: the three speakers’ conversation as a formal council.
- ***licuerit (si Andabatae licuerit)*** — rendered “if it shall have been permitted to Andabata”. if Andabata agrees / allows it Andabata turns the courtesy into a joke: philosophizing should be as ‘permitted’ as eating and sleeping.
- ***ieiunus*** — rendered “fasting [ieiunum]”. hungry; also ‘jejune, dry’ The dialogue time is morning; Ereunius has not eaten; the sea-voyage image for the long argument follows.

## Translator's notes

- Unit: Dialogue II, Knot xiv (complete in one part). PDF pp. 270–272 = printed pp. 259–261. Offset +11 holds (running heads 259, 260, 261). Begins within Ereunius’ speech at ‘Oblitus eram malorum…’ (margin ‘Nodus XIV. Falsam esse motus circularis suppositione[m]’, p. 259); ends with Andabata’s speech ‘…supra invidiam collocatae?’ on p. 261, where Knot xv (margin ‘Nodus XV. Caeterorum planetarum ratio[nes] ex dictis aestimari posse’) begins with ‘Ereu. Nolite me in novam vocare operam.’ The FM2 table title (‘Falsam esse circularis motus suppositionem’) has the words in a different order from the margin.
- The signature at the foot of p. 261 appears to read ‘Kk ij’ again (p. 259 also carries ‘Kk ij’); presumably a misprint for ‘Kk iij’, or a misreading of the faint scan.
- Uncertain points: (1) ‘Quod opus cum rudis adhuc foret scientia, incepta est’ — incepta (fem.) does not agree with opus; it agrees with Bulimia (as does the following eandem). Possibly ‘quod opus [erat]’ = ‘which [being] needful’; rendered literally as ‘Which work’. (2) ‘Nullo … cum robore fixum existat’ — taken as potential subjunctive (‘would it stand fixed with no strength’ = it cannot be firmly established); it could also be read as a loose indicative sense (‘it stands fixed with no strength’). (3) ‘Postquam enim … patuit evidentia.’ — the full stop after evidentia is printed but the postquam-clause continues into ‘Celeriores … esse demonstratum’ and the main clause ‘quis ulterius locus superest…?’; punctuation kept as printed, translation treats it as one sentence. (4) ‘apparenter’ — ‘apparently’ kept; ‘manifestly’ is likelier in sense.
- Verification pass: 3 corrections after the sentence-by-sentence check — (1) ‘demonstratum’ re-attached to the postquam-clause (‘[after it was] demonstrated’) instead of an independent ‘has been demonstrated’; (2) ‘suadeat’ given characteristic force (‘which should persuade’) instead of ‘might’; (3) ‘sit’ in the si-clause apodosis rendered ‘would be’ (subjunctive) instead of ‘is’. Transcription re-checked against a 300-dpi crop of p. 260 (robore, Idolomaniam, latis, caeci [printed ‘cęci’]).
- Conventions: as in earlier units — long s normalised, æ/œ/ę → ae/oe, accents dropped, u/v normalised to v, nõ → non, suppositionē → suppositionem; foeliciter, sydera, charius kept as printed; supplied words in [square brackets]; Latin of technical or ambiguous words in [brackets].
