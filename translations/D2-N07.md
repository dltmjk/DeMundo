# De Mundo — Dialogue II (De forma mundi, seu de motu magnorum corporum), Knot vii

## Dialogue II, Knot vii (printed pp. 196–201)

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| [p. 196] Ereu. Ardua est haec difficultas, Asphali, quam ut enodem, sic accipe. Inveniunt Astronomi decessorum suorum calculis per singulas aetates comparatis, aequinoctium in succedentibus ex ordine Zodiaci signis ea lege contingere, ut centum fere annis unum gradum in [p. 197] consequentia signorum proficiat, ab Ariete versus Taurum, &c. Hoc unde contingat, illi non definiunt. Natum autem est ex duabus causis originem trahere. Nam & potest usuvenisse ut ea telluris pars, quae olim aequales nobis dies ferebat, non eodem modo nunc sese habeat; & potest fieri ut immotus telluris aequator sub alio iam signo soli sit perpendiculariter obversus, quam sub quo erat solitus. Hoc posterius si acciderit, Axis telluris paulatim fit axi Zodiaci parallelus, & inclinatur quotannis ad faciendum maiorem & maiorem angulum, cum supposito mundi axe. Unde non Mathematice, sed ad sensum dumtaxat est eidem parallelus. Priorem si sequar modum, eaedem telluris partes eandem orbis partem semper intuebantur, sed diurnus motus super alio, & alio axe quotidie fiet, sed lentissima variatione: Horum autem duorum modorum, uter sequendus sit, Astronomia consulenda est. [Margin: Nodus VII. Unde sit praeventio Aequinoctiorum.] | [p. 196] Ereu. Hard is this difficulty, Asphalius; [but] that I may unknot [enodem] it, take [it] thus. The Astronomers find, the calculations of their predecessors [decessorum] through the single ages having been compared, that the equinox happens in the signs of the Zodiac succeeding in order by such a law [lege], that in about a hundred years it advances [proficiat] one degree in [p. 197] the consequence [consequentia] of the signs, from the Ram towards the Bull, &c. Whence this happens, they do not define. But it is apt by nature [natum est] to draw [its] origin from two causes. For both it can have come to pass [usuvenisse] that that part of the earth [telluris] which formerly bore equal days for us, does not now hold itself in the same mode [modo]; and it can come about that the unmoved equator of the earth [telluris] is now turned perpendicularly towards the sun under another sign than [that] under which it was accustomed. If this latter should have happened, the Axis of the earth [telluris] little by little becomes parallel to the axis of the Zodiac, and is inclined yearly to making a greater and greater angle with the supposed [supposito] axis of the world. Whence not Mathematically, but to sense [ad sensum] only, is it parallel to the same. If I should follow the former mode, the same parts of the earth [telluris] were [intuebantur] always looking at the same part of the orb [orbis], but the diurnal motion will be made every day upon another and another axis, but with a most slow variation: but of these two modes, which is to be followed, Astronomy is to be consulted. [Margin: Knot VII. Whence is the anticipation [praeventio] of the Equinoxes.] |
| Asphal. At ex iis quae hactenus prodidisti principia, utri horum nativitatem foelicius auguraris? | Asphal. But from those [things] which up to now you have put forth [prodidisti] [as] principles [principia], to which of these do you more happily augur [auguraris] a birth [nativitatem]? |
| Ereu. Posterioris modi nullam fere rationem ineo. Manente enim eodem aquarum fluxu axem inclinari perdifficile mihi est; non enim sine resistentia aliqua, vel in ipso corpore, vel in occurrente medio talem muta-[p. 198]tionem intelligere valemus: hanc autem cum ratione quomodo fingam non invenio. Posteriorem vero modum ab ipsa natura haurio. Nihil siquidem notius est quam aquas ipso suo discursu alios alveos implere, alios arefacere, & decubitu in unum latus aequore tellurem commutare: Fingatur itaque Oceanus circulum suum quo stringit humanis substratum pedibus solum, ingenti temporis lapsu Septentrioni magis subiicere, non necesse erit ad fluminis inclinationem etiam cursus diurni cingulum variari, & quod sequitur, Polos & aequatorem? Aequator enim non aliud est, quam linea, Polis, super quibus centrum circumvehitur, media. Temporis autem legitimum est spatium, cum enim centesimo cuique anno unus in longitudinem distribuitur gradus, vix millesimo cuique in latitudinem gradus unus respondebit: praesertim in principiis ubi adhuc sumus. Accedit quod Galilaeus nescio quid de meridianorum mutabilitate notatum esse meminit, quod si concorditer huic coniecturae acciderit, rem factam habemus. Sed & de puteis Syenes incertam retineo memoriam, modernos aliud quidpiam ab antiquorum assertionibus reperisse. Verum tam incerta vel meminisse nimium est. | Ereu. Of the latter mode I enter into [ineo] almost no reckoning [rationem]. For, the same flux [fluxu] of the waters remaining, that the axis is inclined is very difficult for me; for we are not able to understand such a change [muta-[p. 198]tionem] without some resistance [resistentia], either in the body itself, or in the medium meeting [it]: but how I may feign [fingam] this with reason [cum ratione] I do not find. But the latter [Posteriorem] mode I draw from nature itself. For nothing is better known than that the waters by their very running-about [discursu] fill some channels, dry up others, and by [their] lying-down [decubitu] towards one side change [commutare] the earth [tellurem] in [its] level [aequore]: let it be feigned [fingatur] therefore that the Ocean, in a huge lapse of time, subjects more to the North its circle, with which it binds [stringit] the ground laid under human feet: will it not be necessary that, at the inclination of the stream [fluminis], the girdle [cingulum] of the diurnal course also be varied, and, what follows, the Poles and the equator? For the Equator is nothing other than the line midway [media] between the Poles, upon which the centre is carried round. And the space of time is lawful [legitimum]; for since to each hundredth year one degree is distributed in longitude, scarcely to each thousandth will one degree in latitude correspond: especially in the principles [principiis] where we still are. It is added that Galileo mentions that something or other [nescio quid] has been noted concerning the mutability of the meridians, which, if it should have happened concordantly with this conjecture, we have the thing done [rem factam]. But also concerning the wells of Syene I retain an uncertain memory, that the moderns have found something other than the assertions of the ancients. But even to have remembered things so uncertain is too much. |
| Asphal. Non satis capio quae prodis: videtur enim quod dicis adhuc ambigui quip-[p. 199]piam in sese claudere. Inquiro proinde cum nova pars telluris fit aequinoctialis, an soli sese ostendit sub eodem signo Zodiaci an sub alio? | Asphal. I do not sufficiently grasp what you put forth [prodis]: for what you say seems still to shut in itself something [quip-[p. 199]piam] of ambiguity. I inquire accordingly, when a new part of the earth [telluris] becomes equinoctial, whether it shows itself to the sun under the same sign of the Zodiac or under another? |
| Ereu. Sub eodem prorsus, & ex eo fit praecessio quam dicunt aequinoctiorum, immutata manente, quoad locum, tellure, ut semper centrum telluris eodem haereat mundi puncto, seu in eadem distantia a magnis corporibus quam prius obtinebat. Haec enim mutatio cum in superficie fiat, ipsum totum corpus loco non exigit. | Ereu. Under the same altogether, and from that comes about the precession [praecessio], as they call [it], of the equinoxes, the earth [tellure] remaining unchanged as regards place, so that the centre of the earth [telluris] always sticks at the same point of the world, or in the same distance from the great bodies which it held before. For this change, since it is made in the surface, does not drive [exigit] the whole body itself out of [its] place. |
| Andab. εὕρηκα, εὕρηκα. | Andab. Heureka, heureka [εὕρηκα, ‘I have found [it]’]. |
| Ereu. Deus bone! boni quid portas? | Ereu. Good God! what good [thing] do you bring? |
| Andab. Video iam cur non trepidet terra ad subsultationem pulicis. Cum enim per additionem ponderis tantilli clarum sit graviorem esse ex hac parte terram quam prius erat, si certum orbis punctum virtutem centralem aliquam haberet, manifeste tellus aliquantulum cederet, ut centrum gravitatis locum quoque medium occuparet. Nunc postquam centrum terrae illud est punctum quodcumque a caeteris partibus telluris iusta dimensione absistit, mutatur denominative centrum ad pulicis motum, absque eo quod loco quicquam solito emoveatur. | Andab. I see now why the earth [terra] does not tremble at the leaping-up [subsultationem] of a flea. For since, through the addition of so little a weight, it is clear that the earth [terram] is heavier on this side than it was before, if a certain point of the orb [orbis] had some central virtue [virtutem], manifestly the earth [tellus] would yield a little, so that the centre of gravity might also occupy the middle place. Now, since [postquam] the centre of the earth [terrae] is that point, whatever [it be], which stands off [absistit] by a just dimension from the other parts of the earth [telluris], the centre is changed denominatively [denominative] at the motion of the flea, without [absque eo quod] anything being moved out [emoveatur] from [its] accustomed place. |
| Asphal. Ingenti nos metu liberasti, sed cave vulges, pulices enim si rescierint [p. 200] quanta eos gloria spoliaveris, infestos patieris. | Asphal. You have freed us from a huge fear; but beware lest you make [it] public [vulges]: for if the fleas should find out [rescierint] [p. 200] of how great a glory you have despoiled them, you will suffer [them] hostile. |
| Ereu. Interea vide, Andabatam non leve nobis argumentum exhibuisse quo constet non esse locum aliquem sua natura centripotentem: corpus siquidem in eo collocatum perpetuo tremore febriret. | Ereu. Meanwhile see, that Andabata has shown us no light argument, by which it may be established that there is not any place by its own nature centre-powerful [centripotentem]: for a body placed in it would be fevered [febriret] with a perpetual trembling. |
| Asphal. Non vidisti fortasse Gillebrandum Greshami in urbe Mathematicum, quod nihil de meridianorum mutatione magnetis indicio patefacta exponis? | Asphal. Have you perhaps not seen Gillebrand [Gillebrandum], the Mathematician of Gresham in the city, since you set out nothing about the change of the meridians laid open [patefacta] by the indication [indicio] of the magnet? |
| Ereu. Vidi, sed non habeo quid certi de libello hoc affirmem. Animadversio diligens est, sed non ausim tantam molem tam paucarum animadversionum fundamento fidere. Ut magnes eodem loco tam paucis annis tot graduum deflexionem patiatur? aut mea me principia in errorem trahunt, aut tanta ex ea magnetis instabilitate naturae secutura foret varietas, ut prorsus tot saecula latere nequaquam potuisset. Ea itaque omissa, notant Astronomi etiam anomaliam quandam in hoc progressu, ut aliquando celerius, aliquando lentius promoveatur. Notant etiam summas Aequatoris & Zodiaci telluris distantias interdum variare. Sed quoniam haec levissimis differentiis, puta minutorum aliquot in pluribus saeculis consistunt: sunt autem Astronomicae vel observationes vel scripta subiecta [p. 201] multis casibus, nobis tutius est huiusmodi effectorum causis quaerendis supersedere, quam dubiorum, & fortassis etiam falsorum investigationem inaniter persequi. Cum itaque haec irrefragabilibus experimentis erunt percepta, nihil est magnae difficultatis eis causas statuere. Interea de ipsis effectis litem decidant Astronomi. Quod autem de vi magnetica e Gilberto meministi, mera coniectura erat. Satis autem ex praedictis constat nullam vim magneticam requiri, ad hoc ut terra directionem semel sibi positam observet, sed magis ne sit causa aliqua removens, ut explicatum est. Potior est Galilaei ab aqua in pelui ducta comparatio, quae si in aliquo deficit (ut omnes necesse est) in principali certe congruit. Et quoniam his expedita manet sive telluris sive solis via, sermonem ad reliquos, si placet, planetas convertamus. | Ereu. I have seen [it], but I have not anything certain that I may affirm about this little book. The observation [animadversio] is diligent, but I would not dare to trust so great a bulk [molem] to the foundation of so few observations. That a magnet in the same place in so few years should suffer a deflexion of so many degrees? Either my principles drag me into error, or from that instability of the magnet so great a variety of nature would have been going to follow, that it could by no means altogether have lain hidden for so many ages. This therefore being omitted, the Astronomers note also a certain anomaly in this progress, so that sometimes it is moved forward more quickly, sometimes more slowly. They note also that the greatest [summas] distances of the Equator and of the Zodiac of the earth [telluris] sometimes vary. But since these consist in the slightest differences, say [puta] of some minutes in several ages: and Astronomical either observations or writings are subject [p. 201] to many chances [casibus], it is safer for us to forbear [supersedere] from seeking the causes of effects of this kind, than vainly to pursue the investigation of doubtful, and perhaps even false, [effects]. When therefore these shall have been perceived by irrefragable experiments [experimentis], there is nothing of great difficulty in establishing [statuere] causes for them. Meanwhile let the Astronomers decide the lawsuit [litem] about the effects themselves. But what you mentioned about the magnetic force [vi] from Gilbert, was mere conjecture. But it is sufficiently established from the things said before that no magnetic force [vim] is required for this, that the earth [terra] should keep [observet] the direction once set for itself, but rather that there be not some removing cause, as has been explicated. Preferable is Galileo's comparison drawn from water in a basin [pelui], which, if it falls short [deficit] in something (as all [comparisons] must), certainly agrees in the principal [point]. And since by these [things] the way, whether of the earth [telluris] or of the sun, remains cleared [expedita], let us turn the conversation [sermonem], if it pleases, to the remaining planets. |
| Andab. Si haec sic auferas, non ego sim Andabata, cum ab ipso Aristotele & Ptolemaeo & tot doctissimis non minus quam ingeniosissimis viris, infinitae congestae sint in oppositum demonstrationes, tum Mathematicae, tum Physicae: tu expectas non solutis argumentis simplici τῷ (αὐτὸς ἔφα) haec tibi sepulturae trophaea recondere? | Andab. If you should carry these [things] off thus, may I not be Andabata, since by Aristotle himself and Ptolemy and so many most learned, no less than most witty [ingeniosissimis], men, infinite demonstrations have been heaped up to the opposite, both Mathematical and Physical: do you expect, the arguments not having been solved, by a simple ‘he himself said’ [τῷ (αὐτὸς ἔφα)] to lay up [recondere] for yourself these trophies of burial [sepulturae]? |

## Words and phrases with alternative or special meanings

- ***praeventio / praecessio (Aequinoctiorum)*** — rendered “anticipation [praeventio] / precession [praecessio]”. praeventio: a coming-before, forestalling; praecessio: a going-before; also praeversio (‘outrunning’) in knot vi. White uses three words for the precession of the equinoxes; kept distinct in English so the reader can see which he uses where (praecessio is introduced with ‘quam dicunt’, i.e. as the astronomers' technical term).
- ***enodare*** — rendered “unknot [enodem]”. explain, untie, resolve. Pun on nodus, White's section-unit (‘Knot’).
- ***in consequentia signorum*** — rendered “in the consequence [consequentia] of the signs”. in the order of the signs (eastward, Aries → Taurus), as opposed to in antecedentia. Technical astronomical phrase. Note that White makes the equinox move Aries → Taurus, i.e. eastward, which is really the apparent eastward motion of the stars relative to the equinox.
- ***natum est (+ inf.)*** — rendered “it is apt by nature [natum est]”. it is wont, it naturally can. Scholastic idiom (aptum natum); glossary rendering kept.
- ***usuvenire*** — rendered “come to pass [usuvenisse]”. happen in practice (usu venire). Printed as one word.
- ***ad sensum*** — rendered “to sense”. perceptibly, sensibly, approximately (vs ‘Mathematice’, exactly). Standard contrast between physical approximation and geometrical exactness.
- ***orbis*** — rendered “orb [orbis]”. the earth's orbit (annual circle), or the world/globe. Here ‘eandem orbis partem’ most likely = the same part of the annual circle (or of the heavens); ‘certum orbis punctum’ in Andabata's speech = a point of the (terrestrial) globe.
- ***rationem inire*** — rendered “enter into a reckoning [rationem]”. take account of, find a way/method for. Accounting idiom; Ereunius means he sees no plausible way for the axis to tilt.
- ***cum ratione fingere*** — rendered “feign with reason [cum ratione]”. frame (a hypothesis) reasonably. fingere = frame hypotheses (glossary).
- ***discursus (aquarum)*** — rendered “running-about [discursu]”. course, flowing to and fro. Cf. glossary ‘running-about’ for planetary discursus.
- ***decubitus*** — rendered “lying-down [decubitu]”. settling, subsidence, leaning. Water settling to one side shifts the earth's balance of surface — White's mechanism for a shifting equator.
- ***aequor*** — rendered “level [aequore]”. level surface, sea. Etymological sense kept (glossary).
- ***flumen*** — rendered “stream [fluminis]”. current (here of the Ocean), river. Glossary: current or river.
- ***cingulum (cursus diurni)*** — rendered “girdle [cingulum]”. zone, belt; i.e. the equator as the circle of daily rotation.
- ***legitimus (spatium temporis)*** — rendered “lawful [legitimum]”. sufficient, proper, according to rule. Roman-law/term-of-time sense: the time available is adequate.
- ***in principiis*** — rendered “in the principles [principiis]”. in the beginnings (of the world's history), or in [our] first principles. Ambiguous: either ‘we are still at the beginning’ (of the world, or of observations) or ‘in the elements of the inquiry’.
- ***rem factam habere*** — rendered “have the thing done”. the matter is settled; we have it. Idiom of completion.
- ***putei Syenes*** — rendered “the wells of Syene”.  Classical allusion: the well at Syene (Aswan) where the noon sun shone vertically at the summer solstice (Eratosthenes; Strabo XVII.1.48; Pliny NH II.183). Modern travellers' reports of a change would imply a change in obliquity or latitude.
- ***Galilaeus … de meridianorum mutabilitate*** — rendered “Galileo … concerning the mutability of the meridians”.  Galileo (Dialogo, Day 4, end) suggests that if the earth's surface shifted, meridian lines and latitudes might be found altered over time; White uses it as possible support.
- ***εὕρηκα*** — rendered “Heureka”. ‘I have found [it]’. Archimedes' cry (Vitruvius IX pr. 10), apt since Andabata has found the centre of gravity.
- ***subsultatio*** — rendered “leaping-up [subsultationem]”. hop, jump. Rare noun.
- ***virtus centralis / centripotens*** — rendered “central virtue [virtutem] / centre-powerful [centripotentem]”. an inherent power of a place to be the centre. White denies Aristotelian natural place as an active ‘centre’; centripotens appears to be his coinage.
- ***denominative*** — rendered “denominatively [denominative]”. by extrinsic denomination, in name only. Scholastic: the centre changes by relation, not by any real motion.
- ***absque eo quod*** — rendered “without”. without its being that … Scholastic connective (glossary).
- ***absistere*** — rendered “stand off [absistit]”. be distant. Glossary keeps distare/abesse/absistere apart.
- ***vulgare*** — rendered “make public [vulges]”. divulge.
- ***febrire*** — rendered “be fevered [febriret]”. shake as with fever. Rare verb; comic image continuing the flea joke.
- ***Gillebrandus Greshami in urbe Mathematicus*** — rendered “Gillebrand, the Mathematician of Gresham in the city”.  Henry Gellibrand, Gresham Professor of Astronomy in London, whose Discourse Mathematical on the Variation of the Magneticall Needle (1635) announced the secular change of magnetic declination. ‘in urbe’ = in the City (of London). ‘meridianorum mutatio’ here = change of the magnetic meridians.
- ***animadversio*** — rendered “observation [animadversio]”. notice, remark; also censure. Here empirical observation.
- ***moles*** — rendered “bulk [molem]”. mass, weighty structure (here a theory). Glossary rendering kept.
- ***anomalia*** — rendered “anomaly”. irregularity of a celestial motion. Trepidation/variable rate of precession, as in the Alfonsine and Copernican models.
- ***summae distantiae Aequatoris & Zodiaci*** — rendered “the greatest [summas] distances of the Equator and of the Zodiac”. maximum separation = obliquity of the ecliptic. Change in obliquity, a disputed observational question in White's day.
- ***puta*** — rendered “say [puta]”. for example. Imperative of putare used adverbially.
- ***experimentum*** — rendered “experiment [experimentis]”. experience, observation. Glossary: = experience, observation.
- ***litem decidere*** — rendered “decide the lawsuit [litem]”. settle the dispute. Forensic metaphor.
- ***causa removens*** — rendered “removing cause”. a cause that removes (the earth's direction). Scholastic removens prohibens; White's inertial account of the axis' parallelism (knot vi).
- ***Galilaei … ab aqua in pelui ducta comparatio*** — rendered “Galileo's comparison drawn from water in a basin [pelui]”.  Dialogo, Day 3: a ball floating in a basin of water carried round keeps its orientation, illustrating why the earth's axis stays parallel.
- ***expeditus*** — rendered “cleared [expedita]”. freed from obstacles, settled.
- ***non ego sim Andabata*** — rendered “may I not be Andabata”.  Asseverative self-curse; Andabata (a blindfolded gladiator) plays on his own name.
- ***τῷ (αὐτὸς ἔφα)*** — rendered “by a ‘he himself said’”. ipse dixit. The Pythagoreans' appeal to authority (Cicero, ND I.10), Doric ἔφα; Andabata turns it against Ereunius' appeal to Galileo.
- ***sepulturae trophaea recondere*** — rendered “to lay up … trophies of burial”. to bury (the opposing arguments) and set up trophies over them. Mixed military-funerary image; sense somewhat uncertain.

## Translator's notes

- Unit: Dialogue II, Knot vii (complete). PDF pp. 207–212 = printed pp. 196–201, from ‘Ereu. Ardua est haec difficultas, Asphali’ (p. 196, beside the marginal ‘Nodus VII. Unde sit praeventio Aequinoctiorum’) to Andabata's ‘…sepulturae trophaea recondere?’ (p. 201). The table of nodi gives knot viii at p. 202, but the marginal ‘Nodus VIII. Aerem usque ad confinia lunae a terra circumferri’ stands on p. 201 beside ‘Ereu. Habes Galilaei systema’ (running onto p. 202); following the book, knot viii starts there. Note that the marginal title is fuller than the table's (‘usque ad confinia lunae’).
- Offset +11 holds (running heads 196–201; signatures Bb iij p. 197, Cc p. 201).
- Uncertain or corrected readings: (1) p. 197 ‘intuebantur’ (imperfect) is printed where sense wants future ‘intuebuntur’ (parallel to ‘fiet’); translated as printed, ‘were looking’. (2) p. 198 ‘Posteriorem vero modum ab ipsa natura haurio’: after rejecting the ‘latter’ (axis-tilting) mode, Ereunius here describes the former (water-shifting) mode; ‘Posteriorem’ is probably a slip for ‘Priorem’; translated as printed. (3) p. 199 ‘reſcierint’ normalised as rescierint (= resciverint). (4) p. 201 ‘Qod’ printed, normalised to Quod. (5) The Greek on p. 199 is printed without proper accents (‘εὐρηκα’); normalised εὕρηκα. (6) p. 201 Greek ‘τῷ (αὐτὸς ἔφα)’ read from a 300-dpi crop. (7) p. 198 ‘Fingatur … Oceanus … subiicere, non necesse erit …?’ — the sentence is printed as a question (‘aequatorem ?’); the opening ‘Fingatur’ clause is construed as a protasis.
- Verification pass: the Latin was re-read sentence by sentence against the page images (150 dpi, with 300-dpi crops of the Greek and the margin on p. 201) and against the English. 6 corrections: (1) ‘decessorum’ rendered ‘predecessors’ (not ‘successors’); (2) ‘quam sub quo erat solitus’ — ‘than [that] under which’ supplied; (3) ‘postquam’ in Andabata's speech rendered causal ‘since’ rather than temporal ‘after’; (4) ‘secutura foret’ rendered ‘would have been going to follow’ (future participle + subj.) rather than ‘would follow’; (5) ‘patefacta’ construed with ‘mutatione’ (abl.) — ‘change … laid open’; (6) ‘nequaquam potuisset’ — ‘could by no means … have lain hidden’ restored ‘by no means’, which the draft had dropped.
- Conventions: as in the glossary — tellus ‘earth [tellus]’ and terra ‘earth [terra]’ bracketed throughout; supplied words in square brackets; page breaks marked [p. n] inside the row where they fall.
